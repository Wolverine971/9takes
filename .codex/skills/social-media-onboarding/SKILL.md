# Social Media Onboarding

Build the orientation model that ongoing social-media decisions will use.

The deliverable is not a questionnaire transcript or a generic brand exercise. It is an evidence-aware onboarding dossier that explains who the account is, where it is going, what environment it occupies, how it intends to compete, what it can realistically do, and which decisions remain open.

## Relationship to Social Media SLAM and OODA

Onboarding and ongoing operation occur at different speeds:

- **Onboarding** establishes the durable orientation: identity, destination, audience states, credibility, return promise, strategic posture, current capabilities and edges, terrain, guardrails, and initial beliefs.
- **Social Media SLAM** runs the faster operating loop: observe new signals, orient using this baseline, decide the next move, act when authorized, and learn from the result.

Use `$social-media-slam` after the baseline is approved. Do not rerun full onboarding for every account change; refresh only the sections invalidated by a material change in identity, goals, audience, offer, platform, capacity, or environment.

## Non-negotiable distinctions

- Preserve the owner's actual language, convictions, tensions, and boundaries. Do not polish them into an interchangeable brand voice.
- Separate `owner-stated`, `observed`, `inferred`, `provisional`, and `unknown` throughout the dossier.
- Distinguish desired audience from current audience, ambition from capacity, strategy from tactics, and evidence from attractive guesses.
- Treat content as the audience-facing vehicle, not the whole growth mechanism. Distinguish exposure, attention, resonance, transmission, relationship, and destination outcomes so a large response is not mistaken for strategic fit.
- “What will win” is a hypothesis, not a promise. Define the proposed advantage, evidence, win condition, and test.
- A social goal must connect to a business, creative, reputational, or community outcome. Follower growth alone is not a complete destination unless the owner explicitly chooses it.
- Use public or user-authorized data. Request exports or screenshots rather than credentials. Do not publish, edit profiles, contact accounts, or change external state during onboarding unless separately requested.
- Do not call onboarding complete while a contradiction that materially changes the strategy remains hidden. Surface it as a decision.

## Interview posture

Start broad and let the owner explain the project in their own framing before imposing categories. Use this opening unless the user has already provided the answer:

> Tell me what you are trying to build through social media, why it matters to you, and what you want people to understand or do because of your presence. Do not organize it yet.

Then work in adaptive rounds of three to five questions. Reflect the emerging model after each round, including tensions and unknowns, so the owner can correct it. Ask for concrete examples when answers stay abstract.

Before conducting the interview, read [references/interview-guide.md](references/interview-guide.md). Select only questions that close high-impact gaps; do not dump the full question bank on the user.

Inspect accessible account content, profile information, linked sites, and supplied analytics before asking the owner to restate discoverable facts. Ask for private metrics only when they can change a decision.

## Build the baseline

Adapt the order to what the owner has already supplied, but cover these layers before finalizing.

### 1. Scope the account and evidence

Identify the owner, account, platform, region, language, account maturity, related channels, and whether this is a new build, reset, expansion, or refresh. Inventory the evidence available and its time window.

Do not silently blend multiple platforms. Give each platform a role or treat it as a separate operating surface.

### 2. Establish identity and brand truth

Capture:

- who the person, organization, or account is;
- why it exists and what it stands for;
- its worldview, convictions, useful disagreements, and non-negotiables;
- the credible experience, access, proof, or perspective it can draw from, including which claims it has and has not earned the right to make;
- the promise it wants audiences to associate with it;
- voice, energy, boundaries, recurring language, and topics it will not exploit.

Preserve distinctive phrases in the owner's words. Resolve the difference between who the owner is now, who the account currently appears to be, and what identity it is trying to grow into.

### 3. Define the destination

Connect the account to a prioritized outcome, audience, time horizon, and success signal. Establish what social media is expected to contribute and what it is not expected to solve.

Rank competing goals such as reach, follower growth, authority, community, clicks, leads, revenue, recruitment, or creative expression. Record tradeoffs and non-goals rather than pretending they are all equally primary.

### 4. Understand audiences and communities

Map the people the account wants to matter to, the people it already reaches, and the communities that can connect them. Capture their problems, desires, questions, vocabulary, level of awareness, current alternatives, and desired relationship with the account.

Go beyond a static persona. Capture the **audience states** in which the account can matter: the situations that activate a tension, what the person is trying to understand or change then, what they already believe, what cognitive or emotional shift would be valuable, and what proof they would need. Record why they might save, share, reply, participate, follow, return, or take a next step. These are different behaviors with different mechanisms.

Include direct niches and parallel communities when the account's ideas can travel across them. State why each community is relevant instead of treating adjacency as automatic audience fit.

### 5. Establish current coordinates and capacity

For an existing account, gather the metrics, content history, relationship graph, conversion path, posting cadence, pipeline, current experiments, and recent trajectory needed to understand the starting point.

For every account, establish sustainable production capacity: time, skills, assets, access, collaborators, budget, comfort on camera, writing or visual strengths, and operational constraints. Include the production environment—how evidence, ideas, approvals, feedback, reuse, and learning currently move—because cadence and quality are outputs of that environment. A strategy that depends on unavailable capacity is not initialized.

Inventory the account's **compounding edges**: audience intelligence, creative judgment, relationship access, operating memory, and retained trust or demand. Unlike a brittle platform trick, these capabilities can strengthen through use and transfer across channel changes.

### 6. Map the starting terrain

Identify the relevant peer, aspirational, adjacent, collaborator, curator, customer, and substitute accounts or clusters. Observe audience conversations, common positions, content forms, templates, trends, platform norms, saturation, and credible whitespace. Treat relationships and community position as part of distribution, not merely context around an algorithmic feed.

Use live research for current terrain when tools and authorization permit. Record sources, observation time, sample limits, and confidence. Do not use a single viral example to declare what the niche wants.

### 7. Form the strategic posture

Turn the prior layers into explicit choices:

- the audience promise and positioning;
- the **return promise**: the stable expectation that gives a person a reason to follow and find the next several artifacts valuable;
- the primary audience and platform role;
- content territories or recurring questions;
- the account's distinctive angle, proof, and proposed advantage;
- suitable formats, cadence range, and production model;
- interaction, relationship, collaboration, and distribution posture;
- offer or conversion path and call-to-action posture;
- trend participation rules and brand boundaries;
- measurement priorities and experimentation appetite.

For any proposed growth route, describe the initial **channel edge hypothesis**: why this account may be able to reach this audience through this exact surface and tactic now; which audience knowledge, creative ability, relationships, trust, or timing enable it; and what evidence would invalidate it. A platform name is not an advantage.

Force consequential tensions into the open: reach versus niche authority, timeliness versus durability, originality versus curation, volume versus depth, personal voice versus scalable production, and audience growth versus conversion.

### 8. Surface decisions and initialize action

Separate:

- **Committed decisions:** approved choices the operating skill should treat as constraints.
- **Provisional decisions:** working choices to test.
- **Open decisions:** unresolved choices that block or alter the route.
- **Unknowns:** missing evidence, not disguised decisions.

Create one to three initial strategic hypotheses, each with a predicted signal and a proposed test. When a hypothesis concerns content or distribution, state the intended audience state, recognition or reframe, credibility basis, channel surface and tactic, timing condition, sharing or participation job, and next step at the level necessary to make the mechanism testable. Recommend the first decision or action needed to begin learning, but do not produce a large calendar merely to make the onboarding feel complete.

## Produce and approve the dossier

Read [references/onboarding-dossier.md](references/onboarding-dossier.md) before drafting the durable handoff. Reuse an existing project storage convention when one is present. If no storage location has been authorized, return the dossier in the response rather than creating persistent files automatically.

Present the synthesis to the owner in plain language before treating it as canonical. Ask them to correct identity, priorities, audience, boundaries, strategic choices, and degrees of certainty. Preserve disagreements and minority hypotheses when they remain decision-relevant.

An approved dossier should let `$social-media-slam` begin with:

- a dated source and evidence inventory;
- account and brand coordinates;
- destination and prioritized success signals;
- audience states, communities, credibility, return promise, and conversion path;
- starting terrain and known actors;
- strategic posture, compounding edges, initial channel edge, and action constraints;
- committed, provisional, and open decisions;
- first hypotheses, unknowns, and watchlist.

## Quality bar

Onboarding is ready for operating use when:

- the owner recognizes themselves in the identity and language;
- the destination is prioritized enough to guide tradeoffs;
- the proposed strategy fits actual capacity;
- the priority audience is defined by relevant states and tensions, not only demographics or a broad persona;
- the credibility basis, return promise, and proposed channel edge are explicit enough to test;
- the relevant terrain is bounded and evidenced;
- the claim about what can win is falsifiable;
- unresolved choices are visible rather than buried in prose;
- the first operating decision can be made without repeating the interview.
