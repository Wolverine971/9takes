# Social Media Onboarding Interview Guide

Use this guide to choose a small number of high-leverage questions during a live onboarding. It is a question bank, not a script. Skip questions already answered by the user or observable evidence, and ask three to five per round.

## Opening riff

Invite an unguided explanation before narrowing:

> Tell me what you are trying to build through social media, why it matters to you, and what you want people to understand or do because of your presence. Do not organize it yet.

Reflect back the owner's language, apparent priorities, contradictions, and missing pieces. Ask them what you misunderstood before moving on.

## Identity and brand truth

Choose questions that expose a real point of view rather than cosmetic branding:

- What do people already come to you for, and what evidence says that?
- Which experiences, access, results, or obsessions give you a right to speak here?
- Which claims would sound credible coming from you now, and which would require more proof or experience first?
- What do you believe about this space that many adjacent accounts miss or avoid?
- What should someone understand about you after consuming ten posts?
- Which parts of your personality or voice should become more visible?
- What would feel successful by the numbers but still feel wrong for your brand?
- What will you not say, exploit, imitate, or become for growth?
- Which phrases, stories, or explanations sound unmistakably like you?

Ask for examples of prior writing, posts, conversations, or work when the answers remain abstract.

## Destination and value path

- What real-world change are you hoping social media will contribute to?
- If the account worked unusually well, what would be different in six or twelve months?
- Which matters most now: awareness, audience growth, trust, community, traffic, leads, sales, opportunities, recruitment, or creative practice?
- Who needs to take what next step for that outcome to happen?
- What does the account lead toward: an idea, relationship, offer, product, service, event, email list, or community?
- Which metric would look encouraging but fail to prove progress?
- What are you willing to trade away, and what must be protected?

Convert vague goals into a provisional outcome, audience, horizon, baseline, and signal. Ask the owner to rank competing outcomes.

## Audience and community map

- Whose attention or trust would make the biggest difference?
- What are those people trying to solve, become, avoid, or make sense of?
- In which specific situations does that concern become urgent, emotionally active, or difficult to ignore?
- What language do they use that insiders or brands often replace with jargon?
- What do they experience privately that existing accounts describe too vaguely, cosmetically, or judgmentally?
- What would a valuable post help them feel, understand, explain, or do differently?
- What proof, source, or kind of experience would make them trust that message?
- What do they currently follow, believe, buy, or do instead?
- Which people already respond to you, and how do they differ from the audience you want?
- Which parallel communities share the underlying problem even if they use different language?
- What relationship should each audience have with the account: reader, peer, participant, customer, advocate, collaborator, or something else?
- Why might they save, share, reply, or participate, and what would each behavior help them do for themselves or another person?
- After one useful post, what stable promise would give them a reason to follow and return?

Do not collapse everyone who could enjoy a post into one target audience. Capture priority and strategic relevance.

## Current account and operating reality

- Which recent posts felt most representative, and which performed best? Are they the same?
- What has shown repeatable traction versus one-off reach?
- What are you posting now, at what cadence, and what is already in the pipeline?
- Where do people currently discover you, interact, and take a next step?
- Which relationships, communities, or accounts already connect you to the desired audience?
- What can you sustainably produce each week without resenting the system?
- Which media feel natural: writing, short video, long video, live conversation, audio, graphics, curation, or replies?
- What access, assets, collaborators, budget, or distribution advantages exist?
- What audience knowledge, creative judgment, relationship access, process knowledge, or retained demand has already accumulated?
- Where do ideas, evidence, feedback, approvals, reuse, and performance learning currently get stuck?
- What has been tried and rejected, and why?

Prefer account analytics and content evidence over memory when both are available. Request exports or screenshots, never credentials.

## Terrain and opportunity

- Which accounts feel like true peers, aspirational models, useful contrasts, collaborators, or substitutes?
- What is everyone in the niche repeating right now?
- Which audience questions receive shallow, stale, or overly polished answers?
- Which formats and templates are common, and which appear to be changing?
- Where would copying a successful account fail because its scale, persona, access, or audience is different?
- Which adjacent communities could plausibly care about this perspective, and why?
- What is timely enough to exploit now, and what is durable enough to build around?
- Where can this account be both recognizable and meaningfully different?

Corroborate trend claims with multiple current observations. Treat whitespace as a hypothesis until audience response supports it.

## Strategic choices and tensions

Use these questions to turn possibilities into choices:

- If only one audience and one outcome could be primary for the next phase, which would they be?
- What recurring promise could the account reliably fulfill?
- Which few content territories best connect credibility, audience demand, and destination?
- Should the account win through depth, speed, access, synthesis, entertainment, utility, taste, community, or another advantage?
- Which platform has what role, and what should not be cross-posted unchanged?
- How much should the account participate in trends versus establish its own recurring formats?
- What balance of original creation, response, curation, and relationship-building is sustainable?
- Which call to action fits the audience's current level of trust?
- What evidence would prove the strategic thesis wrong?
- What exact channel surface and tactic fit the audience state, rather than merely being popular in the category?
- What could make this route work specifically for this account now: knowledge, source credibility, creative ability, relationships, timing, or an offer?
- Which part of that advantage could compound even if the platform tactic later weakens?

Label the answers committed, provisional, open, or unknown.

## Closing synthesis

Before drafting the dossier, reflect:

1. Who the account is and what it stands for.
2. Who it is trying to matter to.
3. What destination social media should advance.
4. What the current account and terrain suggest.
5. What strategic advantage appears plausible.
6. What should cause the intended audience to recognize, trust, transmit, follow, or act—and which of those behaviors actually matters.
7. Which durable edges and return promise can compound beyond one post or platform tactic.
8. Which consequential choices are committed, provisional, or open.
9. What should be tested first.

Ask the owner to correct meaning and priority, not just wording. If an unresolved answer could reverse the strategy, continue the interview rather than polishing around it.
