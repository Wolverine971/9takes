# Social Media SLAM

Treat social strategy as a navigation problem under changing conditions. Social Media SLAM supplies the changing world model inside an OODA operating loop:

`Observe -> Orient (Locate + Map + Aim) -> Decide -> Act -> Learn -> Observe`

For social media:

- **Observe:** collect account, content, audience, relationship, conversion, and terrain signals.
- **Orient:** determine where the account is, what surrounds it, and how that relates to its identity and destination.
- **Decide:** choose one move and the tradeoff it implies.
- **Act:** publish, engage, change, research, or instrument when authorized.
- **Learn:** compare the prediction with the result and update the account state, terrain map, and strategy.

The result is not a timeless content plan. It is a current position estimate, a bounded map of the relevant terrain, a destination, and a testable next move.

## Model growth as adaptive fit

Treat content as the audience-facing vehicle, not the whole growth mechanism:

- **Fit creates response:** a message and artifact meet a specific audience state with useful recognition, reframe, or utility.
- **Distribution creates access:** the exact surface, relationships, and tactic determine who gets an opportunity to respond.
- **Trust compounds response:** credibility and repeated fulfillment of a recognizable promise change how work is interpreted and whether people return.
- **Learning renews fit:** the durable advantage is the ability to diagnose, adapt, and retain local knowledge as conditions change.

Do not collapse exposure, attention, resonance, transmission, relationship, retention, conversion, and destination progress into “engagement.” They are different stages with different causes. Read [references/fit-resonance-and-channel-edges.md](references/fit-resonance-and-channel-edges.md) when diagnosing why growth or content did or did not work, forming a distribution or resonance hypothesis, evaluating a channel opportunity, or reviewing a material win or miss.

## Load the onboarding baseline

At the start of a run, look for an approved onboarding dossier or equivalent user-provided context. `$social-media-onboarding` establishes the durable baseline: identity, destination, audience states, credibility, return promise, capacity, compounding edges, strategic posture, terrain, guardrails, decisions, and initial hypotheses.

Treat the dossier as a dated prior, not immutable truth. Reuse it instead of interviewing the owner from scratch, and update only what new evidence invalidates. If no reliable baseline exists and the user is initializing a durable social program, use `$social-media-onboarding` first. For a narrow or urgent decision, proceed with a clearly provisional quick baseline.

## Operating principles

- Calibrate recommendations to the account's stage, brand, audience, capacity, pipeline, platform, and evidence. Do not begin with generic best practices.
- Map the account's **relevant neighborhood**, not the whole internet: audience-overlap accounts, direct peers, aspirational accounts, adjacent niches, recurring sources, and format analogues.
- Treat current state as a trajectory, not just a snapshot. Direction, velocity, consistency, and recent changes matter alongside totals.
- Separate `observed`, `owner-stated`, `inferred`, and `unknown`. Add an as-of time and confidence to time-sensitive claims.
- Distinguish a durable audience pattern from a platform trend. A trend matters only when it is evidenced, timely, usable by this account, and compatible with the brand.
- Treat the next action as a hypothesis, not a certainty. State why it should work, what signal would support it, and when to reassess.
- Require the next action to either move the account toward its destination or reduce an uncertainty that could materially change the route. Motion without one of those functions is activity, not learning.
- Prefer compounding edges—audience intelligence, creative judgment, relationship access, operating memory, and retained trust or demand—over a hunt for hidden platform hacks. A channel advantage is usually a situated capability, not a secret fact.
- Optimize for progress toward the stated destination, not vanity metrics by default. Reach, followers, clicks, leads, authority, and community are different destinations.
- Use public or user-authorized data. Do not infer private or sensitive traits about followers, and do not publish, message, follow, unfollow, or change an account unless the user explicitly asks.

## Choose the run mode

Infer the lightest mode that answers the request:

- **Quick bootstrap:** create a provisional state and action brief when full onboarding is unnecessary or deferred.
- **Pulse check:** identify what changed since the last checkpoint and whether the route should change.
- **Decision:** compare candidate topics, formats, campaigns, or actions and choose the next move.
- **Review:** compare a prior prediction with results, extract the lesson, and update the state and map.

If the user provides partial information, work with it. Ask only for missing information that could materially reverse the recommendation. Otherwise mark the gap and make a bounded provisional recommendation.

## 1. Observe: collect current signals

Start with the smallest evidence set that can change the decision. Depending on the run, inspect account analytics, recent content, audience responses, comments or authorized message themes, relationship activity, profile and conversion behavior, pipeline changes, relevant account activity, live discourse, templates, trends, and platform shifts.

When diagnosing performance, collect evidence at the narrowest useful stage: qualified exposure, attention and completion, audience recognition or reframe, credibility, emotional or practical consequence, sharing and participation, follow or return behavior, conversion, and destination outcome. Do not blame content for a distribution failure or buy more distribution for a broken promise or offer.

Bound the observation window and record freshness. Do not collect data without knowing which decision it could inform, and do not mistake a loud signal for a representative one.

## 2. Orient

Orientation gives the observed signals meaning through the account's identity, destination, capacity, and surrounding terrain.

### Locate the current position

Build an honest account-and-brand state from available analytics, account observations, content inventory, pipeline, and user context. Include what matters for the decision:

- platform, account, region, language, and evidence window;
- brand identity, promise, voice, topics, positioning, and boundaries;
- intended and actual audience, including meaningful follower or engagement clusters when known;
- current audience states: activating situations, live tensions, current beliefs, desired shifts, and evidence of why people save, share, reply, follow, return, or act;
- relationship graph, including followed accounts, visible or authorized follower patterns, mutuals, and important audience or peer clusters;
- follower and reach trajectory, engagement quality, clicks or conversions, and other goal-relevant outcomes;
- posting cadence, consistency, topic and format mix, repeatable wins, misses, and unexplained anomalies;
- return promise and retention: what expectation caused people to follow, whether later work fulfills it, and which audience behaviors repeat;
- content already drafted, scheduled, reusable, or blocked;
- production capacity, strengths, constraints, dependencies, and willingness to experiment;
- current strategy, assumptions, and active experiments;
- compounding edges and gaps: audience intelligence, creative judgment, relationship access, operating memory, and retained trust or demand.

Do not mistake follower count for position. Two accounts of the same size can occupy different positions because one has stronger topic authority, distribution momentum, conversion paths, relationships, or production capacity.

### Map the relevant terrain

Map only the environment that can affect the route. Depending on the request, inspect:

- direct peers, aspirational accounts, substitutes, collaborators, curators, customers, and adjacent voices;
- topics, questions, tensions, vocabulary, narratives, and audience problems receiving attention;
- recurring post structures, hooks, templates, formats, visual conventions, calls to action, and posting rhythms;
- exact distribution surfaces, relationship paths, audience sharing or participation jobs, and conversion friction;
- what is emerging, accelerating, crowded, durable, declining, or merely anecdotal;
- where the account sounds interchangeable, where it differs, and where useful whitespace exists;
- platform mechanics or cultural norms that materially change distribution or audience behavior;
- whether an observed change affects the whole platform, a surface, tactic, format, message, audience segment, relationship path, cost structure, or conversion path. Do not declare a channel dead when only one layer is decaying.

For current terrain or trend claims, research live sources when tools and authorization permit. Prefer first-party analytics and direct platform observations; use secondary commentary as context rather than ground truth. Record the platform, sample, region or audience context, and observation time. Never label a single viral post a trend without corroboration.

When live access is unavailable, say what could not be observed and offer the smallest useful evidence request, such as an analytics export, screenshots, account list, or recent-post sample. Do not fabricate a fresh map from remembered platform lore.

### Aim at the current destination

Translate vague ambition into a direction the skill can navigate toward. Capture:

- the business or personal outcome;
- the social outcome expected to contribute to it;
- priority audience and platform;
- baseline, target or desired direction, time horizon, and success signal;
- acceptable tradeoffs and brand guardrails;
- what is explicitly _not_ being optimized.

If the destination is broad, propose a measurable working version and label it provisional. Resolve conflicts explicitly—for example, short-term reach may pull against niche authority, or high posting volume may pull against a founder's available time.

## 3. Decide: choose the next best action

Compare the current position with the destination through the terrain map. Rank actions by expected movement toward the destination, evidence strength, learning value, effort, reversibility, and brand fit.

Consider the full set of social operating surfaces: profile and positioning, content, conversation and community, relationships and collaborations, distribution, offer and conversion path, measurement, and research. Do not assume the answer must be another post.

Recommend one primary next move by default. It may be a post, research task, positioning change, relationship action, profile change, instrumentation fix, content experiment, or decision to keep the current course. Include:

- **Action:** the concrete next move and owner.
- **Why now:** the state, terrain, and destination evidence it connects.
- **Prediction:** what should happen if the model is right.
- **Signal:** the leading and outcome metrics to observe.
- **Cost and risk:** required effort, dependencies, and likely downside.
- **Checkpoint:** when or after how many observations to review it.
- **Contingency:** what to try or re-check if the prediction fails.

For a content, growth, or distribution move, add the mechanism needed to interpret the result:

- **Audience state:** the activating situation, live tension, current belief, and intended cognitive, emotional, or practical shift.
- **Resonance mechanism:** the recognition or reframe, credibility basis, and personal or social utility expected to create that shift.
- **Distribution bet:** priority audience, exact surface, tactic or format, message, timing condition, sharing or participation job, and next step.
- **Signal stage:** which stage should move first—exposure, attention, recognition, trust, consequence, transmission, relationship, conversion, or outcome.

Use only the detail necessary to make the hypothesis falsifiable. Do not force this record onto an action whose mechanism is already obvious or unrelated to content and distribution.

Name the important alternative not chosen and the signal that would justify reconsidering it. Do not hide a weak recommendation inside a long content calendar. When evidence is thin, prefer a small, high-learning, reversible experiment.

## 4. Act: perform or package the move

If the user requested execution and the action is authorized, perform it with the stated scope and preserve the instrumentation needed to evaluate it. If execution is not requested, produce an action-ready brief with the content, owner, dependencies, timing, and measurement needed for the user or their team to act.

Record what actually happened, including deviations from the plan. A decision is not evidence until action produces an observation.

## 5. Learn and update

At the checkpoint, compare predicted and actual results. Diagnose the earliest weak or unexpectedly strong stage in the signal chain before drawing a conclusion. Consider content quality, distribution, audience state, source credibility, timing, sharing utility, follow and return behavior, offer or profile conversion, audience quality, and measurement gaps.

For a material win or miss, preserve a causal review:

- the best explanation and credible alternatives;
- whether the result came from the idea, artifact, source account, distribution, relationship, timing, external event, paid support, offer, or measurement artifact;
- which enabling conditions must remain true;
- what appears repeatable versus dependent on a particular person or event;
- the smallest replication or correction test;
- which early signal would indicate saturation or decay.

Update the account position, terrain beliefs, destination if the user's priorities changed, and action queue. Preserve failed predictions and surprises; they are part of the map. Do not rewrite history to make the strategy appear correct.

Maintain a small watchlist of account signals, actors, topics, templates, channel dependencies, and platform changes that could alter the route. Set the next check based on the speed and importance of the signal; do not collect trend data continuously without a decision it can inform.

While exploiting a working route, keep a bounded, capacity-aware exploration queue when replacement risk is material. Exploration should build useful knowledge, relationships, judgment, or portable demand even when it does not immediately increase reach. Do not diversify for its own sake or disrupt a working system merely to appear adaptive.

When maintaining a durable snapshot or comparing checkpoints, read [references/state-model.md](references/state-model.md). Reuse the project's existing storage convention if one exists and append experiment evidence rather than overwriting history. If no location has been authorized, return the structured record in the response rather than creating persistent files automatically.

## Default deliverable

Unless the user requests another format, return a compact **navigation brief**:

1. **Current coordinates:** account, brand, audience, content system, momentum, and constraints.
2. **Destination:** desired outcome, horizon, audience, and success signal.
3. **Terrain that matters:** live patterns, actors, trends, whitespace, and threats relevant to that destination.
4. **Route judgment:** the most important gap, weakest signal-chain stage, or leverage point.
5. **Next best action:** action, mechanism, rationale, prediction, measurement, and checkpoint.
6. **Following queue:** up to three later moves, clearly subordinate to the next action.
7. **Unknowns and watchlist:** missing evidence and environmental changes worth monitoring.

Cite or link the observations that materially drive the decision when sources are available. Make freshness and uncertainty visible.

## Quality bar

A strong run lets the user answer:

- Where is this account now, and which parts are fact versus inference?
- What nearby part of the social landscape actually matters?
- Where is the account trying to go, by when, and for whom?
- Why is this the next move for this account at this moment?
- What should cause the intended audience to notice, recognize, trust, transmit, return, or act—and which of those behaviors matters for the destination?
- What result is predicted, what will be measured, and when will the model update?

If the response could be pasted unchanged onto an unrelated account, it is not localized enough.
