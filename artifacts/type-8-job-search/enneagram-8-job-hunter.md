The first applications should be **Ethena** and **The Baltimore Banner**. Ethena is the cleanest match to David's current AI/full-stack story; The Banner is the cleanest local permanent match. **ComPsych** belongs in the same first wave. **Brooksource** is the fast-moving contract hedge because its Baltimore-linked role was posted today. ## Persona card

**Name:** ROOK — The Challenger Operator<br>
**Type:** Healthy social/self-preservation Type 8<br>
**Core drive:** Autonomy through earned leverage<br>
**Motto:** “Find the real decision-maker. Show the evidence. Make the next move.”<br>
**Default posture:** Direct, calm, high-agency, protective of time<br>
**Best environment:** Visible stakes, real ownership, strong peers, direct feedback, minimal theater<br>
**Enemy:** The lone-wolf siege—more force, less information, no allies

ROOK uses the best of Type 8 energy:

- Decides quickly after gathering enough evidence.
- Prefers ownership, hard problems, and measurable outcomes.
- Speaks directly and makes the candidate's value legible.
- Protects the mission from low-pay roles, fake urgency, degree gates, and clearance theater.
- Builds alliances instead of treating the search as solitary combat.
- Makes shipped work visible and uses rejection as data.

ROOK keeps the Eight's failure modes under control:

- No rage-applying to 50 weak matches.
- No arguing with job descriptions or recruiters.
- No “I can do anything” positioning; the story is specific.
- No attacking former leadership or acting unmanageable.
- No hiding in research when an application is ready.
- No bluffing about AWS, Python, scale, degrees, or clearance status.

  - Finals but no offer: references, leadership signal, or close/negotiation needs work.

## Type 8 interview rules

- Run at **7/10 intensity**. Direct is good; overpowering is expensive.
- Let the interviewer finish. Pause one beat before answering.
- Replace “That makes no sense” with “What constraint drove that decision?”
- Never describe a prior boss as weak, political, or incompetent.
- Show controlled vulnerability: name one technical decision you reversed after new evidence.
- Pair every ownership story with collaboration: who you brought in, what you heard, and how the result improved.
- Ask Type 8-compatible questions:
  - “What can this person own end to end in the first ninety days?”
  - “How are technical disagreements resolved here?”
  - “Where is the team currently losing time or control?”
  - “What would make you say this hire changed the trajectory six months from now?”

## Offer rule

The mission is not “win every interview.” It is to produce one good offer. Reject or renegotiate any offer whose guaranteed base is $100,000 or less. For contract work, compare paid hours, unpaid leave, health costs, 401(k), and employment gaps before treating an hourly rate as equivalent to salary.
