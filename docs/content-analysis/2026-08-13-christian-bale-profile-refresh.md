## Personality evidence and counterevidence

### Evidence supporting Type 5

- Bale connects limited personal disclosure to preserving audience belief in the character.
- He reports collecting dialect recordings, interviewing, listening, and observing before performing.
- He selectively limits social contact when it disrupts his concentration on a role.
- He repeatedly frames usefulness through becoming someone else rather than through public self-presentation.

### Evidence against or complicating a simple Type 5 read

- Direct anger and enforcement of standards in the `Terminator Salvation` incident.
- Public accountability through his later apology.
- Sustained collaboration with repeat directors and a coalition-based foster-care project.
- Early financial responsibility and durable family duty.
- Public advocacy and willingness to use celebrity access for an institutional goal.
- Artistic ambition and conspicuous reinvention.

### Alternatives

- Type 1: standards, corrective anger, moral seriousness, and reform.
- Type 4: identity reinvention, singular roles, and discomfort with an ordinary public persona.
- Type 6: preparation, family duty, loyalty, and long partnerships.
- Type 8: confrontation, boundary enforcement, and protective advocacy.
- Type 3 remains a secondary explanation for competence and professional adaptability.

The retained conclusion is probable 5w4. It is explicitly presented as an editorial inference, with no claim that Bale has confirmed it. Connecting-line explanations are treated as weak evidence because they can absorb both anger and charity only after assuming the core type.
