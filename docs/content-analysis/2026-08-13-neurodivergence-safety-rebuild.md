# Neurodivergence Guide Safety Rebuild

**Date:** 2026-08-13

**Canonical page:** `https://9takes.com/enneagram-corner/mental-health/enneagram-neurodivergence-guide`

**Tasker:** `docs/taskers/T-30-neurodivergence-safety-rebuild.md`

## Outcome

The published guide was rebuilt around one boundary: an Enneagram interpretation can describe the meaning a person gives an experience, but it cannot diagnose, rule out, predict, or explain away a neurodevelopmental condition. The old type-by-condition grid was removed. It was replaced with a smaller framework that separates observable behavior, functional barriers, environmental demands, and interpreted motivation.

The canonical URL, content path, publication state, redirect direction, `lastmod`, and flat quality metadata remain unchanged. The ADHD page, autism draft, frozen mental-illness page, redirects, and other mental-health articles were not edited.

## 1. Source methodology

Research was refreshed on 2026-08-13. Sources were selected in this order:

1. Official public-health and diagnostic bodies for diagnostic status and criteria.
2. Peer-reviewed clinical guidelines and systematic reviews for terms needing more detail.
3. Official employment and accommodation guidance for the United States section.
4. Autistic-led sources for language and disability framing, kept separate from clinical evidence.
5. An education authority for the jurisdiction-specific meaning of twice exceptionality.

Generic SEO health sites, unsourced condition summaries, type-condition anecdotes, and AI-generated explanations were not used as evidence. A negative-evidence rule was also applied: when no authoritative source supported an Enneagram-to-condition, treatment, medication, or accommodation mapping, the mapping was removed rather than softened.

### Clinical and research sources

- [NIMH, Attention-Deficit/Hyperactivity Disorder: What You Need to Know](https://www.nimh.nih.gov/health/publications/attention-deficit-hyperactivity-disorder-what-you-need-to-know): ADHD status, persistence, impairment, and the role of history, functioning, rating scales, and differential assessment.
- [CDC, Clinical Testing and Diagnosis for Autism Spectrum Disorder](https://www.cdc.gov/autism/hcp/diagnosis/index.html): current DSM-based autism criteria, early development, impairment, sensory reactivity, and the possibility that learned strategies can mask characteristics.
- [World Health Organization, ICD-11 Clinical Descriptions and Diagnostic Requirements](https://www.who.int/publications/i/item/9789240077263): current international diagnostic framework. Used with official DSM materials to check whether RSD is a formal diagnosis.
- [American Psychiatric Association, DSM-5-TR criteria and text updates](https://www.psychiatry.org/psychiatrists/practice/dsm/updates-to-dsm/updates-to-dsm-5-tr-criteria-text): official DSM publisher and current update record. RSD is not listed as a diagnosis or ADHD criterion.
- [NICHD, Learning Disabilities](https://www.nichd.nih.gov/health/topics/learningdisabilities): learning-disability scope and the boundary between learning disability and intelligence.
- [Blank et al., International clinical practice recommendations for developmental coordination disorder](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/full/10.1111/dmcn.14132): current clinical terminology and assessment focus for DCD.
- [NHS, Developmental co-ordination disorder (dyspraxia) in children](https://www.nhs.uk/conditions/developmental-coordination-disorder-dyspraxia/): current public-health terminology and the relationship between DCD and the broader term dyspraxia.
- [American Academy of Pediatrics, Sensory Integration Therapies for Children With Developmental and Behavioral Disorders](https://publications.aap.org/pediatrics/article/129/6/1186/32067/Sensory-Integration-Therapies-for-Children-With): lack of a universally accepted framework for sensory processing disorder as a standalone diagnosis.
- [Autistic-led systematic review of 389 camouflaging studies](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC13087159/): conceptual, measurement, and representativeness limits in masking and camouflaging research.
- [Hook et al., systematic review of the Enneagram literature](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/33332604/): mixed reliability and validity evidence across 104 samples, with little support for several secondary parts of Enneagram theory.

### Disability-led and education sources

- [Autistic Self Advocacy Network, About Autism](https://autisticadvocacy.org/about-asan/about-autism/): autistic-led framing of autism and variation in support needs.
- [Autistic Self Advocacy Network, Identity-First Language](https://autisticadvocacy.org/about-asan/identity-first-language/): rationale for identity-first language while respecting individual preferences.
- [Autistic Self Advocacy Network, Functioning Labels Harm Autistic People](https://autisticadvocacy.org/2021/12/functioning-labels-harm-autistic-people/): reason to describe actual access and support needs instead of using high-functioning or low-functioning labels.
- [Colorado Department of Education, Twice-Exceptional Students](https://ed.cde.state.co.us/gt/twice-exceptional): one formal education-system definition of 2e. The page now identifies 2e as an education term whose eligibility rules vary, not a medical diagnosis.

### Employment sources

- [EEOC, Disability Discrimination and Employment Decisions](https://www.eeoc.gov/disability-discrimination-and-employment-decisions): U.S. pre-employment boundaries, accommodation requests, limited documentation, and confidentiality.
- [EEOC, Small Employers and Reasonable Accommodation](https://www.eeoc.gov/laws/guidance/small-employers-and-reasonable-accommodation): plain-language accommodation requests and when documentation may be requested.
- [U.S. Department of Labor, Disclosure and the Workplace](https://www.dol.gov/agencies/odep/publications/fact-sheets/youth-disclosure-and-the-workplace-why-when-what-and-how): disclosure as an individual decision rather than a universal recommendation.
- [Job Accommodation Network](https://askjan.org/): current U.S. accommodation guidance and a reader resource, not evidence for type-specific accommodations.

### Retained and elevated

- **Clinically supported:** Neurodevelopmental assessment considers development, persistence, functioning, setting, and differential explanations.
- **Clinically supported:** Similar observable behavior can arise from different mechanisms.
- **Carefully bounded editorial framework:** Ask separately what happened, which function was difficult, what the environment demanded, what meaning the person attached, and which support changed the outcome.
- **Disability-led framing:** Describe actual support needs and access barriers, avoid functioning labels, and respect individual language preferences.
- **Non-clinical Enneagram use:** Treat a type interpretation as a hypothesis about meaning and coping, never as clinical evidence.

No first-person lived experience was invented. Lived-experience framing is attributed to autistic-led advocacy rather than presented as clinical proof.

## 3. New thesis and page structure

The thesis now appears in the QuickAnswer and opening section: the Enneagram is a non-clinical framework for interpreted motivation and coping, while neurodevelopmental conditions concern development and functioning assessed through clinical criteria and support needs. An Enneagram interpretation cannot diagnose, rule out, predict, or explain away a condition.

The rebuilt structure is:

1. Direct QuickAnswer and scope boundary.
2. Motivation, processing, and context as separate layers.
3. A sourced terminology section covering ADHD, autism, dyslexia, DCD or dyspraxia, sensory differences, 2e, masking, RSD, and executive dysfunction.
4. Three mechanism examples: leaving a room, missing a deadline, and using routines or scripts.
5. A five-question reflection framework.
6. Function-based support options and a medication boundary.
7. A bounded evaluation section that includes access inequity.
8. A jurisdiction-labeled U.S. workplace accommodation and disclosure section.
9. A clear can-and-cannot-do summary.
10. Six visible FAQs mirrored in FAQPage JSON-LD.

The old type anchors were retired because the supporting content was unsafe and the fresh search data did not justify preserving it.

## 4. Editorial decisions

### Medical safety

The disclaimer is not used as a substitute for safe body copy. Diagnostic, treatment, medication, and evaluation claims were removed or rewritten throughout. The article answers diagnosis-seeking queries directly with a non-diagnostic answer rather than preserving an unsupported type answer for search traffic.

### Disability language

The guide uses identity-first language for autism because it is common in autistic self-advocacy, while explicitly respecting individual preferences. It avoids high-functioning and low-functioning labels, euphemisms such as different abilities, superpower framing, and moral labels such as lazy. Support needs are described as variable and context-dependent.

### Workplace scope

The employment section is labeled for the United States. It does not promise eligibility, require a particular disclosure decision, or tell a reader what an employer must do in an individual case. It distinguishes an accommodation request from disclosing an Enneagram type and sends high-stakes cases to current official or qualified guidance.

### Representativeness

The article does not present one autistic, ADHD, dyslexic, DCD, sensory, or 2e experience as universal. The masking discussion explicitly notes inconsistent definitions and samples. Race, gender, age, cost, geography, clinician bias, and masking are included as factors that can affect recognition and access without claiming a universal diagnostic pattern.

## 6. Known uncertainties and non-goals

- The Enneagram literature does not establish clinical prevalence or condition profiles by type. This rebuild does not propose a substitute mapping.
- Neurodivergent is a broad community and identity term without one universally agreed clinical boundary.
- Executive dysfunction describes functional difficulties and is not a diagnosis by itself.
- RSD remains community shorthand rather than a formal diagnosis or ADHD criterion.
- Sensory terminology and masking research remain conceptually uneven. The article states those limits instead of resolving them editorially.
- Education eligibility for 2e and workplace rights vary by jurisdiction and individual facts.
- The guide does not diagnose readers, determine eligibility, recommend treatment, or replace medical or legal advice.
- The guide does not merge with or rewrite the separate ADHD page, autism draft, frozen mental-illness page, or neighboring mental-health content.
- This implementation is an editorial safety rebuild, not a clinician, lawyer, or disability-community endorsement.
