## Content requirements

- Identify Bryan Johnson and Blueprint in the opening without assuming the reader already knows him.
- Use the August 2026 Douthat interview as the current narrative spine, especially the tension between
  Johnson's 5-MeO-DMT "yes to existence" and Douthat's control interpretation.
- Give the father-at-the-window scene its own weight. Do not treat it as a typology proof or reduce
  his childhood to a diagnosis.
- Explain the Braintree/Venmo sale, simultaneous depression/divorce/religious break, and loss of
  professional identity as one collapse.
- Separate standard health practices from Johnson's larger claims and N-of-1 experiments.
- Include criticism fairly. Johnson himself admits he may be shortening his life and that the work is
  early science. Douthat's best objection is philosophical: control may be the project, not merely
  its tool.
- Treat Enneagram Four as argued analysis, with One and Three pressure-tested in the research packet.

## Claims to avoid or qualify

- Do not say Blueprint proves age reversal or life extension. Johnson is a single experimental
  subject, many biomarkers remain contested, and he admits the ultimate answer is unknown.
- Do not call the $2 million figure his current annual spending without a date. It describes the
  reported protocol in 2023 and became a durable media shorthand.
- Do not claim he sold Braintree personally for $800 million. PayPal acquired Braintree/Venmo for
  $800 million; Johnson says he personally received a few hundred million.
- Do not describe him as vegan without qualification; current references note collagen-peptide use.
- Do not turn his autoimmune gastritis diagnosis into a prognosis or repeat his confidence that he
  will cure it as medical fact.
- Do not speculate about his former marriage, children, or previous relationship beyond material
  relevant to the public record and his own account.
