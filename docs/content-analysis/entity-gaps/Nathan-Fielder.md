## The private-life content-farm trap (read before drafting)

This is the most important new finding, and it is a **warning, not an opening**.

The retrieved sample of results naming Fielder's ex-partner or a current girlfriend consisted of content farms or aggregators:
soapcentral, legit.ng, celebsuburb, amomama, briefly.co.za, nexteramagazine.co.uk, freelymagazine.uk,
dailymagazineny.com, thesalfordmagazine.com, newsbreak, plus syndicated Yahoo aggregator copies. Not one
reputable outlet appeared in that sample. This is a limitation of the retrieved evidence, not a claim to have reviewed every publication.

Three converging signals say the facts are unestablished:

1. **Wikipedia does not name the ex-partner.** Its text describes a former marriage and 2014 divorce.
   That omission does not establish the editors' reason. The independent reason to omit a private person's name here is the lack of an appropriate verified primary source.
2. **The farms contradict each other** on the divorce year (2014 versus 2015), which is what happens when
   pages are generated from each other rather than from a source.
3. **The girlfriend claim is self-labeled speculation** — the source calls Amber Schaefer an "alleged"
   girlfriend and concedes it was never confirmed.

Per the command's guardrails, private third parties are not fair game. **Do not name the ex-partner. Do
not name or imply a current partner. Do not let a farm claim reach the draft via a research summary that
quietly repeated it.**

One fact _is_ defensible because it is his own on-record statement, not a farm claim: the divorce is
datable to **2014** via Fielder's own 2015 interview (he described it as the prior year), which agrees
with Wikipedia. Attribute it to the interview, describe her only as a children's librarian, and stop
there.

These queries carry gossip intent. Treat them as `do_not_optimize` at the query-family level: answer
"not publicly established" where that is the truthful answer, and do not build the page to chase them.
