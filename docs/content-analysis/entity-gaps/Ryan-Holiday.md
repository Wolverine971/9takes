## The 9takes angle

The contradiction is the best thing about this subject and it is not subtle:

> The man who wrote the field manual for manufacturing outrage now sells stillness — and he sells it
> using the machine he built.

He is the author of _Trust Me, I'm Lying_, a confession of engineering fake controversy for American
Apparel and Tucker Max, who now runs the most successful equanimity brand on the internet. "Ego is
the enemy" is the thesis of a man whose business is his own name, whose bookstore is a personality
extension, and whose first-page search results are ten-for-ten self-owned. That is not hypocrisy —
and a cheap-shot page would be both wrong and boring. It is the more interesting thing: someone who
learned exactly how attention is manufactured, decided the machine was corrosive, and then chose to
keep operating it in service of something he believes is good. Whether that resolves the problem or
just launders it is the question the page should sit inside, without pretending to settle it.

Second layer, verified in the SERP audit: he built his career on the claim that character is a
practice, not a trait — thirteen books arguing that virtue is what you _do_. That is a live,
substantive quarrel with the premise of a typology site. A 9takes page that names the quarrel is far
more interesting than one that quietly types him anyway.

**Niche fit:** the self-improvement cluster is already built — Robert Greene (his mentor),
Tim Ferriss, Andrew Huberman, David Goggins all published. Marcus Aurelius sits in the queue at
priority 43 explicitly to pair with him. He is the hub node of a cluster that currently has no hub.

## If the pipeline writes him anyway

He is already `inProgress`, and there is a legitimate non-SEO case: cluster completion, an
exceptional source record, and a genuinely ownable thesis. If the run proceeds, the brief changes
shape — **do not write the biography of record. It already exists and he wrote it.**

- **Write the psychological page, not the encyclopedia page.** Every fact query is answered
  elsewhere; competing on facts is a guaranteed loss. Wikipedia will win "Ryan Holiday age" forever.
- **SEO title must carry a falsifiable thesis**, not a bio label. The distinctive claim is the
  manipulator-turned-Stoic tension, not "Ryan Holiday: Author and Speaker."
- **Lead with the contradiction, not the résumé.** The _Trust Me, I'm Lying_ → Daily Stoic arc is the
  opening, and it is strong enough to carry a reader who has never heard of him.
- **Name the quarrel with typology in the piece.** He rejects the premise on the record. A page that
  types him without acknowledging that reads as unaware; a page that engages it is the most
  interesting Ryan Holiday page on the internet, and it is the one page Wikipedia structurally
  cannot write.
- **Realistic traffic forecast: Robert Greene's, i.e. near zero on the name.** Value this page as
  cluster infrastructure and as a link target for Marcus Aurelius, Robert Greene, Tim Ferriss and
  Goggins — not as a traffic acquisition.
- **Build the cluster deliberately:** Robert Greene (mentor, published), Marcus Aurelius (queued,
  p43), Tim Ferriss, Andrew Huberman, David Goggins. This is the strongest argument for writing him.

## Claims to avoid or qualify

1. **Net worth — do not publish a figure.** Sources range $8M to $50M with no reconciliation, and
   the entire SERP is AI slop, several instances of it parasitizing hijacked .edu subdomains. If the
   question is addressed at all, the sourced answer is "not publicly established."
2. **Total copies sold — conflicting and self-reported.** His own bio and Amazon say "more than 5
   million copies" in "more than 40 languages"; at least one secondary (bestwriting.com) says "more
   than 10 million." **Wikipedia carries no total-sales figure** — only "By December 2016, [_The
   Obstacle Is the Way_] had sold more than 230,000 copies." Every large figure traces to publisher
   or author marketing copy. Attribute it ("his publisher says…") or omit it.
3. **The Quora "personality tests are a complete waste of time" quote is NOT verified.** It reached
   this audit as a search-index summary of `quorasessionswithryanholiday.quora.com`; the page
   returned **HTTP 403** on direct fetch, and personality-database also 403'd. The quote is load-
   bearing for the recommended angle, so it must be pinned to a fetchable primary source — his own
   podcast, an essay on ryanholiday.net, or a rendered Quora Session archive — before it appears in
   the draft. **Do not ship the angle on an unfetched snippet.** (See the quote-pinning and
   perspective-gate memories: a wrong evidence-packet row can ride a repair into a draft and pass.)
4. **The CCNY philosophy professor critique needs a name and an outlet.** Same problem: summary-level
   only. Find the original.
5. **The UK _Wisdom Takes Work_ date (2026-11-05, Profile Books) is unverified** and a year after US
   publication, which is atypical. Confirm before treating it as a catalyst.
6. **Naval Academy specifics must be cited precisely.** Load-bearing facts: 381 books removed from
   the Nimitz Library; Executive Order 14151; canceled roughly an hour before the 2025-04-14 talk;
   the Navy football team talk canceled as well. Anchor to Baltimore Sun (2025-04-19) and the
   Scholars at Risk record (2025-04-15), not to aggregator rewrites.
7. **Do not characterize him as a hypocrite.** It is the cheap read, it is contestable, and it makes
   the page attackable. The defensible framing is the unresolved tension, held open.
8. **Dov Charney / American Apparel is legally sensitive territory.** Holiday's own account of his
   marketing tactics is fair game as self-reported fact. Charney's conduct and the company's
   collapse are not this page's subject; do not import allegations.
9. **Parents' occupations, marriage year, and residence are all Wikipedia-sourced** — fine to state,
   but the celebrity farms (StarsUnfolded, thecityceleb, FamousPeopleToday) repeat and embellish
   them. Cite Wikipedia or the primary, never a farm.
