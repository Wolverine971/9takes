FEEDBACK (2026-08-21):

- **The backflip is the signature detail, and the draft knows exactly why.** Most drafts in this corpus pile up evidence; this one disqualifies two-thirds of its own. "Two of the three happened while he was hiding, and being closeted does most of that work on anybody, whatever their type. The third has nothing pressing on it at all, and that is the one that carries the type." A thirty-seven-year-old, safe and decorated, talking himself out of a backflip he can still land in case a failed one hits TikTok — with no closet in it — is the load-bearing exhibit, and the piece earns it by throwing the easier evidence away first. That epistemic move is the best thing here and it is not in the other recent drafts.
- **Critic pressure faced twice, at full strength, without a defensive shrug.** The Richard II section concedes both three-star reviews before answering them and supplies a mechanism rather than an excuse ("Charm is a transaction with a room. Sorrow is not"). "The toolkit is not in doubt, the arrival time is" is the best sentence in the draft. The dating section then states the cynical read at maximum wattage — "ambiguity is commercially useful for a romantic lead... He has never had to prove he would protect the privacy at a cost to himself, because so far it has cost him nothing" — and reframes without absolving. The empathy turn uses his own operational reason ("I don't know if I would be able to be as confident to speak out on other things if I felt that my whole life was up for grabs"), not house grammar; no `armor` / `scar tissue` construction appears anywhere.
- **Unverifiable load-bearing claim handled as a subject rather than laundered.** The 1p-per-£100 figure is Bailey's own count, and the draft says so, then supplies the sourced contradiction (LGBT+ Consortium / Dr Cat Walker, March 2025, 10p in every £100) and names the reason it is not a refutation (different denominator). That is the correct treatment of a stat the subject campaigns on and no publisher carries.
- **Discoverability is near-textbook.** meta_title 57 chars with the exact head term and the name first; description 155 chars and specific; answer block is an H3 declarative at 37 words; 5 FAQs sitting on the real query families (type, partner, quit-acting, ballet, Richard II reviews); `same_as` carries Wikidata QID, IMDb and an LoC authority; 6 of 8 H2s are search-intent or hybrid. The dating FAQ correctly answers "not publicly established" instead of inventing a status.
- NEEDS WORK — **link text and destination disagree in one place.** Line 292: `he told [Esquire](https://www.eonline.com/news/1424863/jonathan-bailey-clarifies-acting-hiatus) in November`. The anchor says Esquire; the URL is E! News. The ledger discloses the syndication ("E! News (carrying the Esquire correction)") but the reader and any fact-checker do not see the ledger. Either relabel the link to E! News and keep "told Esquire" in the prose unlinked, or find the Esquire original. Same class of issue, lower severity: the Evening Standard and British GQ quotes are prose-only via syndication, which is honest but leaves three of the piece's most-quoted lines untraceable by click.
- NEEDS WORK — **the emotional interior is front-loaded and the back third goes to reportage.** There is exactly one `<p class="inner-thought">`, at thirteen. The second pass declined to add a second in the sabbatical section on the grounds that no quote sits under his interior state at thirty-seven, which is the right call over inventing one — but the consequence is real: from "Did Jonathan Bailey Quit Acting?" to the Barbican section, roughly 1,400 words run as timeline and casting news with the type mostly parked. The Grande withdrawal is thematically perfect and is reported rather than felt. This is the single reason Enneagram Integration sits at 8 rather than 9.

TO REACH A (9.0+):

1. **Fix the Esquire/E! News mismatch** (line 292) — relabel the anchor to match its destination, or source the original. This is a five-minute fix and it is currently the only clean fact-checker trip in an otherwise exemplary sourcing record.
2. **Give the year-off section one interior beat with a quote under it.** The material exists and is already in the draft, unspent: "twinkly," "expertise way beyond mine," and the People 2025 line about confidence followed by "a deep spike of fear and self-doubt" (currently used only in the rabbit hole's stress-arrow paragraph). Move or echo that spike into the sabbatical section and you have a sourced interior beat at the exact point the piece goes flat — a man handed the largest validation available in his industry, answering it with a spike of self-doubt and a hiring spreadsheet.
3. **Make the Barbican section argue, not just report.** It currently carries excellent facts (RSC at seven → Georges at thirty-nine, same venue, same director as the Olivier) and one good joke about the painter whose model leaves him. What it does not do is connect Grande's withdrawal to the thesis in a sentence: she stepped out of public life because being looked at became unsurvivable; he built a fence so it would stay survivable; both were on the same press tour for the same film. The contrast is sitting right there and the section walks past it.
4. **Vary two more section closers.** Six aphoristic drops remain (down from eight, per the ledger — verified). "He practised." and "Nobody is going to photograph that." are protected and should stay. But the dating section ("if it is for anyone") and the diagnosis section ("neither one is settled here") both land on the same falling cadence within a few hundred words of each other; letting one end mid-thought would break the metronome.
5. **Consider trimming ~400 words from the front half.** At 5,292 words the piece is long for a Google visitor. The leotard is still touched seven-plus times across title, meta_title, description, cold open, TL;DR, diagnosis, prompt book and rabbit hole. The second pass already cut the prompt-book restaging; one more pass on the diagnosis section's recap would tighten it without losing the spine.
