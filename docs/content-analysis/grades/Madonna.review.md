FEEDBACK (2026-10-13):

- Sourcing is the strongest thing here. Her own words run from Time 1985 to the VMA stage in September 2026, with outlet and year at each one, and the sales leg of the type argument rests on Billboard for both albums. The Copacabana crowd is attributed as an estimate. Nine other people speak, including a hostile sibling and two reviewers from the week _Sex_ came out.
- The page cross-examines its own thesis, which few in the corpus do: "nearly everything known about her mother's death comes from Madonna, and Madonna films things." Christopher Ciccone is weighed as a hostile account, and bell hooks is left standing: "That critique deserves to stand without a rebuttal."
- The Type 8 answer lands in one behavior: "Someone who doesn't care what you think doesn't explain camera optics to you." The tense read on "I still have to prove" does the same job for Type 3.
- The 2026 VMA speech is used as evidence, not as an update: she wins Artist of the Year and opens on the 1984 night she was told her career was over. The ending ("She found audiences. Audiences go home.") belongs to no other page.

- NEEDS WORK 1, the last third reads as an itinerary. "Why Is Madonna Still Performing?" is 619 words carrying nine dated beats: the ICU, the O2, three family deaths, the stalled film, the album, the VMAs, Leno in 2012 and the VMAs in 2018. The family-deaths paragraph and the list of earlier tellings read as inserts between the two strongest beats in the piece (the second thought, and the speech). The page is 4,408 words against a 3,200 to 3,900 band. The revision notes say these paragraphs are held by PROTECT-11 and PROTECT-12; that is DJ's call against the jury.
- NEEDS WORK 2, a contradiction inside the type argument. "Why not Type 8" says "outrage alone rarely moves her" and that her changes of product followed albums that undersold. The backlash section then introduces the American Life video as "The reflex kept firing": she pulled it three weeks before release, on outrage she expected, with no sales yet. That is the Three's read-the-room move and it helps the typing, but it cuts against the sentence above it. Concede it in the type section in one clause.
- NEEDS WORK 3, one speech narrates four sections. The 2016 Billboard speech supplies the middle row of the $35 timeline, the account of 1992, the closer of the backlash section and the opener of the aging section. The 1992 section now has Kakutani and James beside it. The $35 section still has only her and Stein.
- NEEDS WORK 4, the serious critic gets no words. hooks is summarized in about 60 words with no quotation, and the notes say the essay was not read in print. One sentence of hers in quotation marks fixes both problems.
- NEEDS WORK 5, how sections end. Seven of ten close on a one-line aphorism ("and she stayed." / "and Madonna films things." / "Naming the radar does not excuse it." / "she has lost control of the close-up."). Each works alone; the regularity is the template showing. Three reader commands remain ("Notice the tense," "look at what she is afraid of," "Weigh that as a hostile account").

- NEEDS WORK 7, small sourcing gaps, none load-bearing: "in her words, the children were still grieving" names no source; the Alan Parker letter and the Joan Lader training carry no source; "In Los Angeles in 2024" omits the venue; "2.1 million for Gaga" is stated flat while her own 1.6 million is hedged as an estimate.
