FEEDBACK (2026-08-05):

- **The revision found the piece's missing center.** "Too F\*ckin' Nice" (May 2021) is a people-pleaser anthem Justice announced by dedicating it to everyone who has "people pleased/made themselves small" and "apologized when they shouldn't have & didn't speak up for themselves when they damn well know they should have." That is a Type 9 self-diagnosis in the subject's own vocabulary, and it was absent from the earlier draft entirely. It now anchors the epigraph, a new H2, and an FAQ. The closing turn on it — she wrote a song instead of making a phone call, so "even the act of finally speaking up took the form of a thing you could scroll past" — is the sharpest new line in the piece.
- **The feud section gained a genuinely surprising fact.** The "I think we all sing" clip is a 2010 Popstar! interview that resurfaced in 2017, and Grande agrees with her on camera — the agreement is edited out of the meme version. A decade of death threats rests on a truncated clip. That reframes the most-searched thing about this subject and no competing page frames it that way.
- **The comeback section is now honest instead of triumphant.** "Love Zombie" was written at seventeen while she was signed to Columbia, i.e. the same vault as "Gold": one song handed to her that died, one she wrote herself that waited fifteen years. And the piece refuses the easy ending — "An algorithm and a few million strangers picked a song out of her drawer and handed her a second act, and she said yes to it... the thing arrives, and she receives it graciously." That resists the redemption arc the material invites.
- **Two unverifiable quotes and one factual error were removed.** The Adam Demos "drive / initiative / very courageous" testimony could not be traced to any *A Perfect Pairing* junket source; "I wasn't always confident... insecurities that I'm trying to grow out of" could not be traced anywhere; "Everyone wants me to be perfect, but I am so far from perfect!" existed only on quote-aggregator sites. All three cut. And she plays **Dylan Pryor, an in-demand actress** in *Suits LA*, not "a young, confident attorney" — the corrected casting actually serves the thesis better ("She is very good at playing the person other people are managing").
- **Sourcing correction with analytical consequences.** The "when to say no" quote is from The Daily Beast, **April 2021**, not the 2025 comeback era where the old draft's placement implied it. Re-dating it produces a better argument: she named the lesson in 2021 and didn't act on it until 2025. The four-year gap is now load-bearing.

- NEEDS WORK: **Third-party testimony is thin — exactly 2 quotes, at the gate minimum**, and one (Gillies, Nick.com) has no year. Cutting Demos was correct, but it left the piece without a single dated collaborator voice from the last decade. This is the main thing holding Evidence at 8 rather than 9.
- NEEDS WORK: **The interior beat is written in generalized second person** rather than anchored to a specific sourced scene. The four-seconds passage works, but the strongest version would render the actual "Gold" studio session or the meeting where the label handed her the song, using her own recalled detail. That is the difference between Enneagram 8.5 and 9.

  3. **The epigraph is the last vague attribution.** If a primary link for the May 2021 announcement post can be pinned (the United By Pop write-up is in `citations` and quotes it), naming the platform would move it to inline and clear the last non-blocker flag.
