## Bottom-line verdict

Even setting aside whether Adéla would accept the Type 3 label, most of this piece treats her as a whole person: it grants her agency over her own choices ("Ain't in LA"), lets her sit with unresolved ambition rather than diagnosing her, keeps a live Type 4 alternative instead of forcing a clean verdict, and holds her accountable for the Manon remarks without letting that define her. One passage breaks that discipline: the retelling of her ballet feet-stretching story recasts a moment she herself frames as proof of toughness into something the draft calls "uncomfortable," attaching an interior lesson to her ("a child had learned...") that she never states and that inverts the pride in her own telling. That is a fixable wording problem, not a structural one — the rest of the piece shows the writer already knows how to keep observation and inference separate.

## What landed

- **SUBJ-H1**: The Google Doc / identity-recovery framing running through the intro and "The child who treated distance like homework" is built almost entirely from her own account ("recovering her identity," per The Line of Best Fit) rather than the writer's diagnosis of her. It lets her explain herself in her own terms before the Enneagram framework is introduced.
- **SUBJ-H2**: "That is meaningful counterevidence to an account centered entirely on approval. She needed the song to exist before the audience supplied encouragement." ("Why ADÉLA is a character," on "Ain't in LA.") This is the piece actively arguing against its own thesis where the evidence calls for it, rather than folding every fact into the Type 3 narrative. It treats her judgment as real authorship, not a symptom.
- **SUBJ-H3**: The Manon passage does the hard thing correctly: "The strongest criticism of that response is specific: she reduced another young woman's appeal to looks... Her account of effort could be sincere and still too narrow." It holds her responsible for a specific, real fault without generalizing it into a character verdict, and it doesn't let the later Emily-comfort scene retroactively excuse the remark.
- **SUBJ-H4**: The concealed-childhood-list claim — verified against The Line of Best Fit directly in this review — turns out to be a faithful paraphrase of her own stated reasoning ("I didn't want [my parents'] lack of belief to affect my belief in myself"), not an invented motive. Same for the "feeling of being lesser because she was Slovak" line in the accent passage — her own words in Vogue go further ("an inferior existence") than the draft's softer paraphrase. Both of these could have been red flags; they check out as fair.

## What missed

- The feet-stretching passage (see SUBJ-R1 below) is the one place a promised effect — showing her as someone who "learned that even the shape of her body could become an assignment" — doesn't land as fair, because the source material she's drawn from tells the opposite emotional story.
- The truncated "I'm basically myself" quote (SUBJ-C1) misses a chance to let her state the persona/private-self relationship in her own fuller words, where the sourcing was already sitting right there.

## What I expected

I expected, for a piece hypothesizing a personality type from press interviews, to have to hunt for places where inferred motivation gets stated as settled fact. I found fewer than expected — the piece is generally careful to hedge ("appears to be," "reads as," "helps explain") exactly where it's extrapolating. I also expected the Type 3 framing to eventually flatten her into an approval-seeking cliché; it doesn't, because the "Ain't in LA" and schedule-dependency material are allowed to sit as real counterweight rather than token concessions.

## What surprised me

The degree of restraint around the Manon controversy surprised me — it would have been easy to either excuse the remark (via the friendship-with-Emily material) or use it to indict her character, and the draft does neither. I was also surprised that two claims I expected to be writer inventions — the reason for hiding the childhood list, and the "lesser because Slovak" line — turned out to be near-verbatim from her own interviews. The one place the piece's discipline slips (the feet passage) stood out more, not less, because of how careful the rest of it is.

## Red flags

**SUBJ-R1** (blocker)
- **Passage**: "**Feet.** Ballet began at three. In her conversation with Coco Mocoe, published in February 2025, she recalled asking her father to press his weight onto her feet during nightly stretching. The detail is uncomfortable because she requested it: a child had learned that even the shape of her body could become an assignment." ("The child who treated distance like homework")
- **Reader/trust effect**: Presents an interior conclusion ("a child had learned...") as an established fact about her psychology, and characterizes the memory as "uncomfortable" — a framing that is not hers.
- **Evidence**: The source transcript (`youtube-transcripts-people/adela-coco-mocoe-interview.md`, ~19:12–20:47) shows this story told as part of an extended, proud narrative about becoming "weirdly super tough" — she links it to her mother's bluntness, her own choice to seek harsher correction ("it always came from me like I always asked them to... correct... what am I doing"), and an unsentimental ballet culture she frames as making her resilient, not as something done to her. Nothing in the source suggests she experienced this as her body "becoming an assignment"; that reading was supplied by the draft, then presented as what "a child had learned," not as the writer's interpretation.
- **Minimum viable repair**: Attribute the framing explicitly as interpretation rather than fact — e.g., replace "The detail is uncomfortable because she requested it: a child had learned that even the shape of her body could become an assignment" with something that keeps the observation (she requested the correction) but drops the unearned interior conclusion, or that acknowledges she frames the same history as the source of her toughness.
- **Expected benefit**: Removes the one passage in the piece that inverts the subject's own stated meaning of her memory rather than reporting it.
- **Confidence**: High — verified directly against the primary transcript, not secondhand.
- **Acceptance test**: A version of this passage should be defensible to someone who has read the full Coco Mocoe transcript as an accurate description of what she conveyed, not just of what she did.

**SUBJ-C1** (concern)
- **Passage**: "In a conversation published by V Magazine on September 8, 2026, she put the continuity plainly: 'I'm basically myself.' Her switch into performance mode amplifies a person already there." ("Why ADÉLA is a character")
- **Reader/trust effect**: Minor — the full quote is more specific and more supportive of the draft's own point than the truncated version, so nothing is misrepresented, but her own more precise words are swapped for the writer's paraphrase where a direct quote was available.
- **Evidence**: Full sentence, verified live: "I'm basically myself, but I do find that something happens before I walk on stage where it's time to turn it on." (V Magazine, 2026-09-08.)
- **Minimum viable repair**: Quote the full sentence, or at minimum the "something happens before I walk on stage where it's time to turn it on" clause, rather than replacing it with editorial paraphrase.
- **Expected benefit**: Lets her describe the persona/private-self relationship in her own words at the exact point the piece is making that argument.
- **Confidence**: High.
- **Acceptance test**: The passage should use her own language for the "switch," not just the writer's paraphrase of it, when her language was already sourced and available.

**SUBJ-C2** (concern)
- **Passage**: "There is a recognizable hurt underneath the complaint. Adéla had believed training could prevent her origins from limiting her chances. When audience votes became part of the decision, she confronted another way of being judged that she could not simply practice harder to control." ("When effort lost the vote")
- **Reader/trust effect**: States a specific belief about her motivation for joining the program ("had believed training could prevent her origins from limiting her chances") as settled fact rather than as the writer's bridge between two separately-sourced facts (her stated feeling of being "lesser" as Slovak, and the program's originally merit-based framing per Teen Vogue).
- **Evidence**: No single source in the packet has her stating this specific causal belief about the program; it is a plausible synthesis, not a quote or paraphrase of one.
- **Minimum viable repair**: Soften to mark it as inference — e.g., "the same conviction may explain why..." — consistent with the hedging the piece uses elsewhere ("That reads as a threat to earned value").
- **Expected benefit**: Keeps the empathetic reading intact while being honest about where reporting ends and interpretation begins.
- **Confidence**: Medium — the underlying components are each sourced, but their combination here is the writer's, not hers.
- **Acceptance test**: A reader should be able to tell this sentence is the writer connecting two facts, not a third fact she stated herself.
