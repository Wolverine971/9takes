## Executive verdict

The piece's architecture is sound: a hedged Type 3 hypothesis with a genuinely live Type 4 alternative, a Manon episode that holds Adéla accountable without a character verdict, an "Ain't in LA" beat that lets real evidence complicate the thesis, and unusually disciplined absolute-date sourcing. Four of six reviewers converge on protecting the same handful of sentences, and none found a fabrication, a wrong central fact, or a typology claim stated as settled certainty.

Two passages fail the fairness standard and are mandatory repairs. First, the ballet feet-stretching passage inverts Adéla's own stated meaning of the memory — she tells it as a proud toughness story; the draft assigns her an unstated interior wound ("a child had learned that even the shape of her body could become an assignment") and calls it "uncomfortable." This was independently verified against the primary transcript. Second, the Manon material closes on a redemptive beat (comforting Emily Kelavos) whose structural effect — harsh remark, psychological explanation, warm anecdote — reads as the matter being settled, when independent reporting shows Manon's situation with the group was still unresolved as of the article's own 2026 refresh window. Neither repair requires touching the piece's protected material; both are scoping/framing fixes to sentences immediately adjacent to content every reviewer wants kept.

Beyond the two P0s, ten P1 items are accepted, nearly all single-clause, well-sourced, and already convergently flagged by two or more independent reviewers (or by the draft's own embedded fresh-eyes note): an Adele/Adéla disambiguation, an early KATSEYE gloss, the never-named Provocateur EP, the unresolved "indie rock" detour (now fully sourced to an on-the-record Rolling Stone quote), two internal-consistency tests against 9takes' own Type 3/Type 4 pillar pages, and three small quote-fidelity/hedging fixes. None of these require new research; the reviewers who raised them already did the sourcing.

**P0-01 — The feet-stretching passage assigns Adéla an interior wound she never states, inverting her own framing of the memory as pride in toughness**

- Originating reviews: SUBJ-R1 (subject, blocker); indirectly touched by UNFAM-C (unfamiliar), which praised the sentence's craft without checking the source.
- Location: "The child who treated distance like homework," lines 172-173: *"**Feet.** Ballet began at three. In her conversation with Coco Mocoe, published in February 2025, she recalled asking her father to press his weight onto her feet during nightly stretching. The detail is uncomfortable because she requested it: a child had learned that even the shape of her body could become an assignment."*
- Adjudicated problem: "The detail is uncomfortable" and "a child had learned that even the shape of her body could become an assignment" are presented as fact, not as the writer's inference. Checked directly against the source transcript (`youtube-transcripts-people/adela-coco-mocoe-interview.md`, ~19:12-20:47): she tells this story as part of an extended, proud account of becoming "weirdly super tough," explicitly says the correction was something she asked for and pushed for herself, and frames the ballet culture as the source of her resilience — not as something imposed on her that she now reads as ominous. The draft's framing is the inverse of what she conveyed.
- Evidence and confidence: High. Verified against the primary transcript, not a secondhand characterization — this is the strongest kind of subject-fairness evidence available to this jury.
- Minimum repair: Keep the factual content (she requested nightly foot-stretching correction from her father) and the self-aware acknowledgment structure that flagged for the reader that this detail might land hard — but replace the invented interior conclusion with her own framing. E.g., "The detail could read as harsh — except she frames this history as the origin of her toughness, not as something done to her," rather than asserting an unstated lesson about her body "becoming an assignment."
- Expected reader benefit: Removes the one passage in the piece that puts words in the subject's mouth about her own inner life, in the exact direction — implying quiet suffering — that a subject-fairness read is built to catch.
- Protected hit or tradeoff at risk: The unfamiliar reviewer independently praised this exact sentence's craft ("handled with explicit self-awareness in the prose itself... which pre-empts the reaction rather than needing a reader to supply it") without checking against source. The repair must preserve that pre-emptive, reader-aware move — it should still name the discomfort a reader might feel and address it head-on — while correcting what it attributes to Adéla's own understanding of the memory. Also preserve the surrounding "timetable" counterexample sentence, which multiple reviewers protect as the section's disconfirming-fact anchor (see PROTECT-01).
- Acceptance test: A reader who has read the full Coco Mocoe transcript would recognize the rewritten passage as an accurate description of what Adéla conveyed, not just of what she did.

## Conflicts and editorial tradeoffs

**Feet-stretching passage: craft praise vs. subject-fairness verification.** The unfamiliar reviewer, working only from the reader-visible text with no primary-source check, praised the feet-stretching sentence's self-aware handling of a potentially uncomfortable detail. The subject reviewer checked the same sentence against the primary transcript and found it inverts what Adéla actually conveyed (pride in toughness, not discomfort). Verification against source outranks craft approval that never checked the source; P0-01's repair keeps the pre-emptive, reader-aware structure the unfamiliar reviewer liked while correcting its content to match Adéla's own framing, so nothing the unfamiliar reviewer valued is lost.

**The Emily-comfort beat: fan's "must preserve" vs. critic's fairness flag.** The fan reviewer's preserve list explicitly protects the Emily sentence and says it should not be cut for length; the critic flags the same sentence as creating a false impression of resolution given Manon's real, unresolved 2026 situation. Both are right about different things: the sentence itself is good, necessary editorial balance, and the section's overall shape still misleads a reader who knows the current state of the Manon/KATSEYE relationship. P0-02 resolves this by keeping the sentence verbatim and adding an adjacent scoping clause rather than cutting anything.

**The "Ain't in LA" counterevidence sentence: three-reviewer protection vs. one reviewer's precision critique.** CRITIC-R3 argues that "an account centered entirely on approval" attacks a strawman version of the Type 3 case rather than the sharper Three/Four opposition the Rabbit Hole itself uses. Fan and subject both independently name this exact passage as must-preserve, load-bearing evidence that the piece argues against its own thesis. Given two independent, higher-confidence protections against one reviewer's self-rated low-medium-confidence rhetorical critique, this synthesis sides with preservation — see Rejected feedback.
