> "I got fourth place, so I snuck in there."
>
> — Alexandr Wang, _This Past Weekend w/ Theo Von_, February 2025

<p class="firstLetter">There was a math competition in New Mexico. The top four finishers in the state won an all-expenses-paid trip to Disney World. Alexandr Wang was in sixth grade, and he has said that the trip was the most motivating thing he could imagine. He came fourth.</p>

Twenty years later Theo Von asked him about it. He did not describe the math. He described the prize, and then his own margin, on _This Past Weekend_ in February 2025: "I got fourth place, so I snuck in there."

Today he is Meta's first Chief AI Officer. He runs Meta Superintelligence Labs, the division Mark Zuckerberg built around him in June 2025 by paying $14.3 billion for 49% of Wang's company, Scale AI. On August 5, 2026, that division shipped its first commercial product. He was born in January 1997, and in January 2026 one of the men who built the field went on the record to say he does not belong in the chair.

The interesting thing is that Wang has been telling us he snuck in since sixth grade.

## Who is Alexandr Wang, and how did he end up running Meta's AI?

He was born in January 1997 in Los Alamos, New Mexico. Both of his parents are physicists, Chinese immigrants who worked at Los Alamos National Laboratory. The missing letter in his first name is deliberate. Alexandr runs to eight characters rather than nine, reported as a nod to an auspicious number in Chinese culture, and it is the only thing separating him from Alexander Wang the fashion designer. His mother studied plasma, "how plasma worked inside of stars and stuff like that." He puts the geography plainly: "All the New Mexico shots in Oppenheimer, that's exactly where I grew up."

Los Alamos is a company town, and the company is the bomb. Wang has described the social effect of that with no drama at all. "Everybody I knew, effectively, their parents worked at the lab or some affiliate with the lab." Every adult was doing something enormous. That is the water an ambitious kid swims in there.

He got good at competitions. He qualified for the Math Olympiad Program in 2013 and the US Physics Team in 2014, and reached the USACO finals in 2012 and 2013. He wrote software for Addepar and for Quora while still a teenager. He went to MIT for computer science and math and lasted one year, spending part of it building a camera for his refrigerator that would tell him when his roommates were eating his food.

In March 2016, AlphaGo beat Lee Sedol. "I remember feeling like, oh wow, AI, it's happening," Wang said. He left school. "I remember I flew straight from Boston to San Francisco, and then started the company, basically." He was 19. He founded Scale AI with Lucy Guo out of a Portola Valley pool house belonging to the investor Sarah Guo, went through Y Combinator, and started selling labeled training data to machine learning teams. From there the private life goes off the record. His relationship status is not publicly established, and the several names circulating online have no evidentiary basis behind them.

Scale grew into the supply chain underneath most of the industry. By 2021 it was worth $7.3 billion and Wang, holding roughly 15%, became the youngest self-made billionaire in the world at 24. Revenue reached about $1 billion in 2025, up from $870 million the year before. The Pentagon became a major customer. In June 2025, Meta paid $14.3 billion for 49% of the company at a valuation near $29 billion, and the thing it was really buying walked across the street to become Chief AI Officer.

## What is Alexandr Wang's personality type?

### Alexandr Wang is an Enneagram Type 3

The <a href="/enneagram-corner/enneagram-type-3">Type 3</a> is the Achiever, and the shorthand for the type is ambition. That shorthand is close enough to be useless. What defines a Three is where their sense of existing comes from: the scoreboard, and the standing on it.

Wang has been explicit about this since childhood without ever framing it as a confession. On _This Past Weekend_ in 2025 he told Theo Von, "I ended up getting really competitive about math in particular. So my sport was math." Then, unprompted: "That was like a big part of my identity was being a mathlete." He was a nationally ranked mathlete through middle and high school, and the word he reaches for is ranked.

Later he authored a core value for his own company, three words long, and issued it to the whole building as an operating instruction: **Ambition shapes reality.**

The tell is not the ambition. Silicon Valley runs on ambition, and the Type 5 investigator who is the archetype of this industry has plenty of it. The tell is what survived from the math competition. He kept the trip and the placement. The problem set is gone.

A Five would have kept the problem. That is the real fork, and what settles it is the direction Wang moved every time he reached it. He left MIT one year in and flew to San Francisco to sell. He built a business whose product is the mobilization of expertise rather than the accumulation of his own.

That choice has now been sitting for ten years, and the company it produced is the most detailed thing he has ever said about himself.

## Why Alexandr Wang built a company out of other people's expertise

Scale AI is a strange company to have chosen, and the strangeness is the whole story.

It does not build models. It builds the substrate models learn from. A distributed workforce of human annotators looks at images, text, code and video, and their judgment gets converted into labeled data that a model can train on. Later Scale added evaluation, which is the same operation aimed the other way: humans grading machine output so the machine knows what good means.

Wang describes the business in language that is unusually explicit about what is happening. "The only model that we have in the world for the level of intelligence that we seek to create is humans, or is humanity." And then the mechanism: "The production of frontier data looks a lot like a sort of marriage between human experts and humanity with technical and algorithmic techniques around the models to produce huge amounts of this kind of data."

Read that twice. The company's raw material is human expertise. Its product is that expertise, industrialized.

<div class="pull-quote">He spent a decade building the machinery that decides whose judgment is good enough to become training data.</div>

The judgment has to come from somewhere, and it is worth being specific about where. Scale's annotation work ran through a subsidiary platform called Remotasks. In August 2023 the Washington Post published an investigation into it: of 36 current and former Filipino taskers interviewed, all but two said payments had been delayed, cut or cancelled after they finished the work, and one 23-year-old said she was paid 30 cents for four hours. The following March, Rest of World reported that Remotasks had abruptly cut off workers in Kenya, Nigeria and Pakistan. Scale cited security protocols, said the notice failed to reach Kenyan workers because of an internal error, and said all work completed under its guidelines was paid.

Scale's answers were specific. The shape of the business survives them: the judgment gets bought cheap from people a long way from the money, and industrializing it is what made the company worth $29 billion.

The customers were satisfied, and the most consequential of them was the government. Scale won Project Thunderforge, a military planning program worth roughly $500 million, and was selected for the Golden Dome missile-defense effort. Wang testified before the House Armed Services Committee in 2023. "Their ability to pull in vast amounts of different data and make sense of that data, and structure it in a way that we can actually train algorithms off of it, it's pretty unique," said Cameron Stanley, Chief Digital and AI Officer at the Department of War, in Forbes in May 2026. Sarah Guo, the investor who had the founders in her pool house, called Wang "11/10 smart and ambitious" and "superspecial."

The scoreboard had gone national. The boy who wanted top four in New Mexico was now supplying the substrate underneath American military planning.

There was a cost inside the building. Lucy Guo, who co-founded the company with him, was gone by 2018 after telling someone she thought Wang should be fired and having it get back to him. "I was bitter about who I thought told him that because I thought there was a certain level of trust," she told Forbes. "I felt a little betrayed."

She kept roughly 3% of the stock, and it kept appreciating without her. In April 2025, two months before Meta's money arrived, that stake made her the youngest self-made woman billionaire in the world by Forbes' count, displacing Taylor Swift. The person Wang removed from the company got ranked anyway, on the same list that ranks him.

Wang had already published his own theory of who deserves to be ranked. In June 2024 he coined MEI, for merit, excellence, and intelligence, arguing for hiring "the best possible person, regardless of their demographics" with no demographic targets. Critics read it as a rebranding of an old argument that ignores how bias actually operates in hiring. It is also, read a different way, the most personally revealing thing he has ever published: a man who has been sorted by measured performance since sixth grade, proposing measured performance as the only legitimate way to sort anyone.

The trouble with that proposal was visible from inside his own company. Somebody wrote the rubric the taskers were graded against. Somebody decided what a good label looks like, what counts as a violation, when an account gets closed. A meritocracy is only ever as clean as whoever holds the scorecard, and Wang has spent his life on both sides of that: measured, then measuring.

## What Yann LeCun said about Alexandr Wang, and what he got right

On January 5, 2026, the Financial Times published an interview with Yann LeCun, who had spent more than a decade as Meta's Chief AI Scientist and who shares a Turing Award for the work that made the current era possible. He called Wang "young" and "inexperienced." Then the specific charge: "no experience with research or how you practice research." He described the new hires as "completely LLM-pilled" and repeated his long-held position that large language models are a dead end for superintelligence.

Declare the interest, because it is large. LeCun left Meta in November 2025 to start a company that competes with the one he was describing. He has argued against the LLM paradigm since before Wang was hired, so a verdict that Wang's team is on the wrong path is also a verdict that LeCun was right all along. None of that makes the criticism false. It makes it interested, and interested criticism is often the sharpest kind, because the person delivering it has thought about the target longer than anyone being polite.

What makes the line land is that a friendly witness had already said the same thing six months earlier and meant it as praise. "Alex Wang is not a researcher, he is a leader," Alex Ren of Fellows Fund told Fortune in July 2025. Both men are describing the same person accurately. Only one of them thinks it is a problem.

There is a second charge underneath LeCun's: that Wang's lab was assembled with a checkbook. His answer to that one is the most revealing thing he has said about the job. Meta had spent the talent war buying researchers at prices that leaked, packages reported above $100 million, and on the _Core Memory_ podcast in May 2026 Ashlee Vance put the obvious reading to him. Wang rejected it. "I think it's like an incorrect assumption to think that, like the researchers are just money motivated or anything," he said. "People joined because there was high compute per researcher, so they could make more progress." The man who built a business out of pricing human expertise was arguing that you cannot read these particular humans off their price.

Here is where the reading gets uncomfortable, and where it stops being about org charts.

The heart triad is the strange part of the Enneagram for most people. Types 2, 3 and 4 sit at the center of feeling, and the Three is the one furthest from their own. Shame is the underlying weather there, and the Three's response is to outrun it. Every win quiets the voice for a while. Then the voice comes back with a higher number attached, and this is the machinery that makes the type's cruelest sentence so effective. Tell a Three they have not earned their position and you have not insulted them. You have agreed with them.

<p class="inner-thought">Don't do this. Not here. Not in front of them. Finish the sentence, get to the part about the pool house, they'll laugh, you can sit down.</p>

That was the Scale all-hands in June 2025, the day after the Meta deal was announced. Wang walked down the stairs into applause from the company he was leaving. "I literally teared up," he told Forbes. He started telling the founding story and began to cry, and an attendee heard him ask, out loud, of himself: "This is so stupid. Why am I doing this?"

The tears got scored and marked wrong in real time.

So take the harshest version of the case against him. He is a salesman who sold Mark Zuckerberg $14.3 billion of a company mostly to deliver himself, installed above researchers with decades on him, running a lab he could not staff on technical reputation alone. Watch him try to hold it: a year in, CNBC reported that Meta's stock trailed every other tech megacap and that developers remained skeptical the company could compete with OpenAI, Anthropic and Google at all.

Now put it next to the sixth-grader who came fourth of four.

He has made every room he has ever been in by exactly one spot. Ranked, seeded, cut lists, top four in the state. A person who grows up on the qualifying side of a threshold learns something specific: the ranking is the only thing anyone remembers, and the people who set the criteria have more power than the people who meet them. Then he built one. Scale AI is a machine for deciding whose judgment is good enough to become training data, and he ran it for nine years.

The man who industrialized the ranking of expertise got ranked, publicly, by the highest authority in his field, and came up short.

That is not a defense of him. Wang has real gaps and LeCun named one of them precisely. It is the difference between a person doing something cynical and a person doing the only thing that ever worked, at a scale where it might not work anymore.

### Alexandr Wang's Wing: 3w2

The 3w2 achieves through people, and Wang's record is a recruiting record before it is anything else. He reportedly took one-to-one calls with every new Scale hire well past the point where headcount made that reasonable. A former Scale manager told Fortune he was "a great recruiter, a really savvy commercial person." He built the relationships that got him a pool house from an investor, a Y Combinator batch, Pentagon contracts, a seat at a presidential inauguration in January 2025, and meetings with Starmer, Modi and Macron. Then he built a lab by hiring away other companies' researchers. That is the 2 <a href="/enneagram-corner/enneagram-wings-complete-guide">wing</a> at work: warmth deployed as infrastructure.

The 3w4 case is not empty. The eight-character spelling of his first name is a curated choice about how he lands, and a clean 3w2 does not usually cry at its own farewell. The 2 wing still wins on volume of evidence.

### Alexandr Wang's Instinctual Subtype: Social

The social 3 is the classic one: prestige, standing, being known by the people who matter. Wang's public life is organized around rooms rather than around possessions or intimacy. Honest limit first: the public record does not contain a clean instance of Wang under disintegration. ### Counterarguments: Why Alexandr Wang Might Not Be Type 3

The strongest alternative is Type 5. The evidence is real: two physicist parents, an olympiad track, self-taught coding, a decade-long fixation on data as the substrate of intelligence, and genuine technical depth from Quora and Addepar onward. Plenty of people read him as a quiet systems thinker.
