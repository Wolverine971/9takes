## Identity and scope

**Verified fact.** Alexandr Wang (born January 1997, Los Alamos, New Mexico) is an American
technology executive. He co-founded Scale AI in 2016 and served as its CEO until June 2025, when
Meta paid $14.3 billion for a 49% non-voting stake and hired him as its first Chief AI Officer,
leading Meta Superintelligence Labs (MSL). He remains on Scale's board. (S-01, S-02, S-13)

**Verified fact — name and disambiguation.** His first name is spelled "Alexandr," without the
second "e." Fortune reports this is "to give it the eight characters associated with good fortune in
Chinese culture." He is a distinct person from **Alexander Wang**, the fashion designer, whose
Wikipedia entry surfaces inside the same result set. (S-03, S-15)

**Verified fact — handles and identifiers.** X/Twitter `@alexandr_wang`; personal Wikipedia article
at `en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alexandr_Wang`; Forbes real-time billionaire profile; Meta leadership page.

**Why 9takes covers him (from the pipeline's own record, not an external fact).** The entity-gap
packet (`docs/content-analysis/entity-gaps/Alexandr-Wang.md`, 2026-08-18) scored him **43/100,
classification `pass`, action `watch`** — a strong subject in a saturated SERP. Its stated rationale
for covering him anyway is an **interpretive** gap: "no top result explains what kind of operator
Wang is or why the LeCun critique lands." Evaluators should know the draft exists against a `pass`
recommendation, and that the packet named the signature contradiction the draft then built on.

**Scope note.** Two figures the packet explicitly forbade and the draft correctly omits: a bare age,
and a net-worth number. One it forbade naming — a partner or spouse — the draft handles with a
"not publicly established" sentence. See CLM-31.

### The math competition (the draft's spine)

> "There was this one math competition where if you got top four in the state, state of New Mexico,
> then you would get an all expense paid trip to Disney World."
> — _This Past Weekend w/ Theo Von_ E563, 2025-02-18 (S-04)

> "I remember as a sixth grader, that was the most motivating thing I could possibly imagine was an
> all-expense paid trip to Disney World."
> — same (S-04)

> "I got fourth place, so I snuck in there."
> — same (S-04)

**Transcript-order finding (material to the draft's central move).** The HappyScribe transcript
places the placement disclosure as an **answer to a direct question**: Von asks whether Wang won;
Wang answers "I got fourth place, so I snuck in there." (S-04)

- What this **can** support: that when narrating the competition, the prize and the placement are
  what Wang supplies, and the mathematics is not.
- What this **cannot** support: that Wang volunteered his rank unprompted at that moment. The draft
  does not claim he did for this line — it reserves "unprompted" for the mathlete quote — but the
  read "he did not describe the math, he described the prize" is an **interpretation** of a
  conversational sequence partly steered by the host.

**Resolved unresolved question.** The prior research file (`docs/content-analysis/research/Alexandr-Wang.md`,
§2 and §7.1) flagged that a transcript extract appeared to render Wang saying "I won it" after Von
asked "Did you win it?", and instructed: _do not claim he won_. Re-reading the HappyScribe transcript
returns the exchange as Von asking whether he won and Wang answering with fourth place. The "I won
it" line appears to have been a mis-segmentation artifact in the earlier extract. **The draft makes
no win claim, and the spine survives.** Residual caveat: this reading came through a page-summary
fetch, not a verbatim block quote — see limitations.

## Disputes and unresolved questions

1. **Does the $500M belong to Thunderforge?** (CLM-13) Forbes 2026-05-14 says yes, in terms. Defense
   trade press covering the March 2025 DIU award published no figure, and Forbes' own May 2026 piece
   attributes $500M to a CDAO contract that was 5× a September 2025 $100M deal. Any conclusion that
   Scale "won a $500M military planning program" in the Thunderforge sense must stay qualified, or
   be re-sourced to a primary contract record.

2. **Scale's 2025 revenue.** (CLM-09) Company-reported "just shy of $1 billion" (Forbes, May 2026)
   versus a widely republished $2B figure that traces to an April 2025 Bloomberg _projection_.
   Treat ~$1B as the better-sourced actual, and never present $2B as a 2025 result.

3. **How many MSL hires never formally started?** (CLM-18) Reporting supports one (Verma). "Two"
   is not established. The underlying Wired original was not read.

4. **What exactly does the Muse Code contributor tier consume?** (CLM-25) Secondary coverage says
   "your code." Meta's own research blog, when fetched, carried neither the pricing tiers nor the
   training-data language. Whether "prompts and completions" is the correct characterization is
   unresolved, and it matters because the draft's closing image rests on it.

5. **Scale's response to the Washington Post investigation.** Not retrieved. Only the Rest of World
   response is verified. Any sentence implying Scale answered the WaPo findings specifically is
   currently unsupported.

6. **Wing 3w2 vs 3w4, and the social subtype call.** Under-evidenced in both directions. The name
   spelling — the draft's own 3w4 counterweight in earlier passes — is now sourced to Chinese
   numerology rather than self-presentation, which weakens it as a 4-wing signal in either location.

7. **Whether the mathlete line was volunteered.** The draft says "Then, unprompted." Prompting could
   not be verified from the transcript material accessed. Low stakes, easily checked by anyone with
   the full transcript.

8. **The all-hands self-directed line.** One unnamed attendee, one outlet, no recording. It is the
   emotional load-bearing point of the entire piece and it rests on the thinnest evidence in it.

9. **Base rates and the Type 5 alternative.** The draft (correctly, per its own trim notes) removed
   the 9takes corpus statistic. That removal also removed the only quantitative reason given for
   treating Type 5 as the field's archetype. The claim now stands on assertion.

10. **A moving target.** Zuckerberg announced open-sourcing Muse Spark 1.2's weights on 2026-08-10,
    five days after the draft's current-tense anchor and eight days before the draft's date. The
    piece's "current" frame is already slightly behind the record.

## Research limitations

1. **No paywalled original was read in full.** The Financial Times LeCun interview, Bloomberg's
   layoff and revenue pieces, Wired's MSL-departures story, and the Washington Post Remotasks
   investigation were all reached through secondary relays or search summaries. Where quotes appear
   in ≥3 independent outlets with identical wording, they are marked verified; where they do not,
   they are marked attributed. CNBC returned HTTP 403 to direct fetches twice.

2. **Podcast transcripts were read through page summaries, not verbatim.** This applies to both
   Theo Von E563 and Core Memory EP 71. Speaker attribution on the Los Alamos and Oppenheimer lines
   was explicitly re-queried and returned Wang for all three, but a verbatim block-quote read would
   be stronger. The "did you win it" resolution in particular deserves a direct transcript check by
   anyone who wants to rely on it heavily.

3. **The House Armed Services Committee witness bio was not read.** The prior research pass logged
   HTTP 403 at docs.house.gov and this pass did not retry. The fact of July 2023 testimony is
   supported by Wikipedia; nothing may be quoted from the document.

4. **Six long-form first-person sources remain unmined**, as the prior research file also noted:
   Lightcone (Jun 2025), CSIS AI Policy Podcast (May 2025), Washington Post Futurist Summit
   (Oct 2023, full official transcript exists), No Priors, Accel "Spotlight On: AI", Stanford eCorner.
   Additional interior and emotional material likely exists there and would materially strengthen or
   weaken the Type 3 hypothesis, particularly on the missing stress-arrow question.

5. **No primary contract record was retrieved** for either Thunderforge or the CDAO award. The
   CLM-13 dispute is therefore a conflict between two reputable secondary sources, not a resolved
   error. A USAspending or DIU contract-announcement lookup would settle it.

6. **Meta's own Muse Code documentation did not yield the pricing or data-use terms** when fetched;
   the contributor-tier language in this packet is entirely secondary. The precise scope of what
   Meta trains on under that tier is unresolved.

7. **No Enneagram self-report, and no reputable typing of Wang, exists.** The only crowd-typing found
   addresses MBTI. The Type 3 hypothesis rests entirely on behavioral inference from the record
   assembled here.

8. **Scale's response to the Washington Post** and any Scale statement on the "$500M Thunderforge"
   characterization were not located.

9. **Living, fast-moving subject.** The 2026-08-10 open-weights announcement already post-dates the
   draft's current-tense anchor. Anything in the Meta/MSL section can be overtaken within days.
