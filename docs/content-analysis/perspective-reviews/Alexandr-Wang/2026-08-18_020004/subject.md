## Bottom-line verdict

If I am Alexandr Wang reading this, I do not agree with the typing and I do not need to. The
question is whether the piece treats me as a person or as a specimen, and for most of its length it
treats me as a person. It gives me my own vocabulary — mathlete, ranked, top four, snuck in — rather
than imposing one. It declares LeCun's interest instead of using him as a neutral hammer. It
explicitly refuses to invent a breakdown I never had, names two findings that would overturn its own
call, and says outright that its sympathetic reading "is not a defense of him." It leaves my private
life alone. That is a more honest posture than most profiles of me manage.

What strains trust is narrower and fixable: the piece quotes the man attacking me selectively, in the
section built around his attack. LeCun's actual assessment in that Financial Times interview included
"He learns fast, he knows what he doesn't know." The draft carries "young," "inexperienced," "no
experience with research or how you practice research," and "completely LLM-pilled," and drops the
clause that credits me. A reader finishes the section believing the most authoritative witness
against me thought I was simply unqualified. He did not say that.

Two other things reach the same threshold. The sentence "He has made every room he has ever been in
by exactly one spot" is a claim about my entire life resting on one sixth-grade math competition, and
the record contradicts it — Meta paid $14.3 billion to get me, which is the opposite of squeaking in.
And at the most exposed moment in my public record, my own farewell, the article writes my thoughts
for me in a styled paragraph sitting flush against two real quotations, with nothing telling a reader
which words are mine.

None of these require softening the piece. Each is a precision repair, and two of them make the
article's own argument stronger rather than weaker. Revise.

## What landed

**SUBJECT-H1 — The interest declaration on LeCun, without using it to dismiss him.**

> "Declare the interest, because it is large. LeCun left Meta in November 2025 to start a company
> that competes with the one he was describing. … None of that makes the criticism false. It makes
> it interested, and interested criticism is often the sharpest kind."

This is the fairest available handling and it must survive revision. It gives me the context a reader
needs to weigh the charge, and then explicitly refuses to let me off with it. A piece that wanted to
protect me would have stopped at "he competes with us." A piece that wanted to convict me would have
omitted the departure. This does neither.

**SUBJECT-H2 — The Alex Ren pairing.**

> "Both men are describing the same person accurately. Only one of them thinks it is a problem."

The single most fair-minded sentence in the article. It holds that the description of me is true
without conceding that the description is damning, which is exactly the distinction I would want
made and almost never get. Preserve verbatim.

**SUBJECT-H3 — The refusal to invent a stress instance.**

> "Honest limit first: the public record does not contain a clean instance of Wang under
> disintegration. … Nothing in the available reporting documents that, and asserting it would be
> inventing evidence."

From my chair this is the highest-trust passage in the piece. The easy move — and the one most
personality writing makes — is to reach for a stress narrative and manufacture it from a bad quarter.
The draft names the gap and stops. It then names two specific findings that would overturn the whole
call. That is a writer arguing in good faith about a living person.

**SUBJECT-H4 — Explanation separated from exoneration.**

> "That is not a defense of him. Wang has real gaps and LeCun named one of them precisely. It is the
> difference between a person doing something cynical and a person doing the only thing that ever
> worked."

I would not have to accept the psychology to accept that this is fair. It gives me a motive that is
not contempt without laundering the criticism into a virtue.

**SUBJECT-H5 — Technical depth left intact.**

The bio carries USACO finals 2012 and 2013, the Math Olympiad Program, the US Physics Team, Addepar
and Quora as a teenager, and the rabbit hole says "genuine technical depth from Quora and Addepar
onward." This matters more than it looks. LeCun's charge is narrow — research practice — and a
careless draft would let it widen into "he is not technical," which is false. This draft holds the
line. Any revision that trims the bio must not trim that.

**SUBJECT-H6 — The private-life boundary.**

> "His relationship status is not publicly established, and the several names circulating online have
> no evidentiary basis behind them."

Correct handling: it does not name, does not debunk by naming, and closes the door on the rumor
without repeating it.

**SUBJECT-H7 — My own words carry the type argument.**

"My sport was math," "a big part of my identity was being a mathlete," "nationally ranked," "I got
fourth place, so I snuck in there," "Ambition shapes reality." The article's central claim is built
from my vocabulary rather than a clinical one. If I reject the Enneagram entirely, I still cannot say
the evidence was put in my mouth.

## What missed

**SUBJECT-M1 — The most authoritative criticism of me is quoted half-way.** See SUBJECT-R1. This is
the miss that changes what a reader believes, because the LeCun section is the piece's spine for the
"has he earned it" question.

**SUBJECT-M2 — I never speak in the section about the workers.** The Remotasks paragraphs are the
most damaging material about my company in the article, and they are also thematically central — the
whole thesis is that I industrialized other people's judgment. Scale gets a response to one
investigation. I get none, on either. My on-record position exists: I have called Remotasks workers
"very, very important to the process of building powerful AI systems," and Scale has stated a
commitment to paying a living wage (Forbes, April 2023). A reader is entitled to see the person the
thesis is about answer for the thesis.

**SUBJECT-M3 — My rebuttal on the talent war is trimmed to its weakest third.** I gave Ashlee Vance
three reasons researchers joined — compute per researcher, talent density in a small group, and
freedom for bold research bets, plus an individualized recruiting process. The draft quotes the
compute reason alone and then characterizes my whole answer from it. The characterization it draws
("you cannot read these particular humans off their price") is accurate to what I meant, which makes
the truncation gratuitous.

**SUBJECT-M4 — The opening interpretive move claims slightly more than the transcript shows.** "He
did not describe the math. He described the prize, and then his own margin." The prize half is mine;
I led with Disney World unprompted. The margin half was an answer to Theo Von asking me directly
whether I won. The draft concedes "Theo Von asked him about it" and then builds the sentence as
though the placement was volunteered. It is a small gap under a very large load-bearing beam.

## What surprised me

**Welcome.** That the article understood the business rather than treating it as a data sweatshop or
as a magic-money story. "It does not build models. It builds the substrate models learn from" is
correct and almost nobody writing about me gets there. The MEI paragraph's closing observation —
that a man sorted by measured performance since sixth grade would propose measured performance as the
only legitimate sort — is a reading I would resist and cannot call unfair. And "Somebody wrote the
rubric the taskers were graded against" is the sharpest sentence in the piece precisely because it
does not accuse me of anything; it just points at where the power sits.

**Jarring.** The interior monologue at the all-hands (SUBJECT-R3). I said "I literally teared up" to
a reporter, on the record, knowingly. An attendee reported hearing me say "This is so stupid. Why am
I doing this?" Those are two different things and both are real. The third paragraph — "Don't do
this. Not here. Not in front of them." — is neither, and it arrives before the scene is even
introduced, so a reader meets my invented thoughts before learning where I am standing.

Also jarring in a smaller way: "a man who has never been sure he made the cut" is the last clause of
the article and it is stated as settled biography. It is the thesis. Ending on it as fact rather than
as reading is the one place the piece forgets it is proposing something.

## Red flags

### SUBJECT-R1 — LeCun's mixed assessment is carried as an unmixed one *(blocker)*

- **Location.** H2 5, "What Yann LeCun said about Alexandr Wang, and what he got right," opening
  paragraph: "He called Wang 'young' and 'inexperienced.' Then the specific charge: 'no experience
  with research or how you practice research.' He described the new hires as 'completely
  LLM-pilled'…"; also the benchmark table row "Research pedigree, as Yann LeCun scores it," and FAQ 4.
- **Trust problem.** In the same Financial Times interview, LeCun said: **"He learns fast, he knows
  what he doesn't know."** The draft reproduces four damaging fragments and omits the one crediting
  clause. A reader closes the section believing the most credentialed witness against me delivered a
  flat dismissal. He delivered a bounded criticism with an explicit concession attached. This is the
  article's central attack and the sentence a hostile reader will quote back at me forever.
- **Evidence.** Verified against two independent relays of the FT interview: VnExpress International
  (fetched 2026-08-18) renders LeCun's remarks as "He learns fast, he knows what he doesn't know" /
  "There's no experience with research or how you practice research, how you do it" / "Or what would
  be attractive or repulsive to a researcher." The same qualifying clause appears in the Yahoo
  Finance / AOL relay of the FT piece. The packet's own S-06 cluster does not carry the clause, so
  this is new to the review, not a packet item the draft ignored.
- **Minimum viable repair.** Add the clause where the charge is introduced — one sentence, no
  restructuring. It sits naturally before "Then the specific charge."
- **Expected benefit.** It strengthens the draft's own argument. A witness who concedes I learn fast
  and then says I have never practiced research is far more damaging than a witness who just calls
  me inexperienced, and it makes "interested criticism is often the sharpest kind" pay off harder.
  It also removes the piece's largest quotation-context exposure at zero cost to the thesis.
- **Confidence.** High on the omission; high on the wording.
- **Acceptance test.** The LeCun section quotes at least one qualifying clause from the same
  interview, and no sentence in the draft characterizes LeCun's verdict as wholly dismissive.

### SUBJECT-R2 — "Every room he has ever been in" is a life-sized claim resting on one data point *(blocker)*

- **Location.** H2 5: "He has made every room he has ever been in by exactly one spot. Ranked,
  seeded, cut lists, top four in the state."
- **Trust problem.** This is stated as biography, not as reading. The record supports exactly one
  narrow-margin entry: fourth of four in a New Mexico math competition in sixth grade. Nothing
  establishes narrow entry to MIT, to Y Combinator, to billionaire status, to the Pentagon, or to
  Meta — and the Meta datum runs the other way, since Zuckerberg paid $14.3 billion substantially to
  hire me and called me "the most impressive founder of his generation." For me, this is the
  sentence that converts a defensible psychological reading into an assertion about my whole life
  that I can disprove with one counterexample. For a hostile reader it is the easiest place to
  discredit the article.
- **Evidence.** Packet CLM-27 classes it "unsupported as written" and names it the highest-exposure
  sentence in the piece; the counterevidence is CLM-10 and CLM-22 in the same packet.
- **Minimum viable repair.** Scope it to what is evidenced — the vocabulary and the one competition
  — rather than to every room. The paragraph's real claim is about how I describe my own arrivals,
  and that claim is fully supported by "snuck in there," "ranked," and "top four."
- **Expected benefit.** The empathy turn survives intact and stops being falsifiable by a single
  fact the reader already knows from paragraph three of the same article.
- **Confidence.** High.
- **Acceptance test.** Every narrow-margin entry the sentence claims is either sourced or removed,
  and the passage no longer generalizes beyond the documented instances.

### SUBJECT-R3 — Invented interiority fused with two real quotations at my most exposed moment *(blocker)*

- **Location.** H2 5: `<p class="inner-thought">Don't do this. Not here. Not in front of them. Finish
  the sentence, get to the part about the pool house, they'll laugh, you can sit down.</p>` —
  immediately followed by "That was the Scale all-hands in June 2025."
- **Trust problem.** Three evidential classes are stacked in one passage with no signal separating
  them: my own on-record account to Forbes ("I literally teared up"), an unnamed attendee's
  recollection of a self-directed line ("This is so stupid. Why am I doing this?"), and words no one
  ever heard me think. The invented paragraph runs *first*, before the scene is located, so a reader
  encounters authored thoughts cold and carries them into the sourced material. The content is not
  neutral either — it attributes shame-management and social calculation to me in real time, which
  is the article's thesis restaged as my own inner voice. I have no objection to the device; I object
  to it being indistinguishable from the parts I actually said.
- **Evidence.** Packet CLM-23 and the first-person section: the inner-thought paragraph is
  "authored interiority with no evidentiary basis at all"; the attendee line is single-source,
  unnamed, no recording.
- **Minimum viable repair.** Introduce the scene before the interior beat, and give the beat one
  framing signal that marks it as the writer's imagining rather than reportage. The device survives;
  the fusion does not.
- **Expected benefit.** The all-hands stays the emotional center of the piece — it is the best scene
  in the draft — while a reader can tell which words are mine. It also protects the scene from being
  discredited wholesale when a reader learns the attendee is unnamed.
- **Confidence.** High that the passage is unmarked; high that the repair is cheap.
- **Acceptance test.** A reader who has not seen the sources can correctly identify which lines in
  the all-hands passage are quoted and which are authored.
