## Bottom-line verdict

The article survives the skeptical case that rapid speech, ideological certainty, and public correction are trained media tactics rather than personality proof. It includes strategic and status explanations, tests a Type 6 alternative, and grounds the stronger claims in repeated self-directed behavior.

## What landed

The BBC failure, public mistake list, Breitbart cost, Owens ambivalence, and device-versus-persona tension prevent a flattering principle-only profile. The final disclaimer makes the interpretive boundary explicit.

## What missed

The yeshiva-to-debate lineage is persuasive synthesis, not a causal statement from Shapiro. The article's moral bookkeeping metaphor can make several different political and professional motives look like one engine.

## What I expected

I expected commercial incentives, critic pressure, alternative typing, counterevidence, and acknowledged uncertainty. All are reader-visible.

## What surprised me

The strongest evidence is not certainty on stage but the speed with which Shapiro published a verdict against himself after Andrew Neil.

## Red flags

No P0 blocker. The article neither validates Shapiro's political positions nor treats controversy as automatic evidence for Type 1.

At maintenance, attach full immediate source labels to the remaining vague quote slots and preserve the current caveats around inferred motive.
