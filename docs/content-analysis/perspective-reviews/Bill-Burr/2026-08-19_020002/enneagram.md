## Bottom-line verdict

The type call is right-shaped and unusually well argued. Every theory claim I could check against
9takes' own canon holds: the 6→3 stress direction, the 6→9 growth direction, the 6w5/6w7
contrast, the self-preservation description, and the Six/Eight discrimination are all consistent
with `src/blog/enneagram/enneagram-type-6.md`, `enneagram-connecting-lines.md`,
`enneagram-wings-complete-guide.md`, and `enneagram-instinctual-subtypes.md`. The load-bearing
quotation is exact. The Rabbit Hole contains a real falsifiability clause and an honest "what this
type does not explain." That is a higher epistemic standard than anything on this SERP.

Two things break my trust, and neither requires new research to fix.

First, the article's central Six-vs-Eight discriminator — the public/private register split at
Riyadh — is presented with the private passage's own framing removed. On the tape, the dry-mouth
material sits inside a run of favorite international gigs and Burr calls Riyadh "one of my top
three gigs of all fucking time" and "a great one." A war story told as a highlight is not the same
evidentiary object as an unguarded admission, and the draft's strongest claim ("the version he
gives when nobody is grading him") is built on the difference.

Second, the paragraph that establishes the typing "has competition" offers MBTI types as rivals to
an Enneagram type, and the only two sources in it that state an Enneagram type both state 6w7 —
the draft's own answer. On an Enneagram-authority page, in the diagnosis section, that reads as
treating typologies as interchangeable labels.

Both are repairs of a sentence or two. Underneath them the case is strong, and the strongest
version of it is one the draft has not quite claimed: the site's own vocabulary for what Bill Burr
is doing is *counterphobic Six*, and the word appears exactly once in 4,500 words, in the Rabbit
Hole, describing a category rather than the man.

## What landed

**ENN-H1 — The certainty is calibrated where it counts.** (Rabbit Hole, "What would change our
mind")

> "Evidence that the fear talk is a bit. If the dry-mouth material only ever surfaced in
> interviews promoting something, the honest read would be brand management."

This is the single best thing in the piece from my standpoint. Naming the observation that would
overturn the call is the discipline typology writing almost never practices, and it converts the
page from assertion to argument. It must survive revision verbatim in intent. (The specific
supporting clause needs work — see ENN-R1 — but the move itself is the page's spine.)

**ENN-H2 — "The counterattack arrives before the verdict does."** (§5, "Riyadh, told twice")

> "A man who is genuinely unbothered lets criticism land and ignores it. Burr gets out in front of
> it and destroys the standing of the people delivering it, which is what you do when you have
> already decided the verdict is coming and cannot afford to be caught standing still."

This is a motivational read, not a behavioral one, and it discriminates: it distinguishes the
Eight's post-hoc indifference from the Six's pre-emptive defense using *timing* rather than
intensity. It is also person-first and jargon-free. Preserve exactly.

**ENN-H3 — The Ronda Rousey half-point.** (§6)

> "Nobody asked. There was no scale in play. He reached for one anyway, mid-joke, about a
> hypothetical, because somewhere in there a gauge is always running and he can read it to a
> half-point."

The best type evidence in the article, because it is involuntary. Nothing about it is retrospective
self-narration, which is the weakness of most of the other exhibits. A continuously-running threat
gauge that surfaces unbidden inside a joke about nothing is exactly the ambient-scanning signature,
and the draft earns it without a single piece of jargon. Preserve.

**ENN-H4 — The 6w7 argument is canon-accurate and evidence-linked.** (Rabbit Hole)

> "A 6w5 metabolizes dread by researching it into submission and going quiet; Burr metabolizes it
> by talking, for an hour, every week, for nineteen years."

This tracks the wings guide almost verbatim ("6w5s manage anxiety by _researching_ it away") while
sounding nothing like it, and it grounds the wing in a nineteen-year behavioral record rather than
a trait list. The wing is correctly framed as shading delivery, not driving the engine.

**ENN-H5 — The growth-arrow read is the correct one, not the flattering one.** (Rabbit Hole)

> "He accepts that some of it does not come out, and commits to the work anyway."

9takes' Type 6 page defines integration as "right action despite fear," explicitly *not* the
elimination of fear. The draft's pairing of the inner-peace line with the NPR "I can't fix it …
my job is that that stuff dies with me" is that definition instantiated in his own words. Most
celebrity typings render growth as a redemption arc; this one renders it as a floor. Preserve.

**ENN-H6 — The authority axis, stated plainly.** (Rabbit Hole)

> "Sixes neither reject authority nor submit to it, they audit it continuously and reassign trust
> when the audit fails."

Accurate, non-dogmatic, and it does real work: it is what makes the 2020/2025 political material
one pattern instead of a conversion narrative. The §8 payoff ("a man who has never once had a
coherent party line is dead consistent about this one thing") is the best non-obvious insight the
lens produces on this subject.

## What missed

**The three-tellings structure has a weak third leg exactly where the type lives.** (§1, lines
180–186) The argument is "Three tellings. One motive. Safety." Tellings one and two contain the
safety clause — "so that no one would hurt me," "stop beating the shit out of me." Telling three,
as quoted from *Drop Dead Years*, is:

> "because that was the easiest way to walk into a room full of a bunch of people that I didn't
> know and make everybody like me."

There is no safety clause in it. "Make everybody like me," standing alone, is not
type-discriminating — it is equally at home in Two, Three or Nine. The paragraph's conclusion
("One motive. Safety.") is therefore carried by two of the three exhibits. See ENN-C3; the packet
holds the fix.

**The strongest evidence is not the evidence the draft leans on.** The Riyadh pair is rhetorically
vivid but interpretively contested (ENN-R1). The 1992 year-long runway and the 2015 helicopter
license are structurally better: both are *decisions*, not narrations; neither has an audience or a
punchline at the moment it was made; and the helicopter is independently corroborated by a 2015
newspaper interview ("the ultimate 'F--- this, I'm out'"), so it is not a podcast riff. The draft
has both and frames neither as a discriminator.

**The type is read as harm-avoidance rather than support-seeking.** See ENN-C9. This is the
subtlest miss and the most upgradeable.

## What I expected

- **The word "counterphobic," applied to Burr.** It appears once, in the Rabbit Hole, as a
  category ("the counter-phobic end of Six"). 9takes' own Type 6 page devotes a section to it and
  describes the counterphobic Six as one who "challenges authority publicly," "becomes the
  whistleblower," and "pushes back against bad ideas even when it's politically stupid to do so."
  That is a description of Burr's entire public record. See ENN-C1.
- **The centers.** Not named anywhere. The public reads Burr as a gut/anger type; the claim is that
  he is a head type whose anger is downstream of anticipatory thinking. The draft states this
  perfectly in plain language — "The rage is the output. The input is…" — and is one clause away
  from the structural version. I do not want jargon density here, but the centers point is the
  cheapest available upgrade to explanatory precision and it is currently absent rather than
  deliberately traded away.
- **The Six's defense mechanism.** Projection is unnamed, though ENN-H2 describes it in action —
  he assigns the verdict before it is delivered. Optional; the plain-language version already
  works.
- **The Reactive harmonic group.** The Type 6 page places Six with Four and Eight among the types
  who "get emotionally intense under stress and need others to mirror that intensity back." For a
  stand-up comedian whose whole instrument is escalating intensity into a room that mirrors it,
  this is unusually on the nose and goes unused.

I am not asking for all four. I am noting that the article reaches its conclusions through
narrative alone while four pieces of on-point structural vocabulary sit unused in the site's own
canon, and one of them (counterphobic) would resolve the article's central apparent paradox in a
sentence.

## Red flags

### ENN-R1 — BLOCKER — The central discriminator omits the framing that undercuts it

**Passage.** §5, line 279:

> "On camera, the account contains no fear at all. On his own show, with nobody to perform
> invulnerability for, it is twenty minutes of dry mouth and a running threat assessment of
> everyone who stood up in an 8,000-seat room. … It is the version he gives when nobody is
> grading him."

Reinforced in the Rabbit Hole, line 374: "It surfaces on a free weekly podcast, at minute forty,
next to a segment about garage doors."

**Trust problem.** This pair is the article's designated Six-vs-Eight discriminator — the reason a
reader should accept fear as the engine rather than the Type 8 reading the draft itself calls
"real." The packet's verbatim transcript of MMP 8-10-26 shows the dry-mouth material embedded in a
run of *favorite international gigs* (Saudi Arabia, Italy, Germany, Budapest), introduced with
"It's like one of my top three gigs of all [ __ ] time" and closed with "that one was a great one."
The draft carries one hedge from that episode ("he still calls the criticism 'false flag outrage'")
but omits the triumph framing, which is the part that changes what kind of utterance this is. A
reader who opens the episode the article cites hears a war story told as a highlight, with fear as
color, not a confession. The packet classifies this as "the highest-leverage omission in the draft"
and lists the register question as formally unresolved.

Second defect in the same passage: "with nobody to perform invulnerability for" and "when nobody is
grading him" are stated as properties of the medium. MMP is a monetized, twice-weekly show with a
mass audience — a performance venue with a different register, not an absence of one. The packet
flags this explicitly: "'nobody is grading him' is an **interpretation**, not a property of the
medium."

**Evidence.** Packet CLM-12 and "Closest alternative and discriminating evidence"; S-07 verbatim
transcript, YouTube `8NYGbY4Tmkc`.

**Minimum viable repair.** Two moves, both swap-shaped so they respect the zero word headroom:
1. Add the triumph framing to the table cell or the paragraph beneath it — six words will do:
   he calls it *one of his top three gigs of all time*. Then argue through it rather than around
   it: the fear and the triumph coexisting is itself the counterphobic signature, and that is a
   stronger claim than the one currently made.
2. Replace "with nobody to perform invulnerability for" / "when nobody is grading him" with a claim
   about *register* rather than *audience* — the version he gives when he is not being asked to
   defend it. Fund the words by cutting the redundant "so this is no confession and no retraction,"
   which the repaired passage makes self-evident.

**Expected benefit.** The discriminator survives contact with the source. It also gets stronger:
"he can hold the fear and the win in the same breath" is better Six evidence than "he only admits
fear in private," because the second claim is falsified by the tape and the first is not.

**Confidence.** High. The omitted wording is verified verbatim in the packet.

**Acceptance test.** A reader who plays MMP 8-10-26 from the Saudi Arabia segment encounters
nothing in the episode that the article did not prepare them for, and the article contains no
sentence asserting that Burr has no audience or no incentive on his own podcast.

### ENN-R2 — BLOCKER — MBTI types offered as rivals to an Enneagram type, and the "split" is manufactured

**Passage.** §1, line 190:

> "This typing has competition. The databases that own the search results for his name split three
> ways: Boo and So Syncd say 6w7, SunSigns and personalitylist say ISTP, Personality Database
> commenters argue ESTP."

**Trust problem.** Three defects in one sentence, in the diagnosis section of an Enneagram page:

1. **Category error.** ISTP and ESTP are MBTI types. They are not competing answers to "what is
   his Enneagram type?" Presenting them as one of three ways a typing question "splits" tells an
   Enneagram-literate reader that the writer treats typologies as interchangeable labels — on a
   site whose entire product is this one system.
2. **The split is backwards.** Per the packet, Boo and So Syncd *each* carry both an MBTI type
   (ISTP) and an Enneagram type (6w7). So the only two sources here that state an Enneagram type
   state the draft's own answer. The claim "this typing has competition" is, as to Enneagram
   sources, unsupported — the record shows agreement, not contest.
3. **Two named sources are unverified.** SunSigns and personalitylist appear nowhere in the
   packet's source ledger; its verification pass covered Boo (S-39), So Syncd (S-40) and a PDB
   comment (S-41). They are load-bearing attributions in the diagnosis paragraph with no confirmed
   backing in this review chain.

The draft's real point — that none of these sources argues from evidence — is verified and is the
better point. It is currently buried under a contest that does not exist.

**Evidence.** Packet CLM-05 ("misleading as framed"); S-39, S-40, S-41; source ledger.

**Minimum viable repair.** Rewrite to the defensible claim, roughly: the databases that own this
SERP agree on 6w7 where they say anything about the Enneagram at all — Boo and So Syncd both land
there — but none of them argues from evidence; the MBTI labels they also carry (ISTP) answer a
different question. Then let Type 8 carry the "competition" load, which it already does in the
next clause and does honestly. Drop SunSigns and personalitylist unless a verification pass
confirms them. This is net-neutral or shorter, so the word ceiling is not a constraint.

**Expected benefit.** Removes a credibility break in front of the exact reader most likely to
scrutinize the call, and replaces a false contest with a stronger claim: the competing databases
agree with us and still cannot show their work.

**Confidence.** High on defects 1 and 2 (packet-verified). Medium on defect 3 — absence from the
packet is not disproof, but it is an unverified citation.

**Acceptance test.** No sentence in the article presents an MBTI type as an alternative to an
Enneagram type; every external typology source named in the body appears in the packet's source
ledger; and the "none of them argues from evidence" point is the paragraph's conclusion rather
than its aside.

## Specific improvements

### ENN-C1 — Name him a counterphobic Six (highest-value single change)

**Location.** §1 diagnosis (line 188–190) and/or Rabbit Hole line 370.
**Problem.** The article's structural insight is that the loudest man in comedy is running a fear
strategy — and 9takes' own Type 6 page already has the name for that: the counterphobic Six, who
"challenges authority publicly," "picks fights," "becomes the whistleblower." The draft uses the
word once, generically, while rebutting Type 8. It argues counterphobia for 4,500 words without
claiming it, which leaves the reader assembling the frame the article could hand them.
**Sharper still:** the same canon page notes that most Sixes are counterphobic in some domains and
phobic in others — "counterphobic at work … but phobic in relationships." That is precisely the
draft's public/private architecture: counterphobic on stage (Philadelphia, SNL, Riyadh, the clap-
back) and phobic in private (the avoided MRI, the friend who had to push him, the dry mouth, the
half-point gauge). The article has both halves of the evidence and never names the frame that
unifies them.
**Repair.** One sentence in §1 claiming the term, and one clause in the Rabbit Hole applying the
by-domain split. Fund it by cutting the sentence fragment "and each runs the other way once you
know what set it off" (line 192), which the sections themselves demonstrate.
**Benefit.** Resolves the page's apparent paradox explicitly, links to an existing pillar page, and
gives the reader a portable concept — the thing a typing page is for.
**Confidence.** High.
**Acceptance test.** A reader who finishes the article can say "counterphobic Six" and explain that
Burr is counterphobic in public and phobic in private, citing one example of each.

### ENN-C2 — The subtype call claims more confidence than its evidence supports

**Location.** Rabbit Hole, line 360.
**Problem.** "Self-preservation dominant, social secondary, and the evidence is unusually concrete
for a subtype call." The sp evidence *is* canon-consistent — 9takes' subtypes page describes SP 6
as people who "keep emergency kits, double-check locks, and plan for worst-case scenarios," which
is the helicopter, the no-debt rule and the avoided MRI exactly. That part is right and should
stay. The problem is the confidence and the stacking. The same canon page describes SX 6 as
"fiercely protective of loved ones" and SO 6 as "devoted to teams, clubs, or communal projects" —
and the article's entire thesis-carrying narrative is protective and communal: the Philadelphia
rescue, "he swings when the room turns on people he has decided are his," the fifty comedians at
Riyadh, "who stays when it goes wrong," the club as "going home," All Things Comedy. By the site's
own descriptions, the draft's spine is evidence for the two instincts it does not name dominant,
and the paragraph asserts past that rather than arguing it.
**Repair.** Keep the sp call; drop "unusually concrete"; add one clause acknowledging the pull —
the protective material is real and is why social sits second rather than absent. Roughly
net-neutral on words.
**Benefit.** The subtype claim becomes honest about being the softest call on the page, which
protects the core-type call from being judged by it.
**Confidence.** Medium-high. Subtype attribution is inherently the least determinate layer; the
point is the mismatch between stated confidence and available evidence, not that sp is wrong.
**Acceptance test.** The subtype paragraph names at least one piece of evidence that pulls against
sp dominance, and no longer describes its own evidence as unusually concrete.

### ENN-C3 — Repair the third telling, or stop calling it a third telling

**Location.** §1, lines 184–186.
**Problem.** As quoted, the *Drop Dead Years* soliloquy contains no safety clause, so "Three
tellings. One motive. Safety." rests on two. The packet also flags this quote as second-hand
(CLM-04): it was verified through reviews, not against the special's audio.
**Repair, in order of preference.** (a) Use the stronger line multiple independent reviews carry
from the same soliloquy — "where's the place I have the least chance of being hurt?" — which is a
*strategy* statement and far more type-discriminating than "make everybody like me"; attribute it
carefully, since it too is review-sourced. (b) If (a) cannot be sourced to the writer's
satisfaction, substitute the Ferriss #265 line, which is verbatim-verified and carries the clause:
"I thought I'm going to get on stage, show people that I'm a funny guy, and people will stop
fucking with me." Note that (b) makes the tellings 2017 ×2 + 2022, which weakens "three formats
over eight years" — so pair it with the ENN-C5 date fix and reframe the span honestly.
**Benefit.** The article's strongest structure stops having a soft leg in the position where the
type actually lives.
**Confidence.** High that the quoted third telling is not type-discriminating; medium on which
replacement is best, since both candidates have sourcing caveats.
**Acceptance test.** All three quoted tellings contain an explicit safety or harm-avoidance clause,
and the count/span claim matches the dates actually used.

### ENN-C4 — The corpus base rate is stated as a fact about comedians

**Problem.** "The field is what's strange: Type 7 takes 43.8% of the comedian set … Nearly half

**Benefit.** The one genuinely novel statistical move on the page stops being its most attackable
sentence.
**Confidence.** High.
**Acceptance test.** Every inference drawn from the 43.8% / 9.4% figures is scoped to the 9takes
corpus in the same sentence that draws it.

### ENN-C5 — The Type 8 rebuttal is quantified with an unstable number

**Location.** §1, line 192: "Someone operating from genuine invulnerability does not spend sixteen
years wincing at it."
**Problem.** "Sixteen years" counts 2006 → 2022, which requires the Ferriss #602 interview to be

inconsistency lands inside the sentence that rebuts the strongest alternative type.
**Repair.** Pick the interview year (2017) throughout, and either recompute to eleven years or drop
the number for a formulation the date cannot destabilize ("does not still wince at it a decade
later").
**Benefit.** The counter-Type-8 argument stops being checkable-and-wrong. Expect other perspectives
to flag the underlying date inconsistency as a factual matter; this note is about the load it
carries for the typing.
**Confidence.** High that the article is internally inconsistent; the packet resolves the source
dating.
**Acceptance test.** The article dates Ferriss #602 the same way in all three places it cites it,
and any duration computed from it matches that date.

### ENN-C6 — The TL;DR opens the type case with a victimization scene

**Location.** TL;DR accordion, bullet 1, under the heading "Why Bill Burr is an Enneagram Type 6":

**Problem.** Being disarmed by bigger kids is not evidence of a type. Every type has a driveway.

**Repair.** Re-point the bullet at the response rather than the event — what he built afterward is
the type evidence. Same length.
**Benefit.** The skim path stops asserting trauma-as-type-proof while the body argues something
more defensible.
**Confidence.** Medium-high. Presentation-level, but the TL;DR is the most-read block on the page.
**Acceptance test.** No TL;DR bullet offers a childhood injury as evidence for the type without the
strategy it produced.

### ENN-C9 — The type is framed around harm, when its own evidence is about alliance

**Location.** §1, line 188: "Type 6 runs on the management of fear."
**Problem.** True but under-specified, and the under-specification costs the article its sharpest
distinction. Fear of being hurt is universal; it does not pick a type out of nine. The Six's fear
is specifically about being *without support* — no one reliable at your back — which is why the
type organizes around trust, alliance and who can be relied on. The draft's own best evidence is
alliance-shaped and it reads it as harm-shaped. Its load-bearing quotation is "make them like me
**so that no one would hurt me**" — safety obtained by *manufacturing allies*, not by escape. Add
the club where he finally "fit in" and called it going home; the wife who is "publicly on his side"
every time the internet comes for him, which the draft itself glosses as "a man whose operating
system is the question of who stays when it goes wrong"; All Things Comedy; the friend whose push
was the only thing that got him into the MRI; and the fifty comedians he stepped in front of. That
is one coherent motivational picture and the draft assembles all of it without ever naming it.
**Repair.** One clause in the §1 framing sentence moving the object of the fear from harm to
support — the fear is not of pain, it is of facing it with nobody at your back. Then the article's
existing "who stays when it goes wrong" line becomes the thesis restated rather than an aside.
Word-neutral.
**Benefit.** The type call stops resting on a fear anyone could have and starts resting on the one
that distinguishes Six from every other type — which also makes ENN-C2's protective evidence an
asset instead of a loose end.
**Confidence.** Medium-high. This is a refinement of a correct call, not a correction of a wrong
one.
**Acceptance test.** The article states, once, what the Six is afraid of *being without* — not only
what he is afraid of happening — and at least one existing exhibit is read through it.

## Follow-on questions

**ENN-Q1 — Does the *Drop Dead Years* soliloquy contain a safety clause in its own audio?**
The article's three-telling structure depends on it (ENN-C3), and every rendering in the chain is
review-sourced. *What would change:* if the special says "where's the place I have the least chance
of being hurt?" in or near the quoted passage, the third leg becomes the strongest of the three and
ENN-C3 closes entirely; if the soliloquy really is only "make everybody like me," the structure
should be recast as two tellings plus a restatement. *Best source:* the special itself on Hulu,
first three minutes — the only unwatched primary source in the chain.

**ENN-Q2 — Is there any instance of Burr describing pre-performance fear on a promotional
appearance?** This is the falsifier the Rabbit Hole itself proposes, and ENN-R1 shows the current
private/public contrast is softer than stated. *What would change:* if the fear talk appears in
promo interviews too, the "only when ungraded" framing must go entirely and the type case should
rest on the structural behaviors (1992 runway, helicopter license) instead — which are stronger
anyway. *Best source:* the 2025 *Drop Dead Years* press run and the 2026 *Social Reckoning* press
cycle once it starts.

**ENN-Q3 — What did Burr say about the Riyadh fear at the time, not ten months later?**
Both current accounts are retrospective. *What would change:* a contemporaneous September/October
2025 statement mentioning nerves would convert the register question from unresolved to settled and
would materially strengthen the discriminator ENN-R1 currently weakens. *Best source:* MMP episodes
from late September through October 2025, which are indexed and grep-able by the same method the
packet used for 8-10-26.

**ENN-Q4 — Does Burr's own account of the twelve minutes' *duration* exist anywhere?**
