## Identity and scope

Bryan Johnson is an American technology entrepreneur and longevity advocate. He founded Braintree,
which acquired Venmo before PayPal bought the combined business in 2013, and later founded OS Fund,
Kernel, and Project Blueprint. The frozen article tests an Enneagram Type 3w4 hypothesis against
Type Four and Type One. It covers his childhood account, post-sale identity collapse, public body
experiment, commercial interests, closest relationships, workplace reporting, and 2026 autoimmune
gastritis announcement. Enneagram claims remain interpretations of public evidence.

## Dated timeline

- **Childhood:** Johnson says his divorced father repeatedly promised Saturday pickups and usually
  failed to arrive. He remembers waiting from early morning until about 11 or 11:30.
- **Late 1990s:** He served a Mormon mission in Ecuador and says poverty there shaped a plan to
  become wealthy and later do something useful for humanity.
- **2007:** He founded Braintree.
- **2012-2013:** Braintree acquired Venmo for a reported $26.2 million, then PayPal bought the
  combined company for $800 million. Johnson says he personally received a few hundred million.
- **2014-2021:** He launched OS Fund, Kernel, and then Project Blueprint.
- **2023:** TIME published independent scientific criticism of Blueprint's methods and intervention
  stack from Eric Verdin and Nir Barzilai.
- **2025:** Johnson released his childhood film. The New York Times reported on broad
  confidentiality agreements and National Labor Relations Board complaints. Johnson disputed the
  framing in a subsequent WIRED interview.
- **2026:** Johnson announced autoimmune gastritis. On August 13, he discussed Blueprint, illness,
  5-MeO-DMT, and bodily control with Ross Douthat on _Interesting Times_.

## First-person evidence

1. In his 2025 childhood film, Johnson says he prepared hours early for his father, waited after his
   siblings gave up, felt sadness rather than anger, and learned to tuck problems away and carry on.
   These are retrospective claims and do not prove a developmental cause or subtype.
2. In the requested 2026 podcast, Johnson describes the Braintree period as a convergence of
   burnout, depression, divorce, religious departure, and loss of professional identity. He recalls
   asking, "Who am I? What do I care about? What do I want to do?"
3. He says he and Kate Tolo decided to make his body the philosophy, calls the project performance
   art, and names himself the first "Don't Die" human prototype.
4. He admits that his regimented life partly resembles a cage and that his interventions could be
   shortening his life. He says the science is early and ultimately uncertain.
5. He describes 5-MeO-DMT as a choice to say yes to existence instead of reclaiming control.
   Douthat asks whether that acceptance could include death; Johnson answers that autonomous health
   will eventually allow release.
6. Johnson frames autoimmune gastritis as a gift and a test of the philosophy. This establishes his
   public meaning-making, not a cure or prognosis.

## Third-party testimony

- Kate Tolo, Johnson's co-founder and partner, describes him as frugal, supportive of strong women,
  internally grounded, playful with hostile attention, and willing to challenge rigid righteousness.
  Her closeness provides access but limits independence.
- Talmage Johnson says his father did not force Blueprint on him and responded patiently when he
  failed to follow it. This is specific testimony from one son and cannot represent the other
  children.
- Ross Douthat challenges generalizability, commercial conflicts, sacrifice, power, and the tension
  between control and surrender.
- TIME quotes Eric Verdin saying Johnson's age-assessment tools were too opaque to evaluate and Nir
  Barzilai warning about simultaneous interventions and scientific validity. Johnson said he did
  not remember ignoring Verdin and later exchanged friendly emails with him.
- The New York Times reported NLRB complaints alleging overbroad confidentiality agreements and an
  opt-in workplace acknowledgement around nudity and sexual discussion. Johnson denied the framing
  in WIRED and defended explicit confidentiality boundaries as normal and fair.

## Disputes and unresolved questions

- Public evidence cannot directly reveal whether achievement, identity, correctness, knowledge, or
  survival is Johnson's primary private motive.
- The combination of role loss, public proof, distribution, commerce, and historical positioning
  favors Three; the archetype and performance-art language still supports a coherent Four reading.
- Blueprint's full stack has not been validated as a controlled population-level experiment.
- The labor complaints and Johnson's response remain disputed and must stay attributed.
- The diagnosis, treatment course, and cure claim may change after the evidence cutoff.
- Close testimony cannot establish every family member's or employee's experience.
