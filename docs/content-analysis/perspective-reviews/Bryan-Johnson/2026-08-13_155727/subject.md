## Bottom-line verdict

The portrait is skeptical without becoming contemptuous. It gives Johnson's philosophy its strongest
form, includes scientific and workplace criticism, and distinguishes public self-presentation from
private motive. The Type Three conclusion is clearly marked as an inference.

## What landed

The article treats the body as laboratory, ledger, manifesto, product demo, and character. That
captures how Johnson consciously operates on several levels at once. The childhood section also
protects dignity by marking the adult connection as suggestive rather than causal proof.

## What missed

The workplace paragraph is necessarily compressed and may feel abrupt to a subject who disputes the
reporting. Its inclusion is justified by the control theme, and the rebuttal prevents it from becoming
a verdict.

## What I expected

I expected ridicule, a supplement inventory, or a simplistic fear-of-death diagnosis. The draft
instead addresses purpose, faith loss, family, commerce, and surrender.

## What surprised me

The strongest humane moment is the distinction between a strategy that can be excessive and the same
strategy giving a broken person a reason to keep acting.

## Red flags

No blockers. One bounded concern: future reporting could change the labor or medical sections.

## Specific improvements

No current revision is required. On refresh, update only adjudicated outcomes and medical facts.

## Follow-on questions

Would Johnson describe his private motive as usefulness, historical achievement, authentic identity,
or escape from death when no audience is present?

## Preserve list

Preserve the body-role table, the admission of scientific uncertainty, the Douthat dialogue, and the
qualified childhood interpretation.
