## Identity and scope

Bryan Johnson is an American technology entrepreneur and longevity advocate who founded Braintree,
OS Fund, Kernel, and Project Blueprint. The frozen article argues that his public record best fits
Enneagram Type 4w3, probably self-preservation/social. Type Three is the strongest countercase. The
scope includes his childhood account, post-sale identity collapse, body experiment, commerce,
relationships, workplace reporting, scientific criticism, and 2026 autoimmune diagnosis.

## Dated timeline

- **Childhood:** Johnson says his divorced father repeatedly promised Saturday pickups and usually
  failed to arrive. He remembers waiting from early morning until about 11 or 11:30.
- **Late 1990s:** He served a Mormon mission in Ecuador and says poverty shaped a plan to become
  wealthy and later serve humanity.
- **2007-2013:** He founded Braintree, acquired Venmo, and sold the combined company to PayPal for
  $800 million. He says he personally received a few hundred million.
- **2014-2021:** He launched OS Fund, Kernel, and Project Blueprint.
- **2023:** TIME published independent scientific criticism of Blueprint from Eric Verdin and Nir
  Barzilai.
- **2025:** Johnson released his childhood film. The New York Times reported on confidentiality
  agreements and labor complaints; Johnson disputed the framing in WIRED.
- **2026:** Johnson announced autoimmune gastritis and appeared on the August 13 episode of
  _Interesting Times_ with Ross Douthat.

## First-person evidence

1. Johnson says he felt sadness rather than anger toward his absent father and learned to tuck
   problems away and carry on. This supports contained pain and endurance but cannot prove subtype.
2. In the requested podcast, he describes burnout, depression, divorce, religious departure, and
   professional identity loss, then recalls asking, "Who am I? What do I care about? What do I want
   to do?"
3. He says he and Kate Tolo decided to make his body the philosophy, calls the project performance
   art, and calls himself the first "Don't Die" human prototype.
4. When offered Dorian Gray, Dracula, and Prometheus as possible archetypes, he accepts all of them.
   He calls love and hate "raw energy."
5. He describes his regimented routine as partly a cage, says his interventions could shorten his
   life, and concedes that the science is early.
6. He describes 5-MeO-DMT as an uncompromising yes to existence. When Douthat asks whether surrender
   includes death, Johnson predicts autonomous health will eventually allow release.
7. He frames autoimmune gastritis as a gift and gauntlet. This establishes public meaning-making,
   not diagnosis verification, cure, or prognosis.

## Third-party testimony

- Kate Tolo, Johnson's co-founder and partner, describes him as frugal, internally grounded, playful
  with hostile attention, and willing to challenge rigid righteousness. Her closeness gives access
  but limits independence.
- Talmage Johnson says his father invited rather than forced participation and responded patiently
  when he failed to follow Blueprint. He represents one child's experience.
- Ross Douthat challenges generalizability, commerce, power, sacrifice, and control.
- TIME quotes Eric Verdin and Nir Barzilai questioning transparency, combined interventions, safety,
  and scientific validity. Johnson says he and Verdin later exchanged friendly emails.
- The New York Times reported NLRB complaints alleging overbroad confidentiality agreements and an
  opt-in workplace acknowledgement around nudity and sexual discussion. Johnson disputed the story
  in WIRED and defended explicit boundaries as normal and fair.

## Disputes and unresolved questions

- Public evidence cannot directly establish Johnson's primary private motive.
- Four is supported by identity language, embodied philosophy, mythic ambiguity, radical disclosure,
  and meaning made from suffering. Three is supported by role replacement, metrics, execution,
  audience strategy, commerce, and historical-first positioning.
- Direct envy and deficiency evidence is limited, so Four confidence should remain medium.
- Blueprint's full stack lacks population-level controlled validation.
- Labor complaints and Johnson's response remain disputed.
- Medical diagnosis, treatment, and cure claims may change after the cutoff.
