## Identity and scope

Bryan Johnson is an American technology entrepreneur and longevity advocate who founded Braintree,
OS Fund, Kernel, and Project Blueprint. The frozen article independently adjudicates his public
record as Enneagram Type 4w3, probably self-preservation/social, with medium confidence. Type Three
is the strongest alternative. Scope includes childhood, Mormon identity, the Braintree sale,
embodied self-reinvention, family relationships, Blueprint science and commerce, workplace power,
psychedelic surrender, and his 2026 autoimmune diagnosis.

## Dated timeline

- **Childhood:** Johnson says his divorced father repeatedly promised Saturday pickups and failed to
  arrive. Johnson got ready by 5:30 for 9 a.m. and sometimes waited until 11 or 11:30.
- **Late 1990s:** A Mormon mission in Ecuador shaped his plan to become wealthy and then do work
  useful to humanity.
- **2007-2013:** He founded Braintree, acquired Venmo, and sold the combined company to PayPal for
  $800 million. He says he personally received a few hundred million.
- **2013-2014:** He says that within roughly a year he sold the company, divorced, left Mormonism,
  and emerged from a decade of depression. He recalls dancing for six or seven hours at a Brooklyn
  warehouse party soon afterward.
- **2014-2021:** He launched OS Fund, Kernel, and Project Blueprint.
- **2023:** TIME published independent scientific objections to Blueprint. Johnson also conducted a
  three-generation plasma exchange and later reported no benefit for himself.
- **2024:** Johnson says he stopped rapamycin after infections and adverse metabolic signals. On
  Rich Roll, he described a six- or seven-year rupture with two of his children after leaving the
  church.
- **2025:** He released his childhood film. The New York Times reported confidentiality agreements
  and labor complaints; Johnson disputed the framing in WIRED.
- **2026:** Johnson announced autoimmune gastritis. _Nature_ and _Scientific American_ examined
  influencer-led longevity experiments. He appeared on the August 13 episode of _Interesting Times_.

## First-person evidence

1. Johnson says he felt sadness rather than anger toward his absent father, tucked problems away,
   and carried on. This supports endurance but cannot establish cause or subtype.
2. He describes Mormonism as his complete identity and reality. Leaving it required reconstructing
   a self outside religion, marriage, community, and eternity.
3. His post-exit dance story places the body at the center of liberation before Blueprint made the
   body a measured symbol.
4. He contrasts his former pursuit of society's prescribed objectives with a present game he chose
   without needing others' expectations or acceptance. This directly supports a Four-over-Three
   self-authorship interpretation while leaving achievement evidence intact.
5. In the requested podcast, he says he rebuilt from the question "Who am I?", made his body the
   philosophy, calls Blueprint performance art, and accepts Dorian Gray, Dracula, and Prometheus as
   associations with the character.
6. He concedes his regimented life is partly a cage and says his interventions may shorten his life.
7. He describes 5-MeO-DMT as an uncompromising yes to existence, yet answers the surrender challenge
   by predicting that autonomous health will eventually make release safe.
8. He frames autoimmune gastritis as a gift and gauntlet. This is public meaning-making, not a
   medical conclusion.

## Third-party testimony

- Kate Tolo, Johnson's co-founder and partner, describes him as frugal, internally grounded, playful
  with hostile attention, and uninterested in conventional approval. Her proximity provides access
  and creates relational bias.
- Talmage Johnson says his father invited rather than forced participation and responded patiently
  to failed adherence. His positive account represents one child, not all three.
- Johnson's Rich Roll account supplies counterevidence to the harmonious family portrait: he says
  leaving Mormonism made him "other" and nearly cost him two children.
- Ross Douthat challenges generalizability, commerce, intergenerational power, sacrifice, and the
  control paradox.
- TIME quotes Eric Verdin and Nir Barzilai on transparency, combined interventions, safety, and
  scientific validity. The 2026 _Nature_ / _Scientific American_ report adds current concern about
  a public "shadow phase two" around unvalidated longevity therapies.
- The New York Times reported NLRB complaints alleging overbroad confidentiality agreements and an
  opt-in workplace acknowledgement involving nudity and sexual discussion. Johnson disputed the
  framing in WIRED.

## Disputes and unresolved questions

- Public evidence cannot directly establish Johnson's dominant private motive.
- Direct Four evidence about envy or felt deficiency remains thin. Identity, authorship, symbolic
  meaning, and willingness to carry disturbing associations are much stronger.
- Three explains role replacement, metrics, distribution, commerce, and historical-first framing.
- One explains regimen and reform; Five explains data and abstraction; neither fully explains the
  theatrical embodiment and radical disclosure.
- Blueprint's complete stack is not a controlled population trial. Transparent personal data can
  generate hypotheses without validating general use.
- Labor complaints, medical results, and scientific evaluation may change after publication.
