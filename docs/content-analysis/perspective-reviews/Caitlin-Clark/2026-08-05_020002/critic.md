## Bottom-line verdict

The article does more genuine critical work than most sympathetic profiles: it concedes that Sheryl Swoopes was partly right and specifies what she was right about, it states the commercial contradiction in a sentence that does not flinch ("She also keeps the check"), and it puts the single worst fact about its subject — a league-record 223 turnovers — in a section heading rather than a footnote. Those are real. I want them kept.

But the sympathetic interpretation is not yet earned, because the article's own decision procedure is compromised at every one of its named decision points. The pre-write memo names three tiebreakers that resolve Type 1 over Type 3: the ESPN sit-down, the practice technical, and her public agreement with the Olympic committee. In the frozen draft, the first is compressed in a way that removes an outcome goal, the second is a scene whose specifics no located source supports and whose one sourced element (recurrence) has been converted into a single incident, and the third omits the half of the source that contradicts it. Each edit runs the same direction. Individually any of them could be sloppiness; together they are a tilt.

The second structural problem is that nothing in the article can count against the article. Turnovers become proof of a higher standard. Anger at walk-ons becomes evidence she sought help early. A missing title becomes consistency. A career high becomes dissatisfaction. The section that is supposed to hold the disconfirming case sits inside a collapsed accordion that opens by telling the reader to skip it — and one of its three "what would change our mind" data points is an interior monologue the article invented four sections earlier.

The third is that H2 6, the section nominally about the criticism of Clark, contains almost none of it. The two live criticisms directed at her by named professionals — DiJonai Carrington's in 2024, Monica McNutt's in 2026 — are both absent, and the section narrates in the article's own voice that she "had no standing to argue" with any of it, which is precisely the proposition in dispute.

Revise. Most of the repairs are available from the evidence packet without new research; two would be better with it.

## What landed

**CRITIC-H1 — The Swoopes concession, and the specificity of it.**

> "The take was mocked into the ground, and it was partly correct, and the fan base that spent a year screaming at Swoopes never engaged the part that was. Clark's rookie efficiency was ordinary by superstar standards. … The claim was about the timeline, and on the timeline she was right. The leap came in years two and three."

This is the rarest move in celebrity-profile writing: conceding a hostile critic on the merits and naming the merit. It also does something structurally honest — it grants the criticism _before_ the article needs the reader's goodwill, rather than after. It must survive revision intact, including the clause admitting her rookie efficiency was ordinary, which is the only place in the article where a number is allowed to be simply bad.

**CRITIC-H2 — The commercial contradiction stated without cushioning.**

> "She says the credit belongs to the Black women who built the place. She also keeps the check."

Four words that most drafts would spend a paragraph avoiding. The refusal to resolve it in Clark's favor is the article's single strongest credibility asset, and it is what makes the rest of the sympathetic reading legible as argument rather than PR. (See CRITIC-C7 for the one clause in the same paragraph that undercuts it.)

**CRITIC-H3 — The turnover record presented as a counterexample before it is resolved.**

> "This is where most Enneagram readings of an athlete fall apart. A perfectionist is supposed to be careful, and careful players do not lead a professional league in giveaways by that margin…"

The article volunteers the fact most damaging to its thesis, states the damage accurately, and then argues. That is the correct order of operations, and the "removed it from the conversation by 86" line refuses to soften the margin. Whether the resolution holds is CRITIC-C4; the willingness to stage the problem honestly is a hit and should be preserved verbatim.

**CRITIC-H4 — The reframe with the cost attached.**

> "The reframe changes what the behavior means without changing what it cost."

The right sentence, in the right place, and it is the article's own statement of the standard I am holding it to. Keep it — and see CRITIC-B5, because the sentence immediately following currently takes back what it grants.

## What missed

**The race section does not contain the criticism of Clark.** H2 6 is titled "Why Caitlin Clark's fame turned into a fight about race" and ends on Clark's account of what the coverage costs her: "emotionally exhausting," tense shoulders, an accusation she cannot metabolize. The people the abuse was aimed at appear once, as an unnamed category ("Black players, most of whom had spent a decade building the audience"). No Black player in the league speaks in this section. Angel Reese appears only as the other half of a rivalry Clark calls "fabricated," a characterization presented without response. The promised effect — the hardest available test of the read — does not land, because the section's payoff is a paragraph about Clark's interiority.

**The counterargument is filed where it cannot do work.** "Counterarguments: Why Caitlin Clark Might Not Be a Type 1" lives inside `<details class="enneagram-rabbit-hole">`, behind a click, under a line that reads: _"For the Enneagram nerds. Skip if you're not deep into the system. The rest of the analysis stands on its own."_ The article tells the reader that the disconfirming case is optional and that the argument survives without it. That is not a nuance problem; it is a placement decision that makes falsification unreachable by default.

**"That is not grace."** The Olympic paragraph's closing move is to deny Clark credit for graciousness in order to claim something stronger — that she has "no mechanism for pretending otherwise." It reads as rigor. It is the opposite: the sentence is only available because the contradicting half of the same interview was left out.

## What I expected

- **The criticism that was actually made, by name.** Carrington in June 2024 and McNutt in July 2026 are the two named professionals who put a specific charge to Clark. Both are in the public record; McNutt's remark was generated by the exact interview the draft quotes three times. A section on the fight about race that omits both is steelmanning nobody.
- **A frequency, not an anecdote, on the practice technicals.** Bluder has described calling technicals on Clark in practice as a standing habit — including calling them herself from the bench when hired officials would not. That is a materially different fact from "there is one story."
- **What the standard cost people who did not choose it.** The article's account of the locker room comes entirely from the two head coaches who benefited from her, her successor coach, and Clark. The scout-team players appear only as the objects of a scene. The packet records no substantive teammate testimony was located; the article should say so rather than narrate the cost from management's side alone.
- **A falsification condition that is not a metaphor.** "What would change our mind" is the right heading. Two of its three answers are real; the third is invented.
- **Some treatment of the Maravich record's contested framing.** The article calls her "the leading scorer in the history of Division I basketball" without noting that Pearl Moore (4,061, AIAW) and Lynette Woodard (3,649) are routinely raised against that framing. The NCAA D-I claim is defensible; presenting it as uncontested in a piece whose thesis is about who keeps the ledger is a missed opportunity rather than an error.

## What surprised me

**Welcome.** The article knows where its own weak seam is and says so in the body rather than the memo. Most drafts flag the soft spot in working notes and then quietly resolve it on the page. This one resolves it nowhere, on purpose, and says "there may be no version" out loud. I disagree with that specific clause (CRITIC-C7) but the instinct is right and unusual.

**Jarring.** The `inner-thought` device. Two blocks of invented interior monologue, styled to read as Clark's thoughts, in an article that elsewhere sources scrupulously. The hotel-room one is at least paraphrasing a quote that exists. The Seattle one — _"Three points. Against them. Somewhere in there are the four minutes that made it close, and I will find them tomorrow"_ — has no source at all, and then the Rabbit Hole cites the behavior it depicts as if it happened. I did not expect to find the article quoting itself as evidence.

**Also jarring.** "She was back in for the next drill." That detail exists in no located source, it softens the incident, and the SECOND PASS NOTES describe adding it as a _correction_ for accuracy. A correction that moves away from the only named account, in the flattering direction, is not a correction.

## Red flags

### CRITIC-B1 — The Olympic tiebreaker omits the half of the source that contradicts it _(blocker)_

- **Passage.** H2 3, final paragraph: "Her own position, given to TIME later that year, was that the people who cut her had a point. … Clark argued the committee's case for them, in public, and specified the evidence against herself. That is not grace. She had already run the numbers and reached the same verdict, and she has no mechanism for pretending otherwise."
- **Trust problem.** The article states as a psychological fact that Clark cannot assert her own merit when contradicted. In the same TIME interview she also maintained she was good enough to have made the roster (packet CLM-13, S-08). The reader is told a one-sided version of a two-sided answer, at the paragraph the pre-write memo designates as the Three-vs-One tiebreaker. A reader who opens the cited source finds the sentence falsified by the source.
- **Evidence.** Packet CLM-13 and Disputes item 2: "The verified record has her doing both. Conclusions drawn from 'she argued the case against herself' should carry the qualifier that she simultaneously said she was good enough."
- **Minimum viable repair.** Add the concession-plus-assertion structure to the paragraph and rewrite the conclusion to survive it. The two-part answer is arguably _better_ evidence for a standing self-audit than the trimmed version — an auditor reports both columns — but the article has to make that argument rather than delete a column.
- **Expected benefit.** The named tiebreaker stops being defeated by its own citation.
- **Confidence.** High.
- **Acceptance test.** The paragraph contains, in Clark's words or in summary, her claim that she was good enough for the roster; and the concluding sentence remains true when read alongside it.

### CRITIC-B2 — The Maravich paragraph misstates what Clark said, in the thesis's favor _(blocker)_

- **Passage.** H2 3: "It arrived on a free throw at the end of the second quarter, and she said afterward that she had not realized it happened. The biggest number in the sport went by and she did not feel it, because a career scoring total is an accounting somebody else keeps."
- **Trust problem.** Attributed as reported speech. Her actual answer, to whether she was aware of the record when she stepped to the line: "Not really. When they announced it and everybody screamed, that's when I knew" (packet CLM-08, S-31). Not tracking a total _while shooting a free throw_ is unremarkable for any shooter. The article converts it into "the record did not register," which is the entire evidentiary content of the paragraph.
- **Evidence.** Packet CLM-08, status **disputed** on the awareness gloss; Disputes item 4: "The draft's stronger version is not supported."
- **Minimum viable repair.** Quote her verbatim and drop "did not feel it." If the paragraph cannot carry its point on the accurate quote, cut the point rather than the qualifier.
- **Expected benefit.** Removes a checkable misattribution from a section that is otherwise the article's best-sourced.
- **Confidence.** High.
- **Acceptance test.** The paragraph's claim about her awareness matches S-31 word for word, and no sentence asserts she did not know the record had happened.

### CRITIC-B3 — H2 6 removes the criticism directed at Clark and narrates that she had no standing to answer it _(blocker)_

- **Passage.** H2 6: "Clark has never spent much energy arguing with that class of criticism. What happened around her instead she had no standing to argue with at all." Then: "Twelve days later, she said this:" → the June 2024 "push those agendas" quote.
- **Trust problem.** Two moves in one beat. First, "no standing to argue with at all" is asserted in the article's voice as settled, when whether Clark's platform gave her standing — and obligation — is the exact live dispute. Second, "Twelve days later" frames the statement as an unprompted moral act. Reporting indicates it followed public criticism from Connecticut's DiJonai Carrington that Clark had been insufficiently vocal; Carrington's post ("How one can not be bothered by their name being used to justify racism, bigotry, misogyny…") ran the same day. Clark's statement reads differently as a response under pressure than as a spontaneous intervention, and the article picks the flattering reading by deletion.
- **Evidence.** Packet S-11 (Front Office Sports: "DiJonai Carrington context"); corroborated by [Heavy on Carrington's June 13 post](https://heavy.com/sports/wnba/sun-dijonai-carrington-fever-caitlin-clark-tweet-reaction-comments/) and [Fox News](https://www.foxnews.com/sports/suns-dijonai-carrington-fires-back-claim-she-targeted-caitlin-clark-foul.amp). Additionally, the article truncates the statement's first two sentences ("Everybody in our world demands the same amount of respect. The women in our league deserve the same amount of respect"), which is the part addressed to the league rather than to her fans (packet CLM-19).
- **Minimum viable repair.** Name the criticism that preceded the statement, in one clause; cut or qualify "had no standing to argue with at all"; restore the two truncated sentences or acknowledge the truncation.
- **Expected benefit.** The section stops resolving its own subject by assertion, and the June 2024 quote becomes a documented decision under pressure rather than an unearned character beat.
- **Confidence.** High on the sequencing; high on the "no standing" assertion being unsupported.
- **Acceptance test.** A reader can identify, from the article alone, that a named professional criticized Clark's silence before she spoke; and no sentence tells the reader in the author's voice that she was powerless in the dispute.

### CRITIC-B4 — The counterargument section cites the article's own invented interior monologue as disconfirming evidence _(blocker)_

- **Passage.** Rabbit Hole → "Counterarguments": "What would change our mind: evidence that she adjusts her self-presentation by audience, or that a win actually settles her for a while. Neither shows up. … She followed the greatest scoring night in league history by finding a three-point margin to be unhappy about."
- **Trust problem.** No source has Clark commenting on the Seattle margin. The only depiction of her being unhappy about it is the article's own `inner-thought` block in H2 4 — an explicitly authorial device. The falsification test is therefore partly satisfied by the article's own fiction. This is the single move that most damages the piece's standing as argument: a reader who traces the citation finds it points back at the author.
- **Evidence.** Packet CLM-38 ("explicitly invented interior monologue… No sourcing is claimed or possible") and the discriminator table: "**interpretation only.** No located source has Clark commenting on the Seattle margin."
- **Minimum viable repair.** Delete that third data point, or replace it with a sourced one. The packet supplies candidates — she has publicly called turnovers a "toxic part of me" (S-29), and the "I'm always analyzing my play" line (S-05) is real. Alternatively, keep the sentence but attribute it as the article's reading rather than as behavior that "shows up."
- **Expected benefit.** The falsification list becomes a list of facts. The Three case is then defeated on evidence or not defeated at all, which is the honest outcome either way.
- **Confidence.** High.
- **Acceptance test.** Every item in the "what would change our mind" paragraph traces to a source in the packet, and none traces to an `inner-thought` block.

### CRITIC-B5 — The scout-team scene is unsourced in its specifics and converts a recurring pattern into one incident _(blocker)_

- **Passage.** H2 7: "There is one story that shows what it cost the room. Iowa ran its starters against a scout team in practice, the blue squad, and Clark got into it with them. These were the reserves and walk-ons whose entire job that week was to imitate somebody else's offense, and she went at them hard enough that Bluder blew the whistle, called a technical foul on her own All-American in the middle of a drill, and sent her off the floor. She was back in for the next drill."
- **Trust problem.** Two directions at once. (a) Of that scene, only "practice" and "technical" are sourced. No located source mentions a scout team, a blue squad, reserves and walk-ons, a specific drill, or a return to the floor. The one named account — Jan Jensen — says Bluder "kicked her out of practice and gave her a technical _and those things_," plural and habitual. (b) "There is one story" and "She was back in for the next drill" both minimize: the sourced version is a recurring management tool Bluder used because Clark could not control her emotions, including technicals she called herself from the bench when hired officials would not. A standing habit is worse than an anecdote, and the article renders the worse fact as the milder one while adding exculpatory detail that no source supplies.
- **Evidence.** Packet CLM-05 (**disputed / partly unsourced**, "largest gap in the packet"), S-17, S-21, S-22, and Disputes item 1. The draft's own SECOND PASS NOTES describe this rewrite as an accuracy correction.
- **Minimum viable repair.** Reduce the scene to what Jensen and Bluder actually support, and restore the recurrence: technicals in practice were a repeated tool, not a one-off. If the scout-team detail is real, it needs the _Talkin' Hawks with The VandeBergs_ episode or a Bluder primary before it can be stated. Delete "She was back in for the next drill" unless sourced.
- **Expected benefit.** The article's most vivid fact stops being its least sourced one, and the empathy turn in the following paragraph rests on something checkable.
- **Confidence.** High that the specifics are unsourced; high that recurrence is the sourced framing.
- **Acceptance test.** Every concrete noun in the scene appears in a citable source, or is removed; and the article states that the practice technicals happened more than once.

### CRITIC-C1 — "Neither of them means it as a compliment" hardens two sympathetic quotes _(concern)_

- **Passage.** TL;DR bullet 4.
- **Problem.** White's construction is explicitly normalizing and prescriptive-sympathetic ("as most elite players are… she needs to be able to give herself grace"); Bluder's Gazette material is affectionate ("she was still a goofy kid instead of becoming a prima donna"). White has separately said "We are very much alike in terms of our… perfectionism." The article recruits two people expressing sympathy as two people delivering a verdict.
- **Evidence.** Packet CLM-06, S-20, S-23, S-24.
- **Repair.** Change the gloss to what the quotes support — that two coaches independently reached for the same diagnostic word — and drop the claim about their intent.
- **Confidence.** High. **Acceptance test.** No sentence characterizes either coach's intent beyond what their quoted words carry.

### CRITIC-C2 — The July 2026 interview is mined for Clark's grievance and stripped of the criticism it produced _(concern, high priority)_

- **Passage.** H2 6, from "In July 2026, sitting down with ESPN's Malika Andrews over All-Star weekend" through "She did not complain that anyone had underrated her game."
- **Problem.** That interview's news value was not only Clark's complaint; it was the response, most prominently ESPN's Monica McNutt saying there is "room for a little bit more accountability and self awareness in terms of her role in the larger sisterhood." The article takes the sympathetic half of a single news cycle and omits the critical half from the same event — in the section whose stated job is the hardest test of the read. Relatedly, the article attributes "draining" and "it makes your shoulders tense," neither of which is located in any indexed write-up, while omitting the word she is reported to have actually used — "fearful" — which is the word McNutt keyed on.
- **Evidence.** Packet CLM-20 (both phrases **unresolved**), S-13, S-14; [Awful Announcing](https://awfulannouncing.com/wnba/monica-mcnutt-caitlin-clark-accountability-self-awareness.html) and [Yahoo Sports](https://sports.yahoo.com/articles/espn-monica-mcnutt-criticizes-caitlin-165559733.html) for McNutt's wording and the "fearful" quote.
- **Repair.** Add McNutt's charge in one or two sentences and let the article answer it or decline to; verify "draining" and "shoulders tense" against the broadcast or cut them.
- **Confidence.** High. **Acceptance test.** The section contains at least one named, sourced criticism of Clark's own conduct, and every quoted phrase in the paragraph traces to a locatable source.

### CRITIC-C3 — The disconfirming case is behind a click, under an instruction to skip it _(concern, high priority)_

- **Passage.** `<details class="enneagram-rabbit-hole">` → "For the Enneagram nerds. Skip if you're not deep into the system. The rest of the analysis stands on its own." → "Counterarguments: Why Caitlin Clark Might Not Be a Type 1."
- **Problem.** The body contains one short pressure-test paragraph in H2 1; the fuller alternative case is collapsed and pre-labeled optional. This is the textbook shape of clustering unfavorable material where it cannot affect the argument, and the article's own FORMULA FINGERPRINT LEDGER records the choice ("the fuller case sits in the Rabbit Hole").
- **Repair.** Either move the counterargument into the body, or remove "The rest of the analysis stands on its own" from the accordion preamble, since that sentence is the specific claim a critic disputes.
- **Confidence.** High. **Acceptance test.** No reader is told the analysis stands without the counterargument.

### CRITIC-C4 — The load-bearing resolution is unsourced and makes the thesis unfalsifiable by its own best counterexample _(concern, high priority)_

- **Passage.** H2 5: "Ones are not organized around avoiding mistakes. They are organized around doing the thing correctly, and Clark grades correctness at the moment of decision. Whether the ball arrives is a separate question…"
- **Problem.** This is the article's central move and no source has Clark describing her standard this way. It converts the strongest disconfirming fact in the record — an all-time turnover record exceeded by 86 — into confirmation. Combined with CRITIC-B4, the reader has no remaining test: bad outcomes confirm the type, good outcomes confirm the type, and the stated falsification list contains an invention.
- **Repair.** Label the resolution as the article's inference rather than as a property of Ones; and state plainly what evidence would break it. The packet notes she has called turnovers a "toxic part of me" (S-29) — if she grades the decision rather than the outcome, that phrasing is at least in tension with it, and engaging that tension would strengthen the argument rather than weaken it.
- **Confidence.** Medium-high. **Acceptance test.** The paragraph distinguishes what Clark has said from what the article infers, and names at least one observable that would falsify the inference.

### CRITIC-C5 — The cost of her standard is reported only by the people who managed and benefited from her _(concern)_

- **Passage.** H2 7 throughout; the scout-team players appear as objects, never as sources.
- **Problem.** Bluder, White, Jensen and Clark are the article's entire witness list on what her intensity did to a locker room. Three of the four coached her; one is her. The people with the least power in the scene — reserves and walk-ons — are described and not heard. The packet records that no substantive teammate testimony was located.
- **Evidence.** Packet Research limitations item 7.
- **Repair.** State the limitation in the text ("no teammate has publicly described what this was like") rather than narrating the cost from the coaching staff's side as though it were settled. One sentence.
- **Confidence.** High. **Acceptance test.** The article acknowledges whose account it is working from in the section about what her standard cost other people.

### CRITIC-C6 — Cynthia Cooper is recruited as outside corroboration for a claim her quote does not make _(concern)_

- **Passage.** H2 5: "Cynthia Cooper, asked about her by Women's Sports Now in 2026, named the same faculty from the outside: 'She has a high level of basketball IQ.'"
- **Problem.** "Named the same faculty" asks a Hall of Famer to vouch for the article's decision-grading thesis. Cooper's located remarks from that appearance are about scoring craft and playmaking breadth — three-pointers, back-door cuts, getting teammates involved — not decision quality under risk. The specific sentence quoted was not independently confirmed in the packet's pass.

- **Repair.** Verify the sentence, and if it holds, present it as general praise rather than as corroboration of the specific mechanism. If it does not hold, cut it — the turnover argument does not need it.
- **Confidence.** Medium-high. **Acceptance test.** No third party is described as confirming the article's own inference unless their quoted words state it.

### CRITIC-C7 — "There may be no version of her career in which she can" retires the question it claims to hold open _(concern)_

- **Passage.** H2 6: "Both are true, she has not solved it, and there may be no version of her career in which she can."
- **Problem.** The paragraph earns enormous credit by refusing to resolve the tension, and then resolves it — by declaring it structurally unsolvable, which is exoneration wearing the clothes of humility. It is also contestable on the record: the specific criticism made of her is not that she should exit the market but that she could use the platform differently, which is an action available to her.
- **Repair.** Cut the final clause, or replace it with the actual disagreement: what her critics say she could do, and what she has said about doing it.
- **Confidence.** Medium-high. **Acceptance test.** The paragraph ends without asserting that no action is available to her.

### CRITIC-C8 — The designated critic's block quote is worded in a way no source uses _(concern)_

- **Passage.** H2 6 block quote: "Those are hella good numbers. But, to me, that's not dominating." — Sheryl Swoopes, Gil's Arena, September 2024.
- **Problem.** Indexed renderings give "helluva numbers" or "hell of a numbers"; "hella good" was not located anywhere. This is a block quote attributed to a named Hall of Famer whose credibility the article then adjudicates, so the wording matters more here than almost anywhere else in the piece. Separately, "in the same breath" for her "never questioned if Caitlin was a good player" line is not established — same appearance, not established as the same moment.
- **Evidence.** Packet CLM-15, CLM-16, S-27; corroborated by [SI](https://www.si.com/wnba/sheryl-swoopes-claims-caitlin-clark-not-dominating-wnba) and [Bleacher Report](https://bleacherreport.com/articles/10135989-sheryl-swoopes-caitlin-clark-has-been-very-impressive-but-not-dominating-in-wnba).
- **Repair.** Use "helluva," and change "in the same breath" to "in the same appearance."
- **Confidence.** High. **Acceptance test.** The block quote matches a citable rendering exactly.

### CRITIC-C9 — The two alternatives pressure-tested are the two she most visibly is not _(concern)_

- **Passage.** H2 1 pressure-test paragraph; Rabbit Hole counterarguments (Three, then Eight).
- **Problem.** Type Three is defeated largely because Clark verbally disclaims outcome-orientation on camera, and Type Eight is defeated because her anger visibly lands on herself. Both are the easy cases. The alternative that would actually be hard — a worry-into-preparation loop, deference to an authority's reasoning against her own interest, a standing audit driven by anxiety rather than by a standard — is never named. Choosing the beatable opponents is a form of straw-manning even when each defeat is fairly argued. I am not asserting the typing is wrong; I am saying the counterargument apparatus has not been aimed at the strongest target.
- **Evidence.** Packet, "Closest alternative and the discriminating evidence": "The packet does not endorse this — only records that the draft's pressure-testing covers Three and Eight and stops there."
- **Repair.** One paragraph naming the anxiety-driven alternative and the evidence that distinguishes it. The Bluder COVID-isolation material and "Disappointed isn't a big enough word" are both available.
- **Confidence.** Medium. **Acceptance test.** The counterargument section engages an alternative that the article cannot defeat with a single Clark quote.

## Specific improvements

Ranked by how much each changes whether the sympathetic reading is earned.

1. **Restore the contradicting half of the Olympic answer** (CRITIC-B1). One clause plus a rewritten conclusion. Highest leverage in the piece: it repairs the article's own named tiebreaker.
2. **Replace the invented Seattle datum in the falsification list** (CRITIC-B4). One sentence. Removes the only instance of the article citing itself.
3. **Cut the scene down to its sourced parts and restore recurrence** (CRITIC-B5). Costs vividness; buys the section's factual footing. If DJ wants the scene, the repair is research: the _Talkin' Hawks with The VandeBergs_ Jensen episode.
4. **Quote the Maravich answer verbatim** (CRITIC-B2). Two-sentence fix from S-31.
5. **Name Carrington's prior criticism and cut "no standing"** (CRITIC-B3). Two sentences. Converts the June 2024 quote from a character beat into a documented decision.
6. **Add McNutt's charge to the July 2026 beat, and verify or cut "draining" / "shoulders tense"** (CRITIC-C2). The section currently uses a news cycle about criticism of Clark to deliver only Clark's complaint about being criticized.
7. **Remove "The rest of the analysis stands on its own" from the accordion preamble** (CRITIC-C3). Six words. If the counterargument can be moved into the body instead, better.
8. **Label the correctness-of-decision resolution as inference and name a falsifier** (CRITIC-C4).
9. **Fix "Neither of them means it as a compliment," the Swoopes wording, the Cooper attribution, and the "no version of her career" clause** (C1, C6, C7, C8). Four small edits, each a direct accuracy repair.

Items 1–5 are net-neutral or near it if the corresponding overreaches are cut alongside the additions: "That is not grace… no mechanism for pretending otherwise" is longer than the accurate version, and the unsourced scene detail is longer than the sourced version.
