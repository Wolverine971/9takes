## Bottom-line verdict

The Type 1 call is probably right, and the article contains one genuinely excellent piece of type
reasoning — the filter in H2 6 that explains why "you are not dominating" bounces off her and "you
are a bad person" does not. That paragraph is the house's own Type 1 fear ("Am I good?") rendered
as an observable behavioral filter, and it is the best type work I have read in this corpus.

The problem is the evidence handling underneath it. Three separate times, at exactly the points
where the draft distinguishes One from Three, the half of the record that cuts toward Three has
been removed: a clause containing "I want to be the best player in the world" is silently cut out
of the middle of the obsession quote; her simultaneous insistence that she was good enough for the
Olympic roster is dropped; the swagger beat three seconds after the six is dropped. Each cut is
small. Together they mean the tiebreakers the draft names in its own memo are tiebreakers the draft
manufactured.

Separately, the article's load-bearing mechanism — that Clark grades correctness at the moment of
decision and treats the outcome as somebody else's business — is invented. Clark has explained her
turnovers in her own words, and her explanation is a different mechanism: miscalibrated optimism
about outcomes, which she calls "maybe a toxic part of me" and declines to fix. That quote was
available and is not in the piece.

The subtype call in the Rabbit Hole contradicts 9takes' own Type 1 pillar page in the paragraph
that links to it.

None of this sinks the Type 1 read. All of it is repairable, mostly from the evidence packet, and
the repairs would make the typing argument stronger rather than weaker — because the harder version
of the evidence is still winnable. Revise.

## What landed

**ENN-H1 — The character-versus-output filter (H2 6, the paragraph beginning "Look at what
survives the filter").**

> "'You are not dominating' is a claim about output, and output is a number she is already grading
> more harshly than Swoopes ever could, so it bounces: the internal auditor got there first with a
> worse verdict. 'You are a bad person' cannot be handled that way, because the audit is the moral
> instrument."

This must survive revision untouched. It is the only place in the article where the type does
falsifiable work: it predicts which criticisms she absorbs and which she goes on television about,
and the prediction matches the record (she has never litigated the Swoopes take; she did litigate
the character coverage). It also happens to be the discriminator the piece needs elsewhere and
does not use — see ENN-C6. It aligns exactly with the house pillar's core-fear framing
(`src/blog/enneagram/enneagram-type-1.md:240`: "Type 1 asks: *Am I good?* The fear is moral
corruption. The self-criticism focuses on character, integrity, and doing things right").

**ENN-H2 — Relocating the obsession from outcome to hours (H2 1).**

> "A player driven by winning can be paid off by a scoreboard. A player driven by hours cannot be
> paid off at all, because the ledger runs forward and there is always more time available to have
> spent."

The mechanism is correct for Ones and it is stated in plain language with no jargon. It is also the
sentence that makes the closing bookend work. Preserve the sentence; fix the quote it rests on
(ENN-R3).

**ENN-H3 — The smile assignment as a type observation rather than a sad anecdote (H2 7).**

> "Ones run on anger they mostly aim at themselves, and the portion that will not fit gets
> discharged sideways at whoever is nearest. The face she makes at a teammate is the quiet edited
> version of what she is already saying to herself."

This is a near-verbatim match to the house pillar's own formulation (`enneagram-type-1.md:62`:
"The criticism they aim at the world is the quiet, edited version of the voice running at them
since 7 AM") and it is doing real explanatory work on a behavior most coverage treats as
entitlement. Keep. Note that this same paragraph is the evidence that breaks the subtype call
(ENN-R2) — the fix is in the Rabbit Hole, not here.

**ENN-H4 — Naming what the type does not explain, in the body, unresolved (H2 6).**

> "She says the credit belongs to the Black women who built the place. She also keeps the check.
> Both are true, she has not solved it, and there may be no version of her career in which she
> can."

Refusing to let the type absorb the commercial fluency is the single biggest reason this piece
reads as an argument rather than a horoscope. Most typings of a famous person fail precisely by
explaining everything. Keep the paragraph and keep its position in the body — moving it to the
Rabbit Hole would undo the effect.

## What missed

**The turnover section, which is the piece's centerpiece and its weakest evidence.** The draft
promises to resolve the contradiction between a perfectionist and a league-record error rate. It
resolves it by asserting an inner accounting Clark has never described, while an actual first-person
account of that exact behavior exists and says something else. See ENN-R1. The idea is good enough
that fresh eyes called it "the sharpest idea here" — which is why it needs to be built on her words
rather than instead of them.

**The Three pressure-test does not test the Three that competes.** Both the diagnosis paragraph and
the Rabbit Hole rebut a Three who "protects the visible outcome and manages the room." That Three
was never in contention. See ENN-C6.

**The Rabbit Hole's certainty runs well ahead of its evidence.** Wing, subtype, and both arrows are
each asserted at roughly the same confidence as the core type, on materially thinner support. The
core type has two independent coaches converging on a word; the stress arrow has a person being sad
about a lost season.

## What I expected

- **A first-person quote about turnovers in the turnover section.** For a 4,490-word piece whose
  thesis is about her internal grading, the absence of her own account of the behavior under
  analysis is conspicuous. It exists.
- **The word "anger" earlier than H2 7.** Type 1 is a gut-center type whose core emotional fuel is
  anger the person often cannot name. The draft has excellent anger material (Bluder's three
  targets, the practice technicals, the assigned smile) and holds it until section seven, so the
  diagnosis section argues the type on standards alone — the least discriminating One evidence
  there is, since high standards are common to Ones, Threes, Sixes, and most elite athletes of any
  type.
- **Some acknowledgement that "perfectionist" from a coach is a folk word, not a type finding.**
  The draft leans on two coaches reaching for it. Per the house pillar, roughly every conscientious
  competitor gets called a perfectionist; White even says so in the quote itself ("as most elite
  players are"). The convergence is suggestive, not diagnostic, and the piece treats it as heavier
  than it is — including in the TL;DR gloss that neither coach means it as a compliment, which the
  packet shows is not the speakers' register (CLM-06).
- **A stated confidence level somewhere reader-visible.** The pre-write memo says medium-high. The
  article reads as certain.

## What surprised me

**Welcome:** the decision to grade correctness rather than outcomes as the One's organizing
principle. That is a real and underused distinction — most popular Enneagram writing collapses
Type 1 into risk-aversion, and the draft is right that this is wrong. The instinct is good. It is
the sourcing that fails, not the idea.

**Welcome:** using an athlete's box score as the type instrument (H2 4). Reading a stat line against
the subject's own standard is a genuinely new way to show a One's attention, and the final row does
in one line what three paragraphs of theory would not.

**Jarring:** the Rabbit Hole rules out the sexual instinct on the grounds that she has no single
rival, then spends the next section explaining why she is not a Type 8. In the school this site
publishes, the one-to-one One is *the* variant that gets mistyped as an 8. The draft encountered the
signature and interpreted it as a reason to argue against Eight rather than as a reason to revisit
the instinct.

**Jarring:** the most quotable statement of the thesis in the whole piece — "Somewhere in there are
the four minutes that made it close, and I will find them tomorrow" — is written by the author and
formatted as her thought.

## Red flags

### ENN-R1 — Blocker: the load-bearing type mechanism is contradicted by Clark's own account of the behavior it explains

- **Passage.** H2 5, the resolution paragraph: "Ones are not organized around avoiding mistakes.
  They are organized around doing the thing correctly, and Clark grades correctness at the moment
  of decision. Whether the ball arrives is a separate question, settled a half-second later by four
  other people. If the skip pass is the right pass, it is the right pass on the possession it gets
  deflected and on the possession it gets a layup."
- **Trust problem.** This is the article's central resolving move (packet CLM-25: "The article's
  central resolving move… No located source has Clark describing her own standard this way"). A
  reader who knows the subject can find her explanation in one search, and it describes a different
  mechanism. The article does not merely lack support; it competes with the record and does not
  disclose that it is doing so.
- **Evidence.** Asked directly about the record turnover season, Clark said: "But I think the risk
  assessment thing, maybe it's a toxic part of me, but I always think it's going to go well, and I
  throw it and it doesn't go well." Also: "I'm not saying this is a good thing to brag about, but
  that's just how I play," and "You have to think you're better than you actually are at the end of
  the day." (Sue Bird podcast appearance, 2024; rendered by Pro Football Network — the packet's
  S-29, previously headline-level only — and by Basketball Network / Yahoo Sports.) Her account is
  *outcome-expectation that is miscalibrated*, self-labeled as a flaw and knowingly retained. The
  draft's account is *outcome-indifference*. Those are not the same claim: "I always think it's
  going to go well" is a prediction about the ball arriving, which the draft says she does not
  grade.
- **Why it matters for the typing specifically.** "That's just how I play" is a shrug at a
  persistent, league-record error — which is friction for a thesis whose whole engine is an audit
  that never closes. That friction is interesting and probably survivable (a One can ring-fence one
  domain as constitutional), but it has to be argued, not skipped.
- **Minimum viable repair.** Quote her, then argue with her. Two options, either acceptable:
  (a) revise the mechanism to what she describes — she grades the *confidence*, not the outcome,
  and "toxic part of me" is the auditor's verdict on her own optimism; or (b) keep the
  decision-grading read and explicitly reconcile it with "just how I play" in one sentence.
- **Expected benefit.** Converts the piece's weakest-sourced paragraph into its best-sourced one,
  and removes the only place where an informed reader can catch the article inventing her
  interior.
- **Confidence.** High that the quotes exist and describe a different mechanism. Medium on exact
  venue/date — sourced here through aggregators, so an editor should pin the primary before
  quoting.
- **Acceptance test.** The turnover section contains at least one first-person Clark quote about
  her own turnovers, and the resolving paragraph addresses "that's just how I play" rather than
  asserting a standard she has never articulated.

### ENN-R2 — Blocker: the instinctual subtype call contradicts the 9takes Type 1 pillar and rules out the best-fitting variant using a definition of the sexual instinct that no school uses

- **Passage.** Rabbit Hole → "Caitlin Clark's Instinctual Subtype: self-preservation dominant,
  social second (sp/so)," specifically: "What is conspicuously absent is the sexual/one-to-one
  instinct: she has never built her public identity around intensity toward a single rival."
- **Trust problem.** The sexual instinct is not "intensity toward a single rival." Defining it that
  way and then ruling it out is a category error, delivered in the section explicitly addressed to
  readers who know the system, one paragraph away from a link to the site's own guide.
- **Evidence.** `src/blog/enneagram/enneagram-type-1.md:332`: "**The one-to-one One (Zeal):** The
  countertype, and the one nobody guesses. This One's anger runs hot and outward, aimed at
  perfecting other people and society more than the self. Intense, reforming, entitled to improve
  what they love. **Frequently mistyped as an 8.**" Compare the sp One at line 328: "Anger runs
  most repressed here, converted into anxiety, foresight, and relentless self-improvement. Warm,
  conscientious, and hardest on themselves."
  The draft's own body evidence is the opposite of repressed anger: Bluder naming three outward
  targets, anger leaking onto teammates and officials, a sports psychologist intervening
  *specifically because the outward discharge was damaging the room*, and a coach calling technical
  fouls on her in practice. That is the Zeal profile as this site defines it. The draft then
  devotes a Rabbit Hole paragraph to refuting Type 8 — which the pillar identifies as the sx One's
  standard mistype.
- **Additional weakness.** The affirmative sp case ("Watch where her time goes… the gym, the hours,
  the daily accounting") does not discriminate. Every professional athlete's life is a maintenance
  schedule; this evidence would return sp for all of them.
- **Minimum viable repair.** Either (a) recall the subtype to sx-dominant and note that the
  8-mistype the draft already refutes is the expected artifact of that variant — which makes the
  Eight counterargument *land harder*, not softer; or (b) keep sp/so but argue it against the Zeal
  profile on the anger-direction evidence, and delete the "single rival" definition either way.
- **Expected benefit.** Removes a self-contradiction against the site's own pillar page, and
  option (a) turns two currently disconnected Rabbit Hole sections into one argument.
- **Confidence.** High.
- **Acceptance test.** The subtype paragraph's characterization of the sexual instinct is
  consistent with `enneagram-type-1.md:332`, and the anger-direction evidence from H2 7 is
  addressed rather than left contradicting the call.

### ENN-R3 — Blocker: the named One-versus-Three tiebreakers are produced by trimming the half of each quote that points to Three

- **Passages.** (a) H2 1, the ESPN block quote plus "She is sitting in front of a camera being
  invited to say she is obsessed with winning… She corrects the premise instead."
  (b) H2 3: "Clark argued the committee's case for them, in public, and specified the evidence
  against herself. That is not grace… she has no mechanism for pretending otherwise."
  (c) Rabbit Hole subtype section: "I come in here every single day and I want to put in the work
  and get everything done."
- **Trust problem.** The draft's own pre-write memo names these as the tiebreaker ("the 2026 ESPN
  sit-down, where she disclaims winning as the driver… combined with… her public agreement with the
  Olympic selection committee's case against her"). In both, the omitted material is the part that
  reads as Three.
- **Evidence.** Packet S-03 gives the full ESPN passage: "…because you've been so obsessed with the
  time that you've put in. **I come in here every single day and I want to put in the work and get
  everything done because I want to be the best player in the world and I want to help this team
  win.** I don't think it's like an obsession with winning…" The draft joins the sentences on either
  side of that clause with no ellipsis. The same sentence reappears in the Rabbit Hole truncated at
  exactly the word before "because I want to be the best player in the world." So the outcome
  language is removed at both points of use.
  Packet CLM-13 / S-08: in the same TIME interview where she called the committee's reasoning "a
  tremendous point," Clark also maintained that she was good enough to have made the roster. The
  draft presents unqualified self-indictment. "She has no mechanism for pretending otherwise" is
  directly falsified by the omitted half.
- **Mitigating fact I want on the record.** The epigraph does carry "I want to be the best," so the
  outcome language is not absent from the article. But it appears where it reads as intensity and
  is cut where it would function as counterevidence, which is the pattern that concerns me.
- **Minimum viable repair.** Restore the clause (or mark the excision with an ellipsis and engage
  it in the following paragraph); add one sentence to the Olympic paragraph stating that she also
  insisted she was good enough; delete or qualify "no mechanism for pretending otherwise."
- **Expected benefit.** The One read survives the fuller quotes — a One can want to be the best
  player in the world and still locate the driver in the hours — and it survives *as an argument*
  rather than as a selection. That is the difference between persuading an informed reader and
  losing one.
- **Confidence.** High; both omissions are packet-verified.
- **Acceptance test.** The ESPN quote either includes the middle clause or marks its removal; the
  Olympic paragraph states both halves of her position; no sentence claims she is incapable of
  self-assertion.

## Specific improvements

### ENN-C1 — Concern: an unfalsifiable type assertion is presented as evidence

- **Passage.** H2 1: "Threes do not get called for a T by their own coach at a closed practice.
  Ones do."
- **Problem.** Nothing in Enneagram theory predicts practice technicals by type, and the sentence
  is constructed so that no observation could contradict it. It is the exact failure mode an
  informed reader is scanning for. Packet CLM-04 flags it independently.
- **Compounding evidence.** Per S-17 and S-21, Bluder's practice technicals were a recurring
  management habit applied whenever Clark could not control her emotions — so the event is not even
  unique enough to carry a type inference.
- **Repair.** Cut both sentences. The real discriminator is already one clause earlier: she spent
  room-management capital instead of protecting it.
- **Benefit.** Removes the piece's clearest instance of jargon-shaped reasoning.
- **Confidence.** High.
- **Acceptance test.** No sentence in the article asserts that a type is incapable of a specific
  behavior.

### ENN-C2 — Concern: both connecting-line claims rest on behavior any type would produce

- **Passage.** Rabbit Hole → "Stress and Growth Arrows," the Four claim ("The moodiness and the
  sense of being apart from the group are textbook Four under strain") and the Seven claim ("the
  bowling and blueberry bits with Letterman, the golf").
- **Problem.** "Disappointed isn't a big enough word" is ordinary grief at a lost season; being
  playful on a talk show is ordinary talk-show behavior. Neither discriminates, and "textbook" is
  doing certainty work the evidence cannot fund. Packet CLM-37 reaches the same conclusion.
- **Repair.** A discriminating datum is already in the draft and unused: her own "people didn't
  understand how I was wired," which maps directly onto the house pillar's Four-arrow signature
  (`enneagram-type-1.md:270`: "Nobody understands how hard I try"). Swap it in for the moodiness
  claim, and hedge the Seven claim or cut it.
- **Benefit.** Turns the weakest section of the Rabbit Hole into the one place the arrows are
  earned.
- **Confidence.** High.
- **Acceptance test.** Each arrow claim cites behavior that would look different under a competing
  type, or explicitly marks itself as consistent-but-not-diagnostic.

### ENN-C3 — Concern: the 1w2 case is argued with non-discriminating evidence, on a quote that is not in the packet

- **Passage.** Rabbit Hole → "Caitlin Clark's Wing: 1w2," and its supporting quote: "Her work
  ethic, and her demanding excellence from herself, really created everybody else to demand that
  same kind of excellence."
- **Problem.** That quote describes influence by example, which is the *social* One / exemplar
  pattern, not the 1w2 marker. The house pillar's 1w2 is interpersonal and hands-on
  (`enneagram-type-1.md:140`: "The judge who leaves the bench to fix your case personally"). Raising
  a program's standard by being demanding of yourself is what a 1w9 does too. Separately, this
  quote appears in no packet claim (CLM-01 through CLM-39) and in no source-ledger entry — it is
  the only block quote in the article I could not trace.
- **Repair.** Source the quote, and replace or supplement it with one located instance of Clark
  staying to help a specific teammate meet the standard. If none exists, hedge the wing to "1w2
  over 1w9, on thin evidence."
- **Benefit.** Prevents an unverified quote from shipping and stops the wing claim from
  over-reaching.
- **Confidence.** Medium-high — high on the discrimination problem, medium on the sourcing (absence
  from the packet is strong but not proof the quote is wrong).
- **Acceptance test.** The wing section cites at least one packet-traceable instance of the
  staying-to-help behavior it claims, or states its own uncertainty.

### ENN-C4 — Concern: Cooper is recruited as outside corroboration for a claim she did not make

- **Passage.** H2 5: "Cynthia Cooper, asked about her by Women's Sports Now in 2026, named the same
  faculty from the outside: 'She has a high level of basketball IQ.'"
- **Problem.** "The same faculty" claims Cooper independently confirmed Clark's *self-grading of
  decisions*. Basketball IQ is a claim about decision quality, not about how the player audits her
  own decisions — those are different faculties, and the second is the one the section needs.
  Packet CLM-26 also reports the exact sentence was not independently confirmed and that Cooper's
  located remarks concern scoring craft and playmaking breadth.
- **Repair.** Either drop the corroboration framing (keep the quote as third-party praise of her
  reads) or cut the sentence. Do not let an unconfirmed quote carry a load-bearing inference.
- **Benefit.** Removes a stretch that an informed reader will catch precisely because it arrives at
  the moment the argument most needs outside help.
- **Confidence.** High.
- **Acceptance test.** No sentence claims an outside observer confirmed Clark's internal
  decision-grading.

### ENN-C5 — Concern: the opening's diagnostic hinge is unverified, and the confidence beat that immediately followed it is omitted

- **Passage.** Cold open ("Every word of that is defensible. None of it explains how fast the number
  came out") and TL;DR bullet 1 ("The speed is the interesting part").
- **Problem.** No source characterizes her delivery (packet CLM-02). More important for the typing:
  per S-02, Andrews noted she was not capping herself at six and Clark answered "We'll have to see"
  with a wry smile. The article shows the humility beat and drops the swagger beat that arrived
  seconds later — a One-flattering edit of a two-part exchange, and the third instance of the
  pattern in ENN-R3.
- **Repair.** Watch the ESPN segment and either keep the tempo claim with the video cited, or
  re-hinge the opening on the *content* of the answer (60 games, two playoff games, age 24 — an
  audit rendered as a rationale). Add "We'll have to see" somewhere; it costs one line and buys
  credibility.
- **Benefit.** The opening stops depending on an unverifiable observation, and the piece stops
  looking like it is curating her.
- **Confidence.** High.
- **Acceptance test.** Either a citation supports the speed claim or the inference rests on the
  answer's content; and the "We'll have to see" beat appears in the article.

### ENN-C6 — Concern: the strongest Three case is never engaged, because the draft rebuts the wrong Three

- **Passage.** H2 1 ("a Three protects the visible outcome and manages the room") and Rabbit Hole →
  "Counterarguments: Why Caitlin Clark Might Not Be a Type 1."
- **Problem.** Both rebuttals target an image-managing Three. The Three that actually competes here
  is the self-preservation Three — the variant that is uncomfortable with self-promotion, that
  wants to *be* good at the work rather than be seen as good, and that is the single most common
  Three mistyped as a One. Every discriminator the draft offers is also sp-Three behavior:
  disclaiming the outcome, refusing to manage the room, self-criticizing on camera, saying the
  trophy does not keep her up at night. The pressure test therefore leaves the strongest
  alternative standing, and an Enneagram-literate reader will notice that the counterargument
  section is the least rigorous part of a rigorous piece.
- **Repair.** Discriminate on the fear rather than on room management — and the piece already
  contains the discriminator that does it. A Three's dread is worthlessness and it attaches to
  *output*; a One's dread is moral corruption and it attaches to *character*. Clark's own filter
  (H2 6) shows output criticism bouncing and character criticism landing. Point the counterargument
  section at that paragraph in two sentences and the sp-Three is genuinely eliminated.
- **Benefit.** Converts the article's weakest argument into a second use of its strongest one,
  costs almost no words, and closes the alternative that an informed reader will actually raise.
- **Confidence.** High.
- **Acceptance test.** The counterargument section addresses a Three who is not image-managing, and
  discriminates on core fear rather than on self-presentation.

### ENN-C7 — Concern: invented interiority carries diagnostic weight at the two most load-bearing moments

- **Passage.** H2 4 ("Somewhere in there are the four minutes that made it close, and I will find
  them tomorrow") and H2 8 ("Every hour I put in this year bought me nothing").
- **Problem.** The Seattle block is the cleanest statement of the thesis in the article, and it is
  fiction. The packet's own discriminator table lists "followed a career night by finding a
  three-point margin to be unhappy about" as **interpretation only** — meaning a fabricated interior
  monologue is functioning as a One-versus-Three datum. Rendering check: `.inner-thought`
  (`src/scss/blog.scss:592`) styles these as an italic thought-bubble panel, which does distinguish
  them from the cited block quotes, but a thought bubble reads as *what she was thinking*, not as
  *what the author imagines she was thinking*. Nothing on the page marks the difference.
- **Repair.** Keep the device — it is one of the better things in the piece — but ensure no
  discriminator depends on it. One clause of authorial framing before the Seattle block ("If the
  pattern holds, the tape session sounds something like this") costs six words and converts an
  invented fact into an explicit inference.
- **Benefit.** Preserves the interior beats while removing the piece's only unmarked invention.
- **Confidence.** Medium-high.
- **Acceptance test.** No type discriminator in the article rests on content that appears only
  inside an `inner-thought` block.

## Follow-on questions

**ENN-Q1 — What is the primary source, date, and full context of the "toxic part of me" turnover
quote?**
What would change: everything about ENN-R1's repair. If the quote is from the Sue Bird podcast in
2024 with the "risk assessment" framing intact, the draft can quote it and argue with it. If the
surrounding context shows her also describing decision-correctness, the draft's current mechanism is
partly vindicated and needs only a citation. Best source: the Sue Bird podcast episode audio
(*Bird's Eye View*, 2024); secondary renderings at Pro Football Network and Basketball Network
should not be the citation of record.

**ENN-Q2 — In the June 2026 ESPN segment, how fast was the six, and what exactly does the "We'll
have to see" exchange contain?**
What would change: whether the cold open's hinge survives (CLM-02) and whether ENN-C5's omission is
minor or material. Best source: the ESPN video itself, `arXekQ8W4_c` — the auto-transcript cannot
answer either question.

**ENN-Q3 — Is there any located instance of Clark staying with an individual teammate to bring them
up to her standard?**
What would change: the wing. That behavior is the 1w2 marker; without it the piece is calling 1w2 on
program-wide influence, which does not discriminate against 1w9 (ENN-C3). Best source: teammate
interviews from Iowa (Kate Martin, Gabbie Marshall) — the packet notes at limitation 7 that no
substantive teammate testimony was located, which is itself the answer if a search confirms it.

**ENN-Q4 — Does the Jensen account, heard directly, describe a single ejection or a recurring
habit?**
What would change: not the type, but how much weight the practice-technical scene can carry as a
tiebreaker. If it is habitual (as Bluder's own framing in S-17/S-21 suggests), it is weaker evidence
about a specific moment and stronger evidence about a standing pattern — which actually suits the
thesis better. Best source: *Talkin' Hawks with The VandeBergs*, ~July 2025, episode audio.

**ENN-Q5 — Should Type 6 be pressure-tested at all?**
The packet raises it as untested (disputes item 9). My assessment: no, and the draft is right to
skip it. The house discriminator is clean — a Six's dread is danger and misinterpretation, a One's
is moral corruption (`enneagram-type-1.md:240,244`) — and Clark's stated dread is explicitly
character-based ("don't make accusations about my character and who I am"). Adding a Six section
would spend words defending against an alternative no informed reader will raise. Recorded here so
the editor can close the packet's open item without research.
