# Shared Evidence Packet — Caitlin Clark

This packet supplies the common checkable evidence for six independent reviews of the frozen
snapshot `draft-reviewed.md`. It does not evaluate the article. Every entry is labeled
**verified fact**, **attributed claim**, **interpretation**, **disputed claim**, or
**unresolved question**. The draft's thesis (Enneagram Type 1) is treated throughout as a
hypothesis, never as an established fact.

Reviewers should note one structural feature of this subject: Clark is an active athlete whose
season is in progress as the draft publishes. Several of the draft's most specific claims are
about events that happened within the last three weeks and are still moving.

---

## Identity and scope

**Subject.** Caitlin Elizabeth Clark, born January 22, 2002, in Des Moines, Iowa. Professional
basketball guard for the Indiana Fever (WNBA). Middle child of Brent Clark and Anne Nizzi-Clark,
between an older brother Blake and a younger brother Colin. Played college basketball at the
University of Iowa (2020–2024) under head coach Lisa Bluder. Drafted first overall by Indiana in
April 2024. Age 24 at the draft's publication date.

**Aliases and handles.** "CC" and "Caty" (childhood, per family home video). Twitter/X
`@CaitlinClark22`, Instagram `@caitlinclark22`. No TikTok listed in draft frontmatter.

**Domain.** Basketball; also a large commercial and cultural figure in US sports media,
endorsement, and — not by her choosing — in a running national argument about race, media
attention, and the WNBA's history.

**Why the article covers her.** 9takes personality-analysis blog on a high-search-demand living
subject. Draft thesis: Clark is an Enneagram Type 1 whose standard is aimed at correctness of
effort rather than at outcomes, which the draft uses to reconcile her perfectionist reputation
with her league-record turnover season.

**Packet knowledge cutoff.** Evidence gathered 2026-08-05. The compiler's model training data ends
May 2026, so every 2026 in-season claim in this packet was verified against live web sources
rather than recalled. Statistics that change daily (season averages, league ranks, team record)
are true as of the first week of August 2026 and will drift.

## First-person evidence

### The self-rating (load-bearing for the draft's spine)

> Andrews: "On a scale of 1 to 10, 1 being still trying to find your footing and 10 being the best
> player that you can be in this league, where do you think you fall right now?"
> Clark: "That's a great question. Um, honestly, I think I'm at a six, probably. I've only played
> 60 games in this league and I've played two playoff games… I'm only 24. Like I still feel like I
> have so much room to improve."
> — ESPN, Malika Andrews, June 2026 (S-01, S-02)

**What it supports:** that she rated herself low and produced a games-played and age rationale.
**What it does not support:** the *speed* of the answer, which the draft makes the load-bearing
detail ("The speed is the interesting part," "None of it explains how fast the number came out").
No located text source characterizes her delivery as fast. The auto-transcript records the words,
not the tempo. A reviewer wanting to check this must watch the video.

**Additional context the draft does not use:** Andrews followed up by noting Clark was not capping
herself at six, and Clark replied "We'll have to see" with a wry smile (S-02). This is a
confidence beat sitting immediately after the humility beat.

### On what she is obsessed with

> "I'm obsessed with the work. And then you get in the game and then you're competitive with it
> because you've been so obsessed with the time that you've put in. I come in here every single
> day and I want to put in the work and get everything done because I want to be the best player
> in the world and I want to help this team win. I don't think it's like an obsession with
> winning. I think that comes because you're obsessed with all the time you put in."
> — ESPN, 2026; YouTube auto-transcript `arXekQ8W4_c` (S-03)

**Note for reviewers:** the draft quotes a shortened version and omits the middle clause "because
I want to be the best player in the world and I want to help this team win." That clause contains
both an outcome goal and a team-win goal. Whether its removal is fair compression or a material
edit is a judgment the packet does not make, but the full text is above so the question can be
asked.

### On scrutiny and caring (the draft's epigraph)

> "I think people forget that, at the end of the day, I care more than all of you. I want to be
> the best."
> — Yahoo Sports, ~May 2026 (S-04)

**Wording discrepancy.** The draft's epigraph reads: "I think people forget at the end of the day,
like, I care more than all of you. Like, I want to be the best." The two "like" fillers and the
dropped "that" do not appear in the Yahoo rendering. Either the draft is quoting raw audio and
Yahoo cleaned it, or the draft introduced disfluencies. Unresolved.

> "And at the end of her career, there was so much noise. All of the people that wanted a piece of

This resolves an open item in the repo research file, which flagged the venue and date of this

**Note on the draft's TL;DR framing.** The draft states that neither coach "means it as a

**Cynthia Cooper** — Hall of Famer, 2026 All-Star team captain.

## Public record, accomplishments, failures, and controversies

### Accomplishments (verified fact unless noted)

- NCAA Division I career scoring leader, passing Pete Maravich's 3,667 on 2024-03-03. Record had
  stood since 1970.
- Two consecutive NCAA championship game appearances (2023, 2024). No title.
- 2023 AP National Player of the Year; single-tournament NCAA scoring record (191 points, 2023).
- 2024 WNBA No. 1 overall pick; Rookie of the Year with 66 of 67 first-place votes.
- Fastest to 500 career assists (59 games; prior Sue Bird, 82) and fastest to 200 career threes
  (74 games; prior Katie Smith, 81).
- 2026-07-17: first 40-point/10-assist game in WNBA history — 45 points, 10 assists, 4 steals, 2
  blocks, 11-of-18 FG, 6-of-10 3PT, 17-of-19 FT, 4 turnovers.
- Leading All-Star vote-getter, 2026; three-time Eastern Conference Player of the Week in 2026.

### Failures and counterevidence

- **223 turnovers in 2024**, the most in a single WNBA season, against a prior record of 137
  (Alyssa Thomas). Guinness lists it as a world record. This is the central factual counterweight
  to any frictionless-perfectionism reading.
- Rookie shooting efficiency was ordinary by superstar standards; the Fever started 1–8 and were
  3–9 when the Olympic news broke.
- 2025: 13 of 41 games. Three separate soft-tissue/bone injuries. Missed the entire playoff run.
- Left off the 2024 Olympic roster.

### Controversies

- **Race and the culture war.** Clark's rise coincided with a large new audience, a portion of
  which directed racist and misogynistic abuse at Black WNBA players. Clark disavowed this
  (2024-06-13) and, in TIME, named her own racial privilege. Reporting and commentary continue to
  dispute how much responsibility she bears for a fanbase she did not recruit — ESPN's Monica
  McNutt publicly called for more "accountability and self-awareness" from Clark as recently as
  the July 2026 cycle (S-13, S-14). *Attributed claim / disputed.*
- **Physicality and officiating.** The Carter flagrant (2024-06-01) anchored a season-long
  argument about whether Clark was targeted. Clark said in December 2024 that she did not think
  she was being targeted; the Fever's GM called on the league to clean up "targeting actions."
  *Disputed claim.*
- **Commercial position.** Clark is among the highest-earning athletes in women's sports while
  publicly calling the Reese rivalry that markets the sport "fabricated" and assigning credit for
  the league to Black women. The draft names this as unresolved by design. The packet records it
  as a genuine, unresolved tension, not a settled one.
- **The Maravich record's framing.** Clark holds the NCAA Division I career scoring record. She is
  not the highest scorer in the history of women's college basketball: Pearl Moore scored 4,061 at
  Francis Marion under AIAW/non-NCAA governance, and Lynette Woodard's major-college mark (3,649)
  is separately tracked. Maravich also played three varsity seasons with no three-point line and
  no freshman eligibility. The draft's phrase "leading scorer in the history of Division I
  basketball" is defensible as an NCAA D-I claim and is routinely contested outside that framing.
  *Disputed claim.*

## Enneagram hypothesis evidence

The packet takes no position on the typing. It records what the evidence can and cannot carry.

### Evidence consistent with Type 1

1. Two head coaches, four years and one league apart, independently reach for "perfectionist"
   (Bluder, KCRG 2025-02-01; White, 2025). *verified fact*
2. Bluder locates the anger on three targets in order — herself, teammates, coaches. *verified fact*
3. She required, and accepted, a sports-psychologist intervention whose content was a facial
   expression: smile at people. *attributed claim (her own account)*
4. She relocates her obsession from outcome to hours in an interview that invited the outcome
   answer. *verified fact (the words), interpretation (that this is diagnostic)*
5. Every game assigned identical weight, preseason included. *verified fact*
6. "I care more than all of you." *verified fact*
7. The standard does not switch off in a stakes-free setting (the card game). *attributed claim*
8. Publicly agrees with the committee that cut her. *verified fact, but see the omission at CLM-13*

### Evidence against, or unexplained by, Type 1

1. **The commercial fluency.** Marketing major; one of the largest endorsement portfolios in
   women's sports; complete ease on camera; the draft's own memo calls this the softest seam. The
   Type 1 read does not generate this behavior; it has to accommodate it.
2. **The confidence beats.** "We'll have to see," delivered with a smile immediately after the six
   (S-02), and "I gave them a lot of reasons to keep me off the team" sitting next to an assertion
   that she was good enough anyway (S-08). Both cut against a purely self-critical picture.
3. **The 223 turnovers.** A conventional Type 1 read predicts risk-aversion. The draft resolves
   this by redefining the standard as correctness-of-read rather than avoidance-of-error. That
   resolution is *interpretation*, and it is the load-bearing move of the whole article. No
   located source has Clark describing her own decision-grading in those terms; the closest
   located material is her calling turnovers a "toxic part of me" (S-29, headline-level only).
4. **The anger's visibility.** Outward-directed on-court anger at officials and teammates is at
   least as compatible with Type 8 as with Type 1; the draft's counterargument (that Eights
   experience anger as clean and outward-only) is Enneagram theory, not evidence about Clark.

### Closest alternative and the discriminating evidence

**Type 3** is the draft's own named alternative and the packet agrees it is the strongest one. The
discriminators the draft offers:

| Discriminator | Evidentiary status |
| --- | --- |
| Disclaims winning as the driver on national TV | verified fact; genuinely awkward for a Three |
| Lost titles "don't keep me up at night," said to a Goldman Sachs audience | verified fact; but she granted the anger first ("I mean, like, yes") |
| Spent room-management capital at practice hard enough to be ejected | attributed claim, and the specific scene is unsourced — see CLM-05 |
| Argued the Olympic committee's case | verified quote, incomplete presentation — see CLM-13 |
| Followed a career night by finding a three-point margin to be unhappy about | **interpretation only.** No located source has Clark commenting on the Seattle margin. The draft's `inner-thought` block is explicitly authorial. |

**Type 6** is not considered in the draft. It is worth naming as an untested alternative: the
worry-into-preparation loop, the standing audit, and the deference to the selection committee's
reasoning are also legible as counterphobic Six material. The packet does not endorse this — only
records that the draft's pressure-testing covers Three and Eight and stops there.

### Behavior the hypothesis does not explain

Her fluency inside, and profit from, commercial machinery that runs on a rivalry she calls
fabricated, while she assigns the credit elsewhere. The draft holds this open. That is the honest
state of the evidence: no located source resolves it, and Clark has not resolved it either.

## Disputes and unresolved questions

1. **The practice ejection (CLM-05) is the packet's largest gap.** One named source (Jan Jensen)
   says Bluder kicked Clark out of practice and gave her a technical, plural and habitual. Bluder
   separately describes calling practice technicals as a recurring tool. The draft renders a
   single, richly specified scene — scout team, blue squad, reserves and walk-ons, a whistle mid-
   drill, ejection, return for the next drill — of which only "technical" and "practice" are
   sourced. The scene is referenced three times in the article and is the concrete anchor of the
   Three-vs-One tiebreaker. Any conclusion resting on the scene's specifics must stay qualified
   until a primary source (the VandeBergs episode audio, or a Bluder interview) is heard directly.

2. **Whether Clark conceded the Olympic decision or split the difference (CLM-13).** The verified
   record has her doing both. Conclusions drawn from "she argued the case against herself" should
   carry the qualifier that she simultaneously said she was good enough.

3. **Whether the six was delivered fast (CLM-02).** Unresolvable from text. The cold open's hinge
   is a claim about tempo.

4. **Whether she "did not realize" the Maravich record happened (CLM-08).** Her quote says she was
   not tracking it at the line and knew immediately when it was announced. The draft's stronger
   version is not supported.

5. **Clark's current scoring rank (CLM-29).** Second or third in PPG depending on where A'ja
   Wilson's per-game average sits. Assists rank (second) is confirmed. This is a same-week
   verifiable number.

6. **Exact wording of the Swoopes quote (CLM-15).** "Helluva" vs "hella good."

7. **Whether "draining" and "shoulders tense" are real quotes from the July 2026 interview
   (CLM-20).** Not located; may exist only in the broadcast.

8. **The commercial seam.** Not a research gap but a genuine unresolved fact about the subject:
   she earns from machinery that runs partly on a narrative she calls fabricated, and assigns the
   credit for the audience elsewhere. No source resolves it, and she has not.

9. **Whether the Type 1 hypothesis is correct.** The packet does not adjudicate. The strongest
   confirmatory evidence is two independent coaches' word choice plus her own relocation of
   obsession from outcome to hours. The strongest unexplained behavior is the commercial fluency.
   Type 6 was never pressure-tested by the draft.

10. **Her age when she rode the bike (CLM-31).** Unsourced, and the draft states it two different
    ways.

## Research limitations

1. **Two key sources refused direct fetch.** The On3 practice-technicals article returned HTTP 403
   and the Sportskeeda Jensen article returned HTTP 405. Both are known only through search-engine
   summaries. Since these are the *only* sources for the draft's most vivid scene (CLM-05), the
   packet cannot supply a verbatim Jensen quote or confirm whether the underlying podcast contains
   the scout-team detail. Resolving CLM-05 properly requires listening to the *Talkin' Hawks with
   The VandeBergs* Jensen episode.

2. **No primary transcript for the Letterman episode.** The smile assignment and the ruined-
   Christmas line are consistent across at least three independent outlets but were not verified
   against Netflix audio.

3. **The ESPN sit-down transcripts are auto-generated captions** (`arXekQ8W4_c`, `hNBUneM3YqM`),
   inherited from the repo research file. Sentence-level wording is reliable; punctuation and
   disfluency are inferred. This is the likely origin of the epigraph wording discrepancy
   (CLM-39). Neither video was viewed, so the draft's tempo claim (CLM-02) could not be checked.

4. **The *Women's Sports Now* Cooper video was not watched.** The specific "high level of
   basketball IQ" sentence remains attributed rather than verified.

5. **TIME's full text was summarized, not read line by line.** The privilege quote's opening
   sentence and the Olympic "tremendous point" quote are verbatim-confirmed; two continuations are
   paraphrase-confirmed only. Importantly, the summary *did* surface her simultaneous claim that
   she was good enough for the roster (CLM-13), which the draft omits — but the exact wording of
   that assertion was not captured.

6. **Live statistics will drift.** Everything in CLM-29 is a first-week-of-August-2026 snapshot.
   The scoring-rank question in particular should be re-checked immediately before publication.

7. **No substantive teammate testimony was located.** Boston, Mitchell, and Martin material found
   in the repo file is congratulatory rather than analytical. The article's picture of what her
   standard costs a locker room rests entirely on coaches (Bluder, White, Jensen) and on Clark
   herself.

8. **No direct quotes from Blake or Colin Clark were located in primary sources.** Blake's
   snowsuit counter-account (S-25) reaches this packet through an aggregator, which under the tier
   rules cannot carry a factual correction alone — it is recorded as counter-context to be
   verified, not as a refutation of the mother's characterization.

9. **The July 15, 2025 groin-sprain date** is carried from the repo research file and was not
   independently reconfirmed.

10. **Enneagram typing has no external validation standard.** No source in this packet establishes
    Clark's type. Every type claim is hypothesis, and the packet deliberately declines to weigh
    them.
