## Bottom-line verdict

This is a piece written by someone who did real homework on Clark's psychology and got the hard part right — the turnover-record reframe and the box-score table are the kind of connective insight an informed fan reads for. But it was written slightly outside fandom. Two of the sport's most reflexive, load-bearing pieces of fan vocabulary — "logo three" and the "you can't see me" gesture that actually started the Reese rivalry — are either paraphrased around or missing entirely, in a piece that spends a full section arguing about that exact rivalry. A fan reading this will trust the psychology and quietly notice the writer skipped the two things every Clark fan would have reached for first.

## What landed

If the skip pass is the right pass, it is the right pass on the possession it gets deflected and on the possession it gets a layup." This is the delight test passed clean — I knew both facts (perfectionist reputation, 223-turnover record) as a standing contradiction fans argue about constantly, and I had never seen them connected through a single mechanism rather than waved off as "she's aggressive." Must survive revision.

Any fan who watched or followed that Seattle game remembers the "eke out" headline as a minor punchline; running the AP line back through her own grading system and landing on that exact contrast is a genuinely new angle on a game I already knew the box score of. Must survive revision.

**FAN-H3.** Conceding that Swoopes was "partly correct" (H2 6) and specifying what part — the timeline, not the ceiling — is rare. Most Clark content either dunks on Swoopes or ignores her; taking the criticism seriously and then narrowing it is the kind of move an informed fan trusts a writer for. Must survive revision.

## What missed

**FAN-M1 (see FAN-C1).** The shot described in H2 3 ("Threes from the Hawkeye logo at midcourt, with a hand in her face") is never called what every broadcast, the Fever's own YouTube channel, and a CBS 60 Minutes segment call it: the logo three. Describing the shot without ever using its name reads like a writer who watched footage of it rather than watched her play it.

**FAN-M2 (see FAN-C2).** H2 6 builds an entire section on the "fight about race" attached to Clark's fame and quotes her calling the Reese rivalry "fabricated and made up" — without ever naming the actual originating moment: Clark's "you can't see me" gesture in the 2023 Elite Eight against Louisville, and Reese's reciprocal ring-finger-and-gesture at Clark in the title game itself. That's not a footnote to this rivalry; among fans it is the founding document of it. A section built entirely on the aftermath while skipping the origin reads incomplete to anyone who was there for it.
