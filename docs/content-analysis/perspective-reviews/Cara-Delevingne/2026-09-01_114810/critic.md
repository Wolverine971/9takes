## Bottom-line verdict

The article has **not yet earned its sympathetic interpretation**, but it is close, and the repairs
mostly make the piece stronger rather than softer.

The core problem is specific and fixable. The draft nominates one body of evidence as decisive —
"Six separate times across more than two decades, something she could not put into words showed up
on the outside where people could see it. **The pattern is the strongest evidence in the file**" —
and builds both the somatic section and the type tie-breaker on it. That evidence is framed as
involuntary: her body "filing reports," saying things she could not say. The source that section
rests on describes something else. The _Call Her Daddy_ passage about the fractures is reported by
the outlet the packet cites as **self-harm** — deliberately sought injury, danger-seeking, in her
words "I just wanted to feel pain." The draft never uses the word. It converts an act into a
symptom, which is the single most sympathetic transformation available, and it does it at the exact
point where the argument needs to win.

Two things keep this at _strained_ rather than _broken_. First, the corrected fact does not damage
the thesis — a person who deliberately breaks a bone to obtain a visible token of pain is _more_
legible as a bid to be witnessed, not less. The repair costs the draft nothing but accuracy.
Second, the article is genuinely careful elsewhere in ways critics usually have to fight for: the
Weinstein allegation is attributed strictly and never narrated around, the career-collecting sneer
is conceded before it is answered, and the album section refuses to resolve the 4w3 objection it
raises against its own subject. That last move is rare and it is the best thing here.

What the draft has not done is let its skepticism cost anything. Doubt is staged in section five,
dissolved inside the same section; staged again at the end of section six, then followed by a
redemptive final section that gives the sympathetic reading the last word. And the decisive claim
in the whole argument — "the identity problem came first" — is asserted with no source at all,
against a rival reading whose best quotation comes from a better-sourced interview than most of the
draft's pro-Four evidence.

Fix the self-harm frame, source or drop the sequencing claim, correct the tour, and qualify the
authorship claim in the close. Everything else on this list is an upgrade, not a rescue.

## What landed

**CRITIC-H1 — The concession in section five is real, and it names the uncomfortable thing.**

> "Concede what is true in it. She got a major-label deal that thousands of better singers will
> never see, and she got it partly because her name sells."

Most celebrity-sympathy writing concedes a strawman. This concedes the actual charge — structural
advantage — in the subject's own worst light, and does it _before_ mounting the defence rather than
after. **Must survive revision:** it is the load-bearing proof that the article is arguing rather
than advocating. If it goes, every later reframe reads as PR.

**CRITIC-H2 — The album section refuses to resolve the objection it raises.**

> "A Four who finally gets a voice will immediately be offered money for the sound of finally
> getting a voice, and she is now in a business that pays for it. The album is not the test. The
> test is what she makes after the raw thing stops selling."

This is the beat a critic almost never gets: the piece turns its own diagnostic apparatus against
its subject's redemption and then declines to let her off. The Rag & Bone and Topshop details are
placed where they can actually damage the argument, not quarantined. **Must survive revision** —
intact, including the refusal to answer. Softening this into "but the work speaks for itself" would
collapse the piece into the genre it is currently escaping.

**CRITIC-H3 — The counterargument section states a falsifier instead of hedging.**

> "What would change the call: sustained on-record framing of her own motive as escaping limitation
> or boredom rather than as absence of voice."

Committing to a disconfirming condition is the correct epistemic move and it is rare in typology
writing. **Must survive revision** as a _form_, though the specific condition is drawn too narrowly
— see CRITIC-R2.

**CRITIC-H4 — Weinstein is handled correctly.**

> "The clearest statement of the terms comes from her own account on _Call Her Daddy_ in June 2026:
> 'Harvey Weinstein called me and was like, You can't be gay.'"

Attributed as her account, dated, sourced, with no narration asserting the exchange occurred. This
is an uncorroborated allegation against a named third party and the draft does not launder it into
fact. **Must survive revision** exactly as worded.

**CRITIC-H5 — The professional-versus-social reception split is a genuine fairness move.**

> "The music backlash, meanwhile, was a social event more than a critical one."

I tried to falsify this and could not. My own search for a hostile professional review of the two
singles returned only warm-to-neutral coverage (Atwood, When The Horn Blows, FEMMUSIC, RazorFine,
Euphoria Zine, mxdwn). The claim holds. **Must survive revision** — though one of its three named
outlets does not (CRITIC-C6).

## What missed

**The somatic ledger does not describe one phenomenon.** The section promises a single pattern —
"something she could not put into words came out through her body instead" — and then lists six
rows that are categorically different: a hunger strike she herself calls a hunger strike
(deliberate), sleeping in her sister's bed (behaviour, not somatic), a psychiatric breakdown,
psoriasis (genuinely involuntary), fractures (self-harm per source), and paparazzi photographs
(other people's cameras, not her body doing anything at all). Exactly one row — the psoriasis — is
unambiguously the involuntary somatic expression the section's thesis requires. The pattern is
partly produced by the framing that describes it. Detail at CRITIC-R1.

**The unfavorable evidence is clustered where it cannot reach the conclusion.** Every hostile fact
about her work lives in section five, is answered inside section five, and never returns. The
album section's skepticism is aimed at a future she has not lived yet ("what she makes after the
raw thing stops selling") rather than at anything currently checkable. Then section seven closes
warm. Structurally, the doubt is decorative. Detail at CRITIC-C4.

**The tie-breaker is asserted rather than argued.** "The escape layer is real. It sits on top of an
identity problem, and the identity problem came first" is the sentence the entire type verdict
turns on, and nothing anywhere establishes the order. Detail at CRITIC-R2.

**The close overclaims authorship.** "This is the first one she wrote" lands on a record the same
article tells us was co-produced by BJ Burton and co-written with Fiona Apple, released by Warner
inside a fashion campaign. The apparatus critique that powers the first half is switched off for
the ending. Detail at CRITIC-R3.

## What I expected

- **The word "self-harm," once.** An article that quotes "I was so close to ending my life,"
  reproduces a hunger strike at age seven, and opens on repeated fractures is handling self-harm
  material whether or not it says so. The source episode itself carried a content warning for
  suicidal ideation. The draft's only support pointer is a link to an _addiction_ recovery guide,
  which is the adjacent problem, not this one.
- **A steelman of the crowd read that names its actual strength.** The draft's Seven case is a list
  of behaviours. The strongest Seven argument is not behavioural, it is evidentiary: her
  best-sourced statement of motive (Vogue, a cover story) is the escape framing, while most of the
  identity framing comes from a single third-party podcast transcript. The article never
  acknowledges that its own evidence is weaker-sourced than its opponent's.
- **Some accounting of who was harmed.** The piece is attentive to what was done _to_ her —
  Weinstein, the industry, the Sacramento anchors, the paparazzi. It is silent on cost to anyone
  else, including two decades of substance use around family, and a mother whose addiction is used
  as causal machinery. Not a demand for condemnation; a demand that the frame not be uniformly
  one-directional.
- **Vote counts, given the title.** The headline promises to overturn what "the Internet Assigned
  Her." A reader is entitled to know the internet in question is, in part, two votes.

## What surprised me

- **Welcome:** the Rag & Bone paragraph. A sympathetic profile that voluntarily points at its
  subject's current commercial packaging and says _this is what the failure mode looks like once it
  gets good_ is doing something most criticism of celebrity writing asks for and never receives.
- **Welcome:** the voice ladder (model → actor → _Cabaret_ → album) is built from her own repeated
  word rather than from house vocabulary, and the Sally Bowles observation — a woman performing a
  liberation she does not have, eight shows a week — is the kind of connection that justifies the
  article's existence.
- **Jarring:** "Cara Delevingne broke so many bones in one year that she stopped counting them" as a
  first line. Her actual words are "I don't know how many bones in like a year." "Stopped counting"
  is the draft's flourish, and it is placed on the one fact in the piece that most needs literal
  handling.
- **Jarring and unearned:** "Her body had been filing reports for twenty-three years." Beautiful
  sentence, and it is the exact mechanism by which agency disappears from the article.

## Red flags

### Blockers

**CRITIC-R1 — The article's self-declared strongest evidence reframes self-harm as involuntary somatic expression.** _(blocker)_

- **Passage:** the section "What Cara Delevingne's body kept saying for her," specifically its
  premise — "every few years, something she could not put into words came out through her body
  instead, in a form other people could see" — plus the table row "Repeated fractures across
  roughly twelve months," the hook "broke so many bones in one year that she stopped counting
  them," the summary "Her body had been filing reports for twenty-three years," and the TL;DR
  claim "The pattern is the strongest evidence in the file."
- **Reader effect / trust problem:** a reader who checks the source finds that the fractures were,
  by the reporting outlet's characterization, deliberately sought self-injury. The article has
  described an act as a symptom. That is the most sympathetic possible transformation of the
  material and it happens at the load-bearing point. Separately, an article that renders self-harm
  as lyric ("A cast is a sentence the body can say out loud") without ever naming it is handling
  the material less carefully than the podcast it is quoting, which carried a content warning.
- **Evidence:** Daily Front Row's report of the same _Call Her Daddy_ passage frames it as
  self-harm and states the injury was intentionally sought, situating it within a pattern of
  self-harming behaviour alongside disordered eating; contemporaneous summaries describe
  danger-seeking — throwing herself off things — producing multiple fractures inside a year. Her
  quoted words include "So I just wanted to feel pain." (S-06; my fetch, this pass.) The packet
  flags this as its "single most consequential evidence question."
- **Minimum viable repair:** (1) In the fractures row and the tie-breaker, use her own framing —
  that she sought pain and wanted a visible injury she could point to — instead of "came out
  through her body." (2) Rename or re-premise the section so it does not claim involuntary somatic
  expression across rows that are not that; the honest unifying claim is _"evidence other people
  could see, produced when words were unavailable"_, which covers deliberate and involuntary rows
  alike. (3) Name self-harm once, plainly, in her attribution. (4) Add the corresponding support
  pointer alongside the addiction link.
- **Expected benefit:** the tie-breaker gets _stronger_ — deliberately injuring yourself to obtain
  a token a stranger can read across a room is a more pointed bid to be witnessed than an
  involuntary flare — and the article stops being falsifiable by anyone who reads its own source.
- **Confidence:** high on the source characterization and on the mismatch; medium on whether every
  fracture in the twelve-month run was sought, which the audio would settle.
- **Acceptance test:** the words "self-harm" (or her equivalent) appear in her attribution; no
  sentence in the section asserts that the fractures were something her body did rather than
  something she did; the section premise is true of every row it lists.

**CRITIC-R2 — The decisive claim in the type argument is unsourced, and the stated falsifier is drawn to exclude the counterevidence that exists.** _(blocker)_

- **Passage:** "The escape layer is real. It sits on top of an identity problem, and the identity
  problem came first." And in the Rabbit Hole: "What would change the call: sustained on-record
  framing of her own motive as escaping limitation or boredom rather than as absence of voice. That
  framing is not in the record."
- **Reader effect / trust problem:** the article concedes the Seven evidence, then wins on a
  sequencing assertion that no source supports, then defines its own disconfirming condition so
  narrowly that the best counterevidence cannot satisfy it. Her strongest on-record motive
  statement — "you can't run from the things in your life that happen, and that's all I ever wanted
  to do: just pretend they don't exist" (Vogue, 2023) — _is_ sustained on-record escape framing.
  It is excluded only because the falsifier specifies "limitation or boredom." That is a
  gerrymandered test, and it is the move that turns a genuine argument into a foregone one.
- **Evidence:** packet, unresolved question 3 — "Nothing in the record establishes order." Packet
  further notes the Vogue line is "from a **separate, better-sourced interview** than the CHD
  transcript, which makes it harder to discount." Meanwhile the tie-breaker that substitutes for
  the missing sequencing evidence is compromised by CRITIC-R1.
- **Minimum viable repair:** either (a) drop "and the identity problem came first" and let the
  argument rest on the corrected witness-bid tie-breaker, which does not require a sequencing
  claim; or (b) keep it and mark it explicitly as inference — "nothing in the record settles the
  order; what tips it is X." And widen the falsifier to "escape from pain or reality," then say
  plainly that the Vogue line is the closest thing to it and why it does not settle the case.
- **Expected benefit:** the counterargument section stops being a formality. A reader who knows the
  Vogue quote currently catches the article dodging it; one who does not is being under-informed.
- **Confidence:** high. Both halves are verifiable against the packet without further research.
- **Acceptance test:** no unhedged causal-order claim survives; the widened falsifier is stated in
  terms the Vogue quotation could plausibly meet, and the draft addresses that quotation by name
  inside the counterargument section.

**CRITIC-R3 — The close claims sole authorship for a professionally co-written major-label record the article itself credits to other people.** _(blocker)_

- **Passage:** "This is the first one she wrote." Supported by "Music is the first medium in the
  sequence where the words are hers" and the TL;DR's "it is the first work of her career where the
  words are hers."
- **Reader effect / trust problem:** the article spends its first half establishing that this woman's
  authenticity was manufactured by an apparatus, then closes by crediting her, alone, with a Warner
  Records album co-produced by BJ Burton, co-written on at least two tracks with outside writers
  (Fiona Apple on "Need It"; Sarah Aarons, Sammy Witte and Burton on "Crazy, Baby"), and released
  inside a Rag & Bone campaign and a Topshop capsule. The draft supplies every one of those facts
  and files them as charm — Post-it notes, a love song — where they should be pressure. This is the
  clearest instance in the piece of unfavorable evidence placed where it cannot affect the argument.
- **Evidence:** packet CLM-08 and CLM-27 (credits); CLM-21 and CLM-22 (Topshop, Rag & Bone); the
  draft's own section six, which names Burton and Apple.
- **Minimum viable repair:** two clauses. In the voice ladder, make the comparison explicit and
  survivable — the point is that she is a credited author for the first time, not a sole one. In
  the close, qualify "wrote" so it does not assert solitary authorship, or move the emphasis from
  authorship to the one thing that genuinely is unilateral: that she chose the title and declined to
  explain it. That is the close's actual strength and it does not need the overclaim.
- **Expected benefit:** removes the only sentence in the article a hostile reader can refute from
  the album's own credits, and it costs the ending nothing — the unexplained title carries it.
- **Confidence:** high on the credit facts; medium-high that the current wording reads as sole
  authorship rather than as the relative comparison the ladder intends.
- **Acceptance test:** no sentence asserts or implies that she alone wrote the record; the credit
  reality appears at least once where it can bear on the thesis rather than only as texture.

**CRITIC-R4 — The tour is overstated by roughly an order of magnitude, and the inflated figure is used as evidence.** _(blocker)_

- **Passage:** "Her first headline tour opened on 1 June 2026 and runs about six months across
  eleven cities." Echoed in the Rabbit Hole: "Delevingne has a Warner Records deal, an eleven-city
  tour, a fashion campaign built around that tour, a SAG nomination and a West End lead. That is not
  the output of a Four who cannot get out of the house."
- **Reader effect / trust problem:** the published itinerary is 11 shows across about six cities,
  running 1–26 June 2026 — under four weeks. An article dated 1 September tells readers a tour is
  currently running that appears to have ended in June. The critic-specific problem is not just the
  error: the inflated scale is deployed as _evidence_ for the 4w3 productivity argument. The
  article overstates the subject's achievement precisely where the overstatement helps.
- **Evidence:** packet CLM-06 — "DISPUTED — likely wrong on both counts"; itinerary via S-11
  (Berlin, Barcelona ×2, London ×2, Paris ×2, Los Angeles ×2, Brooklyn ×2); Songkick, Bandsintown
  and Ticketmaster show no autumn dates (S-12). The packet traces "six months" to the entity-gap
  packet, asserted with no dated support.
- **Minimum viable repair:** "an eleven-date first headline tour through June 2026," and drop
  "eleven-city." In the Rabbit Hole, keep the tour in the evidence list at its true size — it still
  supports the point.
- **Expected benefit:** removes a claim any reader can falsify at a ticketing site, and removes the
  appearance that the evidence list was padded.
- **Confidence:** high, with the caveat that the packet could not reach a first-party promoter page
  (AEG returned 405), so this rests on the announcement itinerary plus four aggregators.
- **Acceptance test:** no duration or city-count claim in the draft exceeds what the published
  itinerary supports; the Rabbit Hole evidence list matches.

### Concerns

**CRITIC-C1 — The opposition the article exists to overturn is inflated and its thinness is undisclosed.**

- **Passage:** "Six different sites, roughly the same verdict." / "Six crowd-typing sites landed on
  Seven." Plus the title, "Not the 7 the Internet Assigned Her."
- **Problem:** three sites are verified (Personality Database, Boo, Sakinorva); three are not.
  Sakinorva's 7w6 rests on **two votes**, with a competing 3w4; PDB carries multiple competing
  profiles. The article's whole premise is that it is overturning an established verdict. Presenting
  a two-vote page as part of a six-site consensus, without disclosing vote counts, straw-mans the
  position — it makes the opponent both more numerous and more unanimous than it is, which makes the
  overturn look bigger than it is.
- **Evidence:** packet CLM-14 and unresolved question 4.
- **Repair:** verify the remaining three sites (the packet says this is minutes of work), or write
  the true number. Either way, disclose the thinness once — it is more interesting than the
  inflation: a verdict this thin is exactly how a crowd reading calcifies.
- **Benefit:** the premise becomes checkable, and the disclosure supplies a better argument than the
  one it replaces.
- **Confidence:** high. **Acceptance test:** every site counted is named or the count is corrected;
  the vote-thinness appears once.

**CRITIC-C2 — The Sacramento characterization contradicts her own contemporaneous explanation, in the direction that serves the thesis.**

- **Passage:** "She had given them a few flat, bored, unperformed minutes, which is exactly what her
  persona advertised."
- **Problem:** the draft needs this to be authenticity being punished. Her own public explanation at
  the time was "Some people just don't understand sarcasm or the British sense of humour" — i.e.
  she framed it as a _performance_ they misread, not as unperformed flatness. The draft picks the
  reading its argument requires over the subject's own.
- **Evidence:** packet CLM-16; S-18 (Washington Post, Slate, TheWrap, TIME).
- **Repair:** one clause giving her framing — "she called it sarcasm; the anchors heard ingratitude"
  — which preserves the point (the market wanted legible affect on cue) while stopping the draft
  from overwriting her account with a better-fitting one.
- **Benefit:** the incident survives as evidence _and_ the article stops doing to her, in miniature,
  what it accuses the industry of doing.
- **Confidence:** high. **Acceptance test:** her "sarcasm" framing appears; no sentence asserts her
  affect was unperformed.

**CRITIC-C3 — The article's one genuine act of self-criticism rests on an internal citation that does not check out.**

- **Passage:** "The 9takes profile of the wings calls 4w3 'The Aristocrat,' and names its specific
  shadow: performing authenticity rather than embodying it, crafting an image of depth." The
  album-section skeptic beat then explicitly invokes it: "The Rabbit Hole above names the 4w3 shadow
  as performing authenticity rather than embodying it."
- **Problem:** the linked page calls 4w3 "The Aristocrat" but contains none of the quoted shadow
  wording; the closest actual text is that 4w3s "brand their authenticity." A reader who follows the
  hyperlink — which the sentence invites — finds different words. The beat I rated the best thing in
  the piece (CRITIC-H2) is load-bearing on a citation that fails.
- **Evidence:** packet CLM-12, verified by grep against
  `src/blog/enneagram/enneagram-wings-complete-guide.md`; the phrase appears in the corpus only in
  the Lady Gaga draft, as an author formulation.
- **Repair:** quote the page's real wording ("brand their authenticity") and stop attributing the
  rest to it, or attribute the formulation to the article itself. Note "brand their authenticity" is
  _better_ for this subject than the invented phrasing.
- **Benefit:** the skeptic beat becomes unfalsifiable-by-hyperlink, and gets sharper.
- **Confidence:** high. **Acceptance test:** every phrase attributed to the wings page appears on
  that page.

**CRITIC-C4 — Doubt is structurally decorative: it is raised, dissolved in place, and never given the last word.**

- **Passage:** section five's arc (concede → "The sneer overstates itself in two places" → "Now the
  part the sneer misses" → full reframe), followed by section six's unresolved beat, followed by
  section seven's redemptive close ("This is the first one she wrote").
- **Problem:** every hostile fact is answered inside the section that raises it. The one objection
  left standing is aimed at an unlived future, then a warm section follows it. Net structure: the
  sympathetic reading holds the final position on every question the reader can currently check.
- **Evidence:** structural, verifiable by reading order; reinforced by CRITIC-R3, since the close's
  claim is itself the overclaim.
- **Repair:** cheapest version — let one unresolved thing survive into the last section. The
  unexplained title is already ambivalent; a single clause acknowledging that an unexplained title
  is also excellent marketing would keep the ending honest without deflating it.
- **Benefit:** the skepticism becomes structural rather than ornamental; the piece stops resolving
  into the redemption arc it is otherwise working hard to avoid.
- **Confidence:** medium-high — this is a compositional judgment, but it is the judgment the critic
  role exists to make.
- **Acceptance test:** at least one currently-checkable objection is left standing after the final
  section begins.

**CRITIC-C5 — A third party's addiction is used as causal machinery, sourced only to her daughter.**

- **Passage:** the section "How a house with a sick mother taught Cara Delevingne to hold it in" and
  "Delevingne's mother, Pandora, struggled with heroin addiction, depression and medication
  dependency through her daughters' childhoods."
- **Problem:** Pandora Delevingne is a living private individual whose addiction is stated as fact
  and made the causal origin of her daughter's psychology. The draft sources it entirely to Cara's
  account. Pandora has spoken on record about her own heroin addiction — the article is _better_
  supported than it lets on, and by declining to say so it leaves a real person's medical history
  resting on a relative's testimony.
- **Evidence:** packet, third-party testimony item 2, and CLM-17 — "This materially upgrades the
  draft's household claims from 'Cara's account of her mother' to 'corroborated by the mother's own
  on-record account.'"
- **Repair:** one clause noting Pandora has discussed her addiction publicly. (Outlet is Tier 4, so
  attribute to her rather than to the paper.)
- **Benefit:** upgrades the sourcing and treats the third party as a person with her own account
  rather than as a mechanism in someone else's diagnosis.
- **Confidence:** high on the existence of her account; medium on outlet quality, which is why the
  repair attributes to the speaker.
- **Acceptance test:** the household claims cite the mother's own on-record account at least once.

**CRITIC-C6 — The paragraph doing the fairness work is the least-verified paragraph in the draft.**

- **Passage:** section five — Besson "called her a 'natural born actress' and predicted an Academy
  Award"; "Reviews of the singles in Atwood, **Nylon** and When The Horn Blows"; "Nobody handed her
  the Warner contract on a blind listen."
- **Problem:** three separate sourcing failures inside the section that exists to prove the article
  is fair. Besson's quotation and the Oscar prediction were searched for and not found (packet's
  targeted pass). No _Nylon_ review of the singles exists that either the packet or my own search
  could locate. And "nobody handed her the Warner contract on a blind listen" is the draft's own
  speculation presented as a conceded fact — a concession made of invention is not a concession,
  even when it cuts against the subject.
- **Evidence:** packet CLM-23, CLM-24, and the "contested characterizations" list; my search this
  pass returned Atwood, When The Horn Blows, FEMMUSIC, RazorFine, Euphoria Zine and mxdwn, and no
  _Nylon_ review.
- **Repair:** substitute a verified outlet for _Nylon_ (FEMMUSIC or RazorFine both work); either
  re-verify Besson or reduce to the _Valerian_ result, which needs no quotation; and either source
  the Warner point or mark it as inference.
- **Benefit:** the fairness section stops being the most fragile thing on the page.
- **Confidence:** high on _Nylon_ (two independent failed searches) and on the Warner inference;
  medium-high on Besson, since absence of search evidence is not disproof.
- **Acceptance test:** every named outlet in the paragraph maps to a retrievable item; no
  unsourced inference is presented as a concession of fact.

## Specific improvements

These are upgrades, not repairs to red flags.

**CRITIC-I1 — Give the Seven case its evidentiary steelman, not just its behavioural one.**

- **Location:** the pressure-test paragraph in the diagnosis section, and the counterargument
  section in the Rabbit Hole.
- **Current state:** the Seven case is presented as a list of behaviours (raves, festivals, Burning
  Man, four careers) — the weakest form of it, and the one easiest to answer.
- **Reasoning:** the genuinely hard version is about provenance. Most of the draft's Four evidence
  comes from a single third-party podcast transcript; the Seven case's best line comes from a Vogue
  cover story. A critic reading closely notices that the article's own evidence is weaker-sourced
  than its opponent's and that the article never says so.
- **Repair:** two sentences in the counterargument section acknowledging the asymmetry and stating
  why the transcript material is still credited (it is extensive, consistent across years, and three
  quotations are independently corroborated).
- **Benefit:** converts the article's biggest hidden vulnerability into a demonstration of rigor.
- **Confidence:** high. **Acceptance test:** the counterargument section names the sourcing
  asymmetry and answers it.

**CRITIC-I2 — Let the piece acknowledge cost to someone other than Cara.**

- **Location:** anywhere in the household or recovery sections.
- **Current state:** harm flows exclusively toward the subject. Two decades of substance use appear
  only as her suffering.
- **Reasoning:** the critic contract's impact-on-others test. The article is not obliged to
  prosecute her, but a profile in which the subject is only ever acted upon reads as advocacy. Her
  own record supplies the material — she has said "I couldn't love, I couldn't receive love," which
  the draft already quotes and uses only as a setup for the love song.
- **Repair:** let that quotation land as cost before it is redeemed, rather than as a runway to the
  grand gesture.
- **Benefit:** one sentence buys the piece its moral seriousness back at no factual risk.
- **Confidence:** medium-high. **Acceptance test:** at least one passage registers effect on another
  person that is not immediately resolved.

**CRITIC-I3 — Reconcile the conflict in her own record about when the depression began.**

- **Location:** the household section ("had her breakdown at 15") and the somatic table's age-15 row.
- **Current state:** 15 is stated flatly. She has elsewhere said "I think I properly started dealing
  with depression when I was about 16."
- **Reasoning:** minor on its own, but the draft's argument depends heavily on her self-reports being
  reliable. Acknowledging one place they disagree strengthens the rest.
- **Repair:** a parenthetical, or use the breakdown date without also claiming it dates the onset of
  sustained depression.
- **Benefit:** pre-empts the obvious "her memory is inconsistent" objection by raising it first.
- **Confidence:** medium — the packet flags it but does not date the Esquire piece.
- **Acceptance test:** the draft either notes the conflict or drops the onset-dating claim.

## Follow-on questions

**CRITIC-Q1 — Were all of the twelve months' fractures deliberately sought, or only the elbow?**
