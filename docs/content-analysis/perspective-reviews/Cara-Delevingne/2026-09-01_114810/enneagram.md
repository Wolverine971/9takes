## Bottom-line verdict

The Type 4 call is defensible and, in places, unusually well argued. The draft does the thing most celebrity typings never do: it names a motivation, tests it against the strongest rival reading, and states a falsifier. The 4w3 alignment — a literal aristocrat whose commercial product was legible spontaneity — is the sharpest wing argument I have seen in this corpus, and the "four careers as one request for a voice" reframe is a real insight rather than a restatement of the type description.

What strains trust is the theory craft around that thesis, not the thesis. Three things break: the draft asserts the sexual Four is the counter-type when the counter-type of Four is the self-preservation Four (verifiable against Chestnut's own academy); it attributes a 4w3 shadow definition to a hyperlinked 9takes page that does not contain it, then builds the TL;DR and the entire final-act skeptic beat on that fabricated definition; and it rests the tie-breaker that decides the type on a self-harm-adjacent episode which it reframes as involuntary somatic expression, omitting the context the source supplies.

The third is the one worth dwelling on, because it is not merely a handling problem. The Enneagram is a motivational system. Involuntary bodily events carry no motive, so they cannot discriminate between types. The draft's proudest structural invention — the somatic ledger — is therefore its least type-relevant evidence, and the one row that _does_ carry motive is the row it mis-frames. Fixing that framing makes the type argument stronger, not weaker. That is the highest-leverage revision available here.

None of this invalidates Type 4. Every repair is available from material already in hand, including 9takes' own addiction guide, which supplies a more accurate framing than the one the draft chose. Revise.

## What landed

**ENN-H1 — The wing argument.** L190: "Delevingne is a literal aristocrat whose entire commercial value for a decade was performed authenticity, and who has since said the performance was hollow. The wing does not just fit her. It describes the exact trap she fell into."

This is the best type-to-person mapping in the piece. Most wing sections are decoration; this one does argumentative work, because the coincidence between the label ("The Aristocrat") and the subject's literal social position is not a pun — the draft uses it to explain the specific _shape_ of her problem. Must survive revision. (The sourcing of the shadow definition must change; see ENN-R2. The argument itself does not.)

**ENN-H2 — Type theory kept out of the narrative.** The distribution ledger claims two type-theory paragraphs outside the diagnosis and Rabbit Hole, and that checks out against the body. The Rabbit Hole is properly quarantined behind a `<details>` with an explicit "skip if you're not deep into the system" note.

This is correct architecture for a typology piece and it is rarer than it should be. The article never asks a general reader to learn vocabulary to follow the argument, and never rewards jargon density. Preserve the ratio exactly; the repairs below should not be allowed to leak theory into the main body.

**ENN-H3 — A stated falsifier.** L212–214: "What would change the call: sustained on-record framing of her own motive as escaping limitation or boredom rather than as absence of voice. That framing is not in the record."

This is the single most important sentence in the piece from my standpoint. It converts a type claim from an unfalsifiable vibe into something that could be wrong. Almost no celebrity Enneagram content does this. Keep the shape verbatim even if the wording changes.

**ENN-H4 — The empathy turn is built from her vocabulary, not house grammar.** L229–233: the voice ladder (modelling took her voice, acting gave her a screenwriter's, theatre gave her Sally Bowles's, music is the first where the words are hers), closing on "the four careers are one request, refused three times and repeated anyway."

This is the piece's non-obvious insight and it is person-first: it explains behaviour that reads as dilettantism through a motive she has stated in her own word ("voice"), without requiring the reader to accept the Enneagram at all. It also does something the type description alone could never do. Must survive intact.

## What missed

**ENN-M1 — The somatic ledger promises motivational evidence and delivers mostly involuntary events.** The section is titled "What Cara Delevingne's body kept saying for her" and L175 asserts "Her body had been filing reports for twenty-three years." Of the six rows, four are involuntary or ambiguous as to motive (hunger strike at 7, sleeping in Poppy's bed, the breakdown at 15, psoriasis) and one is other people's behaviour (the paparazzi images). Only the fractures row contains a stated motive, and that is the row whose context the draft omits.

The promised effect is "here is the evidence that decides the type." The delivered effect, for a reader who thinks in motivations, is a moving but type-neutral trauma inventory. Psoriasis flaring under stress is true of Fours, Sevens, Ones and everyone else. This is not taste: the section is load-bearing for the thesis and it does not carry the weight the draft assigns it.

**ENN-M2 — The stress arrow is illustrated with evidence that argues for the rival type.** L200: "Under stress the Four moves toward Type 2: clinging, self-abandoning, earning a place by being useful to someone else... Being the funny one. Being the one who makes it fun."

Being the one who makes it fun is a Seven signature. The draft spends its diagnosis section arguing that the gregarious, entertaining public affect is _not_ the real Delevingne, then cites exactly that affect as proof of a Four's stress movement. A reader tracking the argument gets whiplash, and a reader sympathetic to the crowd's Seven read gets handed free ammunition inside the section meant to close the case.

## What I expected

**Serious treatment of the nearest alternative — which is not the one the draft tests.** The draft pressure-tests Type 7 thoroughly, twice (diagnosis L114–120, Rabbit Hole L206–214). That is the crowd's answer, so testing it is correct. But the nearest alternative to a _4w3_ — image-literate, commercially fluent, output-driven, a decade spent embodying a marketed persona — is Type 3, and the packet records a competing 3w4 vote at Sakinorva. The 3/4w3 boundary is the single most common mistyping in this territory. The draft never mentions Type 3. See ENN-C5.

**A statement of what Type 4 does not explain.** My contract asks for it and good typology requires it. The Rabbit Hole names two weaknesses in the _Seven_ case but never names a weakness in the _Four_ case beyond the falsifier. The repository research reportedly conceded that the volume and duration of the substance use is "genuinely gluttonous in the Type 7 sense" (packet, Enneagram hypothesis evidence). That concession never reaches the page in that form; it is absorbed by the layering claim at L120 instead. See ENN-C4.

**Vote counts, or no consensus claim.** L87 and L206 assert a six-site verdict. The packet verified three sites, and found Sakinorva's 7w6 resting on two votes with a competing 3w4. See ENN-C3.

## What surprised me

**Welcome:** L118's tie-breaker logic is genuinely good typology, independent of its sourcing problem. "A Seven's relationship to pain is exit. Delevingne collected casts" is the right _kind_ of argument — it discriminates on motive rather than on behaviour, which is exactly what type-differentiation requires and what crowd databases never do. The draft understands the method even where it misapplies the evidence.

**Welcome:** L108's rendering of the Four's desire as wanting "to be seen accurately" is a tighter, more accessible formulation than the house pillar's own ("depth is currency and authenticity is survival," `enneagram-type-4.md` L60) and it earns its keep because the whole billboard thesis turns on accuracy of perception. I checked whether it distorted the type and concluded it does not.

**Jarring:** L194 pairs a correct Naranjo citation ("competition") with an incorrect one ("the sexual Four is the counter-type") in the same three sentences, and correctly describes SP4 as suffering quietly — which is the counter-type behaviour — immediately before assigning the counter-type label to a different subtype. The error is not ignorance of the literature; it is a label attached to the wrong subtype while the surrounding descriptions stay accurate. That is the kind of slip an expert reader catches instantly and a general reader never sees.

## Red flags

### ENN-R1 — BLOCKER — The sexual Four is not the counter-type; the self-preservation Four is

- **Exact passage:** L194: "The sexual Four is the counter-type, and it is the reason the crowd databases miss her. Where the self-preservation Four suffers quietly and the social Four compares itself into misery, the sx Four gets loud, competitive and angry. Naranjo's word for it is 'competition.'"
- **Reader effect / trust problem:** This sits inside the section explicitly addressed to "the Enneagram nerds" — the one audience guaranteed to know the answer. The article's entire authority claim is that it is more rigorous than crowd-vote databases; a checkable theory error in the expert section inverts that claim. Worse, the linked 9takes subtypes page itself defines countertype (L371–372 of `enneagram-instinctual-subtypes.md`, using SP6 as the example), so a reader who follows the link gets a definition that contradicts the sentence that sent them there.
- **Evidence:** In the Naranjo/Chestnut framework the Four's three subtypes are SP "Tenacity," SO "Shame," SX "Competition," and the **self-preservation** Four is the countertype — stoic, long-suffering, and notably _not_ displaying inner pain. Confirmed against two independent sources, including Beatrice Chestnut and Uranio Paes's own CP Enneagram Academy subtype guide, which states the SP Four "is explicitly identified as the countertype." The draft's "competition" attribution is correct; only the counter-type assignment is wrong. Note the internal irony: the draft's own description, "the self-preservation Four suffers quietly," _is_ the counter-type description.
- **Minimum viable repair:** Delete the counter-type label and keep the argument, which does not depend on it. Replace with the accurate distinction — the sx Four expresses envy as competition and anger rather than as withdrawal or shame, which is why she scans as brash. If the counter-type concept is wanted, assign it correctly to SP4 and note that she is not that subtype.
- **Expected benefit:** Removes the only checkable theory error in the piece and costs nothing argumentatively; the "why the crowd misreads her" claim survives verbatim.
- **Confidence:** High. Two sources, one of them the originating authority's own academy.
- **Acceptance test:** `grep -n "counter-type" src/blog/people/drafts/Cara-Delevingne.md` returns either zero hits, or a hit in which the term is applied to the self-preservation Four. No sentence assigns counter-type status to the sexual Four.

### ENN-R2 — BLOCKER — A 4w3 shadow definition is attributed to a linked 9takes page that does not contain it, and three passages are built on it

- **Exact passage:** L190: "The 9takes profile of the <a href="/enneagram-corner/enneagram-wings-complete-guide">wings</a> calls 4w3 'The Aristocrat,' and names its specific shadow: performing authenticity rather than embodying it, crafting an image of depth." Propagates to L95 (TL;DR: "The 4w3 shadow is performing authenticity") and L261 (the final-act skeptic beat: "The Rabbit Hole above names the 4w3 shadow as performing authenticity rather than embodying it").
- **Reader effect / trust problem:** The draft names an internal source, hyperlinks it, and quotes a definition it does not contain. A reader who clicks — the engaged reader the Rabbit Hole is written for — finds different words. This is a fabricated citation, and it is not decorative: it carries the wing argument, the TL;DR summary line, and the entire final-act tension that the second pass added as its headline upgrade.
- **Evidence:** Verified directly against `src/blog/enneagram/enneagram-wings-complete-guide.md`. The page does call 4w3 "The Aristocrat" (L50, L274) — that half is correct. It does **not** contain "performing authenticity," "embodying," or "image of depth"; grep returns zero hits. Its actual wording (L282): 4w3s "turn personal struggles into art, **brand their authenticity**, and mentally rehearse vulnerable moments before sharing them. Under stress, they get competitive about being special and anxious that their achievements might make them ordinary." Independently corroborates the packet's CLM-12.
- **Minimum viable repair:** Quote the page's real language. "Brand their authenticity" is _stronger_ for this subject than the invented phrase — she is a woman who literally sold authenticity as a branded product — and "mentally rehearse vulnerable moments before sharing them" is a gift for the final-act beat about a record sold on rawness. Swap the phrasing at L190 and update the two downstream references at L95 and L261 to match. Alternatively, keep the author's formulation but stop attributing it to the page.
- **Expected benefit:** Removes a false citation while upgrading the prose; the wing argument and the final-act skeptic beat both get sharper source material than they currently claim.
- **Confidence:** High. Direct file verification, zero grep hits.
- **Acceptance test:** Every phrase the draft presents as the wings guide's wording returns a hit in `grep -o` against `src/blog/enneagram/enneagram-wings-complete-guide.md`. L95 and L261 use the same wording as L190.

### ENN-R3 — BLOCKER — The tie-breaker that decides the type rests on self-harm reframed as involuntary somatic expression

- **Exact passage:** L118: "Delevingne collected casts. She wanted a visible token she could point at, something a stranger could read across a room, because the injury she actually had was not producing any evidence. That is a bid to be witnessed, and it is the single most Four thing in the entire record." Framing established at the hook (L75, "broke so many bones in one year that she stopped counting them"), the somatic-ledger premise (L160, "something she could not put into words came out through her body instead"), and L175 ("Her body had been filing reports for twenty-three years").
- **Reader effect / trust problem:** Two distinct failures. (a) _Typology:_ the Enneagram discriminates on motive. An involuntary bodily event has no motive in it, so framing the fractures as the body speaking _without_ her strips out the very thing that makes them type-evidence — then the draft calls this stripped-down version "the single most Four thing in the entire record." The tie-breaker is weaker than the source permits. (b) _Handling:_ the source context is a self-harm discussion, and the draft never uses the term, presenting sought injury as spontaneous somatic symptom. Self-harm is also transdiagnostic — it occurs across all nine types — so as written it cannot bear the discriminating weight assigned to it.
- **Evidence:** Per the packet (CLM-01, S-06), Daily Front Row situates this _Call Her Daddy_ passage inside a discussion of self-harm, quoting her: "I was in pain inside, but I didn't know how to express it. **So I just wanted to feel pain**," and describing a visible injury as something that let someone "validate you and be like, 'Oh, you must be in so much pain.'" Her actual words are "I broke… I don't know how many bones in like a year"; "stopped counting" is the draft's inference. Independently relevant and apparently unnoticed by the pipeline: 9takes' own `enneagram-addiction-recovery-guide.md` L241 lists **"Self-harm behaviors (turning internal pain external)"** as a primary Type 4 escape route — the house material already supplies an accurate, on-doctrine framing for exactly this material.
- **Minimum viable repair:** Stop describing this row as involuntary. Rest the tie-breaker on the motive she actually states — she wanted a legible token so someone would witness pain she could not articulate — which is _better_ Four evidence than somatic involuntariness, because it is a motive and it maps onto the article's own definition of the Four at L108 (wanting to be seen accurately). Attribute strictly to her account, name the self-harm context in her words rather than narrating around it, and either drop "stopped counting" or replace it with her phrasing. Separately, either retitle the ledger section so it does not promise the body spoke _for_ her across all six rows, or mark the fractures row as the one carrying stated motive.
- **Expected benefit:** Strengthens the type argument rather than weakening it, brings the handling of self-reported mental-health material up to the standard the entity-gap packet requires, and removes a framing the source contradicts.
- **Confidence:** Medium-high on the framing problem (packet-sourced, single Tier 3 outlet's characterization; full audio would settle it). High on the typology point, which holds regardless of how the audio reads: involuntary events do not discriminate type.
- **Acceptance test:** No sentence asserts the fractures were involuntary or that her body acted without her. The tie-breaker paragraph cites a motive she states. The word "self-harm" or her own "I just wanted to feel pain" appears in her attributed voice. `grep -n "stopped counting"` returns zero hits unless supported by a direct quotation.

## Specific improvements

### ENN-C1 — CONCERN — The sequencing claim is unfalsifiable and unnecessary

- **Location:** L120: "The escape layer is real. It sits on top of an identity problem, and the identity problem came first."
- **Problem:** A developmental-order claim about a living person's psyche that no source establishes, and the packet lists as unresolved question #3. It is also the classic move that lets a type claim explain everything: any Seven-shaped evidence gets absorbed by asserting that something Four-shaped preceded it. An Enneagram-literate reader recognises the move and discounts the argument. It is doubly exposed because her best-sourced statement of motive (Vogue 2023, "that's all I ever wanted to do: just pretend they don't exist") is the escape framing, from a stronger source than most of the identity framing.
- **Minimum viable repair:** Argue function, not chronology — the escape serves the identity problem rather than preceding it. Type theory does not require the ordering claim, so dropping it costs nothing. One sentence: what she was escaping _from_ is described, by her, in identity terms.
- **Expected benefit:** Removes an unsupported assertion and an unfalsifiable structure while keeping the argument's actual content.
- **Confidence:** High on the sourcing gap; high that the claim is dispensable.
- **Acceptance test:** No sentence asserts temporal priority between the escape motive and the identity problem. The relationship is stated as functional.

### ENN-C2 — CONCERN — The 4→2 stress evidence argues for Seven and does not match the arrow described

- **Location:** L200: "Under stress the Four moves toward Type 2: clinging, self-abandoning, earning a place by being useful to someone else. Delevingne's account of her worst years is full of it. Being the funny one. Being the one who makes it fun. 'I was so good at hiding it.'"
- **Problem:** The arrow is stated correctly (4→2 under stress is standard), but the evidence does not fit it. "I was so good at hiding it" is concealment, not clinging or self-abandonment-through-usefulness. "Being the one who makes it fun" is a Seven signature, cited inside the section meant to defeat the Seven read — and the diagnosis section has already argued that this exact affect is the misleading surface.
- **Minimum viable repair:** Either replace the illustration with evidence of the actual 2-move (self-abandonment inside relationships, earning a place by being needed — the Minke material and "I couldn't love, I couldn't receive love" are closer), or state plainly that the entertainer role was the cover and the 2-move is visible elsewhere. If neither is available in the record, cut the arrow rather than illustrate it badly.
- **Expected benefit:** Stops the piece from handing its own rival reading a data point, and makes the arrow claim answerable to evidence.
- **Confidence:** High that the cited evidence does not demonstrate a 4→2 movement.
- **Acceptance test:** The stress-arrow paragraph contains no behaviour the draft elsewhere characterises as the Seven-shaped public affect.

### ENN-C3 — CONCERN — The consensus the article overturns is thinner than stated, and the honest version is more persuasive

- **Location:** L87 ("Six different sites, roughly the same verdict"), L206 ("Six crowd-typing sites landed on Seven"), title and `meta_title`. Also L196: "almost every crowd-vote page reads her as a Seven or an Eight."
- **Problem:** The article's premise is overturning an established internet verdict. Per the packet (CLM-14), three sites were confirmed; Sakinorva's 7w6 rests on **two votes** with a competing 3w4; PDB reportedly carries three competing profiles. The draft never discloses vote thinness. Separately, no Eight reading is verified anywhere in the packet, so "a Seven or an Eight" appears to be unsupported. For a reader who checks — again, the Rabbit Hole audience — "six sites" overstates a real but soft signal.
- **Minimum viable repair:** Either verify the remaining three sites (the packet says this is a few minutes' work) or soften to what is confirmed. Better: disclose the thinness. "The crowd verdict is Seven, and it rests on a handful of votes" is a _stronger_ opening than a false consensus, because it reframes the piece as supplying evidence where none existed rather than as picking a fight. Drop "or an Eight" unless a source is found.
- **Expected benefit:** Protects the article's central premise from its most easily checked weakness, and converts an overstatement into a sharper positioning claim.
- **Confidence:** High, inherited from packet verification (S-23).
- **Acceptance test:** Every crowd-database count in the draft matches a verified list, or the count is replaced by a non-numeric characterization. No Eight claim without a source.

### ENN-C4 — CONCERN — The draft never states what Type 4 fails to explain

- **Location:** Rabbit Hole counterargument section, L206–214.
- **Problem:** The section names two weaknesses in the _Seven_ case and one falsifier for the Four, which is more than most typings offer — but it never concedes anything the Four reading cannot account for. The repository research reportedly did concede one: the sheer volume and duration of the substance use and the appetite for stimulation read as "genuinely gluttonous in the Type 7 sense." That concession is absorbed by the layering claim at L120 rather than stated. A type that explains everything explains nothing, and the section is one sentence away from being airtight in the good sense.
- **Minimum viable repair:** Add one sentence naming the residue: the scale and duration of the using years is the part the Four reading accounts for least well, and the Seven reading accounts for it more economically.
- **Expected benefit:** Converts the counterargument section from "here is why the rival is wrong" to "here is what my own answer costs," which is the credibility move the section is already reaching for at L212.
- **Confidence:** High that the omission exists; medium on the exact wording of the research concession, which I did not read directly (packet-reported).
- **Acceptance test:** The counterargument section contains at least one explicit statement of behaviour the Type 4 reading does not explain well.

### ENN-C5 — CONCERN — The nearest alternative to 4w3 is Type 3, and it is never tested

- **Location:** Absent throughout; the alternative-type work at L114–120 and L206–214 addresses only Type 7.
- **Problem:** Testing Type 7 is correct — it is the crowd's answer. But the draft's own case is for a **4w3**: image-literate, commercially fluent, output-driven, a decade spent embodying a marketed persona, four public careers, a documented capacity to ship. That profile's nearest neighbour is Type 3, not Type 7, and the 3/4w3 boundary is the most common mistyping in this territory. The packet records a competing **3w4** vote at Sakinorva, so the alternative is present even in the crowd data the article is answering. A reader who knows the system will finish the Rabbit Hole and immediately ask why 3 was not considered — and the draft has an unusually good answer available that it never gives.
- **Minimum viable repair:** Two sentences in the counterargument section. The available answer: a Three identifies with the successful image, while she describes the image as alien and disbelieved ("it wasn't me"; "I don't believe what I'm selling"), and she repeatedly torched market position rather than optimise it — L237's observation that "every switch costs her credibility and she keeps making them" is already the argument, sitting in the wrong section.
- **Expected benefit:** Closes the most obvious remaining objection for the informed reader, and it is nearly free — the evidence is already written elsewhere in the piece.
- **Confidence:** High that Type 3 is the untested near neighbour; high that the draft already contains the disconfirming evidence.
- **Acceptance test:** The counterargument section names Type 3, states what would favour it, and gives at least one piece of on-record evidence against it.
