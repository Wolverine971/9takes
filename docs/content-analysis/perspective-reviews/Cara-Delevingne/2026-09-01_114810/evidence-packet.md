## Identity and scope

model, actress and, as of 2026, recording artist. Daughter of Charles Hamar Delevingne and Pandora
Stevens Delevingne; sisters Chloe and Poppy Delevingne (also a model, and a recurring
misattribution risk — see Research limitations); paternal half-brother Alexander Jaffe. Raised in
Belgravia, London. Educated at Bedales School, Hampshire, 2003–2009. She was 34 at the draft's
`date:` of 2026-09-01. (Wikipedia; entity-gap packet S-02.)

scores her **37/100, classification `pass`, action `create`** — explicitly _not_ an emerging entity
gap. Her general-biography SERP is owned by Wikipedia, IMDb (plus a dedicated IMDb biography page),

## First-person evidence

### _Call Her Daddy_, 3 June 2026 — the load-bearing source

The episode is real, dated, and widely covered. Repository quotations come from the Singju Post
transcript. Corroboration status is marked per quote.

**Independently corroborated in this pass:**

- On the industry and her sexuality — **verbatim match** to the show's own publisher:
  "But also in that point, the industry didn't want me to be gay either. And when I just started
  acting where like Harvey Weinstein called me and was like, 'You can't be gay.' It's just stuff
  like that where I, again, there were also no real gay models." (SiriusXM blog, S-05.)
  _What it supports:_ that she says the industry pressured her to stay closeted, and that she
  attributes a specific instruction to Weinstein. _What it cannot support:_ that the exchange

## Enneagram hypothesis evidence

Kept deliberately separate from theory conclusions. Nothing here establishes a type.

### Evidence advanced for Type 4

- **Identity-framed self-description, repeatedly.** "I didn't feel like I belonged in my body, in my
  family, in any of it." "When I'd see a billboard of myself, it wasn't me." "It wasn't me. Like, it
  was me, but… what? It didn't make sense." _Attributed claims (self-report), single-sourced to the
  CHD transcript except where noted above._
- **The cast episode as a bid to be witnessed.** Corroborated quotation. The draft treats this as
  the tie-breaker. See the discriminating-evidence caveat below — this is the packet's most
  important contested item.
- **4w3 fit.** She is a literal aristocrat; her commercial product for a decade was legible
  spontaneity; she has since disowned it. _Interpretation._ Note that the 9takes wings guide does
  label 4w3 "The Aristocrat" (verified against the repo file) but does **not** contain the shadow
  wording the draft attributes to it — CLM-12.
- **Output is identity-preoccupied.** Album themes reported as "fear and pain… conflict about doing
  right or wrong… rediscovering sexuality and abusive relationships." _Attributed claim_ (press
  framing, not her words).

### Evidence advanced against Type 4 / for Type 7

- **Her own stated motive for using is escape, in Seven vocabulary.** "I loved not having to think
  about my mum… all of that, it went away." "You can't run from the things in your life that happen,
  and that's all I ever wanted to do: just pretend they don't exist." The second is from Vogue 2023
  — a **separate, better-sourced interview** than the CHD transcript, which makes it harder to
  discount than the draft's pro-4 quotations.
- **Sustained stimulation appetite** across a decade: raves, festivals, Burning Man, breaking into
  events.
- **Career breadth** reads as option-keeping on the résumé alone.
- **A decade of gregarious, comic public affect** — the basis of the crowd verdict.
- **Behaviour the Type 4 hypothesis does not explain** (the repository research concedes this
  explicitly): the volume and duration of the substance use and the appetite for stimulation are
  "genuinely gluttonous in the Type 7 sense." The draft handles this by conceding an "escape layer"
  sitting on top of an identity problem — a sequencing claim (_"the identity problem came first"_)
  for which no source is offered. **Unresolved question:** nothing in the record establishes the
  order.

### Closest alternative

**Type 7 (crowd consensus 7w6).** Verified as the crowd reading at Personality Database (7w6) and
Boo (Type 7); Sakinorva lists 7w6 — **on two votes.** See CLM-14 on the strength of that
"consensus."

### Discriminating evidence — and the problem with it

The draft's tie-breaker is: "A Seven's relationship to pain is exit. Delevingne collected casts."

**Unresolved question / disputed framing.** Contemporaneous coverage of the same _Call Her Daddy_
passage (Daily Front Row) situates the broken-bones material **within a discussion of self-harm**,
quoting her as saying "So I just wanted to feel pain." If the fractures were sought rather than
sustained, then:

- the behaviour is still not Seven-shaped, so the tie-breaker's _direction_ survives; but
- the draft's framing — that her **body** produced involuntary evidence, "filing reports," a
  somatic ledger of things she "could not put into words" coming out "through her body instead" —
  is doing different work than the source supports for at least this row. The draft's single
  hedge is the parenthetical "involuntary or otherwise" in the research file, which does not
  reach the page.

This is the single most consequential evidence question in the packet. It bears on accuracy, on
the tie-breaker that carries the type argument, and on the handling of self-reported
mental-health material that the entity-gap packet flags as requiring strict attribution.

### The subtype claim

The draft asserts the **sexual (sx) Four** as "the counter-type… loud, competitive and angry," and
that "Naranjo's word for it is 'competition.'"

- The Naranjo/Chestnut label "competition" for the sexual Four is standard in the external Enneagram
  literature — _attributed claim, Tier 3, consistent with the field._
- **But the 9takes page the draft links in that section** (`/enneagram-corner/enneagram-instinctual-subtypes`)
  describes SX 4 entirely differently: "Craves intense, romantic relationships," "dramatic mood
  swings tied to a sense of longing," "Idealizes or deeply invests in partners." It contains no
  "counter-type," no "competition," and no Naranjo reference. Verified against the repo file. See
  CLM-13.

### Stress and growth arrows

_Interpretation throughout._ The draft's 4→2 stress reading rests on "I was so good at hiding it"
and being "the funny one"; the 4→1 growth reading rests on 12-step structure and "you're not
invincible." Both are plausible readings of real quotations; neither is established by any source
as a type-motion.

## Disputes and unresolved questions

1. **Were the fractures sustained or sought?** The most consequential open question in the file.
   Contemporaneous coverage places the passage in a self-harm discussion ("So I just wanted to feel
   pain"); the draft renders it as involuntary somatic expression. The full audio would settle it.
   Until it does, conclusions built on "her body kept saying it for her" must stay qualified, and
   the type tie-breaker inherits that qualification.

2. **Is the tour still running?** All published dates end 26 June 2026. No autumn dates found on
   Songkick, Bandsintown or Ticketmaster. Either the "six months" claim is wrong, or dates exist
   that four aggregators and the announcement coverage do not list. The former is far more likely.

3. **Did the identity problem precede the escape motive?** The draft asserts sequencing ("The escape
   layer is real. It sits on top of an identity problem, and the identity problem came first").
   Nothing in the record establishes order. Her best-sourced statement of motive — Vogue 2023,
   "that's all I ever wanted to do: just pretend they don't exist" — is the escape framing, from a
   better source than most of the identity framing.

4. **How strong is the "Type 7 consensus" actually?** Sakinorva: two votes. PDB: multiple competing
   profiles per the entity-gap packet. The article's premise is that it is overturning an
   established internet verdict; the verdict may be thinner than the premise needs.

5. **Verbatim fidelity of the CHD quotations.** Three corroborated, the rest transcript-only,
   including the thesis sentence (CLM-02). No audio was accessed in this pass.

6. **Her own record conflicts on when depression began** — "breakdown at 15" (CHD 2026) vs. "I think
   I properly started dealing with depression when I was about 16" (Esquire). The draft uses 15
   without noting the other.

7. **Playboy, summer 2026** (published 14 July 2026, on sex and intimacy in sobriety) — named in the
   entity-gap packet, never retrieved, still unused. It is the largest untouched first-person source
   and could bear directly on the sx-subtype argument.

8. **Whether the 9takes wings/subtypes pages should be corrected, or the draft should be** — CLM-12
   and CLM-13 are internal-consistency failures that could be resolved from either direction. Not
   this packet's call.
