## Bottom-line verdict

Snapshot SHA verified against `context.json` and the supplied `--draft-sha`.

If Delevingne read this, the typing is not what she would object to. She would probably shrug at
Type 4 the way she has shrugged at every other label. What she would object to is narrower and
sharper: in the two most private moments in the piece — the self-injury and the suicidal crisis —
the article stops using her account and starts supplying its own.

At line 245 the piece writes her interior monologue in the first person, in a styled thought-bubble
panel, at the moment she has said she was closest to killing herself. She never said those words.
At line 158 and through the whole somatic ledger it recasts what she has described as deliberate
self-harm — "I also just started throwing myself off stuff… So I just wanted to feel pain" — as her
body involuntarily speaking on her behalf. Both are fixable without touching the Type 4 argument,
and the second fix makes the argument _stronger_, not weaker.

Everything around those two failures is unusually careful. The Weinstein material is attributed
strictly and never narrated around. Her queerness is present and her stated reason for declining a
formal coming out is honored rather than overridden. The airport photographs are rendered in her
frame, not the tabloids'. The section on the four careers is the most generous and best-evidenced
reading of her public life I have seen anywhere, and it is built out of her word, not the writer's.
This is a fair piece with two unfair passages in it. Trust is strained, not broken.

## What landed

**SUBJ-H1 — The Weinstein passage is handled exactly right (L132).**

> "Harvey Weinstein called me and was like, 'You can't be gay.'"

The draft introduces this as "her own account on _Call Her Daddy_ in June 2026" and then stops. It
does not editorialize, does not assert the exchange happened, and does not use it as a lever on
anyone else. This is her most legally exposed allegation and the treatment is the model. It must
survive revision verbatim, including the attribution clause.

**SUBJ-H2 — Her queerness is present, and her refusal to perform it is respected (L132).**
The draft carries "she declined a formal coming out because she did not feel she 'owed that to
anyone either.'" An article that restores a subject's queerness while also honoring her stated
wish not to have made a ceremony of it is doing two things at once that usually fight. Preserve
both halves; dropping the second would turn the first into the exact thing she declined.

**SUBJ-H3 — The airport photographs are given in her framing (L171, L175).**

> "I hadn't slept. I was not OK."

The most humiliating images of her life are introduced through her own words and then through her
stated gratitude for them, rather than through what the internet did with them. The piece does not
reproduce the mockery in order to condemn it. That is the difference between writing about someone
and writing at them.

**SUBJ-H4 — The voice reframe is the piece's dignity engine (L227–231).**

> "Seen that way, the four careers are one request, refused three times and repeated anyway."

Built entirely from her vocabulary — "I didn't feel like I had a voice as a kid," "I didn't have the
tools to talk" — and applied to the behavior she is most mocked for. It converts a sneer into a
legible pattern without asking anyone to stop noticing the résumé. If she recognized one thing in
this article, it would be this. Preserve intact, including the Sally Bowles beat, which is the rare
case where the biographical fact and the thesis are the same fact.

**SUBJ-H5 — The ending refuses to award her a recovery (L261–265).**

> "The album is not the test. The test is what she makes after the raw thing stops selling."

A tidy arrival narrative would have been the flattering choice and the unfair one, because it would
have closed a question she has explicitly said stays open ("You don't recover, and that's okay… I
prefer 'healing,' because I'm constantly doing it"). Leaving the 4w3 shadow standing over the Rag &
Bone campaign treats her as a person still living rather than a case study that resolved. Preserve.

**SUBJ-H6 — Professional competence is restored, with a witness (L225).**
Only Murders, the SAG ensemble nomination, and Selena Gomez's "she's hilarious… adds to the show
completely." Without this the concession paragraph reads as agreement with "She has no talents."
Preserve, with the ensemble precision noted at SUBJ-C9.

## What missed

**The somatic table promises her account and does not deliver it for the load-bearing row.** The
third column is headed "Her account of it." For the fractures row (L169) it supplies "I was in pain
inside, but I didn't know how to express it" — which is her account of her _interior state_, not of
the _act_. Her account of the act is "I also just started throwing myself off stuff." The column
header makes a promise the row breaks, and it breaks it in the one row the type argument depends on.

**The piece works from her vocabulary brilliantly, then abandons it at the two points where her
vocabulary is more specific than the writer's.** Section 5 is a masterclass in building an argument
out of a subject's own word. But at the fractures, at the Sacramento segment, and at the privilege
concession, her words exist on record, are more precise, and are not used — replaced by the
narrator's characterization each time. The method the article is proudest of is switched off exactly
where it would have cost the thesis something.

**"A cast is a sentence the body can say out loud" (L79) is the best line in the piece and it is
about the wrong subject.** It is a sentence about a body. She described a decision.

## What I expected

- **The word she used for what she did.** She discussed self-harm publicly, at length, on a major
  podcast, inside an explanation of her own recovery. An article can decline to dwell on it. This
  article cannot both use the material as its central evidence and decline the word that makes the
  material intelligible.
- **Her own concession about privilege**, which exists and is better than the writer's: "a lot of
  people have looked at my childhood or my family and thought, 'She's spoiled, there's nepotism, she
  grew up extremely privileged,' which I did, don't get me wrong. But life wasn't all that easy for
  other reasons" (Vogue, April 2023). The draft makes this concession for her at L223 and adds an
  unsourced claim in the process.
- **Her explanation of the Sacramento segment.** She gave one at the time. The draft supplies a
  different one and does not mention hers.
- **Her stated preference for "healing" over "recovery."** Minor, but it is the one place she has
  publicly asked to have her own process described in a particular way, and it is free to honor.

## What surprised me

**Welcome.** The counterargument section (L206–212) states an actual falsifier — "sustained
on-record framing of her own motive as escaping limitation or boredom" — rather than hedging. From
where the subject sits, an article that names the evidence that would defeat it is making a
falsifiable claim about her rather than an unfalsifiable one. That is the difference between a read
and a verdict.

**Welcome, and a correction to the packet.** The evidence packet flags the epigraph's closing
"I'm not this" as "not located" (CLM-03). It is real. In the same consecutive passage of the
transcript she says: "Because it's not— I'm a girl who wears a hoodie, smoking a joint, f***ing
walking around bare feet, like a little rotter. Like, I'm not this. Like, what?" The draft's
epigraph compresses one continuous passage in correct order without changing its sense. This is
ordinary tightening, not fabrication, and a later editor working from the packet should not strip
the line. See SUBJ-C9 for the one remaining precision fix.

**Jarring.** The thought bubble at L245. Everything else in the piece earns its access to her
interior by quoting her. That passage simply writes it.

## Red flags

### SUBJ-R1 — Fabricated first-person interior monologue at her suicidal crisis (BLOCKER)

- **Passage.** L245, inside "Why sobriety unlocked the album Cara Delevingne always knew she would
  make":
  > `<p class="inner-thought">`That's his song. That is the song they played for him. And I am
  > sitting here in a hotel room doing the exact thing that killed him, and if I keep going somebody
  > is going to stand in a room and play a song for me.`</p>`
- **Trust problem.** This is authored thought presented as hers, at the single most private moment
  in her life, with no hedge. I checked how it renders: `.inner-thought` in `src/scss/blog.scss:630`
  is a tinted panel, italic, with a thought-bubble icon and no attribution marker. A reader arrives
  at it immediately after two real quotations ("I was so close to ending my life" / "It is such a
  quick flip") and will read it as a third. The subject has no way to object to a quote she never
  gave, and the passage claims certainty about a suicidal person's reasoning that no one but her can
  possess.
- **Evidence.** Her sourced account of this moment consists of: that she was alone in a hotel room;
  that she was "so close to ending my life"; that a song came up on shuffle that had played at a
  friend's funeral; "It is such a quick flip"; and that she threw the drugs down the toilet
  (Singju Post CHD transcript; packet First-person evidence). The chain of reasoning in the panel —
  particularly the projected image of her own funeral — appears in no source.
- **Minimum viable repair.** Delete the panel. The surrounding paragraph already carries the beat in
  her words and lands harder without a paraphrase competing with it. If an interior beat is
  structurally required elsewhere in the piece, relocate it to a non-clinical moment and mark it
  unmistakably as the writer's inference.
- **Expected benefit.** Removes the only passage in the article that invents her interior, at the
  point of maximum vulnerability and maximum legal/ethical exposure. Costs three sentences.
- **Confidence.** High. The rendering is verified in the repo; the absence from sources is verified
  against the draft's own transcript.
- **Acceptance test.** `grep -c "inner-thought" src/blog/people/drafts/Cara-Delevingne.md` returns 0
  in the body (the FORMULA FINGERPRINT ledger reference at L68 must also be updated), and no
  sentence in the recovery section states her thoughts except as a direct quotation with an outlet
  and year attached.

### SUBJ-R2 — The central frame recasts deliberate self-harm as her body acting without her (BLOCKER)

- **Passage.** The H2 at L158, "What Cara Delevingne's body kept saying for her"; the frame sentence
  at L160, "every few years, something she could not put into words came out through her body
  instead, in a form other people could see"; the table row at L169; and the closing line of the
  section at L175, "Her body had been filing reports for twenty-three years." Echoed in the TL;DR
  (L96) and in the diagnosis tie-breaker (L118).
- **Trust problem.** She described doing something. The article describes something happening to
  her. From her standpoint this is being told that her own account of her agency was mistaken — the
  precise move the article criticizes the fashion press for making about her. It also means the
  article uses the most serious disclosure she has made while declining to name it, which reads less
  like discretion than like using the material with the difficult part filed off.
- **Evidence.** Verbatim from the draft's own source (Singju Post CHD transcript, retrieved this
  pass): "I also just started throwing myself off stuff. I broke, I don't know how many bones in
  like a year. Because I was in pain inside, but I didn't know how to express it. **So I just wanted
  to feel pain.**" Daily Front Row reports the same passage explicitly as self-harm. The draft
  quotes the middle clause and omits both the act and the motive that bracket it.
- **Minimum viable repair.** Two changes, neither of which costs the argument. (1) Restore her
  agency and her stated motive in the fractures row and in the hook: she threw herself off things
  because she wanted to feel pain and wanted a visible token. (2) Rename the organizing frame from
  the body speaking _for_ her to the ways she made pain visible when she could not say it — which
  accurately covers the volitional rows (hunger strike, fractures) and the involuntary ones
  (psoriasis) instead of forcing all six into one category. The draft's own tie-breaker, "That is a
  bid to be witnessed" (L118), is _the accurate frame_ and is stronger under the true version: a
  deliberate bid to be witnessed is far better Type 4 evidence than an involuntary symptom, because
  symptoms have no motive and the Enneagram types motive.
- **Expected benefit.** The article stops contradicting its subject about her own conduct, gains the
  word that makes the section legible, and upgrades its central evidence from a somatic metaphor to
  a motivated act — which is what the type argument actually needs.
- **Confidence.** High on the misframing (verbatim source retrieved). Medium on how far the "body
  speaking" frame should be retired versus rescoped — the psoriasis and airport rows genuinely fit
  it.
- **Acceptance test.** The fractures row's third column quotes her on the act, not only on the
  interior state; the section frame no longer asserts involuntariness for rows where she has
  described choosing; and a reader who then listens to the episode finds nothing in the article that
  contradicts what she says about what she did.

### SUBJ-C1 — Unsourced assertion about how she got her record deal (CONCERN, highest priority)

- **Passage.** L223: "She got a major-label deal that thousands of better singers will never see,
  and she got it partly because her name sells. **Nobody handed her the Warner contract on a blind
  listen.**"
- **Trust problem.** A flat factual assertion about the mechanism of a business deal, damaging to
  her professional standing, made in the writer's voice as a "concession" on her behalf. The packet
  is explicit: "the draft's own inference. No source establishes how the deal was made" (Contested
  characterizations). The article is conceding, as established fact, the strongest version of the
  charge against her — using evidence it does not have.
- **Evidence.** No source in the packet or in this pass establishes the deal's basis. Meanwhile she
  has an on-record concession of her own that is more specific and more honest (Vogue, April 2023,
  quoted under "What I expected").
- **Minimum viable repair.** Replace the writer's inference with her own concession, and attribute
  the remaining claim as perception rather than mechanism — that the deal is _read_ as name-driven,
  not that it _was_.
- **Expected benefit.** The concession gets stronger (a subject conceding her own privilege is more
  persuasive than a narrator conceding it for her) and the article stops asserting an unsourced fact
  about a living person's professional dealings.
- **Confidence.** High.
- **Acceptance test.** No sentence in the section states how the Warner deal was decided; the
  privilege concession carries her attribution and a date.

- **Expected benefit.** Removes a checkable chronology error at the article's most prominent

- **Confidence.** Medium — the sequencing is a trade outlet's, not hers, and is suggestive rather
  than decisive. This is why it is a concern and not a blocker.
- **Acceptance test.** The table row carries a defensible date with a source, and no sentence

### SUBJ-C5 — Unsourced sequencing overrides her best-sourced statement of her own motive

- **Confidence.** High.

### SUBJ-C8 — Nearly every fact about her adult life is metabolized as symptom

- **Passage.** Structural. Between the hook, the TL;DR, the tie-breaker, the six-row ledger, the
  household section, the recovery section and the suicidality beat, the article routes almost every
  biographical fact through injury. Her professional choices appear as evidence of an unmet need
  (L227–231), her decade of work appears as a lie she was selling (L83, L130), and her present work
  appears as possibly the same lie in a new format (L261–263).
- **Trust problem.** Individually each read is defensible and several are generous. Cumulatively the
  article leaves almost no room for her to have done anything for an ordinary reason — because she
  wanted to, was good at it, was offered it, or needed the money. A subject can accept a
  psychological reading of one decision and still not recognize a life in which every decision is
  one.
- **Evidence.** The draft's own distribution ledger (L44–46) counts type-theory paragraphs but
  nothing counts wound-framing density. Her non-pathological framing does exist and is used once
  ("There was this part of me that always thought I was gonna do it," L238) — the point is the ratio,
  not an absence.
- **Minimum viable repair.** Give one or two professional achievements a non-diagnostic paragraph —
  the Burberry decade or the Cabaret run described as work she was good at, before the article
  explains what it cost her. The Cabaret beat is currently used only as thesis material.
- **Expected benefit.** The subject becomes a person with a career rather than a case with a
  résumé, and the wound readings land harder against a contrast.
- **Confidence.** Medium. This is the finding closest to taste, and I am holding it as a concern
  rather than a blocker for that reason.
- **Acceptance test.** At least one section describes something she did well without immediately
  converting it into evidence of what she lacked.

### SUBJ-C9 — Quotation precision: one mangled compression, one missing ellipsis, one status upgrade

- **Passages.** (a) L77: "I broke my elbow, and being like, 'Oh, yay.'" — her actual line is "I
  remember breaking my elbow straight through for the first time and going to the hospital and
  finding out it was broken and being like, 'Oh, yay.'" The draft's compression produces an
  ungrammatical fragment that makes her sound incoherent; compressing a quotation should never cost
  the speaker her syntax. (b) L73 epigraph: three fragments from one continuous passage joined with
  full stops and no ellipsis. Order and sense are preserved — see "What surprised me" — but the
  elision should be marked. (c) L75: "broke so many bones in one year that she **stopped counting
  them**" against "I broke, I don't know how many bones in like a year"; "stopped counting" implies
  a counting she never described. (d) L225: the SAG nomination was for Outstanding Performance by an
  **Ensemble**; the current phrasing may read as an individual nomination (packet CLM-10).
- **Trust problem.** Small individually. Together they are the class of error that tells a subject
  the writer was working from a transcript rather than listening to her.
- **Minimum viable repair.** Repair the elbow quote to a grammatical excerpt; add ellipses to the
  epigraph; soften "stopped counting" to her actual claim; say "ensemble."
- **Expected benefit.** The quotations survive a check against audio, which is the only test that
  matters here.
- **Confidence.** High on (a), (c), (d); high on (b) as a marking issue only.
- **Acceptance test.** Every quoted fragment is grammatical as excerpted, and every elision is
  marked.

thought, 'She's spoiled, there's nepotism, she grew up extremely privileged,' which I did, don't get

attributed to her with an outlet and year.

**SUBJ-I2 — Use her own preferred word for her process, once.** She has said: "You don't recover,
and that's okay… I prefer 'healing,' because I'm constantly doing it" (Vogue, April 2023). The

listened to how she wants this described — and it reinforces the unresolved ending the piece already
commits to. _Confidence:_ high. _Acceptance test:_ her preference appears in her words in the
recovery section.

**SUBJ-I3 — Note her own record's disagreement about when the depression began.** She says
"breakdown at 15" on _Call Her Daddy_ (L150) and "I think I properly started dealing with depression
when I was about 16" to Esquire. _Benefit:_ an article that flags where a subject's own memory

against a reader finding the discrepancy first. _Confidence:_ medium — worth a clause, not a
paragraph. _Acceptance test:_ the draft either notes the variance or stops presenting 15 as fixed.

**SUBJ-I4 — Attribute the industry claim to her, since she made it.** The article asserts in its own

authority behind it. _Confidence:_ medium. _Acceptance test:_ the industry critique carries at least
one sourced sentence from her.

## Limits of this review

- **I am a fairness proxy, not Cara Delevingne.** Nothing here reports her actual opinion of this
  article, of Type 4, or of being written about. Where I say she "would object," I mean the passage
  fails a fairness test the public record lets me apply — not that I know her view.
- **No audio.** Like the packet, I worked from the Singju Post transcript and secondary coverage. I
  retrieved more of that transcript verbatim than the packet did, which is what made SUBJ-R2
  decisive, but I did not listen to the episode. SUBJ-Q1 and SUBJ-Q3 both wait on that listen, and
  the fracture-dating concern (SUBJ-C2) is capped at medium confidence because of it.
- **The Playboy summer 2026 interview remains unretrieved** for a third pass. It is the largest
  untouched first-person source and could bear on SUBJ-Q2 and SUBJ-Q4.
- **I did not evaluate the Enneagram argument on its merits.** Whether she is a Four is the
  enneagram evaluator's question. I tested only whether the article's use of evidence about her is
  fair, and I have deliberately not treated disagreement with the typing as a red flag. Where
  SUBJ-R2 touches the type argument it is because the underlying _evidence_ is misdescribed, not
  because the conclusion is wrong.
- **Out of my lane, flagged once and left to others:** the tour's "about six months… eleven cities"
  claim at L85 (packet CLM-06 finds the itinerary ran 1–26 June across ~6 cities) and the internal
  citation failures at CLM-12 and CLM-13. These are durability and theory-accuracy problems. They
  are also claims about her professional activity, so I note them here rather than pretending they
  are invisible from this standpoint.
- **SUBJ-C8 is the finding closest to taste.** I have marked it a concern and not a blocker on
  purpose; a reasonable editor could decline it without breaking the article's fairness.
