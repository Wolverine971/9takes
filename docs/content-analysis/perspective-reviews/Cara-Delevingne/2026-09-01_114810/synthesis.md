## Executive verdict

Six perspectives, six `trust: strained`, five `revise` and one `hold_for_research`. Every one of them
also returned `delight: clear_hit`. That combination is the whole story: the piece is the best
argument anyone has made about this subject, and it is currently falsifiable in eleven places, most
of them cheaply.

The findings converge on one structural failure with several faces. **The article repeatedly supplies
its own version of something it already had the subject's version of.** She said she threw herself
off things because she wanted to feel pain; the draft says her body filed reports. She said the
Sacramento segment was sarcasm; the draft says she was flat and bored. She has an on-record privilege
concession; the draft invents one for her and asserts a fact about a business deal it cannot source.
She has no recorded thought at the hotel-room moment; the draft writes one in first person inside a
styled panel. The 9takes wings guide says 4w3s "brand their authenticity"; the draft quotes it saying
something it does not say, then builds the TL;DR and the final-act skeptic beat on the invented
wording.

That is one habit, not six unrelated errors, and it is worth naming to the editor as one habit,
because the same repair logic fixes all of them: **use the source's words where the source has
words.** In every single instance, doing so makes the argument stronger. The self-harm correction
upgrades the tie-breaker from an involuntary symptom (which carries no motive and therefore cannot
discriminate type) to a stated motive that maps precisely onto the article's own definition of the
Four. The Sacramento correction turns an asserted point into a demonstrated one. Her own privilege
concession is more persuasive than the narrator's. "Brand their authenticity" is a better phrase for
this subject than the one the draft made up. Not one of these repairs costs the thesis anything.

Two separate classes sit alongside that habit. **Falsifiable factual claims:** a tour the article says
is running that ended on 26 June 2026; a "six sites" consensus of which three are verified and one
rests on two votes; an acting record surveyed only to 2022, missing eighteen episodes of a co-lead
and a Cannes premiere that A24 bought for eight figures three months before this draft was dated. And
**one checkable error in the article's final sentence**: she wrote and released a song in 2017, so
"this is the first one she wrote" is wrong. That fact is not a threat to the piece. It is the best
evidence in the file _for_ it — a woman at the peak of being looked at silently wrote a song called
"I Feel Everything" and released it under someone else's film — and the draft is standing on top of it
facing the other way.

I am overruling the `hold_for_research` recommendation. The unfamiliar reader argues the self-harm
question cannot be settled without the _Call Her Daddy_ audio. It does not need to be. The subject
perspective retrieved the verbatim passage from the Singju Post transcript — **the draft's own
source for every other quotation in the piece** — and it reads: "I also just started throwing myself
off stuff. I broke, I don't know how many bones in like a year. Because I was in pain inside, but I
didn't know how to express it. So I just wanted to feel pain." The draft quotes the middle clause and
contradicts the clauses on either side of it. No new evidence is required to know that is wrong. The
audio settles verbatim fidelity (RQ-01) and the fractures' dating (RQ-02), which are real and open,
but neither blocks the repair.

Recommendation: **revise**. Eleven P0 repairs, most of them one clause. The piece is closer to
excellent than the blocker count suggests, because nine of the eleven make it better rather than
merely safer.
