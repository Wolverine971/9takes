## Bottom-line verdict

I came in knowing roughly one thing about Cara Delevingne — famous model, eyebrows — and I left able
to state the thesis without hesitation: the internet filed her as a Type 7 by reading her public
clowning, and the article argues she is a Type 4 because her whole record is about not being seen
accurately, with the drug-and-escape layer sitting on top of an identity problem rather than being
the problem. That is a clean, memorable, non-obvious claim, and the article delivers it in prose I
did not want to stop reading. On comprehension and momentum this is one of the easier reads I could
ask for on a subject I did not know.

Trust is where it strains, and it strains in a specific and correctable direction. The article is
scrupulous about attribution roughly forty times — outlet and year sit right next to nearly every
quotation — which is exactly why the three places it stops being scrupulous stand out so sharply.
Two of them sit on the article's most sensitive material: a self-harm history the piece reframes
without naming, and an invented interior monologue at a suicidal moment that I could not distinguish
from a quotation. The third is a plain factual claim in the paragraph that orients me to why I am
reading this now.

I am recommending `hold_for_research` rather than `revise` for one reason: the central metaphor —
her body involuntarily "filing reports" — may misdescribe deliberate self-injury, and the packet
says only the full _Call Her Daddy_ audio settles it. The hook, the bespoke somatic ledger, and the
tie-breaking evidence for the entire type argument all rest on that reading. That is not a paragraph
to patch; it is the spine, and it needs the tape.

## What landed

**UNFAMILIAR-H1 — the hook works without any prior knowledge of her.**

> "Cara Delevingne broke so many bones in one year that she stopped counting them."
> […] "I broke my elbow, and being like, 'Oh, yay.' Like, I can finally have a cast and say, 'Ow,
> I'm hurting.'"
>
> "A cast is a sentence the body can say out loud."

I did not need to know who she was for this to land, which is rare and valuable — most celebrity
pieces open on a name I have to already care about. It opens on a human fact instead. **Must survive
revision:** this is the single strongest reason an unfamiliar reader keeps going past line one. See
UNFAMILIAR-R1 for the framing problem sitting underneath it; the repair there should preserve this
opening, not remove it.

**UNFAMILIAR-H2 — the type claim is glossed in plain English before it is used.**

> "Ask a Type 4 what they want and the honest answer is to be seen accurately. The Individualist
> lives with a running suspicion that the version of them everyone else is dealing with is a
> substitute, and will torch a comfortable arrangement rather than keep playing the substitute."

Two sentences, no jargon, no numbers-as-mysticism. I understood the claim well enough to test it
against everything that followed. **Must survive revision:** this is the load-bearing accessibility
sentence for the whole piece. Every later Enneagram move is affordable because this one is cheap.

**UNFAMILIAR-H3 — the table is the best thing on the page for a scanner.**

> The six-row "When / What happened / Her account of it" ledger, then: "Her body had been filing
> reports for twenty-three years, and it took images humiliating enough to run on every celebrity
> site on earth before one reached somebody who could act on it, including her."

I scanned the table before reading it properly, and it gave me the whole argument in ten seconds.
The "filing reports" line is the sentence I would quote to someone else. **Must survive revision:**
the form is doing comprehension work no paragraph could do, and it is the piece's memory hook.

**UNFAMILIAR-H4 — the "voice" reframe made the career switching legible.**

> "Modelling gave her a face and specifically took her voice; the job is to be looked at silently.
> Acting gave her a voice that belonged to a screenwriter. Theatre, in 2024, gave her Sally Bowles
> in _Cabaret_, a woman whose entire act is performing a liberation she does not have, eight shows a
> week, in somebody else's songs. Music is the first medium in the sequence where the words are
> hers. […] Seen that way, the four careers are one request, refused three times and repeated
> anyway."

Before this passage I held the standard outsider view the article names — rich person collects
hobbies. After it, I did not. That is the article changing my mind in real time, using a word she
uses herself, and _Cabaret_ is explained inside the sentence so I never had to know the show.
**Must survive revision:** this is where the piece earns its sympathy instead of asserting it.

**UNFAMILIAR-H5 — the ending refuses to over-claim, and is stronger for it.**

> "She has not explained the title. […] Every earlier piece of evidence about her interior life was
> something other people found first: a cast on an elbow, scabs the stylists would not touch,
> photographs at dawn outside a private airport. This one she chose, and then declined to gloss."

I expected a tidy redemption bow. I got a held silence instead. **Must survive revision:** it is the
one place the article visibly declines to tell me something it does not know, which retroactively
raises my trust in everything it did tell me.

## What missed

**The self-harm frame (see UNFAMILIAR-R1).** I felt a seam here on the unaided read without being
able to name it. The article says the injuries were involuntary — "something she could not put into
words came out through her body instead" — and then, sixty lines earlier, says she "collected casts"
and "wanted a visible token." Collecting and wanting are choices. Filing reports is not. I noticed
the contradiction, could not resolve it, and moved on slightly unsure whether I was being told what
happened or what it meant.

**The interior monologue (see UNFAMILIAR-R2).** The one passage where I stopped and thought _wait,
did she say this?_

**The tour sentence (see UNFAMILIAR-R3).** My "why now" paragraph contains a claim I could falsify
from a ticketing site in under a minute.

**Smaller misses.** "They typed the gurning" is the punchline of the opening movement and I do not
know what gurning is (C1). The TL;DR's answer to the title's question is behind a closed accordion,
so the fastest reader has to click to get the payoff (a preference, not a defect). "She is also back
at Topshop as a co-designer" — _back_? The article never established she was there before. And the
Weinstein paragraph ends on a dangling fragment: she "did not feel she 'owed that to anyone
either'" — either _what_? Nothing precedes it that the "either" attaches to.

## What I expected

- **A one-line answer to "what is the Enneagram."** Not a primer — one clause. I got the Type 4
  gloss (H2) but never learned that this is a nine-type system organised around motivation, which is
  the fact that makes "Type 7 vs Type 4" a meaningful argument rather than two arbitrary numbers.
  (C3)
- **To be told who Harvey Weinstein is, in four words.** The quote is the hinge of section 2 and its
  force depends entirely on his power over her career, which the article never states. (C4)
- **To know which six sites.** The title stakes the piece on overturning an internet verdict; I was
  never told whose. (C2)
- **Not to hit a wall of wings, subtypes, Naranjo and arrows immediately after the most affecting
  passage in the article.** (C7)
- **Some signal of when a "she has said" is doing real work and when it is not.** The article
  trained me to trust that construction, then used it once with nothing behind it. (C8)

## What surprised me

**Welcome:** that the article argues _against_ itself in the final act. After the recovery section I
was braced for a victory lap, and instead got: "A Four who finally gets a voice will immediately be
offered money for the sound of finally getting a voice, and she is now in a business that pays for
it. The album is not the test. The test is what she makes after the raw thing stops selling." A
piece willing to leave its own thesis exposed at the end reads as honest rather than promotional.
That single move did more for my trust than any citation.

**Welcome:** the counterargument section names what would change the writer's mind. I have never
seen a personality-typing article volunteer a falsifier, and it made the type claim feel like a
claim rather than a horoscope.

**Jarring:** the whiplash between "she collected casts" and "her body filed reports." Two theories
of the same event, in one article, neither acknowledging the other.

**Jarring:** the hotel-room monologue arriving in first person with no quotation marks in an article
that quotes everything.

**Unearned:** "quietly failing with dyspraxia and depression she has said nobody named." That
sentence accuses the adults around a child, and it is the only claim in the article that made me
wonder who was being blamed and on whose say-so.

## Red flags

### UNFAMILIAR-R1 — BLOCKER — The central metaphor may misdescribe self-harm as involuntary somatic expression

- **Passage.** L75 hook ("broke so many bones in one year that she stopped counting them"); L118
  ("Delevingne collected casts. She wanted a visible token… That is a bid to be witnessed, and it is
  the single most Four thing in the entire record"); L160 ("every few years, something she could not
  put into words came out through her body instead"); the "Early modelling years" table row at L169;
  L175 ("Her body had been filing reports for twenty-three years"); TL;DR bullet at L96.
- **Reader effect / trust problem.** The article gives me two incompatible accounts of the same
  events and never reconciles them: deliberate ("collected," "wanted") and involuntary ("could not
  put into words came out through her body"). I noticed the seam unaided and could not resolve it.
  Worse, the frame the article chose — the body speaking for her — omits the frame she herself used.
  A reader whose own history this touches is being handed a metaphor instead of the thing.
- **Evidence.** Packet CLM-01: the fractures passage is reported _inside a self-harm discussion_,
  with her quoted as "So I just wanted to feel pain"; her actual words are "I broke… I don't know how
  many bones in like a year," so "stopped counting them" is the draft's inference, not her claim.
  Packet dispute #1 calls this "the most consequential open question in the file" and says the full
  audio would settle it. My one orientation search independently confirms the public framing: Daily
  Front Row's account of the episode describes danger-seeking behaviour and wanting to feel physical
  pain, and the episode is titled around sobriety and self-love. The words "self-harm" appear zero
  times in the draft.
- **Minimum viable repair.** Retrieve the _Call Her Daddy_ audio for this passage. Then either (a)
  name the self-harm context in her own words and rebuild the tie-breaker on what she says the cast
  was _for_ — being able to say "I'm hurting" — which survives either reading and is the actually
  moving part; or (b) if the audio does not support the involuntary reading, drop "her body kept
  saying it for her" as the organising claim and retitle the section around the visible-evidence
  idea, which the other five table rows already support. Replace "stopped counting them" with her
  quotation. Keep the hook.
- **Expected benefit.** Removes the internal contradiction I tripped on, stops the article from
  overwriting her account with a better-sounding one, and puts the type argument on evidence that
  does not depend on an unresolved question.
- **Confidence.** High that the contradiction and the omission are real; medium on which reading the
  audio supports.
- **Acceptance test.** A reader can state, from the article alone, whether the injuries were sought
  or sustained — and the answer matches her recorded account. `grep -ci "collected casts"` and the
  "could not put into words" framing no longer both appear as unqualified assertions.

### UNFAMILIAR-R2 — BLOCKER — Unmarked invented interiority at a suicidal moment

- **Passage.** L245: `<p class="inner-thought">That's his song. That is the song they played for
him. And I am sitting here in a hotel room doing the exact thing that killed him, and if I keep
going somebody is going to stand in a room and play a song for me.</p>` — sandwiched between two
  attributed quotations ("I was so close to ending my life" / "It is such a quick flip").
- **Reader effect / trust problem.** First person, present tense, no quotation marks, no
  attribution, in an article that attributes everything else with outlet and year. I could not tell
  whether I was reading her words or the writer's. That ambiguity is tolerable over a trivial moment
  and not tolerable over a suicidal one — and because it sits between two real quotations, the
  default reading is that it is also real.
- **Evidence.** The house convention is a writer's device, not a quotation:
  `docs/content-generation/blog-furniture-guide.md` L494 defines `.inner-thought` as "Imagining what
  someone was likely thinking." `src/scss/blog.scss` L630 renders it as an italic panel with a
  thought-bubble icon — which signals _interiority_ but not _authorship_ — and provides an
  `.attribution` slot that this draft leaves unused. The corpus already solves this: the Nara-Smith
  grade note praises an `inner-thought` beat as "honestly keyed as authorial ('From where she
  stood:')."
- **Minimum viable repair.** Add an authorial key immediately before the block — the corpus pattern,
  e.g. a lead-in clause naming it as reconstruction from her account — or populate the existing
  `.attribution` span. No rewrite of the monologue needed.
- **Expected benefit.** Removes the one place a first-time reader cannot tell reportage from
  imagination, on the article's most sensitive sentence, at a cost of about six words.
- **Confidence.** High.
- **Acceptance test.** A reader who has never seen a 9takes article can say, without checking
  anything, that the hotel-room paragraph is the writer's reconstruction and not a quotation.

### UNFAMILIAR-R3 — BLOCKER — The orientation paragraph states a present-tense fact that appears to be wrong

- **Passage.** L85: "Her first headline tour opened on 1 June 2026 and runs about six months across
  eleven cities." Echoed at L188 ("an eleven-city tour") and TL;DR L67.
- **Reader effect / trust problem.** This is the paragraph that answers _why am I reading about this
  person now_, which is the unfamiliar reader's single most important sentence. It tells me a tour is
  currently running. It is trivially checkable and, if wrong, it is the first thing a curious reader
  disproves — and the disproof arrives before they have any reason to extend the article credit.
- **Evidence.** Packet CLM-06 rates this "DISPUTED — likely wrong on both counts": the published
  itinerary is 11 shows across roughly 6 cities (Berlin, Barcelona ×2, London ×2, Paris ×2, Los
  Angeles ×2, Brooklyn ×2), running 1–26 June 2026 — under four weeks — with no autumn dates on
  Songkick, Bandsintown or Ticketmaster. The "six months" figure traces to an entity-gap packet
  assertion with no dated support. The article's `date:` is 2026-09-01, more than two months after
  the last listed show.
- **Minimum viable repair.** Confirm the itinerary against a ticketing source, then state it in the
  past tense with absolute bounds: an eleven-date first headline tour that ran 1–26 June 2026 across
  six cities. If autumn dates genuinely exist, cite one.
- **Expected benefit.** The "why now" anchor becomes something a reader can verify and be reassured
  by rather than something that fails on first contact. The album date and the Rag & Bone campaign
  already carry the currency; the tour does not need to be live.
- **Confidence.** High that "six months" and "eleven cities" are both wrong as written; the tense is
  wrong as a consequence.
- **Acceptance test.** Every tour claim in the article matches a dated ticketing or itinerary source,
  and no sentence asserts the tour is in progress on the publication date.

### UNFAMILIAR-C1 — CONCERN — "Gurning" is an unglossed Briticism carrying a punchline

- **Passage.** L81 ("The gurning. The crossed eyes. The eyebrows."), L87 ("They typed the gurning."),
  L93 (TL;DR: "reading the eyebrows and the gurning").
- **Reader effect.** On first use the neighbouring "crossed eyes" half-rescues it. On second use it
  stands alone as the closing beat of the opening movement — the sentence that lands the article's
  premise — and on third use it is in the TL;DR, which many readers hit first. A US general reader
  does not have this word.
- **Repair.** Gloss it once, inline, at L81 — the face-pulling — and leave the two later uses to
  ride on it. No structural change.
- **Benefit.** The premise-landing sentence survives for readers outside the UK, which on this site
  is most of them.
- **Confidence.** High.
- **Acceptance test.** A US reader who has never heard "gurning" can paraphrase "They typed the
  gurning" correctly.

### UNFAMILIAR-C2 — CONCERN — The internet consensus the title overturns is never identified or sized

- **Passage.** L87 ("Six different sites, roughly the same verdict"); L206 ("Six crowd-typing sites
  landed on Seven"); title ("Not the 7 the Internet Assigned Her").
- **Reader effect.** The article's entire frame is a correction of a public verdict, and I am given
  no way to weigh that verdict — no names, no vote counts, no sense of whether "the internet" here
  means thousands of people or a handful.
- **Evidence.** Packet CLM-14: Type 7 confirmed on Personality Database, Boo and Sakinorva, with
  three others unverified; **Sakinorva's 7w6 rests on two votes**, against a competing 3w4 vote; PDB
  reportedly carries three competing profiles. Directionally real, far thinner than "six sites"
  implies.
- **Repair.** Name the two or three verifiable databases and disclose the softness in one clause.
  The honest version is _more_ interesting, not less: a verdict this thin being treated as settled is
  itself the story.
- **Benefit.** The reader can calibrate the antagonist instead of taking it on faith, and the article
  stops overstating the thing it is correcting.
- **Confidence.** High.
- **Acceptance test.** A reader can name at least one database the article is disagreeing with, and
  no unsourced count is asserted.

### UNFAMILIAR-C3 — CONCERN — The Enneagram is never introduced

- **Passage.** L87 is the first mention: "The Enneagram crowd databases have her filed as a Type 7,
  the Enthusiast." The Type 4 gloss arrives at L104; Type 7 is not explained until L114.
- **Reader effect.** "Type 7, the Enthusiast" is a label with no content at first contact, and it is
  asked to function as the article's opposing position for roughly 800 words before I learn what a
  Seven is. I also never learn that this is a nine-type system organised around motivation — the
  fact that makes 4-vs-7 an argument rather than two numbers.
- **Repair.** One clause at first mention establishing the system (nine types, sorted by motive), and
  move a short Seven gloss — appetite for stimulation, aversion to pain — up to L87 rather than
  leaving it at L114.
- **Benefit.** The reader can hold both sides of the argument from the moment the article picks a
  fight, instead of holding one side and a placeholder.
- **Confidence.** High.
- **Acceptance test.** A reader who has never encountered the Enneagram can state, by the end of the
  opening section, what a Seven is supposed to want and what a Four is supposed to want.

### UNFAMILIAR-C4 — CONCERN — Harvey Weinstein appears with no indication of who he is or why it mattered

- **Passage.** L132: "'Harvey Weinstein called me and was like, "You can't be gay."'"
- **Reader effect.** This is the hinge of the billboard thesis — an industry buying her honesty while
  instructing her to hide the truest thing about her. Its force is entirely a function of his power
  over casting, which the article never states. A reader without that context reads it as a rude
  remark from a stranger rather than a career threat.
- **Evidence.** Packet CLM-05 verifies the quotation and praises the strict attribution; the gap is
  purely one of reader orientation, not sourcing. The packet timeline also shows this is consistent
  with her 2017 and 2019 accounts.
- **Repair.** Four words of role identification at first mention. Keep the strict attribution and the
  no-narration handling exactly as they are.
- **Benefit.** The section's central evidence lands at full weight for a reader who does not carry
  2017 film-industry context.
- **Confidence.** High.
- **Acceptance test.** A reader can explain why that phone call was a constraint on her career and
  not merely an insult.

### UNFAMILIAR-C5 — CONCERN — An internal link is cited for a definition the linked page does not contain

- **Passage.** L190: "The 9takes profile of the wings calls 4w3 'The Aristocrat,' and names its
  specific shadow: performing authenticity rather than embodying it, crafting an image of depth."
  Depended on by the main-body skeptic beat at L261 and the TL;DR at L95, and by `persona_title`.

- **Evidence.** Packet CLM-12: `src/blog/enneagram/enneagram-wings-complete-guide.md` does call 4w3

- **Benefit.** The strongest wing argument in the piece stops resting on a misquotation of the site's

- **Confidence.** High — verified by grep against the linked file.
- **Acceptance test.** Every phrase presented as the wings page's wording appears verbatim on that

### UNFAMILIAR-C6 — CONCERN — "Belgravia" is load-bearing in the TL;DR and unglossed everywhere

- **Benefit.** The TL;DR's third bullet becomes comprehensible to a first-time reader on first
  contact.
- **Confidence.** Medium-high.
- **Acceptance test.** A US reader can explain what "the Belgravia inversion" is claiming after

### UNFAMILIAR-C7 — CONCERN — The jargon block sits at the article's emotional peak

- **Benefit.** Protects the article's strongest emotional run and lowers the most likely abandonment
  point.
- **Confidence.** Medium — this is a reader-behaviour judgement, and the accordion label genuinely
  reduces the cost.
- **Acceptance test.** No collapsed jargon block interrupts the span between the somatic table and

### UNFAMILIAR-C8 — CONCERN — The article's own trust signal used once with nothing behind it

- **Passage.** L150: "…quietly failing with dyspraxia and depression she has said nobody named."
- **Reader effect.** The article trains me across dozens of instances to read "she has said" as a
  guarantee that a real statement exists. Here it introduces an accusation — that the adults around a
  struggling minor failed to identify her conditions — with no statement behind it. I cannot
  distinguish this instance from the ones I trusted, which is the problem: it quietly devalues the

- **Evidence.** Packet CLM-28: "NO SOURCE TRAIL." Dyspraxia and depression during the Bedales years

- **Repair.** Cut "she has said nobody named" unless a quotation is found; the sentence works without
  it. Add three words defining dyspraxia.
- **Benefit.** Removes an unsupported claim about identifiable adults and protects the credibility of
  the ~40 attributions that _are_ sound.
- **Confidence.** High.
- **Acceptance test.** Every "she has said" / "by her own account" construction in the article maps
  to a specific sourced statement in the research file.

### UNFAMILIAR-C9 — CONCERN — A characterization she publicly disputed is presented to me as fact

- **Passage.** L136: "…the hosts of a _Good Day Sacramento_ segment scolded her on air for seeming
  ungrateful and told her she needed a nap. She had given them a few flat, bored, unperformed
  minutes, which is exactly what her persona advertised."
- **Reader effect.** The article offers this as "the cleanest external test" of its thesis, and the
  test only works if her affect really was flat and unperformed. I was given that reading as
  settled fact, with no indication she had said otherwise.
- **Evidence.** Packet CLM-16 verifies the event but flags the characterization as contested: her own

  framed it as sarcasm, not flat affect — and press consensus at the time blamed the anchors.
- **Repair.** One clause noting her account, which does not weaken the point. Sarcasm mistaken for
  ingratitude is arguably a _better_ illustration of "the market wanted the appearance of no filter,
  on cue, at the correct volume" than flatness is.
- **Benefit.** The article stops describing her interior state over her own stated version of it, in a
  passage whose whole subject is people misreading her.
- **Confidence.** High.
- **Acceptance test.** The paragraph includes her framing of the segment, and no assertion about her
  affect that she has contradicted stands unqualified.

## Specific improvements

Beyond the repairs attached to each finding above, in priority order:

1. **Reconcile the two theories of the injuries in a single sentence** wherever the repair for
   UNFAMILIAR-R1 lands. Whichever reading the audio supports, the article should not contain both
   "collected casts" and "could not put into words came out through her body" as unqualified
   assertions. _Acceptance test:_ a reader asked "did she break the bones on purpose?" gives the
   same answer the sources do.
2. **Fix "back at Topshop" (L261).** The word _back_ presumes a prior Topshop history the article
   never mentions. Either establish it in three words or cut the word. _Acceptance test:_ no
   sentence presumes biography the article has not supplied.
3. **Repair the dangling "either" (L132).** "she did not feel she 'owed that to anyone either'" has
   no antecedent in the article; the "either" is an artefact of a longer answer. Trim the quotation
   to the part that stands alone. _Acceptance test:_ every quotation is grammatical in its new
   context.
4. **Clarify the SAG nomination (L225).** "which drew a SAG nomination" reads, to me, as an
   individual acting nomination for her. Packet CLM-10: it was an Outstanding Performance by an
   Ensemble nomination in which she is named among seven. Since this sentence is doing the article's
   fairness work against "She has no talents," it should be exactly right. Also expand "SAG" once.
   _Acceptance test:_ a reader correctly identifies it as an ensemble nomination.
5. **Fix "a private Los Angeles airport" (L171).** Van Nuys is a public general-aviation airport
   (packet CLM-19). It sits in the table — the most-scanned element on the page — and recurs in the
   closing line. _Acceptance test:_ the descriptor matches the airport's actual status.
6. **Downgrade "opened London Fashion Week" (L81).** Packet CLM-11: sources support a runway debut
   for Burberry Prorsum at LFW, not opening the show, still less opening the week. This sits in the
   credentialing sentence an unfamiliar reader takes wholly on faith. _Acceptance test:_ the claim
   matches the strongest available source wording.
