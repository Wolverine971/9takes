## Bottom-line verdict

The article largely earns its sympathetic interpretation, which is rare for this genre. It foregrounds the strongest rival typing by name, states a falsifier that would move its own verdict, holds attribution ceilings exactly at the evidence (Spielrein at "intimate," sales figures "as reported by"), and concedes the Toni Wolff triangle without motive laundering — the concession paragraph explicitly refuses to let the type lens function as an excuse. Against that, the skeptical ledger holds: one clean factual error (Freud's age in the Vienna scene), an exposed book-level citation in a named-author FAQ attribution, a counter-case whose full hearing sits behind a skip-labeled accordion, a tiebreaker that rests on an unsourced characterization of Jung's motive plus his own curated self-narrative, and a shadow section whose singular framing will read as sanitized to exactly the psychology-literate audience this page targets. All are repairable without touching the thesis. Recommendation: revise.

## Red flags

### CRITIC-R1 — Freud's age at the 1907 meeting is wrong (BLOCKER)

- **Passage:** "Freud was 51 and had found his heir." (Section: "Thirteen hours with Freud, then the letter that ended everything.")
- **Effect:** A verifiable factual error in the draft's most heavily dated scene invites the reader to distrust every adjacent date — and this scene carries four of them.
- **Evidence:** Freud was born May 6, 1856; in March 1907 he was 50 (51 that May). Simple date arithmetic; flagged independently as the packet's "one clean factual error" (CLM-08, Dispute 1).
- **Minimum viable repair:** Change "51" to "50."
- **Expected benefit:** Removes the only established factual error in the draft.
- **Confidence:** Certain.
- **Acceptance test:** Body text states Freud's age at the March 1907 meeting as 50, or omits the age.

### CRITIC-C1 — FAQ attributes the 9w1 typing to a book nobody has verified

- **Passage:** FAQ answer 1: "Don Riso and Russ Hudson typed him 9w1 in Personality Types."
- **Effect:** A named-author, named-book attribution that any Enneagram-literate reader can check. The typing itself is solid Riso-Hudson canon (confirmed in _The Wisdom of the Enneagram_'s 9w1 examples and on the Enneagram Institute's Type 9 page), but the packet found no page-level confirmation that _Personality Types_ specifically carries it (CLM-02, Dispute 3), and my own search this pass could not confirm the book placement either. If the book is wrong, the article's very first FAQ contains a false citation about its own central counter-authority.
- **Evidence:** Packet S10 ("_Personality Types_ placement unchecked"); search this pass confirmed the typing via the [Enneagram Institute Type 9 page](https://www.enneagraminstitute.com/type-9/) but not the book.
- **Minimum viable repair:** Re-anchor the FAQ to the verified placement ("in _The Wisdom of the Enneagram_") or drop the book title ("Don Riso and Russ Hudson typed him 9w1").
- **Expected benefit:** The page's most-checkable citation becomes its most defensible one.
- **Confidence:** High that the current wording is exposed; no evidence it is affirmatively wrong.
- **Acceptance test:** Every book title named in a Riso-Hudson attribution is one the pipeline has verified at source level.

### CRITIC-C2 — The shadow section's framing promises a fuller accounting than the governed scope delivers

- **Passage:** Section title "The shadow of the man who mapped the shadow" + opening move "The record shows he had his own. The documented case is Toni Wolff."
- **Effect:** "The documented case" (singular, definite article) implicitly represents the Wolff triangle as the whole of Jung's documented shadow. The psychology-literate reader this page targets knows the 1930s record — the Zentralblatt editorials, the General Medical Society presidency — is the most-discussed criticism of Jung in serious scholarship. For that reader, a section that announces a shadow accounting and inventories only the affair reads as sanitized, and the trust the article has carefully built is spent in one heading.
- **Evidence:** Packet, "Public record" section: the Nazi-era question is "a real, seriously contested area of Jung scholarship," omitted by governed decision (S19), with evaluators told to weigh it as a scope question. I am doing exactly that: the omission itself is governed and I do not contest it — a drive-by treatment would be worse and is correctly banned. The flag is the _framing_, which currently overpromises against the governed scope.
- **Minimum viable repair:** Scope-bound the section's claim to what it actually covers — e.g., "The documented case in his private life is Toni Wolff" — and/or extend the existing italic endnote with one boundary sentence stating that this profile confines itself to his private life and published work. This is a boundary statement, not a drive-by treatment; if synthesis rules even that out of scope, the in-body singularity fix alone closes most of the gap.
- **Expected benefit:** The informed skeptic reads a section that delivers exactly what it promises instead of one that appears unaware of, or hiding, the obvious.
- **Confidence:** High that a meaningful slice of the target audience will register the gap; medium on which repair the scope rules permit.
- **Acceptance test:** No sentence or heading in the shadow section claims or implies completeness of the shadow inventory; the section's promises are satisfiable by its contents.

### CRITIC-C3 — The rebuttal to the Nine reading rests on an unsourced characterization of Jung's motive

- **Passage:** "Riso and Hudson read his reconciling of opposites as a Nine keeping the peace. His own account of why he held opposites was cognitive: he wanted to know what each side knew." (Diagnosis section, third pillar; the same logic recurs in the Rabbit Hole counterargument's close.)
- **Effect:** The article's tiebreaker against its strongest rival is presented as Jung's own testimony, but no quote in the draft or packet has Jung explaining opposite-holding as knowledge-seeking. The closest verified motive statement — _Psychological Types_ was written "to do justice to the culture of Freud... and to find my own bearings" [Houston 27:37] — reads as orientation-seeking at least as naturally as knowledge-seeking; a Nine advocate would cite the same sentence. Compounding this, the whole affirmative chain (manikin → notebooks → tower → vault) and the function-stack self-report come from Jung's curated self-narrative: a posthumous, Jaffé-edited memoir and an 83-year-old's on-camera account of a construct he invented. The draft never acknowledges the self-report limitation it leans on.
- **Evidence:** Packet first-person section (no opposite-holding-as-knowing quote exists in the verified pool); packet's own caution on CLM-05 self-report ("self-report at 83 about a construct he invented"); S4 limitations ("Posthumous, Jaffé-edited; self-report throughout").
- **Minimum viable repair:** Either pin "his own account was cognitive" to an actual verified quote, or soften it to a labeled reading ("the account he gave of it was cognitive — he framed the types book as a way 'to find my own bearings' after losing Freud — though a Nine advocate can read that same sentence as a search for equilibrium"). Add one clause anywhere in the diagnosis acknowledging that the self-portrait is self-report.
- **Expected benefit:** The tiebreaker survives skeptical reading because it claims exactly what the evidence supports; the article's certainty stays proportional to its sources, which is the standard it holds everywhere else.
- **Confidence:** High.
- **Acceptance test:** Every sentence of the form "his own account was X" is adjacent to a verified quote in which he gives that account, or is explicitly marked as the article's reading.

Alternatively, cut "Skip if you're not deep into the system" so the accordion is optional-depth, not waved-off.

### CRITIC-C6 — The invented inner-thought line risks minting a new fake Jung quote

### CRITIC-C7 — Two rhetorical overclaims a psychology-literate reader can check

- **Passage:** (a) Intro: "He coined the vocabulary" following the list "Introvert. Extravert. Complex. Persona. Archetype." (b) H2: "Why every personality test you take is still arguing with Carl Jung."
- **Effect:** (a) "Complex" was coined by Theodor Ziehen; Jung popularized it through the word-association work. "Coined" applied to the whole list overclaims on one item. (b) The Big Five — the instrument scientific psychology actually uses — descends from the lexical/factor-analytic tradition and is not "arguing with Jung"; the header's claim is true for MBTI-descended tests and false for the field's dominant one. Both are the kind of overreach this audience catches, and each costs a little of the precision the article trades on.
- **Evidence:** Packet CLM-15 (Ziehen coinage; "put words in our mouths" survives, "coined" overclaims); standard personality-psychology lineage for (b).
- **Minimum viable repair:** (a) Swap "coined" for a popularization verb ("He minted or popularized every word in that list") or drop "Complex" from the list. (b) Keep the header as rhetoric if desired, but let one clause in the section concede the boundary — e.g., that the trait-based tests took a different road — which also sharpens the MBTI-lineage claim by contrast.
- **Expected benefit:** The article's precision reputation holds at its edges, where checking readers live.
- **Confidence:** High on (a); medium on (b) (headers are allowed rhetoric; the body currently offers no qualification).
- **Acceptance test:** No reader-visible sentence attributes to Jung a coinage that is not his; the "every personality test" claim is either qualified in-body or demonstrably scoped to the typology lineage.
