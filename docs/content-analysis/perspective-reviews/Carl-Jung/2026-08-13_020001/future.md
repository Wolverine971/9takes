## Bottom-line verdict

Twelve months from now this piece will still be accurate, understandable, and valuable, because almost all of its evidence base closed in 1961 and the draft anchors nearly everything to absolute dates. The subject cannot generate news; the thesis (5w4 argued against 9w1) rests on a contest that has been static for decades and that the draft explicitly frames as unresolved. The fragile surface is confined to one section — "Why every personality test you take is still arguing with Carl Jung" — plus the closer's hardcoded year count and one internal statistic that will drift as the corpus grows. Every repair is small and none touches the argument. Zero blockers; four concerns, all of the date-pin or refresh-calendar variety. I recommend `revise` only because two of the fixes (dating the TikTok statistic, time-stamping the corpus stat) are two-minute edits that are far cheaper before publication than after, when the page's "now" claims begin aging silently.

## What landed

**FUTURE-H1 — Absolute-date discipline as the piece's skeleton.** "In March 1959, the BBC sent John Freeman to a house on the shore of Lake Zurich" … "in the film the BBC broadcast that October" … "Three months after the broadcast he wrote to The Listener." The draft triangulates filming (March 1959, age 83), broadcast (October), and correction letter (January 1960, age 84) as three separately pinned events instead of a single fuzzy "in 1959." This is why the age arithmetic survives reader challenge indefinitely (the film's own narration says 84; the draft's 83 is correct for the filming it describes, and the event-pinning makes that defensible forever). The same discipline runs through the whole chronology: "January 3, 1913," "beginning in 1923, two months after his mother died," "In 1984 the family moved it to a Zurich bank vault," "until October 2009," "reached print in 2020," "appeared in 2024." Must survive revision untouched — this is the single biggest reason the piece is durable.

**FUTURE-H2 — Snapshot claims converted to permanent truths via inline attribution.** "sold out before publication, and reached number four on Amazon's overall list at 195 dollars a copy, as reported by the Jung Page." An unaudited sales figure becomes a permanently true claim about what a named source reported. Same move with the Norton tagline ("its publisher called it") and the Franz Jung anecdote ("by a 2009 New York Times account"). This attribution pattern is the template the one weak sentence in the piece (see FUTURE-C1) should have followed. Preserve the pattern everywhere it already exists.

**FUTURE-H3 — The stated falsifier future-proofs the verdict.** "If new first-person evidence surfaced showing harmony, rather than knowledge, as the felt goal, the verdict here would have to move" (Rabbit Hole, counterarguments). This is not decorative: the Jung estate is actively publishing primary material (Black Books 2020, Emma Jung 2024, and — found this run — a Philemon Foundation volume of Aniela Jaffé's interviews underlying _Memories, Dreams, Reflections_, announced for 2025, with a Princeton Critical Edition beginning 2027). New first-person evidence is genuinely plausible inside the twelve-month window, and the draft has already told the reader what would change its mind. The verdict can absorb new evidence without the page becoming wrong — it becomes updatable instead. Must survive verbatim.

**FUTURE-H4 — The thesis owes nothing to the news cycle.** Remove the entire legacy-now section (TikTok, Peterson, the 2020/2024 publications) and the 5w4 case is untouched: it runs on the manikin, the Freud rupture, Bollingen, and the 1959 self-report. Current events are used as appendices ("the excavation is still running"), never as evidence for the enduring pattern. This is exactly the structure the durability contract asks for, and it is rare in celebrity drafts.

## What missed

**The legacy-now section carries all of the fragile material and is the only section without date anchors.** "the #shadowwork hashtag has passed 2.3 billion views on TikTok, driven by a viral workbook" has no date and no inline source — the only reader-visible "current" statistic in the piece is also the only unanchored one (the MM+M attribution exists only in an HTML comment ledger, which readers never see). "Jordan Peterson has spent a decade filling arenas" is present-perfect and status-sensitive. "The excavation of the man himself is still running too" leans on "still." None of these is wrong today; all of them are the sentences that will read stale first, and they are clustered in the one section built to feel current. Detailed repairs below.

## What surprised me

- **Welcome:** how small the fragile surface is. A typical personality-analysis draft carries dozens of decaying present-tense claims; this one has roughly five sentences of exposure across ~4,000 words, because the subject has been dead 65 years and the writer leaned into that.
- **Welcome:** the Listener-correction beat ("He was 84, and still could not leave a sentence unguarded") gives the piece a built-in meta-lesson about records outliving their subjects — thematically, the piece already understands durability.
- **Jarring, mildly:** discovering (via this run's research) that the TikTok statistic is not merely dated but permanently unverifiable — TikTok removed public hashtag view counts in February 2024, replacing them with post counts. The draft's "has passed" phrasing implies a counter that no longer publicly exists.

## Red flags

None at blocker level. No claim in the draft is already misleading; no ongoing controversy is stated as resolved; no temporary status is stated as permanent; the thesis does not depend on any fleeting event. The four items below are concerns.

**FUTURE-C1 — The #shadowwork statistic is undated, unattributed in visible text, and permanently unverifiable.**

- Passage: "the #shadowwork hashtag has passed 2.3 billion views on TikTok, driven by a viral workbook" (section "Why every personality test you take is still arguing with Carl Jung").
- Reader effect: in 2027 a checking reader finds no view counter on TikTok at all (removed February 2024, per Washington Post/CCDH coverage) and no source in the sentence; the claim reads as stale internet folklore, and it sits in the section whose job is to prove currency. The figure itself dates to 2023 trade coverage (MM+M, per the evidence packet), making it already three years old at publication.
- Evidence: evidence packet CLM-14 ("figure dates to the 2023 coverage cycle and will have moved"; "TikTok's own current counter was not checked"); this run's search confirming the counter's removal.
- Minimum viable repair: date-pin and attribute inline, converting it to a permanently true historical claim on the FUTURE-H2 pattern — e.g. the shape "had passed 2.3 billion views by 2023, per trade coverage" (editor writes the words).
- Expected benefit: the sentence never needs maintenance again and survives TikTok's own product changes.
- Confidence: high.
- Acceptance test: the sentence contains a year and a source signal, and remains true even if TikTok shuts down.

**FUTURE-C2 — The hardcoded corpus key-stat will drift out from under the page.**

- Passage: the key-stat block, "33.3% of the Authors & Thinkers category in the 9takes corpus lands on Type 5, its biggest over-representation of any category" (diagnosis section).
- Reader effect: the number is a literal string computed from n=21 (7 of 21, per packet CLM-13) in a corpus the pipeline grows nightly; adding a handful of author-thinker profiles moves the percentage, after which the page contradicts the /corpus-stats page it links to. A one-profile change flips 33.3% to 31.8% or 36.4%.
- Evidence: packet CLM-13 (n=21, "regenerates each deploy"); the pipeline's nightly cadence is visible in the repo's own logs.
- Minimum viable repair: either bind the number to corpus-stats.json at render (if the people path supports it) or time-stamp the claim in copy (an "as of" signal) or round it to a durable magnitude ("about a third"). The first option solves the class, not the instance.
- Expected benefit: the page and the corpus page can never disagree.
- Confidence: high that drift occurs within twelve months; medium on which repair is cheapest (depends on how the people path renders key-stat blocks — see follow-on question FUTURE-Q2).
- Acceptance test: regenerating corpus-stats.json cannot make the sentence false without also changing the sentence.

**FUTURE-C3 — The closer's year count expires in August 2027.**

- Passage: "Sixty-nine years later, we filed him anyway." (final line before the disclaimer).
- Reader effect: the number is computed from the 1957 Houston interviews to 2026; from roughly mid-2027 it is simply wrong, and it is the last sentence of the piece — the single spot where a stale number does maximum trust damage. The intro's "Nearly seventy years on" (from 1959) is safe through ~2029 and needs nothing.
- Evidence: date arithmetic; packet timeline (Houston filmed August 1957).
- Minimum viable repair: this ending is protected by prior review and should not be reworded casually — so the primary repair is a refresh-calendar entry (change to the 2027-appropriate count next August). If an edit is permitted now, a formulation on the order of "Nearly seventy years later" preserves the beat and survives into the 2030s; the editor decides.
- Expected benefit: the strongest sentence in the piece stays load-bearing instead of becoming its first visible crack.
- Confidence: high (the expiry is arithmetic, not judgment).
- Acceptance test: on 2027-08-13 the final line is still numerically true.

**FUTURE-C4 — Community-vote claims are stated in bare present tense across four locations.**

- Passages: "The largest personality-voting site on the internet types him INFJ and Enneagram 5w4" (intro); "The internet's largest typing communities vote 5w4" (diagnosis); "the big voting sites file him INFJ" (legacy section); "Community votes say 5w4" (FAQ answer 1).
- Reader effect: PDB votes move, and "largest" carries no size citation (packet CLM-03 flags "largest" as interpretation without a metric). A vote flip would falsify four sentences at once, including a FAQ answer that surfaces in search. Probability of a flip inside twelve months is low — the 5w4/INFJ lead is longstanding — but the exposure is structural: nothing marks when the observation was made.
- Evidence: packet CLM-03 and S12 (votes confirmed as of 2026-08-13; totals not extractable).
- Minimum viable repair: one as-of signal at the first occurrence (intro or FAQ), which then licenses the shorthand elsewhere; leave the other three sentences alone.
- Expected benefit: even if votes shift, the page states what was true at a named time and stays honest without edits.
- Confidence: medium (low flip probability, high blast radius).
- Acceptance test: a PDB vote flip does not make any sentence in the piece false, merely dated.
