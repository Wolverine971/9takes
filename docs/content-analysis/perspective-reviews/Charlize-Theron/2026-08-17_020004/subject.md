## Bottom-line verdict

Judged against the only question this seat gets to ask — has she been represented fairly, whether or
not she would accept the typing — this draft is close to good and then breaks in a way that is not
close to anything.

The piece mostly earns its sympathy. It uses her own vocabulary ("living at their whim," "I practiced
avoidance," "wait guys, I'm good"), it separates most observed behavior from inferred motive, it
concedes a criticism without dissolving it into excuse, and in one passage it does something I did
not expect: it holds her stated self-assessment and a less flattering reading side by side and
declines to decide between them. That is fair treatment of a living person.

Then it calls her two daughters "boys," one of whom she publicly identified as a transgender girl in
2019, eight lines below a quotation in which she asks that her children be allowed to tell their own
story. That single word is disqualifying on its own, and the piece cannot publish in this state.

Underneath it sit four more trust breaks that share one shape: at every point where the record
contains her own account of a contested moment, the draft reaches past it. Her explanation of the
_Fury Road_ conflict — on the record, in her own words — is absent from the section that adjudicates
her reputation, replaced by a motive the author supplies. A director's ambiguous remark is aimed at
her when the same passage praises her, and his praise is relocated into the Enneagram accordion. A
vulgar quotation with no traceable source is placed in her mouth. And the headline dates the
beginning of her childhood concealment to an age no source establishes.

None of that is hostility. It reads like a piece that trusted its own thesis more than its subject's
testimony. The repair is not softening — several of these fixes make her _more_ formidable, not less.
It is putting her back in as a witness.

## What landed

**SUBJ-H1 — The concession that does not collapse into excuse.**

> "None of which makes the War Rig fun for anyone else. Being done placating and being pleasant to
> work beside are different achievements, and she has one of them."

This is the most honest sentence in the draft and the reason the empathy turn preceding it is
tolerable rather than exculpatory. It refuses the move this genre almost always makes — explaining a
difficult reputation until the difficulty disappears. It grants the cost to other people without
withdrawing the understanding. Must survive revision verbatim; every other fix in this review is
compatible with it.

**SUBJ-H2 — Holding her self-assessment and the harder reading open at the same time.**

> "'I think it's probably one of the healthiest decisions I ever made,' she says, and she may well be
> right. It is also exactly what a person constructs after a childhood spent learning that the other
> adult in the house is a variable you cannot control. The fortress and the healthy choice can be the
> same building, and nobody asking her about husbands has thought to ask which one it is."

This is the correct handling of interiority for a living subject and it should be the template for the
rest of the piece. It does not overrule her, it does not pretend to know, and it does not flatter her
either. "She may well be right" is doing enormous work. Preserve the hedging structure exactly; if a
later editor tightens this for word budget, the hedges are the last thing that may go.

**SUBJ-H3 — Giving her the last word on her own children.**

> "That will be their story to tell. I can only tell you that this is the best way that I know how to
> be a mother to them. And maybe they'll grow up and tell their story. And I will respect that."

Quoting this in full, unglossed, and letting it answer the question before the article asks it, is
right. It is also what makes SUBJ-R1 so damaging: the draft prints her request and then overrides it
in the same section.

**SUBJ-H4 — Declining to amplify a clinical label about an unnamed ex.**

> "She was with a man who told her she was the problem, and she went to a therapist to fix herself so
> the relationship could survive."

Her own telling uses "narcissistic boyfriend." The draft drops the label, keeps her account intact,
and does not hand a diagnosis to a private individual who cannot answer. That is a quiet, correct
call that most drafts get wrong, and it should not be "restored" by a later pass hunting for colour.

**SUBJ-H5 — Refusing to speculate about her private life.**

> "She has never married. No current partner is publicly established."

The accurate answer, in the FAQ and the body, in a genre that reliably invents one. Preserve the
phrasing "publicly established"; it is precise about what is and is not knowable.

**SUBJ-H6 — Letting a colleague complicate the armour thesis instead of the author doing it.**

> "Her vulnerability, to be honest. And how her strength is built out of vulnerability."
> — Baltasar Kormákur

Placed immediately after the day she could not finish the shoot. The article's thesis is control; the
best evidence against a totalizing version of that thesis is handed to a named third party rather
than smuggled in as authorial hedging. Keep the placement, not just the quote.

**SUBJ-H7 — The ending stays inside her own telling.**

> "At fifteen she was inside a door in Benoni, leaning on it with her mother to keep a man with a gun
> from pushing through, which is how she tells it herself."

The clause "which is how she tells it herself" is the load-bearing part for this perspective, not the
door symmetry. It marks the account as hers. The final line — "She still cannot wear a bra without it
hurting" — closes on a fact she volunteered rather than on a psychological verdict about her. Both
must survive.

## What missed

**The "difficult" section is the whole article's fairness test, and it is argued without her.**
The section names Hardy's lateness, Miller's rebuke, Hardy's "absolute nightmare" note, and Rogen's
physical intimidation. Every voice in it is someone else's. Her own on-record account of that set
exists and is absent (see SUBJ-R2). The author then supplies her motive — "she becomes unwilling to
do it again" — which is a psychological inference standing in the exact place her stated reason
should be. The section's argument may well be true. It is not hers, and the draft never tells the
reader that.

**The promise of "recognizability" is half-kept.** She would recognise the childhood, the injuries,
and the vocabulary. She would not recognise her profession. See SUBJ-C1.

**The intro's framing device outruns its evidence in the title.** "It was a costume, and she was
wearing it before Hollywood ever saw her" is well-supported by the alter-ego material. "The Mask She
Was Handed at Ten" is not supported by anything (SUBJ-R4). The body earns the claim; the headline
invents a number the body cannot pay for.

**Where the draft's own discipline lapses, it lapses toward her.** The testimony ledger excludes a
Patty Jenkins line for lacking independent attribution — genuinely rigorous. The same draft prints an
untraceable quotation about "a hot lesbian movie" in her voice (SUBJ-R5). The standard is applied to
third parties and relaxed on her.

## What surprised me

**Welcome.** The draft concedes that a hostile critic has a point: "Kelly is not wrong that Theron was
performing… Some of it plays as a woman narrating her liberation slightly louder than she needs to."
A piece this sympathetic did not have to do that, and doing it buys the empathy turn real credibility.
This perspective does not demand the subject's approval, and I am not asking for this to go.

**Welcome.** The Bombshell/Kelly reversal — "The woman telling Charlize Theron to have some class had
once cried on camera watching Charlize Theron play her" — is a genuine new connection rather than a
retold fact, and it is built entirely from verifiable public events. It does not require any claim
about her interior life to work.

**Jarring.** The misgendering sits roughly eight lines below her request that her children be allowed
to tell their own story, in a piece whose thesis is that other people kept deciding what she was. The
proximity is not a coincidence a reader will forgive.

**Jarring.** Miller's praise of her — the one description of her professional excellence anywhere in
the draft — appears only inside the Enneagram accordion, repurposed as evidence for a 9 wing, while
his ambiguous criticism is promoted into the body and aimed at her.

## Red flags

### SUBJ-R1 — BLOCKER — The draft misgenders her children, one of whom is publicly a transgender girl

**Passage.** Section "Charlize Theron's family, age, and the husband she never wanted" (snapshot line
377): "Gerda lives up the street in Los Angeles and **has helped raise both boys** since the
beginning."

**Reader effect / trust problem.** This is the only place in the reader-visible article that assigns a
gender to her children, and it is wrong. Theron said publicly in April 2019, "I'm raising two
beautiful, proud, black African girls," and in the _Call Her Daddy_ episode the draft mines throughout
(2 July 2025) her words are "no man's moving into our house **while my daughters are there**." The
error therefore contradicts the article's own primary source. Beyond the factual break, it overrides a
child's publicly stated identity in an article whose entire thesis is about a woman refusing to let
other people decide what she is — and it does so eight lines after quoting her asking that her
children be left to tell their own story. Any reader who knows the family, and any journalist checking
the piece, stops reading here.

**Evidence.** Packet CLM-03; sources S-03 (NBC News, April 2019), S-01, S-22. The packet rates this
"highest risk in the file," and the entity-gap packet's standing instruction is not to characterize
her children beyond what she has publicly stated.

**Note on internal inconsistency.** The draft says "children" in the FAQ, "two adopted children" at
line 361, and "two children" at line 375. Only line 377 says "boys." This reads as a single
unreviewed word, not a considered position — which is why the repair is cheap and why shipping it
would be inexcusable.

**Minimum viable repair.** Replace "both boys" with "both children." Do not fix by swapping in "girls":
the correct minimum is to stop assigning gender to minors at all, which also matches every other
reference in the draft and the entity-gap constraint. If a later pass wants to reference their gender,
it must quote her own April 2019 wording and nothing else.

**Expected benefit.** Removes the piece's only unforced dignity violation, restores consistency with
its own primary source, and eliminates a correction-and-apology scenario post-publication.

**Confidence.** Certain.

**Acceptance test.** `grep -inE "\b(boys|sons)\b" src/blog/people/drafts/Charlize-Theron.md` returns no
hits outside HTML comments, and no reader-visible sentence assigns a gender to either child.

### SUBJ-R2 — BLOCKER — The section that adjudicates her character omits her own account of the incident and substitutes an inferred motive

**Passage.** Section "Why people call Charlize Theron difficult," snapshot lines 299–315, from "Kelly
is not the only pressure point" through "she has one of them."

**Reader effect / trust problem.** The section exists to explain a reputation. It presents Hardy's
lateness, Miller's rebuke, Hardy's "you are an absolute nightmare" note, and Rogen's "physically
intimidated" — and not one word from Theron about the event that generates the label. The draft then
supplies her reason: "An eight who has done that job does not become difficult on purpose. She becomes
unwilling to do it again." That is the author's psychological inference occupying the precise slot
where her stated reason belongs. For this perspective, that is the defining failure mode: not
hostility, but the replacement of a subject's own explanation with a more interesting one.

**Evidence.** Theron's account is on the record, in her own words: "It got to a place where it was kind
of out of hand, and there was a sense that maybe sending a woman producer down could maybe equalize
some of it, because I didn't feel safe" (Kyle Buchanan, _Blood, Sweat & Chrome_, 2022; excerpted in
Vanity Fair, 24 February 2022; verified this review via TheWrap, 2022). She has said she requested a
producer's presence as protection. The packet notes this too, without a source ID (Controversies
section); I verified it independently rather than inherit it. Crucially, the draft's testimony ledger
already sources Hardy's wrap note to the same book and press cycle — so the draft reached into that
source, took the unflattering artifact, and left her stated reason behind. This is not a research gap.

**Why it matters more than a missing quote.** With her account restored, the confrontation reads as a
safety response, which is _stronger_ evidence for the draft's own thesis than the author's inference —
a woman who felt unsafe and went and got institutional backing is the seize-the-controls pattern in
its purest form. The omission costs the article accuracy and argument at once.

**Minimum viable repair.** Insert her account into the _Fury Road_ paragraph, before "Here is what the
word difficult is doing in her case": one sentence of setup plus the "I didn't feel safe" quotation
with its source and date. The empathy turn survives unchanged; it simply stops being the only defence
on offer.

**Expected benefit.** The section stops reading as third parties prosecuting while the author defends.
She becomes a witness in the section that decides how readers judge her.

**Confidence.** High on the omission and its effect; high on the quotation, which I verified through a
secondary source quoting the book excerpt rather than the book itself.

**Acceptance test.** The "difficult" section contains at least one dated, sourced first-person Theron
quotation about the _Fury Road_ conflict, positioned before the author's explanation of the label.

### SUBJ-R3 — BLOCKER — Miller's "There's no excuse for it" is aimed at her when the source does not clearly aim it there, and his praise of her is relocated out of the body

**Passage.** Snapshot line 299: "George Miller addressed it publicly in 2024 and **did not hand her a
pass**: 'There's no excuse for it.'"

**Reader effect / trust problem.** The reader takes away: the director publicly blamed Theron. In the
source, the sentence sits immediately after Miller describes **Hardy** as someone who "had to be
coaxed out of his trailer" and **Theron** as "incredibly disciplined — a dancer by training, which told
in the precision of her performance — and always the first one on set," and it continues "…and I think
there's a tendency in this business to use great performances as an excuse for other disruption that
could be avoided." The remark most naturally targets the disruption, and if it leans toward a person,
it does not lean toward her. The draft's four added words assert a target the quote does not carry.

**Compounding factor specific to this perspective.** Miller's exculpatory testimony about her is not
missing from the draft — it is _present_, moved into the Enneagram Rabbit Hole and repurposed as
evidence for a 9 wing. So the same source's praise of her professionalism is available to the piece
and is spent on typology, while his ambiguous rebuke is promoted into the body and pointed at her.
This is the draft's single concession against its subject, which means the balance the section buys
with it is unearned.

**Evidence.** Packet CLM-07 and S-14 (The Telegraph, May 2024, via Deadline/Variety/TheWrap). Note the
draft's testimony ledger credits this to "Deadline / Variety" when the underlying interview is The
Telegraph — a secondary sourcing nit worth fixing in the same edit.

**Minimum viable repair.** Either (a) restore the contrast in-body — "Miller called Hardy someone who
had to be coaxed out of his trailer and Theron the most disciplined person on set, then said of the
conflict: 'There's no excuse for it'" — or (b) delete "did not hand her a pass" and let the quote
describe the situation rather than the person. Option (a) is better: it costs about fifteen words and
converts a distortion into a genuinely complicating fact.

**Expected benefit.** The concession becomes real rather than manufactured, and a checkable quote stops
contradicting its own source on first inspection.

**Confidence.** High. The full passage is quoted in the packet from the syndicated reporting.

**Acceptance test.** No sentence in the draft claims Miller directed criticism at Theron specifically
unless it cites a source in which he names her as the target.

### SUBJ-R4 — BLOCKER — The title dates the beginning of her concealment to an age no source supports

**Passage.** `title` frontmatter: "Charlize Theron: Enneagram Type 8 and the Mask She Was Handed **at
Ten**." Repeated in the TL;DR accordion, bullet 1: "The costume issued **at ten**."

**Reader effect / trust problem.** This is the article inventing the date on which a living person's
trauma response began, and printing it in the headline and the summary block — the first and last
checkable number a sceptical reader meets. Theron has been conspicuously careful about how she
narrates this history and waited roughly twenty-five years to narrate it at all. The article is not
careful with it. For this perspective the issue is not accuracy alone but authorship: the specific age
is a claim about her inner life that only she could make, and she has not made it.

**Evidence.** Packet CLM-01 and Disputes item 1: no source establishes age ten across the draft's own
research file, the entity-gap packet, or targeted web searching. The originating first-person material
answers a question about **high school**; the nearest anchored age is **13**, when she left for
boarding school. The body of the draft is correctly anchored to school years and never mentions ten,
so the title is writing a cheque the article itself does not cash. Per protocol I reused the packet's
search result rather than re-running it.

**Minimum viable repair.** Retitle without an age — the body's own framing supplies alternatives ("the
mask she built to survive a house," "the alter ego from a happy house") — and cut "at ten" from the
TL;DR bullet. If a source for the age surfaces, it must appear in the body with its date before it
returns to the title.

**Expected benefit.** Removes an unsupported autobiographical assertion from the most prominent
position in the piece, at zero cost to the thesis, which never depended on the number.

**Confidence.** High.

**Acceptance test.** No age-of-onset claim for the alter ego appears in the title, frontmatter,
summary, or body unless a dated first-person source is cited inline.

### SUBJ-R5 — BLOCKER — An unverified, coarse quotation is placed in her mouth

**Passage.** Section "Why Charlize Theron started her own production company," snapshot line 255: "She
has said she could feel what the financiers actually wanted, which was **'a hot lesbian movie with me
and Ricci,'** rather than the film Patty Jenkins was trying to make."

**Reader effect / trust problem.** Quotation marks assert verbatim accuracy. No original has been
located. The result is a vulgar characterization of unnamed industry financiers, attributed to her by
name, on a page that will rank for her name. If the wording is wrong or the framing is hers only in
paraphrase, she carries the reputational and legal exposure for a sentence she may never have said.
The draft's own testimony ledger excludes a Patty Jenkins line precisely because it "could not be
independently attributed" — the same standard, applied here, removes this quotation.

**Evidence.** Packet CLM-13 and Disputes item 5: present in the repository research file with no outlet
or date; targeted searching surfaced no original. The adjacent Harper's Bazaar fitting-room quote _is_
verified (S-12), which makes the contrast within a single section conspicuous.

**Minimum viable repair.** Source it with outlet and date, or cut it. A verified and stronger
replacement is already available in the packet and is not currently in the draft: _Monster_ was
Theron's **first producer credit**, and she put her own salary toward the film's finishing costs
(S-16). That is a harder, checkable fact supporting the identical causal claim — she bought the room —
without putting an untraceable sentence in her mouth.

**Expected benefit.** Removes the piece's clearest quotation-fidelity exposure and upgrades the
section's evidence at the same time.

**Confidence.** High.

**Acceptance test.** Every direct quotation attributed to Theron in the reader-visible draft carries an
outlet and date, inline or in the testimony ledger.

## Specific improvements

Concerns, in priority order. None of these independently breaks trust; all of them affect whether she
is fairly represented.

**SUBJ-C1 — The article is about her body and her childhood and almost never about her work.**
_Location:_ whole piece; most visible in "The damage report" and in the Rabbit Hole wing paragraph.
_Problem:_ the recognizability test fails on her profession. Her career appears as a ledger of injuries
and what each one "bought." _North Country_ — one of the three Best Actress nominations the draft
counts — is never named. The single description of her craft anywhere in the piece is Miller's
"incredibly disciplined… the precision of her performance," and it is quarantined inside the Enneagram
accordion as wing evidence rather than used as a description of how she works. A subject reading this
would recognise every wound and almost nothing of the job.
_Repair:_ move Miller's discipline/precision line into the body — it belongs in the damage report's
framing or the "difficult" section, where it also does repair work for SUBJ-R3 — and name _North
Country_ where the third nomination is counted. Roughly thirty words.
_Benefit:_ the piece stops implying that what is interesting about a three-decade career is what it
cost her physically.
_Confidence:_ high. _Acceptance test:_ at least one reader-visible sentence outside the Rabbit Hole
describes how she works as an actor, and all three Best Actress nominations are named.
_Note:_ the draft is at 4496 of a 4500-word ceiling, so this must displace something. SUBJ-R5's repair
frees space.

**SUBJ-C2 — Unhedged certainty about her emotional and sexual life.**
_Location:_ Rabbit Hole, "Charlize Theron's Instinctual Subtype: sp/so" (line 332): "The sexual instinct
sits last: **she is content, she is not lonely**, and she is not reorganizing her life for anyone."
_Problem:_ "she is not lonely" is a flat assertion about a living woman's interior emotional state,
made by strangers, from a public "wait guys, I'm good" that was a rebuttal to pity rather than a report
on her inner life. It is also in tension with the draft's own content two sections earlier, which
notes she discussed a one-night stand and orgasms publicly — making "sexual instinct last" an
assertion the article itself partially contradicts.
_Repair:_ attribute rather than assert — "she says she is good, and nothing public contradicts her" —
and either drop the sexual-instinct ranking or ground it in something other than her romantic status.
_Benefit:_ keeps the subtype argument while removing the one place the draft claims to know how she
feels.
_Confidence:_ high on the fairness problem; medium on whether the subtype call itself needs changing,
which is outside this seat.
_Acceptance test:_ no sentence states her emotional state as fact without attribution to her own words.

**SUBJ-C3 — The causal spine is stated as fact, not argued as a reading.**
_Location:_ intro, "That is the whole woman in one dental record" (line 132); diagnosis section, "There
is a straight line from the unpredictable house to the fully controlled one, and she walks it in
public" (line 167).
_Problem:_ "the whole woman" and "a straight line" are totalizing formulations — one life, one cause.
The packet is explicit that this spine is an inference the draft owns, and that Theron states a softer
motive for at least one link in the chain (producing, "because I love the nuance of storytelling"), which
the draft quotes and then argues past. She spent twenty-five years refusing to narrate this history at
all, and when she finally did, her stated frame was ownership — "this is where I come from, and I don't
want to have shame about it anymore" — not causation. The article converts her disclosure into an
explanatory key for everything she has done.
_Repair:_ two words of hedge in each spot ("almost the whole woman"; "the line from the unpredictable
house to the fully controlled one is short enough to walk"). The argument survives; the certainty
goes.
_Benefit:_ the reading stays vivid without claiming to have solved her.
_Confidence:_ high on the totalizing language, which is in the text. Medium on the strength of her
public resistance to deterministic readings: a widely reported "I didn't want to feel like a victim"
line is attributed by search summaries to a Howard Stern appearance, and I could not pin it to an
outlet and date, so this finding does not rest on it.
_Acceptance test:_ no sentence claims her childhood explains the whole of her adult life without a
qualifier.

**SUBJ-C4 — Gerda is reduced to the shooting at a moment the article chose for effect.**
_Location:_ end of the family section (line 379): "So the woman who shot Charles Theron in 1991 is the
other adult raising his grandchildren."
_Problem:_ accurate, public, and disclosed by Theron herself — so this is not a blocker. But it lands
as a section-closing punchline, and it converts her mother's worst night into a rhetorical device in a
piece that has just quoted Theron's stated reason for twenty-five years of silence: "I felt like people
were involved that didn't ask to be in the spotlight." The article names the cost of exposure and then
takes the shot.
_Repair:_ keep the fact, lose the reveal cadence — lead with Gerda as co-parent and let the 1991
reference sit inside the sentence rather than at the end of it.
_Benefit:_ the same information, without the article performing the exposure she said she feared.
_Confidence:_ medium. This is a framing judgment, and reasonable editors will differ.
_Acceptance test:_ the section does not end on the shooting.

**SUBJ-C5 — The first interior beat scripts a childhood thought she never described.**
_Location:_ "The alter ego Charlize Theron built before Hollywood cast her" (line 193): "Listen at the
door first. Two steps in the hallway tells you what kind of day it is. Then decide which one of me
walks into the kitchen."
_Problem:_ this is invented first-person interiority attributed to a real child in a real house, with
no quotation marks and only a CSS class to signal it is authorial. The second beat (line 231, the
_Tully_ trailer) is closely derived from her own account and is fine. The first extrapolates a
specific tactical routine — counting footsteps, selecting a self — from a general statement about not
knowing what she was walking into.
_Repair:_ tether it to her language, e.g. lead the beat with her own "not knowing what I was walking
into" and let the imagined detail follow as clearly authorial, or cut the third sentence, which is the
part that invents the most.
_Benefit:_ keeps the device — it works — without the piece narrating her private thoughts as if
recorded.
_Confidence:_ medium. The styling probably signals the device to most readers; the risk is that the
detail is specific enough to be mistaken for testimony.
_Acceptance test:_ every interior beat is traceable to a quoted statement of hers on the same page.

**SUBJ-C6 — An 18-year-old quote is deployed undated as her current, settling position.**
_Location:_ FAQ 4 (line 59) and the Rabbit Hole Type 3 tiebreaker (line 344): "It really irks me that
people think I choose these roles because if I'm not looking attractive, they will take me seriously."
_Problem:_ the remark traces to roughly 2008, when she was defending _North Country_ and _In the Valley
of Elah_. It therefore predates _Fury Road_ (2015) and _Tully_ (2018) — the two transformations the
draft leans on hardest — and it is the evidence the draft uses to settle the Type 3 question. Her
position appears genuinely consistent, so this is not distortion; but presenting an undated 2008 remark
as her verdict on work she had not yet done is a fidelity problem a checker will find.
_Repair:_ date it inline ("she said in 2008") and, if the Type 3 tiebreaker needs current support, pair
it with a post-2015 statement.
_Benefit:_ the tiebreaker stops resting on a quote that predates its own examples.
_Confidence:_ high. _Source:_ packet CLM-11, S-17.
_Acceptance test:_ the quote carries a year wherever it appears.

**SUBJ-C7 — Two dates used as rhetorical anchors are wrong.**
_Location:_ opening sentence (line 130), "In 2017, training for _Atomic Blonde_"; intro paragraph 3,
"Torn intercostal muscles **at fifty** for _Apex_"; and the damage report's closing line (line 245),
"She was fifty when they shot it."
_Problem:_ the _Atomic Blonde_ training and the cracked molars fall in roughly September–November 2015;
2017 is when she _disclosed_ it at CinemaCon. _Apex_ principal photography began February 2025, when
she was 49; she was still 49 at the May 2025 Netflix Upfront. Both errors are load-bearing rhetorically
— the first is the article's opening image, the second is a closing beat used twice.
_Why this is a concern and not a blocker in this seat:_ the events are real and her conduct is
described accurately. These misdate her, they do not misrepresent her.
_Repair:_ "In 2015, training for _Atomic Blonde_…" with the CinemaCon disclosure kept where it is; and
"at forty-nine" / "She was forty-nine when they shot it" — which costs the sentence nothing. If the
draft wants "fifty," it needs a source dating _Apex_ reshoots after 7 August 2025.
_Benefit:_ removes two errors any reader with Wikipedia open will catch in the first and middle beats.
_Confidence:_ high on 2017 (CLM-02); medium-high on the age (CLM-04, where undated reshoots leave a
narrow escape).
_Acceptance test:_ every date attached to a physical injury matches the event, not its disclosure.

**SUBJ-C8 — The draft uses the one word its own scope constraint forbids.**
_Location:_ diagnosis section (line 165): "Living at their whim. That phrase is the **entire
