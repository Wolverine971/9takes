## Bottom-line verdict

The article does something most celebrity-psychology writing refuses to do: it states the case against
itself in the strongest form available, names a cost without excusing it, and schedules a falsifier.
Those are real, and I want to say so before anything else, because a critic who cannot credit them is
not reading fairly.

The sympathetic interpretation is nonetheless **not yet earned**, for a reason that has nothing to do
with the typing call and everything to do with evidence handling. At four separate load-bearing
points the draft's treatment of quotation and ledger breaks in the same direction — toward its own
thesis. A thirty-word excision from the thesis quotation removes exactly the external-validation
register that would blur the draft's Type 3 / Type 6 discriminator. A single published sentence is
severed and its two halves deployed as independent evidence in two different sections, with the first
half recharacterized as an unprompted concession about how she was cast. An awards generalization is
made absolute after the draft's own comments document removing the counterexample. And the single
strongest disconfirming fact in the public record — she concealed her _One Battle After Another_
casting from her _Testaments_ castmates until a trailer outed her — is absent, despite sitting in a
_Variety_ interview the draft quotes elsewhere in the very section where the omission matters most.

Any one of these is arguable. Together they are a pattern, and the pattern is made harder to see by
the fact that the draft's most conspicuous act of doubt — "one thing stays unexplained" — is built on
a casting director's craft compliment whose disclaiming context ("not in a scary way," "the same
thing with Elisabeth Moss") has been stripped. The piece performs even-handedness at the exact spot
where its evidence is weakest.

None of this is fatal. Every repair is local, and in two cases the honest version is a _better_
argument than the one on the page. That is why this is revise, not hold.

One finding I expected to make and cannot: I went looking for adverse professional criticism of her
acting and there is none. Variety's _Testaments_ review is titled "Chase Infiniti Is Impeccable"; THR
cites "terrific performances"; the one mixed review I found aims its reservations at the show, not
at her. The draft is not guilty of suppressing a hostile critical record. It is guilty of never
consulting the critical record at all — which cost it the best anti-skeptic evidence it had.

## What landed

**CRITIC-H1 — The skeptic's case is stated properly, in its strongest form.**

> "The skeptic's case is worth stating properly, because it is the one a smart reader brings. A debut
> performance, directed by Paul Thomas Anderson, played opposite DiCaprio, Sean Penn, Regina Hall,
> Benicio del Toro and Teyana Taylor. The most economical explanation is that she was cast well and
> directed well."

This is the actual strongest objection, not a decoy, and it is put in the reader's mouth rather than
smuggled in to be knocked down. Must survive revision: it is the load-bearing reason this piece
reads as an argument rather than a fan artifact, and every repair below is an attempt to bring the
rest of the draft up to the standard this paragraph sets.

**CRITIC-H2 — The cost is named and not laundered.**

> "Now the cost, because there is one. A person who has made a practice of crediting the room has no
> practice at crediting herself. Call it humility if you want. It is closer to a muscle she never
> built, and it does not grow back on its own."

The empathy turn stops exactly where excuse-making would begin. It relocates the cause of a mocked
behavior without absolving the behavior of consequence. Must survive: this is the sentence that
distinguishes explanation from apologia, and it is the model the rest of the piece should be edited
toward.

**CRITIC-H3 — A falsifier that is scheduled, not rhetorical.**

> "What would change our mind: a documented episode where she took the safe, group-approved option
> under pressure rather than the vivid one. That test is already shot. _The Julia Set_, directed by
> Niki Byrne, is in post-production with Infiniti in the title role, which makes it the first film
> she carries alone... Three years and two roles is a thin window."

Most typing pieces state an unfalsifiable "we could be wrong." This names the specific observation
that would overturn the call and points at a dated, real event that will supply it. Must survive: it
is the piece's strongest claim to intellectual good faith and the only place the thin evidence base
is acknowledged as thin.

**CRITIC-H4 — The refusal to invent an interior beat at the highest-stakes moment.**

From the second-pass notes: a second inner-thought paragraph at the Oscar nominations was rejected
because "no sourced account of that morning exists, so it would be inventing a real person's private
feeling at the piece's highest-stakes moment."

That is the correct call and rare discipline. Must survive: it establishes the standard. My concern
CRITIC-C3 is precisely that the standard is applied here and abandoned in the cold open and the
final line.

## What missed

**The Emmy is offered as a verdict the draft never actually adjudicates.**

> "The Academy's omission is where the reading gets tested, and the test result arrived on schedule."

The promised effect is that the skeptic's case has been answered by evidence. But an Emmy nomination
for a lead role in a prestige franchise sequel is exactly the kind of outcome the skeptic predicts
from good casting, so restating it as "different show, different showrunner, different medium, same
verdict" asserts the rebuttal rather than making it. The material that would actually make it exists
and is not used: _The Testaments_ was received as creatively compromised (THR: "something creatively
suffocated"; But Why Tho: 6.5/10, "cringeworthy dialogue... drag down what could have been a stronger
opening season"), her nomination was the show's **only** acting nomination, and her own notices were
uniformly strong (Variety's review is headlined "Chase Infiniti Is Impeccable"). A performer singled
out of a vehicle critics found weak is a far harder fact for the skeptic than a nomination alone. The
draft had the winning card and never turned it over.

**The "unexplained" admission does not survive its own source.**

The paragraph beginning "One thing stays unexplained, and it belongs here rather than in a footnote"
is the piece's marker of epistemic honesty. It rests on Bruce Miller's "I just didn't have any idea
what she was going to say, if she was going to go left or right," which in context is disclaimed as
"not in a scary way" and immediately likened to Elisabeth Moss. Miller is praising an audition-room
craft quality and naming why he hired her. The draft converts a compliment about acting into a
finding about personality legibility. The intended effect — _look, we admit the type does not explain
everything_ — is undercut once the reader checks the source, and the genuine unexplained fact goes
unmentioned (CRITIC-R1).

## What I expected

- **That the Oscar section would say what the Academy actually did.** The section is titled "What the
  Academy Did Not Give Chase Infiniti" and never mentions that the film won six awards including Best
  Picture and Best Director, or that Sean Penn, Benicio del Toro and Teyana Taylor were nominated in
  supporting categories while she was not. The second fact is the strongest disconfirming reading
  available: the Academy watched the same film and distinguished among the cast.
- **That the draft would tell me where its interiority comes from.** Every first-person quotation in
  the piece is press-interview speech gathered during two awards campaigns and filtered through an
  interviewer and an edit. The packet says so explicitly. The draft never does, and writes lines like
  "Nothing to prove is the part she needs" as if reporting from inside.
- **Some engagement with professional assessment of the acting.** One appreciation essay, labeled
  "critic" in the testimony ledger, is the piece's entire critical apparatus for two performances.
- **Her strongest response to the skeptic.** DiCaprio's defense is quoted; her own posture toward the
  omission is not. Per the packet, DiCaprio also said she had "an amazing attitude" about it — the
  one line that speaks to how she actually took it — and it is absent.

## What surprised me

- **Welcome:** the draft argues against itself in public and dates the test that could sink it. In

- **Welcome:** the _Presumed Innocent_ / dance-crew material is used against the thesis rather than
  for it ("a Three's usual move is to shed whatever has stopped advancing the career. She kept
  everything"). Volunteering a counterexample is the behavior I want more of.

- **Jarring:** the disconfirming fact I consider most important (CRITIC-R1) is in the same _Variety_

## Red flags

### CRITIC-R1 — BLOCKER — The strongest disconfirming fact in the record is omitted, from a source the draft already uses

- **Passage / location:** Absent throughout; belongs in "Why Chase Infiniti Thanks Everyone but
  Herself," whose thesis is _"naming every other person in a room is how someone who has never been
  sure of her standing establishes it."_ Also relevant to the diagnosis section's "one thing stays
  unexplained."
- **Evidence:** _Variety_, Jennifer Maas, June 11, 2026 (packet S-11, verified fact): "I hadn't even
  seen the final cut yet. And I didn't tell anybody that I was in the movie until the trailer started
  to play before 'Sinners' at the movie theater and then Brad came to set and told everybody." The
  draft cites this same article, in the section it is weakest in, for the "rules of Gilead" quote.
- **Reader / trust effect:** The draft's central mechanism is that she converts uncertain standing
  into stated standing. Handed the single most standing-establishing credential available to a young
  actor — lead in the new Paul Thomas Anderson film opposite DiCaprio — she sat on it with a brand-new
  peer group until a trailer outed her. A reader who encounters this fact after reading the piece
  concludes the draft either missed its own best counterexample or declined to print it. Both are
  trust breaks; the second is worse, and the shared source makes the second look likely.
- **Minimum viable repair:** Two to three sentences in the empathy section engaging the fact directly.
  It does not have to be conceded — the honest options are (a) it complicates the image-management
  reading and is left standing as a second unexplained behavior, or (b) it is argued as consistent
  (concealment as its own bid for standing-on-the-work). Either is acceptable. Silence is not.
- **Expected benefit:** Converts the section's weakest flank into its most credible passage, and
  supplies a _real_ unexplained behavior to replace the manufactured one at CRITIC-C4.
- **Confidence:** High. The fact is packet-verified and the source overlap is checkable in the draft.
- **Acceptance test:** The published draft contains an explicit treatment of the concealed _One Battle
  After Another_ credential, cited to _Variety_, June 2026, located in or adjacent to the empathy
  section — and the "unexplained" inventory in the diagnosis section either includes it or states why
  it does not.

### CRITIC-R2 — BLOCKER — The thesis quotation is a thirty-word splice with no ellipsis, and the excision is load-bearing

- **Passage:** Intro — _"Her mantra, given to <em>Elle</em> in October 2025: 'I'm capable of doing
  everything. I'm not here by accident.' / That is a sentence you only need if some part of you
  suspects otherwise."_ Referenced again in the diagnosis section as "the capability claim from the
  top of this piece."
- **Evidence:** _Elle_, Nerisha Penrose, October 1, 2025 (packet S-06, CLM-01). Published: "The thing
  I try to do the most is remind myself that I'm capable of doing everything. **I've been given these
  opportunities. I'm so grateful for them - I can handle it, and the people around me know I can
  handle it. I ground myself and remind myself that I'm here for a reason.** I'm not here by accident."
- **Reader / trust effect:** Two problems, and the second is the serious one. (1) Roughly thirty words
  are removed and presented as a sentence break, so the reader is shown a compression the subject
  never uttered and then told what "that sentence" reveals. (2) The removed middle contains "the
  people around me know I can handle it" — an _external_-reassurance register. The draft's diagnosis
  section rests its Type 3 / Type 6 discriminator on the claim that her self-steadying sentence is "a
  capability claim rather than a safety or backup claim." The excised material is the part that would
  make that discriminator contestable. Whatever the intent, the cut strengthens the draft's argument
  by deleting the evidence against it.
- **Minimum viable repair:** Restore an ellipsis at minimum. Better: quote the passage at fuller
  length and let the interpretive line do more work, or move the compressed version into the
  diagnosis section and explicitly address the "people around me know I can handle it" clause when
  adjudicating Three versus Six.
- **Expected benefit:** Removes a citation-integrity defect on the piece's most-quoted line, and
  forces the type discriminator to survive the full quotation — which, if it does, is worth far more
  than the compressed version.
- **Confidence:** High on the splice (packet-verified verbatim). Medium on how much the discriminator
  weakens; that is an interpretive call the revision should make in the open.
- **Acceptance test:** The quotation as printed either contains an ellipsis marking the omission or
  runs long enough to include the omitted clauses; and the diagnosis section's Three-versus-Six
  paragraph explicitly addresses the external-reassurance clause rather than relying on a quotation
  from which it has been removed.

### CRITIC-R3 — BLOCKER — "Every trophy she carried home was for being new" is false of the complete record, and the draft's notes document narrowing the ledger to make it true

- **Passage:** "What the Academy Did Not Give Chase Infiniti" — the enumeration of NBR Breakthrough,
  Chicago Most Promising Performer, San Diego and New York breakthrough prizes, the Florida Pauline
  Kael Breakout and Santa Barbara's Virtuoso, closing on _"Breakthrough. Most promising. Breakout.
  Emerging. Every trophy she carried home was for being new."_
- **Evidence:** Packet S-01 / CLM-06. The same season she also won **AWFJ Best Stunt Performance**
  (an individual award, no novelty term in the category), **Georgia Film Critics Best Ensemble** and
  **St. Louis Film Critics Best Ensemble**. The draft's own FORMULA FINGERPRINT / second-pass notes
  state: "Stunt award pulled out of that ledger sentence so the 'new' claim is literally true." The
  two ensemble wins appear not to have been considered.
- **Reader / trust effect:** This sentence is the hinge of the section's emotional argument — a season
  of potential-prizes reading to a Three as an open verdict. A reader who checks the awards table
  finds a counterexample that the draft's own comments show was identified and removed rather than
  addressed. That is the specific move that turns a strong observation into an untrustworthy one, and
  it retroactively taxes every other ledger claim in the piece.
- **Minimum viable repair:** Quantify instead of universalizing, and absorb the counterexample. The
  honest version is stronger: of the individual prizes she won for this performance, the only one not
  framed around novelty was for **stunt work** — i.e. the single non-novelty trophy she took home was
  for physical execution rather than for acting. That sharpens the argument instead of blunting it.
- **Expected benefit:** The claim becomes checkable and survives checking; the section gains a better
  line than the one it loses.
- **Confidence:** High. Award categories are matters of record and the draft's own comment documents
  the exclusion.
- **Acceptance test:** No universal quantifier over her trophies survives in the section unless the
  AWFJ stunt award and both ensemble wins are consistent with it; and the stunt award appears in or
  beside the ledger sentence rather than only elsewhere in the piece.

### CRITIC-R4 — BLOCKER — A single published sentence is severed and its halves used as independent evidence in two sections, with the first half recharacterized

- **Passage:** "What the Academy Did Not Give Chase Infiniti" — _"She concedes the neighboring ground
  herself, unprompted, in <em>W</em> magazine: 'I know that I've had a privileged entrance into
  Hollywood.'"_ And in the rabbit hole, as 3w2 wing evidence: _"I wanted to help the other girls
  whenever I could..."_
- **Evidence:** _W_, Lynn Hirschberg, June 15, 2026 (packet S-02, CLM-02). The published sentence is
  unitary: "I know that I've had a privileged entrance into Hollywood, **so I wanted to help the other
  girls whenever I could.**"
- **Reader / trust effect:** In context the clause is a preamble explaining why she mentored younger
  castmates — a statement about obligation, not an admission about how she was cast. The draft
  presents it as the subject conceding the skeptic's central point about her, at the exact moment the
  piece is asking for credit for facing that point. Separately, the two halves are then made to
  corroborate two different claims in two different sections without the reader being able to know
  they are one sentence, which manufactures the appearance of two independent data points from one.
  This is the most consequential of the four because it occurs inside the passage the piece most
  wants to be trusted on.
- **Minimum viable repair:** Print enough of the sentence for the purpose clause to be visible, and
  downgrade the characterization from a concession about her casting to what it is — an
  acknowledgment of privileged entry offered as the reason she helped others. If the section needs a
  concession from the subject, source one that is a concession, or state plainly that she has not
  publicly addressed the skeptic's case.
- **Expected benefit:** The steelman survives on honest footing; the wing evidence in the rabbit hole
  stops silently double-counting.
- **Confidence:** High on the severance (packet-verified). Medium-high on the speech-act
  recharacterization, which is a reading of context rather than of wording.
- **Acceptance test:** The Oscar section's rendering of the quotation includes the "so I wanted to
  help the other girls" clause or an explicit note of what follows; and the same sentence is not
  presented in two sections as two independent items of evidence without cross-reference.

## Specific improvements

**CRITIC-C1 — CONCERN — State what the Academy actually did, including the parts that hurt.**
Location: "What the Academy Did Not Give Chase Infiniti," opening paragraph. The section reports 13
nominations and her absence, and stops. Packet S-08/S-10: the film **won six including Best Picture
and Best Director**, and **Penn, del Toro and Taylor were nominated in supporting categories while
she was not**. Effect: withholding the co-star nominations removes the strongest disconfirming
reading — that the Academy distinguished among this cast rather than overlooking the film's young
lead — and a reader who knows awards season will notice the gap and read the omission as protective.
Repair: one sentence adding the co-star nominations and the Best Picture win, then let the section's
existing argument absorb them; the "decorated for potential, not for the thing itself" reading
actually survives this and reads better for having faced it. Benefit: converts the section's most
obvious vulnerability into evidence of good faith. Confidence: high. Acceptance test: the section
names at least the supporting-category nominations of her co-stars before it reaches DiCaprio's
defense.

**CRITIC-C2 — CONCERN — Engage the critical record; the draft's own best argument is sitting in it.**
Location: "The Master Class Chase Infiniti Was Never Enrolled In" (the lone Renshaw paragraph), and
"What the Academy Did Not Give Chase Infiniti." One performance assessment appears in the whole
piece, drawn from The FADER essay "Why the real star of One Battle After Another is Chase Infiniti,"
whose author the testimony ledger labels "critic" though packet S-05 records it as an appreciation
essay, not a review. My own search found the record is broad and favorable: Variety's _Testaments_
review is headlined "Chase Infiniti Is Impeccable"; THR notes "terrific performances" while judging
the show "creatively suffocated"; But Why Tho gives the series 6.5/10, faulting "cringeworthy
dialogue" and Daisy's unevenness while writing that "Chase Infiniti handles the restraint and
pressure placed on Agnes with care." Effect: as written, the reader cannot tell whether the draft
sampled the most flattering available source or whether it is the only one — and the omission costs
the piece its best rebuttal, since a performer praised out of a vehicle critics found weak, and left
as that show's only acting nominee, is far harder for the skeptic than a bare nomination. Repair:
relabel Renshaw accurately and add one or two sentences of independent critical assessment to the
Emmy paragraph, foregrounding the show-versus-performance asymmetry. Benefit: the anti-skeptic
argument stops being asserted and starts being demonstrated. Confidence: high on availability
(sources below), medium on which specific review to pin, since Variety and THR both redirect
automated fetches. Acceptance test: at least one performance assessment from an outlet other than
The FADER appears in the body, and the testimony ledger no longer calls Renshaw a critic.

**CRITIC-C3 — CONCERN — "She was shaking" is an inference printed as fact, and the last line makes it permanent.**
Locations: cold open ("She was shaking. Nobody could tell."), TL;DR ("The casting director is certain
she was shaking"), close ("The shaking never showed. It also never stopped."). Packet CLM-04:
Kulukundis said "**I'm sure she was shaking**," explicitly marking the inference as her own; no
first-person confirmation exists. Effect: the TL;DR's "is certain" is fair; the cold open's bare
assertion is an upgrade from a third party's guess to reported fact, and the closing line extends
that guess into a claim that she is _still_ shaking, permanently — an assertion about a living
person's present interior state that no source supports and that the draft's own second-pass notes
elsewhere refuse to make ("it would be inventing a real person's private feeling"). Repair: the cold
open can keep nearly all its force with the attribution intact ("The casting director is sure she was
shaking. Nobody could tell."); the close needs the metaphor to be visibly a metaphor rather than a
report. Benefit: the piece's spine stops resting on an unattributed inference, and the discipline it
already shows at the Oscar beat becomes consistent. Confidence: high. Acceptance test: no sentence in
the piece asserts as bare fact that she was shaking, and the final line does not assert a continuing
present-day internal state as reportage.

**CRITIC-C4 — CONCERN — Miller's "left or right" is craft praise, not psychological evidence.**
Location: diagnosis section, "One thing stays unexplained..."; pointed at again in the rabbit hole's
counterarguments. Packet CLM-09 / S-16: Miller adds "not in a scary way" and immediately compares her
to Elisabeth Moss — "she's not volatile, but she is unpredictable" — framing it as the reason he cast
her. Effect: an observation about audition-room craft is used to establish that "something in her
working process is not [legible]," which is a claim about personality. Because this is the draft's
showcase admission of uncertainty, a reader who checks it concludes the humility was staged. Repair:
either restore the disclaiming context and demote the quote to supporting color, or replace it as the
"unexplained" anchor with the concealed-credential fact at CRITIC-R1, which is genuinely unexplained.
Benefit: the piece's central gesture of honesty starts resting on something that survives its source.
Confidence: medium-high — the quotation is accurate and the draft does preserve "he cast her
because," so this is a category error rather than a misquotation. Acceptance test: wherever the quote
appears, "not in a scary way" or the Moss comparison appears with it, or the quote no longer carries
the "unexplained behavior" role.

**CRITIC-C5 — CONCERN — Disclose that the interiority is reconstructed from awards-campaign press.**
Locations: throughout; sharpest at "Watch what she reaches for in those moments. She reaches for a
frame... Nothing to prove is the part she needs," and at the inner-thought paragraph. Packet research
limitations 3 and 6: `no_transcripts` stands, there is no long-form unscripted primary material, and
every first-person quotation is filtered through an interviewer and an edit — most of it gathered
during two awards campaigns, a context that structurally rewards warmth, gratitude and
credit-sharing. Effect: the draft narrates from inside a person whose entire recorded speech is
publicity, without ever telling the reader that. A skeptical reader who knows how awards press works
discounts the whole psychological apparatus at once, rather than argument by argument. Repair: one
clause somewhere early acknowledging that the record is press-interview speech from two campaigns,
plus softening the two or three sentences that report her needs rather than infer them. Benefit:
inoculates the piece against its most efficient dismissal, at the cost of about fifteen words.
Confidence: high. Acceptance test: the body contains an explicit acknowledgment of the nature and
limits of the evidence base, and no sentence outside the marked inner-thought paragraph states what
she needs or feels without attribution or hedge.

**CRITIC-C6 — CONCERN — The childhood-to-type causal chain outruns two campaign quotes.**
Location: "Chase Infiniti's Childhood in Indianapolis" — _"That calibration is where a Three's
equation gets written. A child who senses that her standing in a room is provisional..."_ and, in the
intro, _"She built the ability to look like she belonged in a room years before anyone handed her a
razor in one."_ Evidence: the entire support is _Dazed_, December 2025 — she "can definitely
understand feeling like you don't fully belong in your environment" and related to being biracial
"and feeling like you're missing a part of yourself." As the packet notes, these are a working actor
describing a character connection during an awards campaign. Effect: a stated affinity with a
fictional character's isolation is converted into a formative childhood mechanism, and that mechanism
is then made the origin of her professional composure. The move is doing a lot of work on very little
evidence, and it is additionally freighted because the causal engine is her biracial identity —
territory where a confident etiological claim from two interview lines will read to a skeptical
reader as overreach regardless of sympathy. Repair: mark the inference as inference. "She has
described X; if that is where the calibration started, then..." costs one clause and changes the
claim's epistemic status without weakening the writing. Benefit: keeps the piece's most resonant
material while removing its most attackable claim. Confidence: medium-high. Acceptance test: no
sentence in the childhood section asserts as established that her childhood produced the type
structure; the causal link is visibly marked as the draft's reading.

**CRITIC-C7 — CONCERN — "Unprompted" is asserted twice and cannot be known from the sources used.**
Locations: diagnosis section, "She leaves it in, unprompted, in interview after interview"; Oscar
section, "She concedes the neighboring ground herself, unprompted." Evidence: none of the packet's
sources print the interviewer's questions; S-02, S-04, S-06 and S-07 were read through automated
summarizers. Effect: unpromptedness is the entire evidentiary value of both observations — volunteered
material means something different from answered material — and it is exactly what an interview
transcript would be needed to establish. Repair: drop the word in both places, or replace with what
is actually observable ("repeatedly, across interviews and outlets"). Benefit: removes two
unsupportable claims at zero argumentative cost, since repetition across outlets carries most of the
intended weight. Confidence: high. Acceptance test: the word "unprompted" does not appear in the
draft unless a source printing the question is cited.

**CRITIC-C8 — CONCERN — The _Hairspray_ / Rizzo sentence is unresolved and internally implausible as written.**
Location: childhood section — "At ten she was cast in a local production of <em>Hairspray</em>, and
she has pinned the exact moment the thing took hold: watching Rizzo sing, and thinking, I want to do
this." Evidence: packet CLM-03. Rizzo is a character in _Grease_, not _Hairspray_, and the sentence's
construction implies otherwise. WFYI (S-09), the most local and most theatre-focused source located,
lists seven Summer Stock Stage credits 2016-2021, none of them _Hairspray_, and records no age-ten
performance; Tier 4 sourcing describes the age-ten event as a school showcase and the Rizzo moment as
watching a production of _Grease_. Effect: two checkable specifics in one sentence, neither confirmed
by a Tier 1 or Tier 2 source, in the section that anchors the origin argument — and the musical-theatre
error is the kind any reader of this subject's own background will catch instantly. Repair: split the
claims and source them separately, or write the beat without naming the production until a local
source confirms it. Benefit: removes the most easily falsified sentence in the piece. Confidence:
high that the sentence is unsafe as written; the packet marks the underlying facts genuinely
unresolved rather than wrong. Acceptance test: the sentence no longer places Rizzo adjacent to
_Hairspray_, and any named production is attributable to S-09 or an equivalent local source.

**CRITIC-C9 — CONCERN — Two datestamps are wrong in a draft whose own ledger certifies them.**
Locations: childhood section, "She said that to <em>W</em> magazine in 2025, about parallel parking"
(packet CLM-13: the _W_ cover interview publishes **January 6, 2026**; the same source governs the
"scaredy-cat" details used in the diagnosis section); and "In 2026... she was still turning up in

its own sentence, since "she kept doing this _after_ the career took off" is the whole point. Both
also contradict the draft's source-audit note claiming every load-bearing slot is "pinned to outlet +
year," which is the kind of self-certification a checking reader tests first. Repair: correct both;
for the music videos, either restrict the claim to Ateez or reframe around the K-pop crew, which
carries the argument without the dating problem. Benefit: the argument that she retained
non-advancing commitments survives on accurate facts. Confidence: high. Acceptance test: the _W_
cover interview is dated 2026 wherever cited, and no sentence places the Tyler, the Creator video
after the awards season.
