> "Paul, in that screen test, literally handed her a razor and was like, 'Shave Leo's face.'"
>
> Cassandra Kulukundis, casting director, [TheWrap, 2025](https://www.thewrap.com/chase-infiniti-one-battle-after-another-interview/)

<p class="firstLetter">She had been auditioning for months by then. Karate lessons. Weapons practice. Chemistry reads, callbacks, costume fittings for a part that was not hers. Then Paul Thomas Anderson put a straight razor in her hand and pointed her at Leonardo DiCaprio.</p>

Chase Infiniti was twenty-three. She had exactly one job on her résumé. She lathered his face and shaved it.

"I'm sure she was shaking," Kulukundis told TheWrap in 2025, "but she held it all in and didn't look like she missed a beat."

She was shaking. Nobody could tell.

Two years on, that first film has earned her best-actress nominations from the Golden Globes, BAFTA, SAG and Critics Choice, and her second lead role has earned her a 2026 Emmy nomination. Every profile reaches for the same word. Poise.

She has a more useful word for it, and she used it about lace.

Because here is the thing the poise story leaves out. She grew up biracial in Indianapolis, on the seam between the white neighborhoods and the Black ones, and she told <em>Dazed</em> in December 2025 that she understood her character's isolation from the inside: "I can definitely understand feeling like you don't fully belong in your environment." She told that same <em>Dazed</em> interviewer in 2025 that she did not resemble the other mixed kids at her school, and described the specific ache of "feeling like you're missing a part of yourself."

She built the ability to look like she belonged in a room years before anyone handed her a razor in one.

Her mantra, given to <em>Elle</em> in October 2025: "I'm capable of doing everything. I'm not here by accident."

That is a sentence you only need if some part of you suspects otherwise.

## What is Chase Infiniti's personality type?

### Chase Infiniti Is an Enneagram Type 3 (The Achiever)

Chase Infiniti is an Enneagram Type 3, the Achiever, with a 2 wing. [Threes](/enneagram-corner/enneagram-type-3) measure themselves by output, and they learn young to read a room and hand it what it rewards. Underneath sits a suspicion that with the output stripped away, there would be nothing left there to love.

Watch how she talks about the audition process that nearly consumed 2023. Six months of callbacks with no contract, no salary and no guarantee. Most people describe that stretch as uncertainty. She described it as school.

"I treated every single callback and chemistry read like a master class," she told TheWrap in 2025. "I would observe them, and I would try to pick up everything that I could and be a sponge."

Then she trained in mixed martial arts for four straight months for a role she had not been given.

Ambition is the boring part. Every type has a version of it. The tell is what she converts pressure into. Handed a razor, she produced a performance. Handed six months of limbo, she produced a curriculum. Handed a Gilead handicraft she could not do, she produced this line for <em>W</em> magazine in June 2026: "I've struggled with embroidery and lace making. We all did. I'm at a point now where it's convincing on camera."

Convincing. She set the bar inside somebody else's eye.

**The pressure test.** Type 6 is the one reading that could displace this one, and it has evidence behind it. She narrates her career almost entirely through mentors: Jake Gyllenhaal taught her to raise the stakes, Ruth Negga told her not to be scared, Anderson and DiCaprio were "incredible leaders and guides on that film," she told TheWrap in 2025, and Elisabeth Moss is "an encyclopedia." At her IndieWire Honors tribute in 2025 she thanked the crew and said she shadowed anyone who would let her watch. She calls herself a scaredy-cat and covers her eyes at horror movies. That is a real case.

It breaks on the razor. Under acute pressure, a Six's anxiety becomes the material; they reach for the authority in the room. She produced a surface instead. And the sentence she reaches for when she steadies herself is the capability claim from the top of this piece, the one about not being here by accident. She pairs it with an instruction she gave to <em>Elle</em> in 2025: "I try to remember to breathe." The mentors are real. They read as a Three's healthiest instinct rather than a Six's core, and the full case is in the rabbit hole below.

One thing stays unexplained, and it belongs here rather than in a footnote. Threes usually delete the shaking from the story. She leaves it in, unprompted, in interview after interview, in the same conversations where every other edge has been sanded smooth. Bruce Miller, who cast her in <em>The Testaments</em>, saw the same thing from the other side of the camera. He told The Hollywood Reporter in 2026 that he cast her because he could not predict her: "I just didn't have any idea what she was going to say, if she was going to go left or right." Threes are legible on purpose. Something in her working process is not, and three years of public record is not enough to say what.

## Chase Infiniti's Childhood in Indianapolis

What is legible about her has an origin, and it starts with the name she was given.

Her parents watched <em>Batman Forever</em> and decided Nicole Kidman's Chase Meridian was the most formidable woman they had ever seen on a screen. For the middle name they took Buzz Lightyear's line about infinity. Then they handed the whole thing to a daughter and let her carry it.

She has thought about this. "I don't want things to hold me back or to stop me," she has said of her own name. "I am pushing myself creatively and chasing greatness."

Most kids get a name. She got an instruction.

The house was loud with music. Her father, who runs a construction and remodeling business, had played drums in a jazz band. There were musicals, plays, weekend bike rides, tennis and track. At ten she was cast in a local production of <em>Hairspray</em>, and she has pinned the exact moment the thing took hold: watching Rizzo sing, and thinking, I want to do this.

Then there is the other half of that childhood, the half she brought to Willa. She grew up on the line between predominantly white and predominantly Black neighborhoods, in a biracial family, at a French-immersion private school and then a big public high school. She has said she did not resemble the other mixed kids around her. What she describes is quieter than drama. A low-grade, permanent calibration: reading a room to work out how much of yourself will fit in it.

<div class="pull-quote">"I will make sure that I fit. If it's a 38,000-point turn, I'm going to get it."</div>

She said that to <em>W</em> magazine in 2025, about parallel parking.

That calibration is where a Three's equation gets written. A child who senses that her standing in a room is provisional does not conclude that rooms are unsafe. She concludes that standing is something you earn on arrival, every time, by being good enough that nobody has to decide whether you belong. She never experienced any of that as a lie about herself. She took it for a toll everyone pays.

Then she saw Zendaya. "I remember being like, this girl looks like me," she told <em>Elle</em>. "She is mixed. She is singing, acting, dancing, doing the thing that I want to do." What she took from it was proof of concept. The shape she was trying to make herself into already existed, and a room had already let it in.

At Columbia College Chicago she majored in musical theatre, trained people in kickboxing to pay for it, and co-founded a K-pop cover-dance crew with her friends. Her first professional job arrived in early 2023, playing Jake Gyllenhaal's daughter on <em>Presumed Innocent</em>. It put her in Los Angeles alone for five months at twenty-two, the longest she had ever been away from home.

She kept the dance crew anyway. In 2026, with a full awards season and an Emmy nomination behind her, she was still turning up in music videos for Tyler, the Creator and Ateez. Worth noticing, because a Three's usual move is to shed whatever has stopped advancing the career. She kept everything.

Kulukundis, who ran the search, needed someone who could do everything at once: "I definitely needed somebody who could kick some butt... heartbreak. She had to run the gamut of emotions." What she found, she said, was simpler than that. "She's so special. She's so unique. She just felt like such a real person."

Anderson's own summary, sent by email, is the sharpest description of her anyone has offered: "Chase is extremely agile and quick on her feet. She is also able to fight the corner for her character and what she believes."

Agile and quick on her feet. He is describing an adaptive instinct as a professional asset. It is the same instinct she built on a neighborhood line in Indiana, running at full speed in a director's chair.

What reached the screen is smaller than the audition story makes it sound. Willa is a revolutionary's teenage daughter trying to have an ordinary adolescence, and Infiniti plays most of her at the register of an ordinary kid: chewing her father out for drunk driving, correcting him on a schoolfriend's pronouns. Then federal agents take her and the whole film reorganizes around getting her back. Writing in <em>The FADER</em> in September 2025, David Renshaw said she "holds her own alongside DiCaprio, Penn, and Benicio del Toro" and gives Willa "a steeliness that makes you leave the theater thinking having faith in the kids isn't misplaced." She also did her own action work. The Alliance of Women Film Journalists gave her its stunt-performance award for it, on a first film, at twenty-three.

## What Chase Infiniti Does When She Gets Scared

Getting cast did not settle the question the razor asked. Her first day on the film, they shot the breakfast scene. The one from the self-tape. The one she had been carrying since the cold open, now with DiCaprio across the table and Anderson behind the camera.

"I remember being very nervous that day," she said.

<p class="inner-thought">You know this scene. You have known it for a year. You did it alone in a room with nobody watching and it was good enough to get you here, which means the only variable left in it is you, sitting in this chair, in front of the person you grew up watching. Breathe. Do not let it into your hands.</p>

Watch what she reaches for in those moments. She reaches for a frame. Before her first red carpet somebody gave her a line she has repeated since: "You have nothing to prove and everything to show. Enjoy the moment, because you're gonna blink, and it's gonna be over." She keeps the sentence because it relocates the whole event from a test into a display. Nothing to prove is the part she needs.

A frame handles the entrance. It does not reach the part where a Three's fluency gets brittle under real stress and the internal critic goes to work; the pattern is closer to [how a Type 9 stalls out](/enneagram-corner/enneagram-types-in-stress) than most people expect. You can hear the edge of it in the one thing she cannot make herself say.

Asked whether she is proud of the work, she told <em>Dazed</em>: "For me personally, it's hard for me to say I'm happy with what I did." She added that when the feeling does arrive, she tries to hang on to it: "I've been trying to make sure I don't forget that feeling."

A debut with four major best-actress nominations, and she is rationing the memory of feeling good about it.

### Chase Infiniti's Wing: 3w2

The evidence points to a two <a href="/enneagram-corner/enneagram-wings-complete-guide">wing</a> rather than a four. A 3w4 climbs by being distinct, and carries an aesthetic melancholy about the gap between the self and the product. A 3w2 climbs by being wanted, and the warmth is load-bearing.

Almost everything she says in public is relational. <a href="/personality-analysis/teyana-taylor">Teyana Taylor</a> is her "movie mom." Regina Hall is the "central force" of the film. She describes the set as a place where she watched "what great, gentle leadership looks like." On <em>The Testaments</em> she reports taking Ruth Negga's advice and passing it down: "I wanted to help the other girls whenever I could. I carried that into <em>The Testaments</em> and tried to pass it along to my costars too." That is a Three who has decided the way to be indispensable is to be generous.

The four-wing case is not empty. The "missing a part of yourself" language about being biracial has genuine four texture, and a musical-theatre kid who cannot say she likes her own work is walking familiar 3w4 ground. Call it 3w2 with a live four shadow.

### Chase Infiniti's Instinctual Subtype: Social (so/sx)

The social Three wants to be recognized as valuable by a group, and the whole record points there. Her formative wound sits at the level of the group: where she stands in it, and whether the room has decided she counts. Her <a href="/enneagram-corner/enneagram-instinctual-subtypes">dominant instinct</a> shows in what she notices: hierarchies, who is leading well, who taught whom, who deserves to be named. She thanked crew members by function at IndieWire Honors. She told an interviewer that the film matters because "we're in an era where new stories aren't being told," which is a status-of-the-field concern rather than a personal one.

Sexual instinct second, on the strength of the physical commitment: four months of MMA, an award from the Alliance of Women Film Journalists for stunt work on her first film, a background training people in kickboxing. Self-preservation looks last.

### Stress and Growth Arrows

Under pressure a Three moves toward Nine and goes flat, disengaged, quietly unable to start. The public record is too short and too kind to show this clearly, which is worth saying plainly. The closest visible trace is her difficulty landing on her own work, which is what a stalled Three sounds like from inside: the assessment never completes.

In growth a Three moves toward Six, and this is where she is unusually legible. The mentor-mapping, the crew-thanking, the sponge posture, the loyalty to the ensemble, the passing-down of Ruth Negga's advice: those are Six virtues, and she reaches for them constantly. Most Threes have to be dragged toward that. She got there early, which is the best argument that the composure is not brittle.

### Counterarguments: Why Chase Infiniti Might Not Be Type 3

The counterargument with real teeth is Type 6. She self-describes as a scaredy-cat, names her nerves in interview after interview, and organizes her entire professional identity around authorities she trusts. A polished Six with a strong three-fixation would look almost identical from outside.

The second counter is Bruce Miller's unpredictability read, which sits in the main body above because it is the one behavior the type does not account for.

What would change our mind: a documented episode where she took the safe, group-approved option under pressure rather than the vivid one. That test is already shot. <em>The Julia Set</em>, directed by Niki Byrne, is in post-production with Infiniti in the title role, which makes it the first film she carries alone: no Anderson, no DiCaprio, no ensemble to be the fabric of. Three years and two roles is a thin window. Revisit this page after that one opens.

## Why Chase Infiniti Thanks Everyone but Herself

The same difficulty has a public face. There is a version of Chase Infiniti that critics of the awards-season It Girl already have on file. The one who answers every question about her own performance by praising somebody else. Asked why the film landed, she said: "It's Paul Thomas Anderson. People were gonna love anything that he does." Asked about her scene partners, she talks about Hall's subtlety and Taylor's color. Asked about her breakout, she thanks the crew who let her shadow them. The cynical read writes itself: media training, no interior, nothing to quote.

The cynics have the behavior right. They have the cause backwards.

Go back to the seam in Indianapolis, to a kid who has already learned that her presence in a room is a question other people are quietly answering. Read modesty into it if you like, or calculation. What it actually does is convert a room she is unsure of into a room she has a stated relationship with. If you have said out loud what each person there is worth, you have made yourself the person who noticed. That is a position. It cannot be taken from you by a bad review.

This is the fear a Three carries doing the only useful thing it can do. The dread of being disposable turns outward and becomes attention, and the attention is real. Anderson noticed she would "fight the corner for her character." Dowd noticed the preparation: "They're wonderful actors. They're very, very well-prepared." Taylor, who plays her mother, put it to her directly, and it doubles as the sentence Infiniti keeps trying to prove: "You were chosen for a reason."

Now the cost, because there is one. A person who has made a practice of crediting the room has no practice at crediting herself. Call it humility if you want. It is closer to a muscle she never built, and it does not grow back on its own.

## What the Academy Did Not Give Chase Infiniti

Then the Academy declined to credit her too. In January 2026, <em>One Battle After Another</em> collected thirteen Oscar nominations. Her name was not on any of them.

DiCaprio, asked about it, did not hedge: "She was the fabric of this movie. She was the heart and soul." He added that there would be no <em>One Battle After Another</em> without the performance she gave.

Run the full ledger and a pattern comes into focus. In the same season she was nominated for best actress by the Golden Globes, BAFTA, SAG and Critics Choice, and won none of the four. She also won the National Board of Review's Breakthrough Performance, Chicago Film Critics' Most Promising Performer, breakthrough prizes from the San Diego and New York critics, the Florida Film Critics' Pauline Kael Breakout Award, and Santa Barbara's Virtuoso Award for emerging talent.

Breakthrough. Most promising. Breakout. Emerging. Every trophy she carried home was for being new.

For a Three that is a particular kind of torture. Potential is a compliment about a person you have not become yet, and a Three lives in the ledger of what she has already produced and whether it cleared the bar. So a whole season of being told she is going to be somebody arrives as one sentence: the verdict on what you actually did is still open. The four prizes that measured the performance itself went to other women.

The skeptic's case is worth stating properly, because it is the one a smart reader brings. A debut performance, directed by Paul Thomas Anderson, played opposite DiCaprio, Sean Penn, Regina Hall, Benicio del Toro and Teyana Taylor. The most economical explanation is that she was cast well and directed well. She concedes the neighboring ground herself, unprompted, in <em>W</em> magazine: "I know that I've had a privileged entrance into Hollywood."

The Academy's omission is where the reading gets tested, and the test result arrived on schedule. Six months later she was nominated for a Primetime Emmy for Outstanding Lead Actress in a Drama Series for <em>The Testaments</em>, reported as the only first-time nominee in her category, and she won the Gotham TV Award for the same role. Different show, different showrunner, different medium, same verdict.

## What "Convincing on Camera" Means to Chase Infiniti

The role that got her the Emmy nomination is a girl with two names.

In <em>The Testaments</em> she plays Agnes MacKenzie, the immaculately raised daughter of a Gilead commander, being groomed for a marriage the regime has arranged for her. Agnes is not her name. She was born Hannah Bankole and taken as a small child, and what survives of that is a single unattached word: she remembers being called Hannah and has no idea why. Everything else about her is a life somebody else assigned, worn so well that it passes. "She knows the rules of Gilead so well that she can navigate through them easier than anybody just coming in would," Infiniti told <em>Variety</em> in June 2026.

An actor who spent her childhood working out how much of herself a room would take got handed a part about a girl who is entirely the room's construction, and who is starting to feel the seam.

The seam is where the lace comes in. The young women of Gilead have to embroider and make lace on camera, and she could not do either. She trained. Everyone in the cast struggled with it.

Here is where she stopped: "I'm at a point now where it's convincing on camera."

Read that standard again. The bar sits in the viewer. Do those hands read as hands raised inside that world? She spent months on a skill and graded herself on legibility. It is the same grade she has been giving herself since she was a girl in Indianapolis working out how much of herself would fit in a given room, and it is the same grade Kulukundis handed down after the screen test, when she said the shaking never reached the surface.

She has an Emmy nomination now, a film that pulled thirteen Oscar nominations without her name on one of them, and a screen test the people who were in that room still talk about. Ask her whether any of it was good and she will tell you, as she told <em>Dazed</em> in 2025, that it is hard for her to say she is happy with what she did.

The shaking never showed. It also never stopped.
