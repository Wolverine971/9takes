## Identity and scope

**Verified fact.** Chase Infiniti Payne, born May 1, 2000, in Indianapolis, Indiana. Professionally
credited as Chase Infiniti. American film and television actress. Her father is African-American and
her mother is white. (Wikipedia, S-01.)

**Verified fact.** Her entire professional record spans 2023 to the present: one television
supporting role (_Presumed Innocent_, Apple TV+, 2024, as Jaden Sabich, 8 episodes), one lead film
role (_One Battle After Another_, dir. Paul Thomas Anderson, 2025, as Willa Ferguson / Charlene
Calhoun), one lead television role (_The Testaments_, Hulu, 2026, as Agnes MacKenzie / Hannah
Bankole, 10 episodes), one film in post-production (_The Julia Set_, dir. Niki Byrne, as Julia), and
two music-video appearances. (S-01.)

**Why the article covers her.** The repository's own queue entry gives the commercial rationale:
`docs/blog-automation/backlog-queue.json` lists her at priority 62, "gen-z-hollywood-pop-cluster,"
citing "two live award catalysts" and the September 14, 2026 Emmy ceremony. That same entry contains
a factual error, recorded at CLM-16 and in Disputes below.

**Knowledge cutoff of this packet.** Sources consulted run through August 11, 2026. The 78th
Primetime Emmy Awards ceremony (September 14, 2026) has **not** occurred; her Emmy nomination is
pending, not won. _The Julia Set_ has not been released. Any evaluator claim that depends on events
after August 11, 2026 is outside this packet.

**Aliases and name origin.** Birth surname Payne; "Infiniti" is a given middle name, not a stage
construction. Origin story at CLM-11.

## First-person evidence

All quotations below were confirmed against the cited source during this compilation unless marked

### The mantra (the draft's single most load-bearing quotation)

**Verified fact, with a material caveat.** The full passage as published reads:

> "The thing I try to do the most is remind myself that I'm capable of doing everything. I've been
> given these opportunities. I'm so grateful for them - I can handle it, and the people around me
> know I can handle it. I ground myself and remind myself that I'm here for a reason. I'm not here
> by accident."
>
> _Elle_, Nerisha Penrose, October 1, 2025 (S-06)

The draft prints this as a single continuous two-sentence quotation: **"I'm capable of doing
everything. I'm not here by accident."** Both sentences are authentic and both are hers. They are
not adjacent: roughly thirty words separate them, and the draft supplies no ellipsis. See CLM-01.

What the omitted middle contains, stated neutrally: a gratitude register ("I've been given these
opportunities. I'm so grateful for them"), a self-capability claim ("I can handle it"), and an
external-recognition claim ("the people around me know I can handle it").

### On belonging and identity

**Verified fact.**

> "I can definitely understand feeling like you don't fully belong in your environment."
>
> _Dazed_, Simran Hans, December 3, 2025 (S-04)

**Verified fact.** The same piece reports she related to being biracial "and feeling like you're
missing a part of yourself," and that she did not resemble other mixed-race peers at her
Indianapolis school. (S-04)

**Verified fact.**

describing a character connection in a press interview conducted during an awards campaign.

## Third-party testimony

**Cassandra Kulukundis** (casting director, _One Battle After Another_) - **attributed claim,
reported, not primary-read.** TheWrap, 2025 (S-03):

> "Paul, in that screen test, literally handed her a razor and was like, 'Shave Leo's face.' I'm sure
> she was shaking, but she held it all in and didn't look like she missed a beat."

_What this can support:_ that a named professional who was present believes she was frightened and
that it did not show. _What it cannot support:_ that she was in fact shaking. Kulukundis says "I'm
sure she was," which is an inference she is openly marking as her own. The draft converts this into
the flat assertion "She was shaking. Nobody could tell." See CLM-04.

Also: "She's so special. She's so unique... she just felt like such a real person," and on the role's
demands, "I definitely needed somebody who could kick some butt... heartbreak. She had to run the
gamut of emotions."

**Paul Thomas Anderson** (director), by email to TheWrap, 2025 (S-03) - **attributed claim,
reported**:

> "Chase is extremely agile and quick on her feet. She is also able to fight the corner for her
> character and what she believes."

**Leonardo DiCaprio** (co-star), January 2026 - **verified fact as to utterance** (S-08). Given **to
the Associated Press**; THR, E! News and Parade reported it. The draft's testimony ledger credits it

## Enneagram hypothesis evidence

The draft proposes **Type 3 (the Achiever), wing 2, social-dominant**. The repository research file
(S-14) self-reports **"Confidence: medium."** Everything in this section is hypothesis-level.

### Evidence consistent with Type 3

1. **Verified fact.** Her stated grounding line is a capability claim: "I ground myself and remind
   myself that I'm here for a reason. I'm not here by accident." (S-06)
2. **Attributed claim.** She reframed a six-month unpaid audition limbo as instruction: "I treated
   every single callback and chemistry read like a master class." (S-03)
3. **Verified fact.** Her stated competence standard is external legibility, not mastery: "I'm at a
   point now where it's convincing on camera." (S-02)
4. **Verified fact.** She cannot land on her own work: "it's hard for me to say I'm happy with what
   I did." (S-04)
5. **Attributed claim.** Corroborated composure under acute pressure: the razor screen test (S-03).
6. **Verified fact.** Sustained physical preparation for a part she had not been offered: karate,
   four months of MMA, weapons training (S-03), plus an AWFJ Best Stunt Performance win (S-01).

### Evidence against Type 3, or unaccounted for by it

1. **Verified fact.** She names her own nerves repeatedly and unprompted, in the same interviews
   where she is otherwise polished ("I remember being very nervous that day"; "I'm a scaredy-cat";
   the _Interview_ piece is subtitled around Leo and PTA having "her shaking in her boots and
   tutu"). Image-managing performers typically edit this out. (S-03, S-07, S-15)
2. **Verified fact, and absent from the draft.** She hid her _One Battle After Another_ casting from
   her _Testaments_ castmates until the trailer played publicly (S-11). This is a declined
   opportunity to convert a major credential into peer standing.
3. **Attributed claim.** Bruce Miller's unpredictability read (S-16) - though see CLM-09 for the
   context that complicates its use as psychological evidence.
4. **Verified fact.** She retained non-advancing commitments: the K-pop cover-dance crew, and music
   video appearances continuing after her awards season (S-01).

### Closest alternative: Type 6

**Evidence for.** She narrates her career through authorities and mentors almost exclusively:
Gyllenhaal taught her to raise the stakes; Ruth Negga "told me not to be scared" (S-02); Anderson
and DiCaprio were "incredible leaders and guides"; Moss is "an encyclopedia." She self-describes as
a scaredy-cat (S-07). She shadowed crew members (S-14, headline-level only).

**Discriminating evidence the draft relies on.** Under the acute pressure of the razor screen test
she produced a surface rather than reaching for the authority in the room, and her self-steadying
sentence is a capability claim rather than a safety or backup claim.

**Unresolved question.** That discriminator rests on a single incident **narrated by a third party
who was inferring her internal state** ("I'm sure she was shaking"). No first-person account of what
she was doing internally during the screen test was located. The tiebreaker is therefore
structurally weaker than the draft's prose implies.

### Behavior the hypothesis does not explain

**Unresolved question.** The draft itself concedes one: she leaves the shaking in the story. The
packet adds a second: the concealed _One Battle After Another_ credential (S-11). Neither is
resolved by the available record.

### Wing and instinct

**Interpretation (contestable).** The 3w2 case rests on relational public speech, most of which is
press-interview speech given during two awards campaigns, a context that structurally rewards
warmth and credit-sharing. The evidence base cannot cleanly separate a 2 wing from campaign
convention. The draft's own hedge, "3w2 with a live four shadow," is not contradicted by anything
found here.

**CLM-01 — The mantra quotation.**
_Claim:_ "Her mantra, given to _Elle_ in October 2025: 'I'm capable of doing everything. I'm not here

is a sentence you only need if some part of you suspects otherwise") interprets a compression the
subject did not utter as a unit. The omitted middle contains the gratitude and external-validation
registers that the draft treats, elsewhere, as a separate and distinct behavior pattern.

## Research limitations

1. **Tollbit paywall persists.** The Hollywood Reporter, Variety, Deadline and IndieWire all
   307-redirect automated fetches to `tollbit.*`. THR's feature "Chase Infiniti on What Terrifies Her
   Most About 'The Testaments'"
   (https://www.hollywoodreporter.com/tv/tv-features/chase-infiniti-the-testaments-agnes-1236660342/)
   was retried in this pass and **remains unread**. The research file named it the highest-value
   unread source; that assessment stands. Its subject matter (what frightens her) bears directly on
   the Type 3 versus Type 6 question this packet cannot settle.
2. **NPR interview not retrieved.** S-13 timed out. This is the most significant new gap: it is a
   Tier 1 broadcast interview, it is long-form and unscripted, and it directly addresses the pace of
   her career. It would partially mitigate the standing `no_transcripts` limitation.
3. **`research_limitation: no_transcripts` still stands.** No YouTube transcript was retrieved in the
   prior run (IpBlocked / HTTP 429 on all four shortlisted videos) and none was attempted here. There
   is no long-form unscripted primary material in the evidence base. Every first-person quotation in
   this packet is filtered through an interviewer and an edit.
4. **Two Tier 1 sources are headline-only.** _Interview_ magazine (S-15) 403s to automated fetch, so
   the Teyana Taylor testimony is confirmed at headline level only. The IndieWire Honors speech is
   likewise headline-only, which means "I shadowed anyone who would let me watch," used in the
   draft's diagnosis section, rests on a snippet.
5. **TheWrap (S-03) was not primary-read in this pass.** Five of the draft's eight ledger quotations
   originate there, including the razor account that opens and closes the piece. They are carried
   forward from the research file and are marked "reported" throughout. A manual read of that single
   article would upgrade the largest block of load-bearing quotation in the draft.
6. **Automated summarization risk.** Sources S-02, S-04, S-06, S-07, S-09 and S-11 were read through
   a summarizing fetch rather than manually. Verbatim quotations returned by that path are reliable
   enough to cite, but _framing_ judgments drawn from them (specifically CLM-14's "told herself")
   should be confirmed before an evaluator treats them as decided.
7. **The Ann Dowd quotation has no located source** in either research pass.
8. **No adversarial or negative press exists to find.** This is a property of the record, not a
   failure of search. Evaluators should not read the absence of counterevidence about her character
   as evidence of its absence.
9. **Nothing after 2026-08-11.** The Emmy outcome (September 14, 2026) and _The Julia Set_ are both
   outside this packet, and both are the events the research file names as the real tests of the
   type hypothesis.
