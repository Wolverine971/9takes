## Bottom-line verdict

The writer understands her. That is not in question by the third paragraph. The razor open, the master-class
table, and the reading of "convincing on camera" are the work of someone who actually sat with the material
instead of skimming a press cycle, and two passages gave me the thing I read these pieces for: a fact I
already owned, turned ninety degrees.

But this is a piece about a woman whose entire body of work is about being assigned an identity and wearing
it well, and it walks past the three connections that prove it. All three are already sitting in the draft's
own sentences, unfired. Her breakout role is a girl living under a name someone else gave her, exactly like
the role the draft calls "the thesis on screen" and treats as new. Her childhood proof-of-concept, Zendaya, is
now a nominee in her Emmy category, and she named her in her own nomination statement. The kid who co-founded
a K-pop cover-dance crew ended up cast in an actual K-pop group's music video. The draft states each
ingredient and never cooks with it.

Add one canonical error that a musical-theatre-literate reader will hit like a pothole (FAN-R1), one place
where the draft contradicts itself two sections apart (FAN-C3), and a rendering of Oscar night with the actual
facts of the night removed (FAN-C5), and my confidence dips from "this writer knows her" to "this writer knows
her, and I should spot-check." That is the difference between a piece I share and a piece I correct in the
replies. It is a revise, not a hold: everything needed is either in the packet or already on the page.

## What landed

**FAN-H1. The cold open.** "Karate lessons. Weapons practice. Chemistry reads, callbacks, costume fittings
for a part that was not hers. Then Paul Thomas Anderson put a straight razor in her hand and pointed her at
Leonardo DiCaprio." Every fan knows the razor story. Almost every write-up deploys it as a gee-whiz anecdote.
This one front-loads the months of unpaid preparation so the razor lands as the last exam in a sequence rather
than a lightning strike. Must survive: it is the only version I have read that makes the anecdote mean
something structural instead of just being a good story.

**FAN-H2. The master-class table.** Six months of "nothing happened" converted into six graded modules, with
the grading column doing the analytic work ("Whether the ground shifting under her changed the take,"
"Endurance without a contract," "Whether she could hold the shape of a life that was not yet hers"). This is
the single best fan-facing artifact in the piece. It only exists because she called the audition a master
class herself, which means it cannot be transplanted onto another actor. Must survive intact, including the
grading column, which is where the intelligence lives.

**FAN-H3. The "convincing" reading.** "'I'm at a point now where it's convincing on camera.' [...] Convincing.
She set the bar inside somebody else's eye." I have read that lace quote three or four times in coverage and
registered it as a charming craft aside. The draft is right that it is a confession about where she keeps her
scoreboard. This is the piece's clearest pass of the delight test: I knew the moment, I had never connected it
that way. Must survive, including the one-word sentence, which is what makes it land.

**FAN-H4. The thanks-reflex reframe.** "If you have said out loud what each person there is worth, you have
made yourself the person who noticed. That is a position. It cannot be taken from you by a bad review." The
credit-deflecting press persona is the most-mocked thing about her among people who do not like her, and this
is the first treatment I have seen that grants the cynics the behavior and relocates the cause without
flattering her. The cost line -- "closer to a muscle she never built, and it does not grow back on its own" --
keeps it from tipping into fan service. Must survive whole; it is the strongest section in the draft.

**FAN-H5. The Agnes/Hannah unpacking.** "she remembers being called Hannah and has no idea why. Everything
else about her is a life somebody else assigned, worn so well that it passes." Correct about the show, and the
right detail to pull. My complaint (FAN-C2) is that the draft thinks this is the first time she has played it.

**FAN-H6. The transition into it.** "I just heard him say my name" into "The role that got her the Emmy
nomination is a girl with two names." Unassisted, unannounced, and it does real work. Must survive.

## What missed

**The Zendaya beat dies on the page (FAN-C1).** The draft gives me the childhood quote -- "this girl looks
like me. She is mixed. She is singing, acting, dancing, doing the thing that I want to do" -- and reads it as
proof of concept. Fine as far as it goes. Then, twelve hundred words later, it tells me she was nominated for a
Primetime Emmy for Outstanding Lead Actress in a Drama Series and reported as the only first-time nominee in
her category. Any fan who followed the July nominations knows who else is in that category. Zendaya is. And in
her own nomination statement she wrote that being "nominated alongside Carrie, Rhea, Zendaya, and Keri is an
honor" -- she named her, on the record, unprompted. The draft holds both halves and never closes the loop. This
is the piece's largest unclaimed payoff, and it is free.

**Both her lead roles are girls living under assigned names (FAN-C2).** The draft's climax is "The role that
got her the Emmy nomination is a girl with two names," followed by "An actor who spent her childhood working
out how much of herself a room would take got handed a part about a girl who is entirely the room's
construction." Willa Ferguson is an alias. Her birth name is Charlene Calhoun; she was raised sixteen years
under a name her father took off a dead stranger so the two of them could disappear. The draft spends two
thousand words on that film, describes Willa only as "a revolutionary's teenage daughter trying to have an
ordinary adolescence," and then presents the two-names structure as though The Testaments introduced it. She
has now played the same girl twice, for two of the best directors and showrunners working, in her only two
lead roles. A fan notices this in the theater. The piece whose entire thesis is about assigned identity does
not.

**The cover-dance crew and the ATEEZ video are in the same paragraph and never meet (FAN-C8).** "co-founded a
K-pop cover-dance crew with her friends" sits four sentences above "she was still turning up in music videos
for Tyler, the Creator and Ateez." ATEEZ is a K-pop group. The kid who learned other people's choreography in
a Chicago rehearsal room is now in the video. The draft uses the music videos only as evidence that she does
not shed things, which is a smaller and duller point than the one it is standing on.

**Oscar night is rendered with the night removed (FAN-C5).** The section states thirteen nominations and none
for her, then goes to DiCaprio's defense, then to a ledger. What it never says: the film won six, including
Best Picture and Best Director; Penn, del Toro and Taylor were all nominated in supporting categories while
she was not; and Anderson said her name from the Best Picture stage, calling her "my American girl" and "the
heart of this movie." That is the actual shape of the evening -- the biggest win of the year, three castmates
validated, her not, and the director stopping the ceremony to name her anyway. The draft's version is
strictly sadder and strictly less true to what happened.

**The best Chase story of 2026 is absent (FAN-C6).** She hid her One Battle After Another casting from her
Testaments castmates until the trailer played in front of Sinners, and only got found out when Brad came to
set and told everyone. "I hadn't even seen the final cut yet." Fans trade this one. Its absence is not just a
missing anecdote; it is the single hardest fact for this draft's argument to digest, and leaving it out makes
the piece look like it did not find it rather than like it weighed it.

**The Emmy rebuttal leaves its best card face-down (FAN-C7).** The draft states the skeptic's case honestly --
cast well, directed well, surrounded by Anderson, DiCaprio, Penn, Hall, del Toro and Taylor -- and answers it
with "Different show, different showrunner, different medium, same verdict." The fact that actually closes
that argument is one the draft does not use: her nomination was the only acting nomination The Testaments
received. She was not carried by a show sweep. She was singled out of it.

## What I expected

- **Musical-theatre literacy, because she has it.** Columbia College Chicago musical theatre, seven Summer
  Stock Stage credits between 2016 and 2021. Her fans skew toward people who know what Rizzo is in. FAN-R1 is
  the one error that will cost the writer standing with exactly the readers most likely to finish the piece.
- **The filmography pattern named.** Three credits, three daughters of families whose identity is under siege:
  Jaden Sabich, whose father is on trial for murder; Charlene/Willa, in hiding under an alias; Agnes/Hannah,
  taken and renamed. She is being cast as one specific kind of person. Nobody in the coverage has said so, and
  this was the piece to do it.
- **Her performance in The Testaments described, not just her preparation.** The draft gives me embroidery,
  Miller's "left or right," and a plot summary of Agnes. Moss's line about the Lydia scene -- "the way that she
  walks that line of not revealing anything to Lydia, but revealing what she knows" -- is in the record, it
  describes acting rather than credentials, and it is a near-perfect illustration of the draft's own thesis
  about legibility. The FADER paragraph does this job for Willa; nothing does it for Agnes.
- **Award-ledger precision, since the draft chose to run a ledger.** If you enumerate a season, fans will check
  it. FAN-C3 and FAN-C4 are both failures of that check.
- **The Louis Vuitton ambassadorship, or a decision not to include it.** Not a defect -- it is genuinely
  optional -- but for a piece arguing that she measures worth in external legibility, being named a house
  ambassador in 2026 is on-topic and current, and its absence is more noticeable than most omissions here.

## What surprised me

**Welcome.** The unexplained-behavior paragraph in the diagnosis section -- "Threes usually delete the shaking
from the story. She leaves it in [...] three years of public record is not enough to say what." I did not
expect a personality piece to volunteer that its own frame has a hole in it, in the main body, unhedged. That
is the move that made me trust the writer's judgment even where I was arguing with the conclusions.

**Welcome.** "Most kids get a name. She got an instruction." The name-origin story is in every profile as
trivia. This is the only version that makes it load-bearing.

**Jarring.** "At ten she was cast in a local production of Hairspray, and she has pinned the exact moment the
thing took hold: watching Rizzo sing." I stopped reading and went back. See FAN-R1.

**Jarring, mildly.** "she was twenty-three" in the cold open and "at twenty-two" for Presumed Innocent are both
defensible against a May 2000 birth date, but "Two years on" (FAN-C9) is not defensible against any anchor in
the piece, and it appears in the intro, where a fan is still deciding whether to trust the chronology.

**Unearned.** "She lathered his face and shaved it." The source has Anderson handing her a razor and telling
her to shave Leo. The lather is the writer's. It is a small addition to the most-repeated story in her press
history, and it is the kind of detail fans will try to source and fail to find. Logged in Follow-on questions
rather than as a finding, because I cannot rule out that TheWrap's full text supports it.

## Red flags

**FAN-R1 [BLOCKER] -- Rizzo is not in Hairspray.**

- _Passage:_ "Chase Infiniti's Childhood in Indianapolis," the sentence beginning "At ten she was cast in a
  local production of <em>Hairspray</em>, and she has pinned the exact moment the thing took hold: watching
  Rizzo sing, and thinking, I want to do this."
- _Reader effect:_ Rizzo is a character in Grease. The sentence's construction implies she watched Rizzo sing
  in the Hairspray production she was cast in. For a reader who knows musicals -- which is a large share of the
  audience for a Columbia College musical-theatre graduate with seven regional credits -- this reads as a
  writer who does not know the repertoire, in the paragraph that anchors the origin argument. Once I doubt
  that sentence I start auditing the rest, which is how I found FAN-C3 and FAN-C4.
- _Evidence:_ Packet CLM-03 records the claim as DISPUTED. WFYI (S-09), the most local and most
  theatre-focused source located, lists seven Summer Stock Stage credits 2016-2021 with no Hairspray and no
  age-ten performance. Tier 4 sourcing (S-19) describes the age-ten event as a school group showcase of songs
  from musicals, and attributes the theatre bug to being taken to see Grease as a pre-teen -- Rizzo singing
  "There Are Worse Things I Could Do." The repository research file (S-14) itself separates the two events and
  attaches Rizzo to Grease. No Tier 1 or Tier 2 source supports the draft's version of either half.
- _Minimum viable repair:_ Cut the Hairspray casting. Keep the Rizzo moment and attach it to the correct show
  and the correct kind of event -- she was in the audience at Grease, not on stage in Hairspray. If the
  editor is unwilling to rely on Tier 4 sourcing for the Grease framing either, cut both specifics and keep
  the WFYI-supported material (the Summer Stock Stage run, 2016-2021), which is better documented and more
  local anyway.
- _Expected benefit:_ Removes the one error most likely to be caught by the most engaged segment of the
  audience, in the section where the piece is asking for trust in its origin story.
- _Confidence:_ High that the draft as written is wrong (Rizzo is definitionally not a Hairspray character).
  Medium on what the correct replacement is, which is why the fallback repair exists.
- _Acceptance test:_ No sentence in the piece places Rizzo in Hairspray, and every retained childhood
  specific traces to WFYI (S-09) or to a source the editor is willing to name inline.

**FAN-C3 [CONCERN] -- "Every trophy she carried home was for being new" is contradicted by the draft itself.**

- _Passage:_ "What the Academy Did Not Give Chase Infiniti": "Breakthrough. Most promising. Breakout.
  Emerging. Every trophy she carried home was for being new." Contradicted by "The Master Class Chase Infiniti
  Was Never Enrolled In": "The Alliance of Women Film Journalists gave her its stunt-performance award for it,
  on a first film, at twenty-three."
- _Reader effect:_ A reader going straight through hits the contradiction about six hundred words apart. The
  stunt award is not a novelty category. Neither are the two Best Ensemble wins from the Georgia and St. Louis
  critics, which the draft never mentions. The generalization is the hinge of the section's argument, and a
  fan who followed the season can falsify it from memory -- and, worse, from the draft's own text.
- _Evidence:_ Packet CLM-06 and the full awards ledger (S-01): AWFJ Best Stunt Performance (2025), Georgia
  Film Critics Best Ensemble (2025), St. Louis Film Critics Best Ensemble (2025), alongside the six novelty
  prizes the draft enumerates.
- _Minimum viable repair:_ Narrow the claim to what is true and still sharp -- every trophy she won _for her
  own performance_ was a prize for being new. That preserves the argument exactly (the ensemble wins are
  shared, the stunt award is for physical execution, and neither is a verdict on the performance) while
  removing the falsifiable absolute.
- _Expected benefit:_ The section's central move survives a fact-check by the reader most invested in
  checking it, and the draft stops arguing with itself.
- _Confidence:_ High.
- _Acceptance test:_ The sentence is true against the complete 2025-2026 awards table in the packet, not only
  against the six awards enumerated immediately before it.

**FAN-C4 [CONCERN] -- The Tyler, the Creator video is misdated, and the misdating breaks the sentence's point.**

- _Passage:_ "Chase Infiniti's Childhood in Indianapolis": "In 2026, with a full awards season and an Emmy
  nomination behind her, she was still turning up in music videos for Tyler, the Creator and Ateez."
- _Reader effect:_ The sentence's whole force comes from the sequencing -- after the awards, after the Emmy,
  she was still doing this. Half the evidence predates both. Fans track a three-credit filmography exhaustively;
  the music videos are not obscure to this audience.
- _Evidence:_ Packet CLM-12 / S-01: "Darling, I" is dated 2025, "Bad" is dated 2026. Only the ATEEZ video falls
  after the awards season and the Emmy nomination.
- _Minimum viable repair:_ Attribute the 2026 timing to the ATEEZ video alone and let the Tyler video sit
  earlier, in 2025, as the thing she was doing _during_ the campaign. The argument survives on one video and
  arguably improves -- doing a music video mid-awards-season is the stranger choice.
- _Expected benefit:_ Removes a checkable error and sharpens the contrast the sentence was built for.
- _Confidence:_ High.
- _Acceptance test:_ No sentence attributes the Tyler, the Creator video to 2026 or to the post-Emmy period.

**FAN-C5 [CONCERN] -- Oscar night is described without the facts of Oscar night.**

- _Passage:_ "What the Academy Did Not Give Chase Infiniti," from "In January 2026, <em>One Battle After
  Another</em> collected thirteen Oscar nominations" through "I just heard him say my name... I love Paul so
  much."
- _Reader effect:_ Anyone who watched the ceremony knows the film won Best Picture and Best Director and that
  Anderson named her during the Best Picture speech. Withholding that makes the section read as though the
  writer only has the nominations list, and it drains the closing anecdote: "I just heard him say my name" is
  a quiet line about a small moment as written, and a devastating one if the reader knows it happened at the
  summit of the night, in the winner's speech, about someone sitting in the audience with no nomination of her
  own while three of her castmates had one.
- _Evidence:_ Packet S-10 (six wins including Best Picture and Best Director); S-08 (Penn, del Toro and Taylor
  nominated in supporting categories); S-02 (Anderson called her "my American girl" and "the heart of this
  movie" in his Best Picture acceptance).
- _Minimum viable repair:_ Two facts, one clause each -- that the film won Best Picture, and that three
  castmates were nominated while she was not -- plus naming the speech she was named in. No new paragraph
  required.
- _Expected benefit:_ The section stops feeling under-informed to anyone who watched, and its closing quote
  gains the context that makes it hit.
- _Confidence:_ High.
- _Acceptance test:_ A reader who watched the 98th Academy Awards finds nothing in the section that they know
  to be materially incomplete about that evening.

**FAN-C9 [CONCERN] -- "Two years on" does not compute from any anchor in the piece.**

- _Passage:_ Intro: "Two years on, that first film has earned her best-actress nominations from the Golden
  Globes, BAFTA, SAG and Critics Choice, and her second lead role has earned her a 2026 Emmy nomination."
- _Reader effect:_ The preceding paragraphs place us at the screen test. From there it is three years to
  publication; from the film's release it is eleven months; from her casting it is nearly three. The nominations
  listed span January 2026 and July 2026. A reader tracking the timeline stumbles in the intro, which is the
  worst place to spend credibility.
- _Evidence:_ Packet CLM-15; timeline rows for mid-2023 (screen test), October 2023 (cast), September 2025
  (release), January 2026 (Globes/BAFTA/SAG/Critics Choice), July 2026 (Emmy).
- _Minimum viable repair:_ Replace the relative interval with the concrete span the piece can defend -- the
  distance from the screen test to now is three years, and saying "three years" also sets up the draft's own
  later line, "three years of public record is not enough to say what."
- _Expected benefit:_ The intro's chronology survives a fan's mental arithmetic, and the piece gains an
  internal echo it currently misses.
- _Confidence:_ High that "two years" is wrong; medium on which anchor the editor should prefer.
- _Acceptance test:_ Every relative time claim in the intro resolves correctly against the packet timeline
  from a stated anchor.

## Specific improvements

**FAN-C1 [CONCERN] -- Close the Zendaya loop at the Emmy nomination.**

- _Location:_ The Zendaya paragraph in "Chase Infiniti's Childhood in Indianapolis" ("Then she saw Zendaya...
  a room had already let it in"), paid off in "What the Academy Did Not Give Chase Infiniti" at "reported as
  the only first-time nominee in her category."
- _Problem:_ The draft establishes Zendaya as the proof that the shape she wanted to become was possible, then
  reports the Emmy category without mentioning that Zendaya is in it. The two facts are twelve hundred words
  apart and never introduced to each other.
- _Evidence:_ Packet S-12 and S-17 list the category as Coon, Russell, Seehorn, Zendaya and Infiniti. My own
  search confirms it and adds something the packet does not have: her official nomination statement reads
  "Playing Agnes has been a dream and to be nominated alongside Carrie, Rhea, Zendaya, and Keri is an honor."
  She named her. (TheWrap and E! News nominee-reaction roundups, July 2026.)
- _Minimum viable repair:_ One sentence at the Emmy beat noting that the category contains the woman she
  described watching as a kid, and, if the editor wants the knife, that she named her in her own nomination
  statement. Do not over-explain it; the childhood paragraph has already done the setup.
- _Expected benefit:_ This is the piece's highest-yield unclaimed payoff and the clearest available pass of
  the "I never connected them that way" test. It also converts a beat the draft currently spends (Zendaya) into
  a beat that earns.
- _Secondary benefit:_ The full nomination statement -- thanking Bruce, Warren, Elisabeth, Hulu, cast and crew
  before saying anything about herself -- is a documented, dateable instance of the exact reflex "Why Chase
  Infiniti Thanks Everyone but Herself" is built on, and it is the most recent one available. That section
  currently runs on 2025 examples.
- _Confidence:_ High on the facts, high on the effect.
- _Acceptance test:_ A reader who finishes the piece can say, without prior knowledge, that she is nominated
  against the actress she named as her childhood proof that the job was possible.

**FAN-C2 [CONCERN] -- Name the two-names structure in One Battle After Another.**

- _Location:_ Two places. "The Master Class Chase Infiniti Was Never Enrolled In," at "Willa is a
  revolutionary's teenage daughter trying to have an ordinary adolescence"; and the opening of "What
  'Convincing on Camera' Means to Chase Infiniti," at "The role that got her the Emmy nomination is a girl

- _Minimum viable repair:_ Add the birth name where Willa is first described -- one clause. Then change the
  Agnes sentence from a claim of novelty to a claim of repetition: the second girl with two names she has
  played. The existing sentence "An actor who spent her childhood working out how much of herself a room would
  take got handed a part about a girl who is entirely the room's construction" becomes considerably heavier
  when it is the second time she has been handed that part.
- _Expected benefit:_ Converts the draft's climactic observation from a coincidence into a pattern, at a cost
  of roughly two sentences, and repairs the one place where a fan can accuse the piece of not having watched
  its own subject's breakout closely.
- _Confidence:_ High.
- _Acceptance test:_ The piece states that Willa is an assigned identity, and the Agnes beat reads as the
  second instance rather than the first.

**FAN-C6 [CONCERN] -- Include the concealed casting.**

- _Minimum viable repair:_ Two or three sentences. The draft does not need to resolve it -- it already has a

- _Expected benefit:_ Closes the most conspicuous research gap for an informed reader, and strengthens the
  draft's credibility precisely because the fact is inconvenient.
- _Confidence:_ High that it belongs; medium on placement, which is the editor's call.
- _Acceptance test:_ The piece contains the concealed-casting fact and does not resolve it more confidently
  than the evidence allows.

**FAN-C7 [CONCERN] -- Deploy the solo-nomination fact against the skeptic's case.**

- _Expected benefit:_ Turns a rhetorical answer into an evidentiary one at the exact point where the draft

- _Confidence:_ High.
- _Acceptance test:_ The rebuttal to the ensemble objection rests on a stated fact about the second
  nomination, not on the assertion that the venue changed.

**FAN-C8 [CONCERN] -- Fire the cover-dance-crew-to-ATEEZ connection.**

- _Location:_ "Chase Infiniti's Childhood in Indianapolis," the paragraph containing "co-founded a K-pop
  cover-dance crew with her friends" and the paragraph containing "still turning up in music videos for Tyler,
  the Creator and Ateez."
- _Problem:_ ATEEZ is a K-pop group. The draft states that she co-founded a K-pop cover-dance crew in college
  and, four sentences later, that she appeared in an ATEEZ music video after her awards season, and treats the

- _Evidence:_ Packet S-01 confirms both the crew and the ATEEZ video ("Bad," 2026).
- _Minimum viable repair:_ One clause connecting them. This also pairs naturally with the FAN-C4 repair, since

- _Expected benefit:_ A memorable, shareable specific in a section that currently ends on an analytic note,

- _Confidence:_ High.
- _Acceptance test:_ A reader can state that the K-pop cover-dance kid ended up in a K-pop group's video, from
  the text alone.

## Follow-on questions

**FAN-Q1. Does TheWrap's full text support "She lathered his face and shaved it"?**

- _What would change:_ If the lather is not in the source, it is an invented physical detail inside the most
  repeated story in her press history, in the fourth sentence of the piece. Cut it and the sentence still
  works ("She shaved it"). If it is in the source, keep it -- it is a good detail.
- _Best source:_ A manual read of TheWrap's One Battle After Another interview (S-03), which the packet flags
  as reported-not-primary-read and as the origin of five of the draft's eight ledger quotations. One read
  resolves this and several other open items at once.

**FAN-Q2. Where does the "Oooooh" come from?**

- _Passage:_ The master-class table, final row: "Her recorded reaction is one syllable. 'Oooooh.'"
- _What would change:_ It is the funniest beat in the table and the only one I cannot trace. The packet

## Limits of this review

- **Standpoint.** I am the informed-but-non-stan fan proxy. I audited canonical accuracy, chronology, whether
  familiar material was newly connected, and whether the admiration and criticism both read as informed. I did
  not evaluate whether the Type 3 call is correct, whether the typing is fair to her as a person, or whether
  the piece is comprehensible to a newcomer. Where a finding touched those lanes -- most clearly the "She was
  shaking" hardening in the cold open -- I flagged it and stopped.
- **The snapshot contains embedded pipeline commentary** (testimony, heading, distribution and formula
  ledgers; reviewer notes; a fresh-eyes review; second-pass notes; a cohesion pass). I read the file as
  delivered, formed every finding against the reader-visible prose, and did not treat any embedded assessment
  as authority or as a floor. Three of my four largest findings (FAN-C1, FAN-C2, FAN-C8) do not appear in that
  commentary in any form; where it does overlap with me, I reached the finding from the text and the packet.
- **The evidence base has no long-form unscripted material.** Packet limitation 3 stands: no transcripts were
  retrieved in either research pass. Every quotation available to the draft and to me is filtered through an
  interviewer and an edit, and most of it was gathered during two awards campaigns. My judgments about what
  she is "known for" among fans are therefore built on press coverage, not on her unmediated speech.
- **Two sources that bear directly on my findings are unread.** TheWrap (S-03) is the origin of five of the
  draft's eight ledger quotations and was not primary-read in this pass, which is why FAN-Q1 and FAN-Q2 are
  questions rather than findings. THR's "what terrifies her most about The Testaments" remains behind the
  tollbit redirect.
- **FAN-C2 rests on plot sourcing read through search summaries** of Wikipedia and a Fandom wiki rather than a
  manual read of either page, and my attempt at a third corroborating source returned 403. The direction of
  the alias (Charlene is the birth name) is stated consistently across the summaries and matches the packet's
  credit ordering, but an editor implementing that repair should confirm the name against the film's own
  credits before printing it.
- **Nothing after 2026-08-11.** The Emmy ceremony is a month away and unresolved. If she wins, the FAN-C1 and
  FAN-C7 material changes shape, and the draft's "the test result arrived on schedule" framing will need
  revisiting on its own terms.
