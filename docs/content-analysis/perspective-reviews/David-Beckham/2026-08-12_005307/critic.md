## Bottom-line verdict

The article earns its sympathetic interpretation in the places where it is arguing, and loses it in the places where it is narrating.

When this draft states a criticism, it states it well. The Qatar section refuses to let Beckham's own answer close the question, the Rabbit Hole declines to convert his reserve into more Type 3 evidence, and the piece volunteers that its central symbol — the 2am cleaning — cannot be used as proof of type. That is a higher standard than most celebrity typing clears, and it is the reason my verdict is "strained" rather than "broken."

The failures cluster in the connective prose, where the draft stops citing and starts explaining. Four things break trust from this standpoint. First, six reader-visible citations to Netflix's *Beckham* carry the draft's entire sympathetic core, and the draft never discloses that the series was made by Beckham's own production company — a disclosure it does volunteer for the far less load-bearing *Country Life* issue. Second, the clearest unhedged example of deliberate image-staging is contradicted by Beckham's own account, and the draft then tells the same event correctly one paragraph later. Third, the "insurance" reframe answers the commercial-saturation criticism with a causal story that dated fact disconfirms — his multi-million endorsement career was already running before the trauma the draft attributes it to. Fourth, the Rebecca Loos passage quotes three Beckham-side voices and omits hers.

The pattern under all four is the same, and it is the specific failure mode a critic exists to catch: the draft applies its considerable epistemic discipline to *claims* and suspends it for *scenes*. The typing survives all of this. The sympathy has to be re-earned in four places.

## What landed

**CRITIC-H1 — Refusing to let the subject's defence close the question.** Line 328:

> "That is his case. It does not answer the discriminatory laws or labor abuses that human-rights groups documented, and it does not show how a paid ambassadorship improved either problem. The criticism landed because the partnership asked fans to treat access and optimism as sufficient accountability."

This is the single best passage in the draft from this standpoint. It quotes his strongest answer in full, then names precisely what the answer does not reach — and the final sentence identifies the actual mechanism of the objection rather than restating outrage. Most sympathetic profiles present the subject's response as the resolution. This one presents it as the claim under test. **Must survive revision verbatim.** If anything in the Qatar section is cut for length, cut around this.

**CRITIC-H2 — Volunteering evidence the thesis cannot use.** Line 317:

> "No clean typing explains the audience-free cleaning at 2am. It is excluded from the case for type."

The 2am walk is the draft's cold open, its title image, and its most vivid material. Excluding it from the argument is a genuine cost paid for accuracy, and it is the strongest signal in the piece that the writer is not fitting evidence to a conclusion. It also correctly refuses to convert a self-described mental-health matter into typology. **Must survive:** removing this to strengthen the case would invert the draft's credibility.

**CRITIC-H3 — Counterevidence left standing.** Line 315:

> "His reserve remains genuine counterevidence rather than something the Type 3 case gets to explain away."

The standard move is to absorb quietness into the type ("even his calm is strategic"). This sentence explicitly forbids that move. **Must survive** — it is what makes the Nine alternative a real alternative rather than a staged one.

**CRITIC-H4 — Not laundering the father.** Line 199: "Her objection did not change the method, and the method worked." The draft has an available exit here (Ted meant well, times were different) and declines it. Ted's own on-record justification does the indicting, and the draft lets it. **Must survive:** this is the origin section's integrity anchor.

**CRITIC-H5 — The Brooklyn passage.** Line 292: "It marks the one place where the method has nothing to offer, because the man who has answered every crisis of his life by producing something cannot produce this." Both accounts are attributed, neither is adjudicated, and the draft resists the temptation to use a son's allegation as type evidence. **Must survive**, including the explicit "It does not prove David's motive or his Enneagram type."

## What missed

**The disclosure asymmetry.** Line 228 tells the reader that the beekeeping details "appeared in an issue he guest-edited" — an unprompted, correct conflict disclosure on a minor source. The Netflix series, which supplies the father's method, the mother's unease, the 1998 first-person distress, Victoria's account of the marriage crisis, and the 2am routine, gets no equivalent line. The draft demonstrably knows the move and applies it to the source where it costs nothing.

**The pre-emption.** The "insurance" reframe (line 258) supplies a sympathetic reading of relentless commercial expansion *two sections before* the Qatar section asks the reader to judge a commercial decision. By the time the reader reaches Visit Qatar, the draft has already framed Beckham's willingness to attach his name to things as a trauma-response rather than a choice. The Qatar section never revisits or qualifies that frame. Whether or not this was intended, it is the structure of an argument that has answered its own objection before raising it.

**Where the unfavourable material sits.** Of eight H2s, one is a controversy section, it is seventh, and it resolves into a knighthood, a charity record, and an admiring type-arc close (line 332). Nothing after it re-opens the question. The Brooklyn allegation is the exception and is handled well — but it is inside the marriage section, and the closer (line 336) converts it into a lesson the bees are teaching him.

**The objection's messenger.** Qatar's critics are represented in the body by Human Rights Watch as a document and Joe Lycett as a stunt. The named campaigners who made the strongest and most specifically wounding version of the argument are absent.

## What I expected

- **A conflict-of-interest clause on the documentary.** For a piece this careful about epistemics, one clause at first citation.
- **Rebecca Loos's own response,** given that the draft quotes Beckham's denial and Victoria's account in the same three sentences.
- **A human rights voice, not just a human rights report.** HRW's world report is documentation; Peter Tatchell's objection is an argument, and it is the one that lands on Beckham specifically.
- **Some acknowledgment that the self-made narrative is itself a product.** The draft's thesis is that Beckham converts crisis into finished work. The 2023 documentary is the most literal instance of that thesis in his entire career — a finished product about his crises, made by his own company, released three months after Messi signed. The draft notices the pattern at line 330 ("another finished piece of work") and does not notice that its own primary source is an example of it. That is the biggest missed insight in the piece, and it is critical *and* flattering to the thesis at once.
- **Confidence markers on interiority** matching the ones the draft applies to facts.

I did **not** expect condemnation, a verdict on the affair, or a hostile reading of the knighthood. The draft's basic posture — sympathetic, hedged, unwilling to adjudicate private life — is defensible and I am not asking it to change.

## What surprised me

**Welcome:** that the draft names Ted and Sandra's 2002 separation and explicitly declines to assert a cause (line 204). Refusing an available dramatic payoff because the record does not support it is the opposite of what this genre usually does.

**Welcome:** the fee is reported as a disputed range rather than a headline number (line 326). Most write-ups pick the biggest figure.

**Jarring:** "Call it vanity if you like" (line 258). The draft invites the critical reading by name and then immediately overrides it with an unsourced psychological one. Naming an objection in order to dismiss it in the same sentence is the one moment the piece argues like a defence brief.

**Jarring:** "The choice of prop was the argument: the people making it were not strangers to him" (line 326) is a sharp, correct observation — sitting immediately after a sentence that has just told the reader the shredded cash was fake and already donated. The deflation and the insight are in the wrong order, so the strongest framing of the objection arrives already discounted.

## Red flags

### CRITIC-R1 — Blocker. Undisclosed dependence on a source the subject produced

- **Location:** Six reader-visible citations to Netflix's *Beckham* across the body — the cold open (line 110, "In the 2023 Netflix series *Beckham*, he called the routine exhausting"), the Ted quote (line 197), the 1998 section (line 238), the Victoria "hardest period" quote (line 284), and the "at the end of the 2023 Netflix series" family claim (line 288).
- **Trust problem:** The draft's sympathetic core is sourced from a documentary made by Studio 99, David Beckham's own production company, in association with Ventureland. A reader is invited to treat "he called the routine exhausting" and "the whole country hated me" as observed record. They are self-authored narrative, selected and released by the subject's own company. The draft discloses exactly this class of conflict for *Country Life* at line 228 and not for the source doing ten times the work.
- **Evidence:** Studio 99 lists *Beckham* as its own production (studio99.com/films/beckham). Industry criticism at the time questioned how impartial a series produced by Beckham could be, and specifically why he was not pushed harder on the Loos allegation. **His strongest response must travel with the criticism:** Beckham has publicly denied that it was a vanity project, says he exercised no editorial control at the edit stage and had not seen the series before release, and director Fisher Stevens has said he had final cut and joined only on the condition that nothing was off the table (C21Media / Variety, RTS London 2024).
- **Minimum viable repair:** One clause at first citation plus one sentence of his response. E.g. at line 110, "the 2023 Netflix series *Beckham*, made by his own production company" — and, in the 1998 section or a footnote, the Stevens final-cut answer. No restructuring required.
- **Expected benefit:** Every subsequent quotation from the series gets correctly weighted by the reader, and the draft's epistemic posture stops being selectively applied. It also *strengthens* the thesis — see CRITIC-R3's note on the documentary as itself a produced answer to crisis.
- **Confidence:** High on the production fact; high that the omission matters; medium on whether a reader consciously notices (the effect works whether or not they do).
- **Acceptance test:** A reader who learns after finishing that Beckham's company made the documentary should find nothing in the article that misled them. Today, they would find the *Country Life* disclosure and ask why the bigger one is missing.

### CRITIC-R2 — Blocker. The staged-reveal claim is contradicted by Beckham's own account, and it is thesis-supporting evidence

- **Location:** Line 264: "When he shaved his head in 2000, he kept it hidden under a beanie through the entire warm-up, so it stayed a secret from his teammates and the cameras alike until he walked out onto the pitch with the team. The reveal was the whole point."
- **Trust problem:** This is the draft's clearest *unhedged* instance of deliberate image-staging, and it is load-bearing for the Type 3 reading (the Alice band that follows is explicitly hedged with "Whether he planned it or not"). If the strongest example of deliberate staging did not happen as described, the reader who checks loses confidence in every behavioural inference the piece draws.
- **Evidence:** Beckham's own account (Sky Sports, 2021, packet S-06 / CLM-06): the concealed style was a **mohawk**; Ferguson saw it **in the changing room** before the warm-up; Beckham found clippers and shaved it at Wembley before going out; the mohawk was revealed later that season on England duty. The draft then narrates that same Wembley incident correctly in its very next paragraph (line 266) as if it were a separate episode. So one event is told twice and the first telling is contradicted by the subject.
- **Minimum viable repair:** Delete the first telling. The second paragraph already contains the better version and it makes the same point more sharply — Beckham's image ambition ran into Ferguson's authority and lost. If a successful staged reveal is wanted, use the England-duty reveal with its own date.
- **Expected benefit:** Removes a factual contradiction, removes an accidental duplication, and replaces an invented success with a documented and more interesting collision.
- **Confidence:** High. The contradiction is with the subject's own recollection, and the draft's internal duplication corroborates that something went wrong here.
- **Acceptance test:** No sentence in the brand section asserts a completed on-pitch reveal at the 2000 Charity Shield, and the Wembley shaving appears exactly once.

### CRITIC-R3 — Blocker. The "insurance" reframe is exculpatory and disconfirmed by chronology

- **Location:** Line 258: "People roll their eyes at what came after all this: the endless deals, the walking-billboard fame... The man who did that had learned, at his lowest point, exactly how fast a life built on one thing can be taken from you. So he built a life with more than one thing in it. Call it vanity if you like. It worked like insurance, taken out by someone who had already been hunted once."
- **Trust problem:** This is psychological explanation doing the work of a defence. It names the critical reading ("vanity," "put his face on almost anything"), and answers it with a trauma-derived motive the draft cannot know — in a piece that elsewhere insists it will not state private motive as fact. It then sits two sections upstream of Qatar, where the reader is asked to judge exactly the behaviour it has just excused.
- **Evidence:** The causal ordering is wrong. Beckham's commercial identity predates the 1998 red card: the Adidas boot sponsorship was agreed in 1996, and the Brylcreem deal — reported at £4m / $6m — was signed in **1997**, when he was already a tabloid figure (Marketing Week, "David Beckham: a history in ads"; contemporary 1997 launch coverage). The draft's own text corroborates this: the Adidas-and-M3 anecdote at line 270 sits in his early career, before the section reaches 1998 at all. The diversification was not insurance taken out after the hunt; it was already underway before it.
- **Minimum viable repair:** Two options, both cheap. (a) Keep the observation, drop the causation: the 1998 experience plausibly *hardened* an existing appetite rather than creating it — and saying so is both defensible and more interesting. (b) Attribute it: if Beckham has said something like this himself, quote him and let it be his claim rather than the narrator's. Either way, delete "Call it vanity if you like," which is the sentence that converts analysis into advocacy.
- **Expected benefit:** The Type 3 reading actually improves — a man whose commercial expansion *preceded* his public destruction is better evidence for identity-level ambition than one who diversified defensively. And Qatar arrives un-pre-empted.
- **Confidence:** High on the 1996/1997 dates; high that the passage functions exculpatorily; high that the thesis survives the fix.
- **Acceptance test:** No sentence claims the multi-arena career began as a response to 1998. A reader who knows the Brylcreem deal was signed in 1997 finds nothing in the piece that date falsifies.

### CRITIC-R4 — Blocker. The Loos passage is sourced entirely from one side

- **Location:** Line 284: "a former personal assistant named Rebecca Loos sold a story alleging an affair. Beckham called the claims 'ludicrous.' Victoria, in the Netflix documentary two decades later, called that stretch 'the hardest period' of their marriage."
- **Trust problem:** Three consecutive attributions, all Beckham-side, about a named living person who has an on-record response. "Sold a story" is broadly supported by the reported circumstances but is a delegitimising characterisation, and it is the only characterisation the reader gets. The section heading frames the episode as a threat to an enterprise ("Why the Beckham marriage survived its worst year"), so the other party appears as a hazard to a brand rather than as a person with an account. Given the power asymmetry — a personal assistant against a global brand and, two decades later, a Netflix series made by his own company — this is the passage where the draft's sympathy is least earned.
- **Evidence:** Loos responded publicly in October 2023 (ITV News, CNN, packet S-23): Beckham "is portraying himself as the victim and he's making me look like a liar, like I've made up these stories"; she said his account of the period was "all, 'poor me'" and that "he needs to take responsibility"; she reported receiving social-media abuse after the series was released. Beckham's denial is already in the draft, so including hers restores balance rather than tilting it.
- **Minimum viable repair:** One sentence: her allegation remains contested and unresolved, she publicly disputed the documentary's framing in 2023, and reporting notes the abuse she received afterwards. Optionally replace "sold a story" with a neutral verb ("went public with an allegation," which the packet supports) and let the paid-interview detail stand on its own if it is kept.
- **Expected benefit:** The passage stops reading as the brand's version of events, and the draft's own thesis gains a data point — the crisis was answered with a produced narrative in which the other party had no say.
- **Confidence:** High. Her response is well documented and directly contradicts the framing the draft adopts.
- **Acceptance test:** A reader who knows Loos's 2023 statements finds the article has already told them the allegation is contested from both directions.

### CRITIC-C1 — Concern. A narrated scene the transcript does not support

- **Location:** Line 240: "Years later, on Stick to Football, Roy Keane, a man with no taste for sentiment, watched the old footage of the abuse play back and could barely get a sentence out." Also line 248: "When Neville asked him, decades on, how he functioned through it."
- **Problem:** Both sentences stage a scene — a witness overcome, a friend asking the question. The packet (CLM-04, CLM-05, Disputes §1) finds the Keane words bracketed by [Applause]/[Music] markers in a clip run whose immediately preceding line is independently documented as Keane **in the Netflix documentary**, with the live studio reaction arriving after. The questioner in the "I honestly, I actually don't know" exchange is unidentified in the transcript; if it is documentary footage, it is the director, not Neville. The emotional peak of the 1998 section — football's least sentimental man, silenced — may therefore be Beckham's own documentary quoted as independent witness. That compounds CRITIC-R1 rather than duplicating it.
- **Repair:** Drop the staging, keep the quotes. "Roy Keane, a man with no taste for sentiment, has described the abuse in terms he uses for almost nothing else" costs one clause and asserts nothing about where he was sitting. Attribute the "I don't know" exchange to the source without naming an interlocutor.
- **Confidence:** Medium-high that the staging is unsupported; medium on the underlying provenance, which the packet could not close.
- **Acceptance test:** No sentence describes a physical reaction, a room, or a questioner that the cited transcript does not evidence.

### CRITIC-C2 — Concern. The objection is personified by a comedian rather than by its strongest advocates

- **Location:** Line 326, the Qatar backlash paragraph.
- **Problem:** HRW appears as a document; Joe Lycett appears as a performer with fake money. That is the objection's weakest available face. The strongest version is not a stunt: LGBT campaigner Peter Tatchell criticised the ambassadorship while noting Beckham "supported our battle for equality when many others did not," and the End Sportswashing group argued he should be using his position to draw attention to Qatar's record on migrant workers instead. That version indicts him with his own history rather than with a shredder.
- **Repair:** One sentence adding a named campaigner's objection before the Lycett stunt. The Lycett material is worth keeping — the prop observation is genuinely good — but it should follow the argument, not stand in for it.
- **Confidence:** High that a stronger steelman is available and cheap.
- **Acceptance test:** The Qatar objection is stated once by someone whose job is the objection, before any stunt is described.

### CRITIC-C3 — Concern. The controversy section resolves itself

- **Location:** Line 332, the closing paragraph of the Qatar section: the knighthood, the charity record "that predates the sportswashing argument by seventeen years," "The boy once hung in effigy had become Sir David Beckham," and an admiring type-arc close.
- **Problem:** The charity clause does rebuttal work it cannot do — that good works predate a controversy is not responsive to the controversy, and the "seventeen years" precision gives the non-sequitur the texture of an argument. The section then ends on standing, honour and survival. Placed seventh of eight, with nothing after it re-opening the question, the effect is that the criticism is bounded and the reputation is restored inside the same section.
- **Repair:** Keep the charity facts, delete the "predates the sportswashing argument" clause, and let the paragraph end without converting the honour into a verdict. The Type 3 observation can stay; the exoneration should not be inside it. (I searched for documented objection to the 2025 honour itself and did not find it, so this repair is about not closing the argument, not about adding new critics.)
- **Confidence:** Medium-high. Reasonable editors can disagree about section order; the non-sequitur clause is not a matter of taste.
- **Acceptance test:** The Qatar section ends without any sentence that functions as a defence, and the knighthood is reported rather than deployed.

### CRITIC-C6 — Concern. Interiority asserted flat where facts are hedged

- **Location:** Line 200 ("Feelings were a luxury. Delivery was the job. That is the making of a Three: a child who works out that being loved and being useful are the same transaction") and line 214 ("he has never once tested what happens if he lets it").
- **Problem:** The draft hedges wings, subtypes, arrows, diagnosis and family disputes, then states a child's inner conclusion and a lifelong private negative ("never once") as fact. "Never once" is unfalsifiable and unknowable. A critic reading the hedges as sincere hits these and re-reads the hedges as decoration.
- **Repair:** The inner-thought block is explicitly marked as interpretation by its form and can stay if "never once" becomes something the form can carry. Line 200 needs one modal: what a boy raised this way *would plausibly* learn. This costs almost nothing in force.
- **Confidence:** Medium-high. This is a consistency judgment, not a factual one, but the inconsistency is internal to the draft's own standard.
- **Acceptance test:** No sentence outside a marked interpretive block asserts what Beckham concluded, felt, or never did in private.

### CRITIC-C7 — Concern. The thesis-opening quote rests on an unverified citation

- **Location:** Line 122, Ferguson cited to *Managing My Life*, 1999.
- **Problem:** It is the first evidence the reader meets and the anchor for "the gift was manufactured." - **Confidence:** Medium-high that the citation is unconfirmed; low that the words are wrong.

## Specific improvements

Beyond the repairs attached to each finding, three changes would raise the draft from defensible to genuinely strong from this standpoint. Each is diagnosis; the editor writes the words.

1. **Turn the conflict into the insight.** The draft's thesis is that Beckham answers crisis with finished work. The 2023 documentary — produced by his company, released in the same quarter as the Messi signing — is that thesis performing itself, and the draft already half-sees it at line 330 ("another finished piece of work"). Naming the documentary as an instance of the pattern *while relying on it as a source* resolves CRITIC-R1 and produces the piece's sharpest non-obvious observation in the same move. **Benefit:** disclosure stops being a concession and becomes evidence. **Confidence:** high. **Acceptance test:** the article can be read by someone who knows about Studio 99 without their learning anything the article did not already tell them.

2. **Let one criticism survive the article.** Every objection currently terminates inside its own section: Qatar closes on the knighthood, the affair closes on the rebuilt brand, Brooklyn's allegation closes on the bees teaching him patience. The closer (line 336) is the place to leave one unresolved, and it nearly does — "consent is the one thing no amount of finished work has ever bought him" is the right sentence pointed at the family only. Pointing it at Qatar as well costs a clause. **Benefit:** the sympathy reads as a conclusion rather than a destination. **Confidence:** medium-high. **Acceptance test:** a reader finishing the article can name one criticism the article did not answer on his behalf.

3. **Apply the disclosure standard uniformly.** *Country Life* is disclosed as guest-edited; Netflix is not disclosed as self-produced; *Stick to Football* is not noted as the podcast of the man who was best man at his wedding (a fact the packet establishes and the draft itself uses at line 128, "who spent seven years beside him"). One consistent policy, one clause each. **Benefit:** the reader can calibrate all three friendly sources. **Confidence:** high. **Acceptance test:** no source in the piece has an undisclosed relationship to the subject that a reader would consider material.

## Follow-on questions

**CRITIC-Q1 — Did Beckham exercise editorial control over the Netflix series?**
*What would change:* If a credible account establishes meaningful editorial involvement beyond the executive-producer credit, CRITIC-R1 escalates from a disclosure fix to a reweighting of every documentary-sourced quote in the piece. If Stevens's final-cut account holds unchallenged, one disclosure clause plus his response is sufficient and the draft is close to fine.
*Best source:* Fisher Stevens's own interviews around the October 2023 release and the RTS London 2024 session where Beckham addressed the vanity-project charge directly (C21Media, Variety).

**CRITIC-Q2 — Are the Keane and Neville lines live studio speech or documentary clips?**
*What would change:* If clips, CRITIC-C1 escalates to a blocker on both citation and narration, and the draft's two most persuasive third-party testimonies collapse into the same self-produced source as CRITIC-R1. If live, the citation stands and only the invented staging needs cutting.
*Best source:* A speaker-labelled transcript or the video of the October 2023 *Stick to Football* episode, timestamped against the Netflix episode 2 footage.

**CRITIC-Q3 — Is there an on-record Beckham statement about why he diversified commercially?**
*What would change:* It would convert CRITIC-R3's narrator-asserted motive into an attributed claim, which the draft is allowed to make. Absent one, the chronology fix is mandatory.
*Best source:* His own memoirs (*My Side*, 2003; *Both Feet on the Ground*), which the packet lists as not attempted, and contemporary 1997–98 endorsement coverage.

**CRITIC-Q4 — Was there documented public objection to the 2025 knighthood on Qatar grounds?**
*What would change:* If yes, CRITIC-C3 becomes a straightforward omission with a named source rather than a structural note. I searched and did not find it; I have therefore not asserted it.
*Best source:* Contemporary June 2025 honours coverage, and statements from the campaigners named in CRITIC-C2.

**CRITIC-Q5 — Did Beckham's Qatar relationship continue after 2022?**
*What would change:* The draft's arc implies the episode is closed and survived. An ongoing or renewed arrangement would make "survive the next verdict" read as premature rather than observational, and would strengthen CRITIC-C3.
*Best source:* Visit Qatar and Qatari tourism-authority announcements 2023–2026; trade coverage of his ambassadorship renewals.
