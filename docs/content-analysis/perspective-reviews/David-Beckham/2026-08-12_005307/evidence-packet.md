## Identity and scope

**Subject.** David Robert Joseph Beckham, born 2 May 1975 in Leytonstone, east London. Age 51 as of this packet's compilation date. English footballer (Manchester United, Preston North End on loan, Real Madrid, LA Galaxy, AC Milan on loan, Paris Saint-Germain; 115 England caps, England captain 2000-2006), later co-owner of Inter Miami CF, brand ambassador, and fashion/business figure. Knighted in the 2025 King's Birthday Honours; correctly styled Sir David Beckham from that point. Wikidata Q10520.

**Aliases and labels used in the draft.** "Becks"; "Posh and Becks" (with Victoria Beckham, née Adams, Spice Girls); "the flash Cockney" (teammate nickname); "metrosexual" (a label popularised in 2002-2003 partly through him).

**Why the article covers him.** The draft is a 9takes personality-analysis piece proposing Enneagram Type 3 (the Achiever) with a 3w2 wing and self-preservation/social subtype as lower-confidence hypotheses. Its primary thesis: Beckham answers rejection with finished work and visible results, having learned young that praise follows delivery. Its secondary thesis: beekeeping is off-scoreboard work that his method cannot accelerate.

**Knowledge cutoff and freshness.** The compiler's training cutoff is May 2026. Every claim in this packet dated after roughly mid-2025 was checked against live web sources during compilation on 12 August 2026, not recalled. The most recent events the draft relies on are Brooklyn Beckham's January 2026 Instagram statement and a Victoria Beckham interview dated April 2026; the latter was **not independently verified** (see Research limitations).

**Scope note.** Two repository transcripts exist and were used as primary material:
- `youtube-transcripts/david-beckham-stick-to-football-neville-keane.txt` (1,223 lines, auto-generated, unlabeled speakers, no punctuation)

## First-person evidence

**On his father and emotional expression** (verified verbatim against the local *Stick to Football* transcript, lines 405-421; auto-transcript, lightly cleaned):
> "if I turn around to my dad at the time and said dad I don't feel great today can I see a therapist I want need to talk about it he'd be like boy just get on with it you know and that's that's how I was bought up"

Supports: that Beckham describes a household norm against articulating distress, in his own words, in 2023. Cannot support: that this norm caused any specific later behaviour, or that he holds the interpretation the draft builds on it.

**On OCD** (ITV interview broadcast April 2006). Full reported wording:
> "I've got this obsessive-compulsive disorder where I have to have everything in a straight line or everything has to be in pairs. I'll put my Pepsi cans in the fridge, and if there's one too many, then I'll put it in another cupboard somewhere ... everything has to be perfect."

Supports: that Beckham self-describes rituals and uses the term OCD. Cannot support: a clinical diagnosis, a severity, or an Enneagram inference. **Note:** the draft's body quotation removes the Pepsi-cans sentence and joins "in pairs" directly to "Everything has to be perfect" without an ellipsis. The draft's internal testimony ledger does use an ellipsis; the rendered quote block does not.

**On the 1998 aftermath** (Netflix *Beckham*, ep. 2 "Seeing Red", released 4 Oct 2023): "The whole country hated me"; "I felt very vulnerable and alone"; "I wasn't eating, I wasn't sleeping. I was a mess." Widely reported. Supports: severe self-described distress. Cannot support: a diagnosis.

**On the 1996 goal:** "It changed my life." Widely reported. Supports: his own framing of the goal as a turning point.

**On the 2000 Charity Shield haircut** (Sky Sports, 2021):
> "I walked in the changing room and he hadn't seen it because I was too scared to even show him." / "I giggled and he was like 'no, I'm serious. Go and shave it off'." / "So I had to find a pair of clippers and I shaved it off in Wembley Stadium." / "Manager always rules."

Supports: that the concealed style was a mohawk, that Ferguson saw it in the changing room, and that it was shaved before he went out. **Directly contradicts** a staged on-pitch reveal at that fixture. Reporting adds that Beckham revealed the mohawk later that season on England duty.

**On Qatar** (Sky Sports, 4 Oct 2023, at the documentary premiere):
> "I had a lot of conversations with the LGBTQ community when I was there. They said they had enjoyed the games and they felt it was the safest World Cup they'd had for a long time, it was an important competition and one I was proud to be a part of."

Supports: the "spoke with LGBTQ community members who felt safe" and "proud to have taken part" elements of the draft's summary. Does **not** contain the draft's third element, "he believed engagement through sport mattered" — that idea appears in Beckham's 2021-2022 statements, not in this retrieved quotation.

**On sexuality and prejudice** (*Attitude*, June 2002):
> "It shouldn't matter what people are and it never has done to me. It's just not an issue."

Verified verbatim, including via *Attitude*'s own later coverage. Supports the draft's use exactly as written.

**On the countryside and bees** (*Country Life*, October 2025 guest-edited issue): describes beekeeping as "like a miracle"; separately says he never saw himself as a beekeeper. The "peace" quotation attributed by the draft to a companion interview was not independently retrieved (see limitations).

**On spending** (archival footage played during the *Stick to Football* episode; transcript lines 517-529): "I like nice stuff ... as soon as we got money it was like I want to buy a nice watch I love shopping I love cars ... it's a Rolex ... that's Gucci you got to look good for the pap ... a contract with Adidas for £50,000 and I went and bought an M3 for £50,000". Note this is Beckham speaking in **archival footage**, not to teammates and not in the 2023 studio.

## Third-party testimony

**Sir Alex Ferguson** (manager). The "relentless application" quotation is widely and consistently attributed to Ferguson about Beckham:
> "David Beckham is Britain's finest striker of a football not because of God-given talent but because he practises with a relentless application that the vast majority of less gifted players wouldn't contemplate."

Supports: a named authority attributing Beckham's quality to practice over gift. **Sourcing caveat:** the specific attribution to *Managing My Life* (1999) could not be confirmed to a primary or high-authority source; the quote circulates chiefly through quotation aggregators (Tier 4), and at least one listing dates it to May 2013. Treat the words as well-attested and the book-and-year citation as unverified.

**Gary Neville** (teammate, best man at the 1999 wedding, seven-plus years alongside Beckham on the right):
> "it wasn't enough for him he wanted be more than a football player"

Present in the local transcript at lines 1051-1053 (the missing "to" is an auto-transcript artifact). Internal evidence identifies Neville as the speaker: the surrounding words are "he was practicing free kicks I was practicing throwings" and "I was subservient ... because I need David to go and do something magical", consistent with the right-back beside Beckham. **Provenance caveat:** the run is introduced by "let's have a look at this where is it there we go **Gary Neville**" and contains 1990s archival Neville, which indicates the line sits inside a captioned clip played on the podcast rather than live studio speech. See Disputes.

**Roy Keane** (teammate and captain):
> "it's inhumane what he had to put up with inhumane it would have broke everybody would have broke probably 99.9% of footballers"

Present in the local transcript at lines 465-468. Immediately preceding it in the same run: "lucky for Beck he's got players in his team that can flatten people ... you go after him you're going after all of us". That preceding line is independently documented as **Roy Keane speaking in the Netflix documentary *Beckham***. This is strong evidence that the "inhumane" words are Keane's from the documentary, played as a clip during the podcast, and not a live studio reaction. See CLM-04.

**The spending anecdote.** Secondary reporting attributes to Gary Neville a line that Beckham "would get his salary on a Friday, spend it all on Saturday, and wait for a week until his next pay cheque" for matching clothes. The local transcript (lines 534-543) renders the anecdote and then continues "**where me and Gary were like** let's save it for a rainy day", a first-person construction that excludes Gary Neville as the speaker of at least that sentence. Because the transcript is unlabeled, the speaker boundary is unresolvable from it alone. See Disputes.

**Ted Beckham** (father). Asked in the Netflix documentary whether he had been too hard on his son:
> "No ... if I told him how good he was, then he's got nothing to work at."

Independently corroborated in reporting on the documentary. Supports the draft's use directly, and is the strongest single item of testimony for its origin thesis, because it states the mechanism in the builder's voice.

Ted's separate, attributed account of the marriage breakdown, from his autobiography: "All the hype, all the stress, all the attention - it wasn't a world we were used to and it was very tough." This is an **attributed claim by an interested party**, not an established cause.

**Sandra Beckham** (mother; hairdresser, reported as working in nursing homes). Reporting on the documentary states that Sandra struggled with how hard Ted pushed David, that Ted's shouting often brought David to tears, and that she asked Ted to tone it down without effect. Corroborates the draft's characterisation.

**Victoria Beckham** (wife). On 1998: "He was absolutely broken. He was in pieces ... He was really depressed, absolutely clinically depressed." On 2003-2004, asked by director Fisher Stevens whether it was the hardest time in the marriage: "100 percent. It was the hardest period for us ... it felt like the world was against us. And here's the thing; we were against each other." Both verified. These are a spouse's retrospective judgments, which is how the draft frames them.

**Brooklyn Beckham** (son). 19 January 2026 Instagram Stories statement, reported at 821 words: describes the family dynamic as "performative", "inauthentic", a "façade"; states that "Brand Beckham comes first"; alleges his parents "have controlled narratives in the press about our family"; states "I do not want to reconcile with my family"; alleges they tried to damage his relationship with Nicola Peltz Beckham before their 2022 wedding. Contemporary reporting states his parents did not comment on the allegations.

**Rebecca Loos** (former personal assistant). Alleged a 2003 affair; went public in 2004; in October 2023 criticised the documentary, saying Beckham "portrayed himself as a victim". An attributed claim, contested by Beckham.

**Critics.** Human Rights Watch documented migrant-worker abuses and laws discriminating against LGBT people in Qatar. Joe Lycett's shredding stunt is documented, including his own confirmation that the shredded cash was fake and the £10,000 had already been donated.

## Public record, accomplishments, failures, and controversies

**Accomplishments.** Six Premier League titles, the 1999 Champions League and treble, La Liga (2007), MLS Cup (2011, 2012), Ligue 1 (2013) — the last making him a champion in his final season, as the draft's timeline states. 115 England caps; England captain 2000-2006. PFA Young Player of the Year 1997. OBE 2003; Knight Bachelor 2025. UNICEF Goodwill Ambassador since 2005; 7 Fund launched 2015. Co-owner of Inter Miami CF; the $25m expansion option is widely described as one of the most lucrative clauses in sports history, with the franchise later valued in the billions and MLS reported to have offered $50m to buy the option back.

**Failures and reversals.** The 1998 sending-off and the sustained national abuse that followed. Being sold by Manchester United in 2003 after the deterioration of the Ferguson relationship. A decade of stalled Miami stadium sites and political fights between exercising the option (2014) and the first match (2020).

**Controversies.**
- *Qatar.* Visit Qatar ambassadorship during the 2022 World Cup. Fee never disclosed; contemporary reports ranged from about £10m for the tournament role to figures of £125m-£150m over roughly a decade, themselves disputed. Criticism centred on Qatar's criminalisation of same-sex relationships and documented migrant-worker abuses, and was sharpened by Beckham's own two-decade association with gay audiences. Beckham's public answer (4 Oct 2023) rested on conversations with LGBTQ visitors and pride in participating. Contested characterisation: whether the arrangement constitutes "sportswashing" is a critical judgment, not a fact.
- *Rebecca Loos, 2004.* Allegation of an affair; denied; unresolved in the public record. The draft's phrase "sold a story" reflects widely reported circumstances but is a characterisation.
- *Brooklyn Beckham, 2026.* Competing family accounts; unresolved and unresolvable from public sources.
- *Effigy reporting.* Both "outside a London pub" and "outside Upton Park" at the West Ham away fixture appear in reputable retrospectives. These may be separate incidents or variant accounts of one.

**Commercial ventures not in the draft** (context for a completeness judgment, not a defect): the IM8 supplement line (see the local CBS Miami transcript), Studio 99 production company, Golden Beez / bee-related product lines, and the King's Foundation ambassadorship (2024).

## Enneagram hypothesis evidence

The draft's Type 3 call is a **hypothesis**. Nothing in this section promotes it.

**Evidence consistent with Type 3 (Achiever).**
- Ferguson's testimony that the quality came from practice, not gift (attributed claim, well-attested).
- Neville's "it wasn't enough for him ... more than a football player" (attributed claim; provenance contested, see CLM-03).
- Documented serial reinvention across distinct arenas: United, Madrid, LA, Miami ownership, fashion, media.
- Deliberate image management: the concealed mohawk, the post-boot Alice band appearance, the *Attitude* cover, the guest-edited magazine issue.
- The 1998-2001 sequence in which public rehabilitation came through performance (captaincy 2000, Greece free kick 2001) rather than explanation.
- Ted Beckham's own stated method: withheld praise as a motivational engine.

**Evidence against, or unexplained by, Type 3.**
- Sustained calm, deference, soft-spokenness — the draft itself names this as the strongest counterevidence and routes it to Type 9.
- Precision, exactness and "everything has to be perfect" — routes to Type 1. The draft correctly declines to use self-described compulsions as type evidence, which also removes this from the Type 1 column.
- The 2am cleaning with no audience. The draft explicitly excludes it from the case for type, which is an integrity move rather than a gap.
- Long-horizon loyalty: a decade-plus on Inter Miami, twenty years with UNICEF, a marriage sustained through public crisis. Routes to Type 6 or to the draft's Six growth-line hypothesis.
- Beekeeping's tolerance for uncontrolled timelines, which the draft frames as the counter-thread.

**Closest alternative.** Type 9, per the draft. Type 1 is the second alternative.

**Discriminating evidence.** The distinguishing question is whether the ambition is *identity-level* (Three) or whether the visible achievement is a byproduct of conscientious craft (One) or of accommodation and going along with what was built around him (Nine). The strongest discriminators available: Neville's "not enough" testimony (if its provenance holds), the unprompted expansion into arenas nobody required of him (Miami ownership, fashion, media), and the pattern of answering reputational crisis with new output rather than withdrawal or explanation.

**Behaviour the type does not explain.** The audience-free night routine; the willingness to be a visible beginner with the bees and to defer to a local expert; the absence of any documented public counterattack against his 1998 abusers.

**Theory-level items kept separate.** Wing 3w2, sp/so subtype stacking, and the stress-to-Nine / growth-to-Six arrows are all framework inferences, not observations. Public evidence cannot establish private motive, and none of the sources in this packet speak to Enneagram type at all.

## Disputes and unresolved questions

**1. Clip playback versus live studio speech on *Stick to Football* (highest-value unresolved issue).** The draft cites four items to "Stick to Football, October 2023" and narrates two of them as live studio scenes. The local transcript shows at least two clip-introduction markers ("well let's look at another clip", "let's have a look at this where is it there we go Gary Neville") and bracketing [Applause]/[Music] markers around the Keane run. The preceding Keane sentence in that run is independently documented as coming from the Netflix documentary. The draft's working notes state the quotations were "checked against the full October 2023 episode transcript", which is true of the words but does not distinguish clip audio from live speech. **What stays qualified:** the venue, date and staging of CLM-03, CLM-04 and CLM-05. The words themselves are not in doubt; where and when they were spoken is. Note that Keane and Neville both appear as interviewees in the documentary, so identical words can legitimately exist in both places — this cannot be settled without the documentary's own footage or a labelled podcast transcript.

**2. Speaker of the "jeans to match" anecdote.** Secondary reporting attributes it to Gary Neville. The panel composition for that episode was not established in this pass.
