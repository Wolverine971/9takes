## Bottom-line verdict

If Beckham read this, he would recognise himself in most of it and would probably accept the Type 3 label as fair comment. He would not accept four things, and three of them are the article's own sourcing failures rather than disagreements about typology.

The draft has real protective instincts. It refuses to convert his self-described OCD into type evidence, twice, in writing. It withholds motive at the exact moment a lesser piece would assert it ("Whether he planned it or not"). It holds the Brooklyn dispute open instead of adjudicating it. It gives him his twenty-year record with gay audiences *before* the Qatar charge rather than clustering the credit afterwards. That is more care than this genre usually shows, and it is why the verdict is strained rather than broken.

The failure is narrower and sharper than "unfair." The draft repeatedly reaches past what Beckham himself has said, on the record, in the very sources it cites, and substitutes a more calculating man. His own explanation of how he survived 1998 — stated four times in the episode the draft quotes four times — is that other people carried him. The draft deletes that and answers its own question: "He delivered." His own explanation for hiding the 2000 haircut is that he was too scared to show his manager. The draft says "The reveal was the whole point." His own on-record answer to the marriage-as-brand charge is "Of course not. We stay together because we love each other." The draft asserts the charge in its own narrating voice and never mentions that he has answered it.

Each of those is repairable from evidence already in hand. But the pattern matters more than the instances: every unsourced addition in this draft pushes the same direction, toward a man who stages things. A fairness proxy has to name that as a tilt, not a set of coincidences. The thesis is defensible; the thumb on the scale is not necessary to defend it.

## What landed

**"Whether he planned it or not, a private humiliation had become a photograph on his own terms."** (brand H2, Ferguson boot paragraph.) This is the single most important sentence in the draft for fairness. It is the one place where the article had a perfect opportunity to assert calculation, had every incentive to, and explicitly declined. It gets the thesis across as *behaviour with an unknown motive*, which is the only honest form the thesis can take. It must survive revision untouched, and it should become the model for the passages named in SUBJECT-R2 and SUBJECT-R3.

**"Those are Beckham's words. A television interview cannot establish a clinical diagnosis, and an Enneagram profile should not absorb a mental-health condition into type theory."** (father H2.) A man who volunteered a mental-health disclosure in 2006 has watched it become a punchline for twenty years. This draft takes it, quotes it, and then formally refuses to spend it. The Rabbit Hole repeats the refusal: "No clean typing explains the audience-free cleaning at 2am. It is excluded from the case for type." That is the difference between analysis and diagnosis, and it is the strongest dignity move in the piece.

**"It does not prove David's motive or his Enneagram type. It marks the one place where the method has nothing to offer, because the man who has answered every crisis of his life by producing something cannot produce this."** (marriage H2, Brooklyn passage.) Both sides attributed, neither adjudicated, and the article resists the enormous pull of using a son's allegation as thesis proof. Preserve the structure exactly.

**"His reserve remains genuine counterevidence rather than something the Type 3 case gets to explain away."** (Rabbit Hole.) Beckham's quietness is the trait most often read as vacancy by people who write about him. Naming it as counterevidence the framework does not get to metabolise treats him as a person rather than a specimen.

**The 1998 street detail, in his own frame.** "He was spat at in the street of the city he was from. Fans punched his car window at traffic lights when they saw who was inside." I checked this against the primary transcript: it is his own live account, including the point he himself makes about the location — "when I was in London and that's obviously where I'm from" (`youtube-transcripts/david-beckham-stick-to-football-neville-keane.txt`, lines 505-511). The draft uses his own words and his own emphasis. This is what the rest of the 1998 section should have done.

**"Results can answer a stadium; repair between adults requires another person's consent, and consent is the one thing no amount of finished work has ever bought him."** (closer.) It states the limit of the man without contempt for him, and it declines to resolve the family dispute in his favour or against him. Best sentence in the draft.

**Ted's method stated in Ted's own voice** rather than paraphrased by the article: "No. If I told him how good he was, then he's got nothing to work at." The builder describes the machine. The article does not have to allege anything.

## What missed

**The 1998 section promises his interior and delivers the article's.** The section's implicit promise is: here is what those three years did to him, in his terms. It quotes his distress accurately, then reaches the causal question — how did he get through it — and fills it with "He delivered." Beckham answered that question himself, unprompted, in the live studio of the same episode: "it didn't feel tough at the time because I had the right people around me I had a manager that looked after me a team that looked after me and and and teammates that every time I stepped out onto the pitch I knew that they had my back" (transcript lines 313-322). He says a version of it four separate times (lines 317, 414, 489, 513). The article's answer and his answer are not variants of each other; they point at different things. See SUBJECT-R1.

**The brand section's "he staged it with real care" is asserted, then illustrated with a staging that did not happen.** The paragraph's job is to prove deliberate image management. Its lead example is fabricated (SUBJECT-R2), which is a shame, because the paragraph that follows — the Alice band — proves the point properly and without inventing anything.

**Four "Stick to Football, October 2023" citations create a false intimacy the piece leans on.** The draft stages two of them as live scenes among old friends: Keane "watched the old footage of the abuse play back and could barely get a sentence out," and "when Neville asked him, decades on, how he functioned through it." Both sit inside clip playback (SUBJECT-R4). The effect the draft is buying — his teammates, in a room, moved — is warmer and more validating than what the record supports.

## What I expected

Beckham has denied the marriage-as-brand reading in the plainest available terms, on BBC Radio 4. The draft advances that exact reading, in its own voice, and does not mention that he has answered it. See SUBJECT-R3.

**The support system in the 1998 account.** Not as a softener — as the thing he says happened.

**The unabridged therapist answer.** The draft cuts him off at "That's how I was brought up" and then tells the reader what kind of man that produced. His next breath complicates it: he says he did not need to see anyone because "I had the right people around me," and then explicitly endorses the alternative — "these days we talk about people being able to talk and it's a good thing and I'm not saying there's anything wrong with that" (transcript lines 405-421). A man who says that is not the man the draft's follow-on sentence describes.

**A full-sentence quotation in the OCD passage.** Given how carefully the draft frames that passage, the compression without an ellipsis is conspicuous (SUBJECT-C1).

Putting the June 2002 *Attitude* cover and his verbatim line *before* Human Rights Watch means the objection is specifically his rather than generic sportswashing — and it means his own twenty-year record is stated as fact rather than offered as mitigation after the charge. Most treatments do the reverse. Equally welcome: the second-pass decision to exclude the widely circulated "honoured to have the tag of gay icon" quote because it could not be primary-sourced. Declining a quote that would have *helped* the passage is the clearest evidence in the file that the pipeline's caution is real.

**Welcome:** "The charity is real and long-running... work that predates the sportswashing argument by seventeen years." A dated fact doing the work an adjective would have done badly.

**Jarring:** "Her objection did not change the method, and the method worked." The article, in its own voice, ratifies a method it has just described as shouting that reduced a child to tears. Whatever is true about outcomes, the subject of that sentence is a crying boy, and "worked" is the article's judgment, not his (SUBJECT-C5).

**Jarring:** "The self-made idol, at 51." The self-made frame runs through `meta_title` ("The Boy Who Built Himself"), `persona_title` ("Football's Self-Made Idol"), the description, and the final paragraph — and it is the specific claim his own account of his hardest years contradicts.

## Red flags

### SUBJECT-R1 — BLOCKER: the draft deletes his own explanation of surviving 1998 and supplies its own, then frames him as having offered none

- **Passage.** 1998 H2: "When Neville asked him, decades on, how he functioned through it, Beckham did not reach for a redemption cliché. He said, 'I don't know. I honestly, I actually don't know.'" Followed by: "What he did next is the most Type 3 thing in the whole story... He answered in the only language that had ever bought him safety. He delivered." Compounded in the father H2, where the therapist quote is cut at "That's how I was brought up" and glossed as "A boy raised on 'get on with it' does not learn to sit inside a hard feeling. He learns to turn it into work." Same claim in the frontmatter FAQ ("his Type 3 answer was to reply in the only language that had ever bought him safety: he delivered") and in the `persona_title` / `meta_title` / description.
- **Trust problem.** The article asks the central question of its own thesis, presents the subject as unable to answer it, and answers it for him — while his actual answer sits in the source it cites four times. His answer is relational: he was carried. The article's answer is autarkic: he produced. Those are different men, and the draft's titles commit to the second one. This is the one finding on which he would be entitled to say the piece misrepresents him rather than merely reads him uncharitably.
- **Evidence.** Primary transcript, verified by me directly, `youtube-transcripts/david-beckham-stick-to-football-neville-keane.txt`: lines 313-322 — "it didn't feel tough at the time because I had the right people around me I had a manager that looked after me a team that looked after me and and and teammates that every time I stepped out onto the pitch I knew that they had my back no matter what"; line 489 — "I was thankful though that I had the right people around me to get over it"; line 513 — "I had good people around me"; lines 414-421 — "I felt that I had the right people around me to not see anyone um but I I also do think you know these days we talk about people being able to talk and it's a good thing and I'm not saying there's anything wrong with that." Also lines 485-489, where he volunteers accountability: "I did make a mistake um and I was obviously punished for it." I confirmed by grep that no body passage mentions the manager, the team, the teammates, or any support system.
- **Minimum viable repair.** Two sentences in the 1998 section, before "What he did next": give his stated attribution in his own words (the right people around him — manager, team, teammates who had his back) and note that he also names his own mistake. Then keep the delivery reading as the article's *added* observation rather than the vacuum-filler. Extend the therapist quote by one clause, or add a following sentence recording that he immediately credits the people around him and endorses talking. Soften one of `persona_title` / `meta_title` so the self-made frame is not asserted in the two fields most readers see.
- **Expected benefit.** The thesis survives — arguably strengthens, because "he was held up by others and still answered in output" is a more interesting Three than "he had no answer but work." And the article stops being contradicted by its own primary source.
- **Confidence.** High on the omission and the quote truncation (verified in the primary transcript). High that it is material.
- **Acceptance test.** A reader who finishes the 1998 section can state, unprompted, what Beckham himself says got him through 1998, and it matches his words. No body or frontmatter sentence asserts that he had no explanation.

### SUBJECT-R2 — BLOCKER: "The reveal was the whole point" inverts his stated motive and misstates the event

- **Passage.** Brand H2, para 2: "When he shaved his head in 2000, he kept it hidden under a beanie through the entire warm-up, so it stayed a secret from his teammates and the cameras alike until he walked out onto the pitch with the team. The reveal was the whole point."
- **Trust problem.** Fear of his manager is recast as brand choreography, and the recasting is the paragraph's proof of "he staged it with real care." It is also the draft's only claim that Beckham personally engineered a media reveal — the most calculating single act attributed to him anywhere in the piece — and it did not happen.
- **Evidence.** Beckham's own account (evidence packet S-06, Sky Sports 2021): the concealed style was a **mohawk**, not a shaved head; he hid it because "I was too scared to even show him"; Ferguson saw it in the changing room *before* the warm-up and ordered it off; he shaved it at Wembley before going out. No reveal occurred at that fixture. Decisively, the draft narrates the *same* Wembley incident correctly two paragraphs later ("Before a Charity Shield at Wembley, Beckham turned up in a mohawk. Ferguson took one look, told him to go and shave it off"), so the article tells one event twice and gets the first telling wrong in the direction of its thesis.
- **Minimum viable repair.** Delete the sentences. The correct telling already exists downstream and is better evidence: a man who hides a haircut from his manager out of fear, then shaves it himself minutes before kickoff rather than miss the game, is a more precise illustration of "the deal he had made with the world" than a fabricated reveal. Recover the genuine staged reveal — he unveiled the mohawk later that season on England duty (S-06) — if the paragraph still needs one.
- **Expected benefit.** Removes a self-contradiction inside one H2, removes the draft's most unfair motive attribution, and upgrades the evidence.
- **Confidence.** High. Contradicted by the subject's own first-person account, and internally contradicted by the draft.
- **Acceptance test.** The 2000 Charity Shield haircut appears exactly once, matches Beckham's own account including his stated reason for concealment, and no sentence asserts that a reveal was intended at that match.

### SUBJECT-R3 — BLOCKER: the marriage-as-brand characterisation is asserted in the article's voice, and he has publicly rejected it

It was the founding act of the brand." / "Posh and Becks were a joint venture from the first day, and the union itself became the most valuable thing either of them would ever build." / "they rebuilt it in plain sight, brick by brick, into the most durable celebrity brand of its generation."

The immediately preceding Keane sentence in the same run is independently documented as Keane speaking in the Netflix documentary (packet S-05). ## Specific improvements

### SUBJECT-C1 — CONCERN: the OCD quotation is compressed without an ellipsis, in the draft's most epistemically careful passage

- **Passage.** Father H2 blockquote: "I've got this obsessive compulsive disorder where I have to have everything in a straight line or everything has to be in pairs. Everything has to be perfect."
- **Problem.** An intervening sentence is removed with no ellipsis. Full 2006 wording (packet S-07): "...everything has to be in pairs. I'll put my Pepsi cans in the fridge, and if there's one too many, then I'll put it in another cupboard somewhere ... everything has to be perfect." The draft's own testimony ledger marks the elision correctly; the rendered quote does not. The meaning survives — which is why this is a concern, not a blocker — but silent compression of a mental-health self-disclosure is the wrong place to save four words, and the passage's authority depends entirely on visible care.
- **Repair.** Insert the ellipsis, or restore the Pepsi-cans clause (it is more vivid than what replaced it and is the detail the public actually remembers).
- **Benefit.** The quote-handling matches the epistemic claims the surrounding paragraph makes.
- **Confidence.** High. **Acceptance test.** The rendered blockquote either contains no elision or marks it, and matches the ledger.

### SUBJECT-C2 — CONCERN: three positions attributed to one dated statement, one of which is not in it

- **Passage.** Qatar H2: "He told Sky Sports that he had spoken with members of the LGBTQ community who felt safe at the tournament, that he believed engagement through sport mattered, and that he was proud to have taken part." Repeated in the FAQ: "He said in October 2023 that he was proud of the World Cup role and believed engagement through sport mattered."
- **Problem.** The retrieved 4 October 2023 Sky Sports quotation (packet S-17) contains the first and third elements but not the second; "engagement through sport" traces to his 2021-2022 statements. Attaching a position to a specific dated occasion where he did not state it is the same class of error as a misquote, and it occurs in the paragraph the draft designates as "his case" — the one place the section is obliged to be exact about what he actually said.
- **Repair.** Either drop the middle element from the dated sentence, or split it: give the 4 October quote as his answer, and attribute the engagement-through-sport position separately to its own earlier date.
- **Benefit.** His strongest public defence is reported accurately, which is what makes the following rebuttal fair.
- **Confidence.** High (packet-verified). **Acceptance test.** Every position attributed to the 4 October 2023 statement is present in that statement; anything else carries its own date.

### SUBJECT-C3 — CONCERN: unsourced precision, consistently tilted toward the vanity read

- **Passages.** Brand H2: "He signed a **boot deal** with Adidas for fifty thousand pounds and drove out in a fifty-thousand-pound BMW M3 **the same week**." / "'You've got to look good for the paps,' **he told them**." / "Keane... made an exception **on one condition: you had better back it up for the weekend**."
- **Problem.** Individually trivial; collectively a pattern. The source says only "a contract with Adidas for £50,000 and I went and bought an M3 for £50,000" — neither "boot deal" nor "the same week" (transcript lines 526-528). The paps line is archival Beckham talking to camera about a Gucci jacket, not something he said to teammates in a dressing room (line 524). I searched the transcript for Keane's "condition" and found "back it up for the weekend" at lines 797-798 without being able to establish it as a condition Keane set for Beckham specifically. Every one of these additions makes him marginally more of a poser than the record does. The "flash Cockney" nickname itself is solid — confirmed live, in his own mouth: "you always used to call me the flash cottony" (line 553).
- **Repair.** Cut "boot," "the same week," and "he told them"; attribute the paps line to the archival footage it comes from. Either source Keane's condition or make it Keane's general attitude rather than a bargain struck with Beckham.
- **Benefit.** The anecdote keeps all its colour and stops inventing the parts that flatter the thesis. It also removes the reviewer's grounds for reading the tilt as deliberate.
- **Confidence.** High on the Adidas and paps additions (transcript-verified); medium on the Keane condition, which I could not locate rather than disproved.
- **Acceptance test.** No detail in the brand section's spending anecdote lacks a source, and none characterises an archival to-camera line as dressing-room speech.

### SUBJECT-C4 — CONCERN: two passages assert private interior states as fact

- **Passages.** Father H2: "The lesson David absorbed never left him. Feelings were a luxury. Delivery was the job." Inner-thought block: "...something he cannot name has already started slipping, and **he has never once tested what happens if he lets it**."
- **Problem.** The draft is otherwise scrupulous about marking inference, which makes these stand out. The first states what he absorbed and what he believes, flatly. The second asserts a factual negative about his private conduct across a lifetime — unknowable, and unfalsifiable. The `inner-thought` styling signals speculation to a reader who knows the house conventions; the sentence's grammar does not. Note also that the first assertion is the one directly complicated by his fuller therapist answer (SUBJECT-R1).
- **Repair.** Convert both to observation or explicit inference. "he has never once tested" → "there is no public sign he has tested." "Feelings were a luxury. Delivery was the job." → attach it to what he says or does rather than to what he concluded.
- **Benefit.** Brings the two remaining unhedged interiority claims in line with the standard the rest of the draft already meets.
- **Confidence.** High that they are unsupported; medium on how much a general reader notices, given the styling.
- **Acceptance test.** No sentence outside a marked quotation states what Beckham believes, feels, or has never done, without hedging or attribution.

### SUBJECT-C5 — CONCERN: the article ratifies his father's method in its own voice

- **Passage.** Father H2: "Sandra is the one voice in the family record that ever questioned it; the same series shows her uneasy about how hard Ted pushed, while Ted's shouting reduced the boy to tears. Her objection did not change the method, and **the method worked**."
- **Problem.** The article's own verdict, delivered in the same sentence as a crying child and his mother's ignored objection. It also disposes of the only person in the record who protested on his behalf in half a clause, then closes the ledger. Whatever the outcome, the article does not need to endorse the method to explain what it produced — and a subject who has spent three years publicly reckoning with his father in a documentary is not well served by a piece that grades the parenting as effective and moves on. Related dignity note: Sandra is reduced to "a hairdresser," where reporting also has her working in nursing homes (packet S-09).
- **Repair.** Replace "the method worked" with what is actually being claimed — the method produced the results Ted wanted — so the sentence describes an outcome rather than issuing a judgment. Give Sandra's objection one more clause.
- **Benefit.** The section keeps its full force (Ted's own quote does all the necessary work) without the article taking a side on whether the childhood was acceptable.
- **Confidence.** Medium-high. This is a tone-and-stance judgment, but it is the passage most likely to sting the subject.
- **Acceptance test.** No sentence in the origin section endorses or grades Ted's methods in the article's voice.

## Limits of this review

- **This is a fairness proxy, not Beckham.** I have no access to his private views and have not attributed any to him. Where I say he "would not accept" something, the basis is his own published statements, not inference about his feelings.
- **The Type 3 call is not challenged here.** Assessing typing accuracy is the enneagram perspective's job. My findings hold regardless of whether the typing is right — three of the four blockers are sourcing and attribution failures that would remain if the type were changed to 9 tomorrow.
- **Venue provenance is confirmed but original venue is not.** I established that the Keane and Neville lines sit inside clip playback rather than live studio speech. I could not establish where the clips came from, because the Netflix series was not consulted. Keane and Neville both appear as documentary interviewees, so identical words may legitimately exist in both places. This is why SUBJECT-R4's repair is to strip the staging rather than re-attribute the venue.
- **Auto-transcript limits.** The repo transcript has no speaker labels and no punctuation. My speaker inferences rest on internal evidence (name cards, clip-introduction markers, [Applause]/[Music] bracketing, first-person constructions like "me and Gary"). Strong for the clip-versus-live question; not sufficient to settle SUBJECT-Q4.
- **Not consulted:** Netflix *Beckham*; Beckham's own memoirs (*Both Feet on the Ground*, 2003); Ted Beckham's autobiography; the full *Attitude* 2002 interview; the CNBC *Squawk Box* appearance. The memoirs are the most consequential gap for this perspective — they are the primary first-person record, and SUBJECT-Q1 turns on them.
- **One packet limitation resolved, others left open.** I verified Victoria's April 2026 WSJ interview. I did not attempt the Ferguson book citation, the *Country Life* article-level dates, or the "peace" quotation; those remain as the packet describes them.
