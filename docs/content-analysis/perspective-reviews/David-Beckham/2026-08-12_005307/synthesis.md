## Executive verdict

The Type 3 call survives every perspective, including the two whose job was to break it. No reviewer challenged the thesis, the packet's discriminating evidence supports it, and the draft's epistemic hygiene around type — refusing to convert self-described OCD into type proof, excluding the 2am cleaning from the case, naming his reserve as genuine counterevidence — is better than this genre normally produces. That is worth protecting and it is not what is wrong here.

What is wrong is one failure mode, repeated eleven times: **the draft applies its epistemic discipline to claims and suspends it for scenes.** Every place the article stops citing and starts narrating, it adds unsourced detail, and the additions tilt in a single direction — toward a man who stages things. It invents a haircut reveal that its own next paragraph contradicts. It stages two rooms on a podcast that the transcript shows were clip playback. It deletes Beckham's own stated explanation of how he survived 1998 — *"I had the right people around me"* — and answers its own question with "He delivered." That is not a set of coincidences; it is a thumb on the scale that the thesis does not need, because the thesis is better without it.

Four perspectives independently found the same fabricated sentence (P0-01). Three found the same staged scenes (P0-02). I verified both in the primary transcript myself, along with the deleted first-person answer, and I confirmed the two external facts that were in genuine dispute: Netflix's *Beckham* was produced by Beckham's own Studio 99, and his commercial career began with Adidas in 1996 and Brylcreem in 1997, which disconfirms the "insurance after 1998" causal claim.

Eleven P0s is a long list, but it is not eleven rewrites. Nine are deletions or single-clause fixes; the net word change across all of them is roughly **negative fifty words**, which matters at 4,442 against a 4,500 ceiling. The one blocker I downgraded is the subtype inversion, which is real but sits inside a collapsible the draft already labels low-confidence.

The delight target is the informed fan, and the fan verdict is the sharpest diagnosis in the jury: *the writer understands the man well and the footballer thinly.* The article argues that Beckham answers rejection with delivery and omits all three canonical football instances of exactly that. Fixing that is where the remaining upside lives.
