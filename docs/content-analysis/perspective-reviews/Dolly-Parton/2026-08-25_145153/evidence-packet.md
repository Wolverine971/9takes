## Identity and scope

Dolly Rebecca Parton was an American singer-songwriter, actor, businesswoman, and philanthropist born January 19, 1946, in Locust Ridge, Tennessee. Her family and publicist announced that she died peacefully in Nashville on August 25, 2026, at age 80. The frozen article interprets her public record as Enneagram Type 2, probably 2w3 with a social instinct. The type, wing, instinct, and arrow analysis are editorial hypotheses rather than clinical findings.

## Dated timeline

- **1946:** Born the fourth of twelve children; Dr. Robert F. Thomas is paid with cornmeal.
- **1964:** Moves to Nashville the day after high-school graduation.
- **1967:** Joins _The Porter Wagoner Show_ and begins the partnership that establishes her nationally.
- **1971:** Releases _Coat of Many Colors_.
- **1974:** Leaves Wagoner's show and takes "Jolene" and "I Will Always Love You" to No. 1.
- **1980:** Stars in _9 to 5_ and writes its title song.
- **1986:** Becomes a partner in the Pigeon Forge park renamed Dollywood.
- **1991:** Launches the Buddy Program for Sevier County students.
- **1992:** Whitney Houston's recording of "I Will Always Love You" becomes a global hit.
- **1995:** Starts the Imagination Library in Sevier County.
- **2016:** Creates the My People Fund after the Smoky Mountain wildfires.
- **2020:** Gives $1 million to Vanderbilt COVID-19 research.
- **2022:** Tries to withdraw from the Rock & Roll Hall of Fame ballot and is inducted anyway.
- **2023:** Releases the rock album _Rockstar_.
- **2025:** Carl Dean dies on March 3; Parton releases "If You Hadn't Been There."
- **2026:** Turns 80; rerecords "Light of a Clear Blue Morning" for pediatric cancer research; East Tennessee Children's Hospital takes her name; the Imagination Library passes 325 million books; her Broadway musical announces a winter transfer; her family announces her death on August 25.

## First-person evidence

- Parton repeatedly identified her father's inability to read as the reason she created the Imagination Library.
- She said her father took pride in children calling her the Book Lady.
- She described crying over Elvis Presley's publishing demand and keeping her copyright.
- She described songwriting as the way she processed "Coat of Many Colors," her departure from Wagoner, and grief after Dean's death.
- She said she liked the business side of her work and described herself as a workhorse who looked like a show horse.
- She tried to withdraw from the Rock Hall ballot because she felt she had not earned the right.
- She said she neglected her health while caring for Dean during his final illness.

## Third-party testimony

- Bryan Seaver said Parton assigned him years earlier to deliver the family announcement when she died.
- Miley Cyrus said her godmother let the public feel close while retaining privacy.
- Porter Wagoner cried when Parton played "I Will Always Love You" and asked to produce it.
- Vanderbilt's Jeff Balser described her COVID-research gift as inspiring and consequential.
- Session musician Lloyd Green said he had never met Carl Dean, illustrating the privacy Parton protected.
- Dolly Parton Children's Hospital president Matt Schaefer described her support as a commitment to care for every child who entered.
- Roger Ebert praised her immediate screen presence in _9 to 5_.

## Draft claim inventory

1. The preplanned family announcement extends Parton's lifelong combination of emotional availability and informational boundaries.
2. The strongest Type 2 pattern is relational causality: a specific loved person's need repeatedly becomes a durable public project.
3. The publishing-rights refusal prevents generosity from being mistaken for poor boundaries.
4. The Three wing explains image control, commercial ambition, and institutional scale.
5. The coat-closet story offers a plausible, clearly interpretive link between early humiliation and adult control over presentation.
6. The Imagination Library's personalized mailing model turns global scale into the feeling of a one-to-one gift.
7. Dollywood joins commercial value, home-region employment, and philanthropic infrastructure.
8. Type 3 is the strongest alternative; Type 7 explains optimism and project volume but fits the emotional record less well.
9. Her death announcement is treated as developing news with no inferred cause.
10. The Broadway musical's schedule is reported as announced, with its post-death status explicitly unresolved.

## Disputes and unresolved questions

- Estate planning can explain a preassigned family spokesperson without personality theory; the opening treats the decision as pattern evidence, not proof.
- The relationship between the coat-closet trauma and Parton's adult image is an interpretation, not a claim Parton is known to have made herself.
- Public philanthropy can serve brand and business interests. The draft concedes the overlap and uses it in the 2w3 analysis.
- Type 3 remains a credible core-type alternative because Parton announced her star ambition early and built a tightly managed brand.
- Some anecdotes are preserved through Parton's own official archive; those pages are valuable primary accounts but not independent verification.
- The initial death announcement disclosed no cause. Later reporting may add funeral, memorial, estate, or project information.
- The Broadway production had not issued a post-announcement schedule update when the draft was frozen.
