## Identity and scope

**Who.** Elizabeth Anne Holmes, born February 3, 1984, Washington, D.C. Founder and former CEO of Theranos, a blood-testing company founded in 2003. Convicted in January 2022 of one count of conspiracy to commit wire fraud against investors and three counts of investor wire fraud. Currently incarcerated. Referred to in 2023 reporting and afterward as "Liz." Federal Bureau of Prisons register number 24965-111 (the draft does not print it, correctly).

**Why the article covers her.** She is the subject of _You Can See Everything_, a documentary by Nathan Fielder and Lance Oppenheim that premiered in a secret screening at Telluride on September 6, 2026 and is reported for October 16, 2026 theatrical release by A24. ## First-person evidence

Holmes's own words. Each entry states what it can and cannot support.

**F-1. The greatness quotation (epigraph).** To Auletta, 2014: _"I grew up with those stories about greatness, and about people deciding not to spend their lives on something purposeful, and what happens to them when they make that choice—the impact on character and quality of life."_
_Supports:_ an adult recollection of a family narrative that paired an ancestor's achievement with cautionary relatives. _Cannot support:_ that this narrative caused anything, or that it was experienced as pressure at the time. **Note for evaluators:** the draft's epigraph ends at "when they make that choice." and drops the final clause without an ellipsis. The omitted clause ("the impact on character and quality of life") arguably strengthens rather than weakens the draft's reading, so the truncation is not tendentious, but it is a quotation-integrity item.

**F-2. The letter at nine.** _"What I really want out of life is to discover something new, something that mankind didn't know was possible to do."_ She reassured her father that Texas suited her "because it's big on science."
_Supports:_ an early articulated ambition, delivered inside an effort to console a parent who felt guilty about the move. _Cannot support:_ a settled childhood personality or a causal account of later conduct.

**F-3. The turtleneck (Glamour, 2015).** Her account: her mother put her in black turtlenecks as a child ("My mum had me in black turtlenecks when I was, like eight"); the outfit is a "uniform" that removes a daily decision; she has roughly 150 of them.
_Supports:_ her own, ordinary explanation of a signature image, which predates the later persona reporting. _Cannot support:_ the provenance of every wardrobe choice, or that the Jobs resemblance was unintentional. **Access limit:** glamour.com could not be fetched this session (Condé Nast block); the wording above is search-corroborated and was directly read by the independent editor on 2026-09-09.

**F-4. The persona account (via Chozick, May 2023).** Chozick reported Holmes describing the low voice and black turtlenecks as "a character" she created at 19: _"I believed it would be how I would be good at business and taken seriously and not taken as a little girl or a girl who didn't have good technical ideas."_ Chozick also described her speaking "in a soft, slightly low, but totally unremarkable voice, no hint of the throaty contralto she used while running" Theranos.
_Supports:_ a reported first-person statement that a business persona was deliberately constructed to be taken seriously. _Cannot support:_ the origin or intent of every vocal change, nor that the 2023 presentation is itself unconstructed — Chozick explicitly raised that possibility about her own subject. **Provenance:** reached via Uproxx's contemporaneous reproduction; nytimes.com is not fetchable from this session.

**F-5. Mad Money, October 15, 2015.** She contested the WSJ report rather than the state of the device, calling the article "factually and scientifically erroneous."
_Supports:_ an immediate public posture of contesting the reporting. _Cannot support:_ the widely circulated "first they think you're crazy, then they fight you, and then all of a sudden you change the world" line — **the draft deliberately paraphrases rather than quotes**, because the clip transcript was unreachable. cnbc.com returned HTTP 403 in this packet's check; the substance is carried by S-10.

**F-6. SEC deposition, summer 2017.** More than 600 "I don't know" answers. Asked whether Theranos technology was deployed "in emergency rooms, hospitals, on the battlefield or in medevac helicopters" — claims made to board members — she answered: "No."
_Supports:_ a documented gap between promotional claims and sworn answers. _Cannot support:_ a stress arrow, an absent self, or a psychological finding; legal strategy explains a great deal of deposition behavior.

**F-7. Trial testimony, November 2021.** She alleged sexual and emotional abuse and control by Balwani, including control of her diet to keep her "pure" and criticism of her business judgment, and testified that he told her to "kill the old Elizabeth," which she explained meant eliminating "the person who could never succeed in life or business."
_Supports:_ her sworn account. _Cannot support:_ that the abuse occurred or did not; Balwani denied it through his attorney and no verdict adjudicated it. **Counterweight:** Reuters reported she did **not** claim Balwani controlled her statements to investors, journalists or business partners (legal memo, S-13n).

**F-8. PEOPLE prison interview, February 2025.** "Theranos failed. But failure is not fraud." / "I refused to plead guilty to crimes I did not commit." / "there are things I would have done differently." The interview is headlined and widely syndicated with "hell and torture."
_Supports:_ continued denial of guilt alongside a described daily routine. _Cannot support:_ rehabilitation, or the insincerity of any described work. **Provenance caveat:** people.com returns 403 from this session and from the 2026-09-09 research. "Hell and torture" is carried by PEOPLE's own headline plus syndication (Yahoo, Fox News, ABC7), not by a fetched read of the article body. The draft presents it as a direct quotation from Holmes.

**F-9. On camera, _You Can See Everything_ teaser and film (2026).** "Why would I deceive you? There's no reason for me to do that." / Fielder: "You're being real right now?" / Holmes: "I'm always being real."
_Supports:_ her insistence, on camera, on her own sincerity. _Cannot support:_ sincerity or deception; this is a selected exchange from a teaser and, for the first line, a quotation reported by NBC from the film. **Sourcing note for evaluators:** NBC News carries "Why would I deceive you?" and "I'm always being real" but, per this packet's fetch, does **not** carry Fielder's question. The question is carried by the A24 teaser itself (transcript inspected at ~0:44 by the 2026-09-09 independent researcher) and by named reporting (S-28, S-29). The draft cites A24's teaser plus NBC for the cold open, which is adequate but not perfectly matched claim-to-source.

## Third-party testimony

**T-1. Tyler Shultz** (former employee; grandson of board member George Shultz), ABC News, January 23, 2019: _"She's really good at telling you what you need to hear to keep going."_
_Class:_ attributed characterization by a named, interested witness. It is the single most type-legible sentence anyone has said about her, and it is **one witness's read of her manner**, not a measurement.

**T-2. Tyler Shultz, documented sequence** (same source): he emailed technical concerns; Holmes replied that the teams would review them; Balwani then emailed separately, calling the comments "reckless" and "insulting" and demanding "the only email on this topic I want to see from you going forward is an apology."
_Supports:_ an observable loyalty-test sequence with each person's conduct separated. _Cannot support:_ that Holmes authored or directed Balwani's reply.

**T-3. Tyler Shultz on reconciliation**, CBS News, January 4, 2022. He described a late conversation in which George recounted getting Holmes invited to speak to Navy sailors during Fleet Week and said he "could not believe that anybody could get in front of these men and women who are willing to put their lives in front of our country and lie directly to their face as convincingly as she lied."
**Disputed rendering.** CBS presents this as _implicit_ validation, and Tyler emphasized that his grandfather **never formally apologized**. The draft states that "George acknowledged he had been right," which is firmer than the source and omits the no-apology detail. Evaluators should treat the draft's sentence as a defensible-but-compressed summary, not a verified quotation.

**T-4. Avie Tevanian** (former board member), ABC News, January 23, 2019: comparing her with Jobs, _"But Elizabeth took it to a new level."_ He said his concerns were unwelcome and that Holmes had board member Donald Lucas Sr. request his resignation.
_Supports:_ a named director's account of being pushed out after raising questions. _Cannot support:_ a general finding about the board; it is one director's account, and the quoted clause is vague in isolation without the surrounding context.

**T-5. Erika Cheung** (former lab associate), Ninth Circuit opinion p. 28: hired immediately after graduating college; ran quality controls; testified, corroborated by documentary evidence, that the Edison frequently failed QC and that she concluded the devices were _"highly unreliable."_
**Two caveats evaluators should hold.** (a) This passage sits inside the panel's treatment of **Balwani's** Rule 702 challenge, not a Holmes-specific holding. (b) The same page records that "devices that failed quality control were not used for patient testing" — a mitigating fact the draft does not carry. Cheung's further quoted concern was about extrapolating a roughly one-in-four failure rate across the system.

**T-6. Phyllis Gardner** (Stanford professor of medicine), Vanity Fair, September 2016: _"I told her, I don't think your idea is going to work."_ Context: Holmes, a sophomore in 2002, proposed a patch that would detect a microbe and deliver antibiotics; Gardner also told her a finger-prick sample could not give precise results for the tests she wanted.
_Supports:_ that expert technical objection was available early. _Cannot support:_ a judgment about Holmes's character — the repository's own ledger classifies this as assessing an idea, not a person. **Note:** the objection was to an earlier patch concept, not to the shipped Theranos product. **Access limit:** vanityfair.com is blocked this session (Condé Nast); quote is search-corroborated and was directly read on 2026-09-09.

**T-7. Henry Kissinger** (then a Theranos board member), TIME 100, April 15, 2015: _"Others will judge the technical aspects of Theranos, but the social implications are vast."_ He also described her as "striking, somewhat ethereal, iron-willed."
_Supports:_ an explicit, on-the-record deferral of technical judgment by a board member whose prestige was itself the endorsement. _Cannot support:_ that he knew of any problem, or any inference about why the board was assembled.

**T-8. Nathan Fielder** (director), Telluride Q&A, September 6, 2026, reported by Vanity Fair via Oxygen: _"Near the end of the process, Billy was trying to exert, we felt, a lot of control over the process,"_ adding that this "caused our communication to break down a little bit."
_Supports:_ a named director's account of the **filming process**. _Cannot support:_ any assessment of Billy Evans as a person, and it must not be read as a parallel to the Balwani allegations. The entity-gap packet flags this explicitly; the draft keeps it in the documentary section, not the Balwani section.

**T-9. Roger Parloff** (Fortune), December 17, 2015: _"As much as I'd like to say that Holmes lied to me, I do believe I was misled—intentionally—but I was also culpable, in that I failed to probe certain exasperatingly opaque answers that I repeatedly received."_
**Nuance the draft's gloss omits:** Parloff expressly declined to say Holmes lied to him and assigned himself part of the fault. The draft's line ("Admiration had carried the story; checking particular claims took it apart") is an editorial gloss that does not contradict him but does not carry his self-implication either.

**T-10. Patient witnesses**, Ninth Circuit p. 15: one patient testified a Theranos HIV test returned positive while two subsequent tests showed she was negative; another testified she received incorrect results about her pregnancy.
_Supports:_ concrete patient harm testimony. _Cannot support:_ Holmes's criminal liability for it — she was **acquitted** on the patient counts the jury considered, and the opinion links this evidence to Balwani's transmission of results.

Critics of the 2023 profile.** Jezebel (Laura Bassett, May 7, 2023): the Times is "clearly helping to rehabilitate this white collar (and white) criminal's reputation ahead of her prison sentence." ## Public record, accomplishments, failures, and controversies

**Verified from the primary record (Ninth Circuit amended opinion, filed Feb 24 / amended Dec 22, 2025; Davila order ECF 1575, Sep 6, 2022):**

- **The pitch.** "[O]ne tiny drop changes everything." Theranos claimed fast, accurate, affordable tests from a fingerstick, against conventional venous draws (p. 8).
- **Scale reached.** Walgreens partnership from 2010; public launch in Walgreens in fall 2013; C-1 and C-2 share sales in late 2013 and 2014 (p. 11). $9B implied valuation in 2014; Forbes ranked her top of its wealthiest-self-made-women list at $4.5B, then revised to $0 in June 2016 (S-09).
- **The seven categories of alleged misrepresentation** to investors: device capabilities; financial health; technology demonstrations; the Walgreens relationship; military work; use of third-party devices; and pharmaceutical companies' purported validation (p. 11).
- **The logo episode.** "Holmes admitted that she personally affixed the logo of various pharmaceutical companies, including Pfizer, GlaxoSmithKline, and Schering-Plough, onto reports containing favorable conclusions about Theranos's device." In emails to Walgreens she described those reports as the companies' "independent and technical validation." Company representatives testified they neither validated the technology nor authorized the logos, and the reports reached investors, who found third-party vouching significant (p. 15).
- **The binder.** Around Rosendorff's November 2014 resignation, Balwani texted that the "Normandy lab is an [expletive] disaster zone"; in the same window Holmes texted about Walton and Murdoch investments; less than two weeks later she texted about what to include in the Murdoch binder; lab concerns, "fires," and the lab director's concerns were excluded (Davila p. 3). **Standard caveat:** this is a sufficiency ruling, evidence viewed in the light most favorable to the prosecution, not a psychological finding.
- **The verdict.** Guilty: investor conspiracy (Count 1) and investor wire fraud (Counts 6–8). Acquitted: patient conspiracy (Count 2) and patient wire fraud (Counts 10–12). Hung: Counts 3–5. Count 9 dismissed at trial (pp. 16–17).
- **Sentence and restitution.** 135 months (Nov 2022); loss found at $120M; ten-or-more-victims enhancement; $452M restitution ordered May 2023, joint and several ($397M to twelve victims, $54.5M to Safeway and Walgreens) (pp. 16–18). Convictions, sentences and restitution **affirmed**.
- **Reduction.** March 26, 2026: Davila reduced 135 → 123 months under §3582(c)(2) / Amendment 821 / USSG §4C1.1. The court found the government could not identify an investor who suffered "substantial financial hardship" despite $452M in losses; noted that Holmes "still has not accepted responsibility for her crimes" but that this was already weighed at sentencing; and cited "the chilling effect of her notoriety" as reducing recidivism risk (S-08).

## Enneagram hypothesis evidence

**Status: hypothesis throughout.** Holmes has never confirmed a typing. Nothing in the court record, the reporting or the film selects an Enneagram number. The house material used below is `src/blog/enneagram/enneagram-type-3.md` (9takes' own Type 3 pillar), cited so evaluators can check that the draft's theory statements match the site's own doctrine rather than being improvised.

**House doctrine, for checking the draft's theory claims (S-31):** Type 3 — role Achiever/Performer; ego fixation **Vanity**; basic fear **Worthlessness**; basic desire **to feel valuable**; vice/passion **Deceit**; virtue **Truthfulness**; disintegration to **9**; integration to **6**. The pillar states the deceit "isn't about lying… It's the lie you tell yourself" and "mistaking image for identity." It states the heart triad's underlying emotion is **shame**, which Threes repress and outrun through achievement. It identifies **Self-Preservation 3 as the counter-type**: least image-conscious, hides accomplishments, "vanity for having no vanity."

**Evidence consistent with Type 3:**

1. A sustained, public pursuit of recognition as a world-changing medical innovator, maintained across a decade of interviews, magazine covers and honor lists.
2. Conversion of personal credibility into technical credibility — the logo episode is the documented, non-interpretive instance (p. 15), and Kissinger's explicit deferral of technical judgment is the social instance (T-7).
3. Unwelcome information withheld from the audience whose belief mattered: the November 2014 lab warning excluded from the Murdoch binder (Davila p. 3).
4. Tyler Shultz's characterization of audience-tailored reassurance (T-1) — the behavior the type predicts, described by a witness, though only by one.
5. A reconstructed identity after the first one stopped earning recognition: the "Liz" rename, the softened presentation, the retired turtlenecks (T-11, F-4).
6. On-camera insistence on her own sincerity, held against a record of knowing deception (F-9 vs. Davila p. 3). This is the packet's **signature contradiction** and the draft's central move.

**Evidence against, or that the type does not explain:**

1. **Knowing deception is not what the type's "deceit" primarily names.** The house doctrine puts the lie inward first. The court found conduct that a self-deception reading does not excuse and largely does not describe. The draft says so; evaluators should check that it keeps saying so.
2. **Legal testimony is weak type evidence.** The 600 "I don't know"s are at least as well explained by counsel's instruction as by any disintegration to Nine. The draft's rabbit hole concedes this.
3. **Ordinary explanations compete at nearly every node.** Founder incentives, Silicon Valley fundraising norms, legal strategy, and the pressure on a young female founder each explain some of the same behavior. The draft names all four.
4. **The prison work cuts both ways.** Reentry-clerk work at 31 cents an hour with no audience is the least recognition-yielding work available. That is either growth within a Three or simply ordinary adaptation; it is not evidence for a different type. The independent editor's first finding was that the draft must not let wrongdoing count as evidence _for_ Three and improvement count as evidence _against_ it.
5. **Behavior the type does not explain at all:** why she remained in an allegedly abusive relationship (no Enneagram reading explains that, and the draft says so); the specific technical decisions inside the lab; and her continued denial after conviction, which is at least as consistent with sincere belief in innocence, legal posture, or appellate strategy.

**Closest alternatives and discriminating evidence:**

- **Type 8** (control, resistance to constraint, protection of autonomy). _Discriminator:_ an Eight reading predicts confrontation and dominance as the organizing aim; the record more often shows her seeking **endorsement and admiration** from higher-status figures (a board assembled from statesmen, pharmaceutical logos borrowed rather than dismissed). Not decisive.
- **Type 6** (trusted authority, securing support against threat). _Discriminator:_ a Six reading fits the board-as-protection and the embattled response to the WSJ, but it under-predicts the sustained self-presentation as the exceptional founder. Not decisive.
- **Subtype.** The draft proposes a tentative **3w2** and a possible **social** emphasis. The house pillar's SP-3 counter-type description (hides accomplishments, "vanity for having no vanity") is the cleanest discriminator available and points **away** from self-preservation dominance — but instinct stacks cannot be ranked from a public record, and neither a prestigious board nor a black turtleneck settles a wing.

**What would change the reading.** Evidence that a different motivation consistently organizes her choices _including where it costs her status_. Accepting obscurity, deferring to unwelcome expertise, and describing her own wrongdoing precisely would weaken the account of defensive image protection — but a healthier Three could do all three, so those behaviors alone would not select a different type.

## Disputes and unresolved questions

1. **The Variety quotation (CLM-26) is the largest unresolved evidence gap.** The phrase "the epic nature of her willingness to lie to herself" cannot be confirmed at the character level from this session: variety.com redirects to a tollbit host that returns HTTP 402, and no syndicated copy surfaced. An exact-phrase search returns the Variety review as the top result and the search summarizer attributes the phrase to it, which is suggestive but is Tier-4 corroboration of a Tier-3 opinion. The draft already presents it as a critic's reading of a film nobody writing has seen and records the snippet provenance in its testimony ledger. **What must stay qualified:** the attribution wording itself, not just the opinion status.

2. **The September 2026 NYT report that Holmes and Evans dispute Chozick's 2023 quotes remains unverified** after three independent attempts (2026-09-09 research, the 2026-09-19 second pass, and this packet). It was reachable in September only via an anomalous `monorepo-sample2.nyt.net` search hostname. This packet's own search surfaced only 2023-era backlash. **The draft has already cut the dispute sentence and the matching FAQ clause**, which is the correct disposition; the voice section now rests on the attributed 2023 profile plus the 2015 Glamour account. Evaluators should not treat the absence of the dispute as an omission.

3. **Release date.** A24's own film page said "2026 (TBD)" when checked today; reporting and Wikipedia give October 16, 2026. The draft's split treatment (teaser says October; page says TBD as of September 19; reporting gives October 16) is accurate as of this packet's cutoff and is the kind of claim that can go stale between packet and publication.

4. **Projected release date.** BOP says 02/22/2030; Wikipedia and 2025-era reporting say 2032. BOP's own record governs, and BOP itself warns the projection can change. Any wording stronger than "listed… as of [date]… a date that can move" would be unsupportable.

5. **Whether George Shultz "acknowledged he had been right"** (CLM-15) is a compression of an implicit acknowledgment, alongside Tyler's statement that no formal apology was given.

6. **Stanford departure** — freshman year 2004 (Wikipedia) vs. "after her sophomore year" (Kissinger, 2015). Unresolved; the draft avoids the question.

7. **Whether the teaser exchange in the cold open is matched to the right source** (CLM-25). The question line is a teaser quotation; NBC carries her answers.

8. **The abuse allegations are not adjudicated and cannot be.** Two separate fraud trials decided fraud. No finding exists about whose account of the relationship is true. Anything written about Holmes's agency during those years has to hold both her documented authority at Theranos and her sworn allegations without resolving them.

9. **Nothing in this record selects an Enneagram type.** Every type statement in the draft must remain a reading. The confidence label ("moderate") is itself a claim evaluators may challenge in either direction.

10. **Blocked sources this session** (see Research limitations) mean several quotations rest on prior direct reads recorded in the repository rather than on re-verification today. Those are marked per claim.
