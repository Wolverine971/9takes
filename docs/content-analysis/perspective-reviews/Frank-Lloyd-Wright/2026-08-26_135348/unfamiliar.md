## Bottom-line verdict

I came in knowing the name "Frank Lloyd Wright" and nothing else, and I left able to state the
thesis without help: this was a man whose whole operating system was refusing to sit still inside
pain, who reframed every catastrophe into forward motion, and who admitted on camera at ninety that
the arrogance was a shell. I can also state the type answer — Enneagram Type 7, 8 wing,
self-preservation — and roughly why. That is a real achievement for a 4,500-word piece about a dead
architect, and most of the credit goes to two decisions: the core-fear gloss at 12% is written in
plain English, and the biography is delivered as scenes rather than a résumé.

Trust is strained rather than intact for one reason I could detect with zero outside knowledge. At
word 1,229 the article tells me Wright "credited those blocks for the rest of his life." At word
1,481 — 252 words later, inside the section the piece treats as its best argument — it tells me
"Wright never named another influence on his work." I noticed the contradiction on a first read,
before I opened any source. When an article contradicts itself inside three hundred words at exactly
the point where it is asking me to accept its most original claim, I start reading the rest more
warily. That is the blocker.

Everything else is a comprehension tax rather than a trust break, and almost all of it is repairable
in single clauses: four terms load-bearing enough to matter arrive unglossed (`growth line`,
`Usonian`, `Type 3`, `Mann Act`), and three major nouns — Taliesin, Mamah, Fallingwater — are used
as evidence long before they are introduced. Recommendation is revise, not hold: nothing here
requires new research, and the piece's spine is sound.

## What landed

**UNFAM-H1 — The cold open.** "Halfway through the meal, water came through the roof Frank Lloyd
Wright had designed and ran down onto Johnson's head, at his own table, in front of everyone." Then:
"'Well Hib,' Wright replied, 'why don't you move your chair?' / Then he hung up."

I knew none of these proper nouns — Wingspread, Hib Johnson, Fallingwater — and it did not matter.
Water on a rich man's head at his own dinner party is legible to anyone alive. The scene carried me
past five unfamiliar names without a stumble, which is exactly what a cold open has to do for a
reader like me. **Must survive revision unedited**, including the paragraph break before "Then he
hung up."

**UNFAM-H2 — The Type 7 gloss.** "A Seven's core fear is being trapped: in pain, in limitation, in
any state that cannot be escaped through the next thing. The defense is reframe."

This is the single most important sentence in the article for a reader who has never met the
Enneagram. Two clauses, no jargon, and it made every subsequent piece of evidence interpretable. I
never needed anyone to define "the Enneagram" as a system because this told me what the _claim_ was.
**Preserve verbatim.** If anything in the diagnosis section has to be cut for length, cut around this.

**UNFAM-H3 — The subtype defined by contrast.** "Sexual Sevens chase intensity; Social Sevens build
movements. Self-Pres Sevens build _compounds_, environments engineered to keep the good life within
reach."

Three parallel clauses taught me a whole sub-taxonomy without a definition. This is the technique the
rest of the article's jargon should have used and mostly didn't.

**UNFAM-H4 — The grave.** "He and his son John and two cousins lowered the casket and covered it in
her flowers. Then Wright took the shovel and filled the grave himself, alone, until the August light
was gone." And: "A marker is an address, and an address is a place you can be expected to return to
and sit in."

This is where the article stopped being an argument and became a person. I needed no background at
all — the image does the work, and then the interpretation lands because I had just watched the man
do the thing. It is also the only passage where I felt the writer liked Wright and was sorry for him
at the same time. **This section is the reason to publish the piece. Protect it.**

**UNFAM-H5 — The Okura telegram.** "Wright handed the telegram to the American press. He did not add
the missing words 'considerable damage notwithstanding.'"

Fully self-contained, requires no prior knowledge, and it is the one moment where I watched the
reframe happen instead of being told about it. For a skeptical newcomer this is the most persuasive
paragraph in the article, because it is externally documented behavior rather than a reading of his
interior.

**UNFAM-H6 — The Johnson reversal.** "The client took the insult and kept writing checks for fifteen
years."

I had been quietly wondering for 3,000 words whether Wright was actually good or just loud. This
answered it without the article having to assert it in its own voice, which I would have discounted.

**UNFAM-H7 — The International Style gloss.** "Mies van der Rohe and Le Corbusier, who treated
buildings as machines for living in, stripped of Wright's hand-laid stone and stained glass."

Two names I did not know, made instantly usable in one relative clause, and it set up the Philip
Johnson jab 2,000 words later so that the jab actually landed. Model of how to introduce a proper
noun before use.

**UNFAM-H8 — The closing image.** "The crowd lined up at Fifth Avenue and 89th Street and climbed
his spiral, looking _at_ the paintings, which was wrong, while a building he had spent sixteen years
fighting for held them up. He had finally built a room nobody could ever move their chair out of."

I understood the callback to "live _with_ the waterfall, not look _at_ it" and to "move your chair"
without being reminded of either. That means the motifs were planted well enough to pay off on a
single read. **Keep the last line exactly.**

## What missed

**The article borrows Fallingwater's fame in sentence one and withholds the building for 3,000
words.** "The architect of Fallingwater" is the only significance marker in the first 1,800 words.
My orientation search (see research log) confirmed Fallingwater is genuinely famous enough to
function as an identifier, so this is _not_ the failure I first took it for. But there is still a
gap: the name tells me "important architect" while what the building actually _is_ — a house built
on top of a waterfall — does not arrive until word 3,058 (68%). The article spends its first
sentence cashing a cheque it does not honour until two-thirds through.

**The external case for Wright's importance arrives at 41%.** Before word 1,835 ("the most
talked-about architect under fifty in America"), every claim of significance is Wright's own boast:
"telling everyone he was the greatest architect who ever lived." A newcomer spends the first 40% of
the article unable to tell whether they are reading about a major figure or an entertaining crank.
This is a promised effect that does not land: the piece needs me to accept that the reframe habit
was _costly and consequential_, and that only works if I already believe the man mattered.

**The TL;DR is the least accessible block on the page.** It is the one element explicitly aimed at
the impatient or unfamiliar reader, and three of its five bullets lean on material the reader has not
been given: "Both times Taliesin burned" (Taliesin unintroduced), "his lover Mamah Borthwick was
murdered" (bombshell, no context), "Self-Preservation Sevens nest" (jargon, no gloss). Bullets 1 and
4 work fine. It inverts its own purpose.

**"Notice the verb. _Delighted._"** This is the one narrator move that pulled me out of the piece. It
tells me to notice something the sentence has already made obvious, and it is the only place the
writer addresses me directly. Recorded as preference, not a defect — but it is a seam.

## What surprised me

**Welcome.** The Sullivan argument. "Wright spent sixty years rewriting it as _form and function are
one_, the only intellectual debt he edited instead of disowning, and the edit reads like a son trying
to finish his father's sentence." I did not know either man an hour ago and this still landed as an
observation rather than a flourish, because the article had already established that Wright's father
walked out. It earns its own setup. This is the passage I would repeat to someone else.

**Welcome.** That the article volunteers the weakness of its own sources — "The essay is paywalled,
so take it as corroboration of a label and nothing further"; "The legend has been picked apart
since"; "though tallies vary by who is counting"; "At least, that is the story her son told." A
newcomer has no way to audit any of this, so visible self-policing is the only trust signal
available. It worked on me. (See UNFAM-C8 for the one place it is mistimed.)

**Jarring.** "When his lover was hatcheted to death in his own dining room, he rebuilt the dining
room and was in love again before the year was out." This is word 587 — 13% in. I had not been told
who she was, when this happened, or that seven people died. A mass murder is used as a brisk evidence
clause, and the sentence passes judgment on Wright's grief before I have been given anything to judge.
It reads as callous on first encounter, and it is the only moment in the article where I disliked the
writer. The full section 2,000 words later is handled with real care, which makes the early version
worse rather than better.

**Jarring, mildly.** "He did not believe it." Stated flat, twice, about a dead man's private
conviction. See UNFAM-C11.

## Red flags

### UNFAM-R1 — The article contradicts itself about Wright's influences, 252 words apart, inside its own centerpiece section — BLOCKER

- **Passage.** H2 "Why Wright always called Louis Sullivan 'lieber meister'": "Wright never named
  another influence on his work." (word 1,481)
- **Contradicted by, in the same article.** H2 "The Welsh prophecy that built Frank Lloyd Wright":
  "Wright credited those blocks for the rest of his life." (word 1,229)
- **Reader effect.** I caught this unaided on a first pass. The two sentences cannot both be true,
  and the second one is the load-bearing premise of the argument I had just decided was the best
  thing in the article — that Sullivan is the one attachment Wright never reframed. If the writer is
  wrong about something I can check against page 3 of his own article, I have to downgrade the
  paragraphs I _can't_ check, which is nearly all of them.
- **Evidence.** Evidence packet CLM-08, status **contradicted**: Wright is standardly described as
  admitting three influences — the Froebel gifts, Sullivan, and the Japanese woodblock print — and
  wrote in _An Autobiography_, "If Japanese prints were to be deducted from my education, I don't
  know what direction the whole might have taken" (packet E-08, S-30). So the sentence is false
  externally as well as internally.
- **Minimum viable repair.** Narrow the universal to what the section actually needs. The argument
  requires only that Sullivan was the sole _person_ Wright never disowned, not that he was the sole
  influence of any kind. Replace with a bounded claim — Wright named three influences and Sullivan
  was the only one who was a man who could leave.
- **Expected benefit.** Removes a reader-detectable self-contradiction; strengthens the section,
  because "the only influence with a pulse" is a sharper distinction than "the only influence."
- **Confidence.** High. Internal contradiction verified by direct word-offset comparison; external
  contradiction verified against packet CLM-08/E-08.
- **Acceptance test.** Grep the reader-visible body for `never named another influence` — zero hits.
  The revised sentence must be compatible with "Wright credited those blocks for the rest of his
  life" appearing 252 words earlier, and must not assert any universal about influences that the
  Froebel and Japanese-print facts falsify.

## Specific improvements

### UNFAM-C1 — "Growth line to Five" is undefined at the exact pivot of the Type 5 rebuttal

- **Passage.** "In Enneagram terms that is the Seven's growth line to Five, where the appetite
  finally narrows to one thing. Wright visited. He never moved in." (word ~924)
- **Reader effect.** `growth line` appears exactly once in the article and is never explained;
  `stress line` never appears in the body at all. This is the first time a newcomer learns that types
  connect to other types, and it is doing the whole job of reconciling "he looks like a Five" with
  "he is a Seven." "Wright visited. He never moved in." is elegant and completely opaque unless you
  already know what visiting a line means. The two preceding sentences give a strong plain-English
  Five rebuttal, so the reader gets the argument but not the resolution — the hole sits exactly where
  the answer should be. This matters disproportionately because the draft's own `meta_title` sells
  the piece as "Type 7w8, Not a 5w4."
- **Minimum viable repair.** One clause on first use establishing that the system maps each type to
  two others it borrows from — one under pressure, one in growth — before the Wright application.
  Roughly nine words.
- **Expected benefit.** The article's stated differentiator becomes fully parseable by its stated
  target reader.
- **Confidence.** High.
- **Acceptance test.** A reader with no Enneagram background can paraphrase what "Wright visited. He
  never moved in." asserts, using only text that precedes it.

### UNFAM-C2 — "Usonian houses" appears once, unglossed, at 82%, inside the emotional peak

- **Passage.** "Wright was ninety by then, the Usonian houses behind him, the museum still
  unfinished." (word 3,707)
- **Reader effect.** Sole occurrence in the reader-visible body; no definition anywhere. It sits in
  the opening sentence of the article's best section, so the newcomer's first move in the passage
  they should be reading fastest is to stop on a word they cannot decode. (The snapshot's own
  cohesion note records that a gloss was cut here for word budget.)
- **Minimum viable repair.** Two or three words of apposition — the affordable houses — or delete the
  clause. It is a résumé beat; the sentence survives without it.
- **Expected benefit.** Removes a stall at the article's highest-value moment at zero or negative
  word cost.
- **Confidence.** High.
- **Acceptance test.** Either `Usonian` does not appear in the body, or its first appearance carries
  an inline gloss.

### UNFAM-C3 — "Type 3" is used as in-group shorthand at 88%; its only gloss is later and hidden

- **Passage.** "Philip Johnson called him the greatest architect of the nineteenth century, and meant
  it the way a Type 3 means most things, as a critique dressed for dinner." (word 3,956)
- **Reader effect.** The only explanation of Type 3 — "Threes adapt the product to the market" — is
  at word 4,464, inside the collapsed `<details class="enneagram-rabbit-hole">` block, i.e. after the
  reference and behind a click most readers never make. So in the main body a whole personality type
  is invoked as an explanation and never explained. "As a critique dressed for dinner" partially
  rescues the sentence, but the reader is being asked to accept a diagnosis of a _second_ person on
  no evidence at all.
- **Minimum viable repair.** Either swap the type label for the plain-English trait already implied
  ("the way a man who has read the market means most things"), or attach a four-word gloss.
- **Expected benefit.** Removes the article's only unearned typing of a bystander and the last
  jargon trip before the ending.
- **Confidence.** High.
- **Acceptance test.** Every Enneagram type number in the reader-visible body outside the Rabbit Hole
  either is glossed at first use or is Wright's own.

### UNFAM-C4 — Mamah's murder is deployed as an evidence clause 1,300 words before it is introduced

- **Passage.** "When his lover was hatcheted to death in his own dining room, he rebuilt the dining
  room and was in love again before the year was out." (word 587, inside the diagnosis section)
- **Reader effect.** She is not named here. The date, the seven deaths, and the circumstances arrive
  at word 2,100+. A newcomer meets an extreme violent event as a throwaway proof point and, in the
  same sentence, a verdict on how the man grieved it. On first read this registers as the writer
  being callous about real deaths in order to win an argument. The later section is careful and
  humane, which makes the early version read worse in retrospect, not better.
- **Evidence.** The evidence packet's own instruction (Public record section, citing the entity-gap
  packet) is that the Taliesin murders be handled "as history rather than true-crime color." Seven
  people died, three of them children or teenagers.
- **Minimum viable repair.** Add a dating clause and a scale marker — that this was the 1914 Taliesin
  massacre and that she was one of seven killed — or hold the example and use a different Seven
  illustration in the diagnosis section, letting the full treatment at 48% carry it.
- **Expected benefit.** Removes the article's only passage that reads as unkind to non-subjects, at
  the exact moment the newcomer is deciding whether the writer is trustworthy.
- **Confidence.** High on the reader effect; this was my strongest negative reaction on the unaided
  read, recorded before I opened the packet.
- **Acceptance test.** The first reader-visible mention of Mamah's death carries a date and the fact
  that she was not the only victim.

### UNFAM-C5 — Chronology whiplash in the first two paragraphs of the walkout section

- **Passage.** H2 "Why Wright walked out on six children for Mamah Borthwick," paragraphs 1–2: "The
  bootleg commission was the Harlan House… Getting fired over it in 1893 was the making of him. What
  American biography skates past is that when Wright ran with Mamah sixteen years later… the head of
  a family of eight." Then paragraph 2 returns to "Wright opened a studio in Oak Park… and across the
  next sixteen years produced the Prairie Style."
- **Reader effect.** The sequence runs 1893 → forward to 1909 → back to 1893 → forward to 1909 again,
  and the 1893 firing was already narrated in the previous section. A reader assembling this life for
  the first time loses the thread here; it is the muddiest passage in the article. The reader is also
  asked to reconcile "a family of eight" with "the first of six children" by arithmetic.
- **Minimum viable repair.** Let paragraph 2 (Oak Park forward) run first and fold the Harlan/1893
  bridge into a subordinate clause, so the section moves in one direction. Drop "a family of eight"
  in favour of the count the heading already uses.
- **Expected benefit.** The article's central biographical turn becomes single-pass legible.
- **Confidence.** Medium-high. This is a structural judgement, but the double-back is objective.
- **Acceptance test.** Read the section aloud cold: the years mentioned advance monotonically.

### UNFAM-C6 — Taliesin is used as evidence at 9% and not placed until 44%

- **Passages.** TL;DR bullet 2, "Both times Taliesin burned, he rebuilt" (word ~381); diagnosis
  section, "Taliesin and Taliesin West were biospheres" (word ~409). First actual placement: "He
  bought land at Spring Green and built Taliesin for Mamah" (word 1,977).
- **Reader effect.** The article's central physical location — the compound that carries the
  self-preservation argument, the site of the murders, the thing that burns twice — is used three
  times as proof before the reader is told what or where it is. "Taliesin West" is invoked at 9% and
  never located at all in the body.
- **Minimum viable repair.** Attach a four-word apposition at the diagnosis-section use — his
  Wisconsin compound — since that instance is doing argumentative work. The TL;DR fix is covered by
  UNFAM-C7.
- **Expected benefit.** The self-preservation evidence becomes evaluable at the point it is offered
  rather than 1,500 words later.
- **Confidence.** High.
- **Acceptance test.** First reader-visible use of "Taliesin" outside the TL;DR carries a location or
  a category noun.

### UNFAM-C7 — The TL;DR presumes both the framework and the biography

- **Passage.** `<details><summary>TL;DR: Why Frank Lloyd Wright is an Enneagram Type 7w8</summary>`,
  bullets 2, 3, and 5.
- **Reader effect.** A TL;DR is the element most likely to be opened by exactly the reader this
  perspective represents — impatient, unoriented, deciding whether to invest. It delivers "Taliesin"
  (unintroduced), "Mamah Borthwick was murdered" (uncontextualised), "Self-Preservation Sevens nest"
  (undefined), and "The 8 wing" (undefined). Bullets 1 and 4 are excellent and prove the format can
  carry a newcomer.
- **Minimum viable repair.** Three appositions, no new bullets: give Taliesin a category noun, give
  the Seven a four-word core-fear gloss in bullet 1 (borrowing the phrasing already used at 12%), and
  either gloss or drop "8 wing" in bullet 5.
- **Expected benefit.** The summary starts serving the reader it is formatted for.
- **Confidence.** Medium-high.
- **Acceptance test.** Every proper noun and type term in the TL;DR is either glossed in place or
  appears earlier in reader-visible text.

### UNFAM-C8 — The Vreeland sourcing disclosure sits at 16%, in the highest-abandonment stretch

- **Passage.** "That subtype call is not ours alone. In July 2024 _Enneagram Monthly_ ran Tim
  Vreeland's… The essay is paywalled, so take it as corroboration of a label and nothing further. We
  have read only the title and the opening lines." (word 738)
- **Reader effect.** The honesty is genuinely admirable and I want it kept. But it lands inside the
  ~600-word analytical block (11%–24%) that already asks a story-driven newcomer for the most
  patience, and it is the most inside-baseball paragraph in the article: an unknown trade publication,
  an unknown author, and a methodological caveat about a source I am simultaneously being told not to
  rely on. It reads as a footnote that escaped into the body, and it is the likeliest exit point in
  the first quarter.
- **Minimum viable repair.** Compress to one sentence and relocate — either later in the diagnosis
  cluster after the counter-typing, or into the Rabbit Hole where the wing/subtype material already
  lives. Do not delete the paywall caveat; it is the part that earns trust.
- **Expected benefit.** Protects the trust signal while removing it from the abandonment zone.
- **Confidence.** Medium. Placement judgement, but the abandonment-risk stretch is objectively the
  densest analytical run in the piece.
- **Acceptance test.** The paywall caveat survives verbatim somewhere in reader-visible text; no
  paragraph between 10% and 20% is primarily about the provenance of a secondary source.

### UNFAM-C9 — Julian Carlton is introduced, kills seven people, and vanishes

- **Passage.** "a servant there named Julian Carlton served lunch to seven people, locked the dining
  room exits, doused the floor with gasoline, set the house on fire, and attacked the survivors with
  a hatchet as they fled." (word 2,164)
- **Reader effect.** This is the loudest question the article creates and does not answer: no motive,
  no arrest, no aftermath, nothing. As a newcomer my hand went to my phone. Every reader who leaves
  to search here is a reader who may not come back, and they leave immediately before the burial
  passage, which is the best writing in the piece.
- **Counter-consideration.** The article is about Wright, and the packet is explicit that the murders
  should not be worked as true-crime color. I am not asking for a true-crime digression — the opposite.
  A single closing clause is what prevents the reader from going and finding the true-crime version
  themselves.
- **Minimum viable repair.** One clause on aftermath at the end of that paragraph — enough to close
  the loop and signal the article knows the answer and is choosing not to dwell.
- **Expected benefit.** Retains the reader across the article's most valuable 400 words.
- **Confidence.** Medium-high on effect; the repair is deliberately minimal to respect the
  no-true-crime instruction.
- **Acceptance test.** The murders paragraph ends with the killer's fate stated in one clause, and
  the section's word count grows by fewer than 20 words.

### UNFAM-C10 — "Mann Act" is unglossed and Olgivanna enters mid-arrest

- **Passage.** "In October 1926 Wright was arrested under the Mann Act for transporting Olgivanna
  across state lines while still married to Miriam." (word 2,780)
- **Reader effect.** Two problems in one sentence. A reader who does not know the Mann Act reads
  "arrested for transporting a woman across state lines," which sounds surreal rather than scandalous
  and makes the sentence's function unclear — is this persecution or wrongdoing? Separately,
  Olgivanna's only prior appearance is as "his widow Olgivanna" 400 words earlier in a forward-flash,
  so the reader must reconstruct on the fly that this is a third partner.
- **Minimum viable repair.** Four words glossing the Act as the anti-vice law it was, and give
  Olgivanna a relationship noun on this use.
- **Expected benefit.** The lean-years scandal becomes legible as a scandal, which is what the
  section needs it to be.
- **Confidence.** High.
- **Acceptance test.** A reader can state what Wright was accused of and who Olgivanna was to him,
  from this sentence alone.

### UNFAM-C11 — Private interior is asserted as fact in two places

- **Passages.** "He did not believe it. He needed _you_ to believe it, out loud, daily…" (intro,
  repeated near-verbatim in the conclusion: "He did not believe he was the greatest."). And: "decided
  around age nine that the only safe thing to do was keep building."
- **Reader effect.** These are the two boldest claims in the article and both are stated as biography
  rather than reading. I felt the overreach without any research: the quote directly above the first
  one has Wright agreeing that arrogance _can be_ a shell and calling his own thin — which is not the
  same as not believing his own claim. The "age nine" figure attaches a specific year to an
  unobservable decision; the only thing dated to nine is the Froebel blocks. Elsewhere the article is
  scrupulous about marking inference ("At least, that is the story her son told"), so these read as
  lapses from its own standard rather than house style.
- **Evidence.** Packet E-01 independently reaches the same conclusion: the draft's reading "is
  **interpretation** layered on a verified quotation. It is a defensible interpretation; it is not
  what the quote says." Packet CLM-19 flags a third instance in the conclusion — the porkpie hat
  "shaped to mirror his own cantilevered roofs" — as unverified and probably interpretation.
- **Minimum viable repair.** Mark them as readings rather than facts. Four words each. The claims can
  stay; they are the article's argument and they are interesting. They just need the same hedging the
  Welsh prophecy already gets.
- **Expected benefit.** Brings the two highest-exposure sentences up to the standard the rest of the
  article already meets, without softening the thesis.
- **Confidence.** High; noticed unaided and corroborated by packet E-01.
- **Acceptance test.** No sentence in the body asserts what Wright privately believed or decided
  without an inference marker or an adjacent quotation that licenses it.

## Follow-on questions

- **UNFAM-Q1.** Does a general reader who has never heard of the Enneagram need the _system_ named
  and sized ("a nine-type model of motivation") before the type claim, or is it inferable? I decided
  it was inferable from the H2 "What is Frank Lloyd Wright's personality type?" plus the core-fear
  gloss, and I did not stumble. **What would change my answer:** evidence that arriving traffic comes
  from Wright-biography intent rather than Enneagram intent, in which case a five-word system gloss
  becomes necessary rather than optional. **Best source:** Google Search Console query data for the
  personality-analysis cluster in `docs/data/gsc/`.
- **UNFAM-Q2.** Is the article's reticence about the six children a deliberate structural choice or
  an unclosed loop? The H2 promises "six children," John Lloyd Wright pays off one, and the other
  five are never mentioned again. I read it as restraint. **What would change my answer:** if the
  record supports a second short receipt of comparable quality, the heading's promise would be
  properly discharged rather than half-discharged. **Best source:** packet T-07 and the Lloyd Wright
  material behind S-11b/S-35.
- **UNFAM-Q3.** Would moving the Fallingwater gloss forward damage the article's design? The piece
  opens on a leak at Wingspread and reveals at 78% that Fallingwater leaks too, which is a real
  structural rhyme I would not want broken. **What would change my answer:** confirmation from the
  editor that the withheld reveal is intentional; if it is, a nine-word apposition at word 60 costs
  the rhyme nothing, because the rhyme depends on the leak, not on the house being a mystery.

## Preserve list

  before auditing that `cae935d9…` is the hash of the live draft `src/blog/people/drafts/Frank-Lloyd-Wright.md`

  snapshot's own location. **No substantive content differs**, and none of the changes alter rendered
  output. I proceeded on that basis and report the contracted SHA in the frontmatter. Flagging for the
  synthesis step: the freeze is not byte-stable against repo tooling, which will recur on every
  perspective run.

  (2026-08-26), SECOND PASS (2026-08-26), and COHESION PASS (2026-08-26) notes, plus a self-assigned
  9.3/A grade, as HTML comments. These are unavoidable when reading the file linearly. I did not
