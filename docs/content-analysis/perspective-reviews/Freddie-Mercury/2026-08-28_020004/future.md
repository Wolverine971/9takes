## Bottom-line verdict

Twelve months from now this article will still be substantively true, comprehensible, and worth reading, because its evidence spine is a closed historical record: the 1985 Wigg tapes, the 1987 Dolezal interview, and memoirs published between 1994 and 2012. Nothing in the argument can be invalidated by news. The durability problems are concentrated in exactly one device — the Sept 5, 2026 Montreux gala used as the current-tense anchor — whose future/present-tense phrasing expires **eight days after the publication date** and whose year is never stated in the body. Add one drifting internal statistic and one aliveness-dependent present-tense ending, and you have a piece that needs a handful of mechanical tense-and-date repairs, not a rethink. Recommendation: revise (small, targeted).

## What landed

- **FUTURE-H1 — Closed-record evidence spine.** The diagnosis is argued entirely from fixed sources: "In the 1985 David Wigg interview he laid the sequence out himself: 'The higher I climb up that ladder, the bigger the barrier becomes around me...'" (diagnosis section). Every load-bearing quote predates 1992 or comes from dated memoirs/interviews (1994, 2000, 2012, 2021, 2025). No claim in the argument chain can be overtaken by events. This must survive revision because it is the reason the page can compound SEO value for years instead of decaying with a news cycle.
- **FUTURE-H2 — Date-anchored streaming claim.** "'Bohemian Rhapsody'... was certified the most-streamed song from the 20th century in December 2018, at 1.6 billion streams (Universal Music Group)" (Live Aid section). This is the model of durable phrasing: it anchors a certification _event_ with a date rather than quoting a live count that will be wrong within months. The raw stream number has already grown past that figure, and the sentence stays true anyway. Preserve the construction exactly.
- **FUTURE-H3 — The permanent hedge.** "If Hutton's dating is right, the most honest artistic statement of his life doubled as the beginning of its greatest concealment" (Scene by Scene). The diagnosis date is permanently unresolvable (packet, Disputes #5); the conditional posture never rots and must survive every future pass.
- **FUTURE-H4 — Durable FAQ surface.** All five FAQ answers rest on fixed biographical facts (birth, name, death, Mary, shyness testimony) with absolute dates ("He died on November 24, 1991, at age 45"). The SERP-facing layer of the page has effectively zero temporal decay. (Its one factual dispute, CLM-07, is another perspective's lane — see Limits.)
- **FUTURE-H5 — The current event is ornament, not evidence.** Run the role's core test — remove the newest event and see what remains — and the answer is: everything. The gala appears only as intro grounding and ending texture; no inference depends on it. And the ending's actual meaning rides on the durable half of its contrast: "The statue is not where he is. One woman knows where he is." That sentence pair is why the ending passes the swap test in 2027 as well as 2026.

## What missed

- The piece has no publication-date visibility in the reader-visible body. A 2027 reader who lands on "This September 5 he would have turned 80" (intro, biography paragraph) has to do frontmatter archaeology to work out that "this" meant 2026. The one device chosen to make the piece feel current is the one device guaranteed to make it feel stale, because it was written with no absolute year anywhere in the prose.
- The estate's event is future tense in three places, with the shortest possible shelf life: "his estate **is throwing** the party in Montreux" (intro), "On September 5 the pretence **gets** its 80th birthday party" (ending), "The statue by Lake Geneva **will get** its flowers" (ending). All three read as stale copy from Sept 6, 2026 onward — roughly a week after publication.

## What I expected

- Absolute dates on load-bearing events — largely delivered (July 13, 1985; November 23/24, 1991; December 2018; "OK! magazine, 2000"). The discipline lapses only around the gala.
- A refresh trigger or date-stamp on volatile numbers — not delivered for the corpus statistic (FUTURE-C2).
- Care with status-sensitive claims about living people — mostly delivered: the draft wisely makes no claims about current Garden Lodge ownership, current estate values, or any living witness's present circumstances. The single exception is the ending's present-tense dependence on Mary Austin (FUTURE-C3).

## What surprised me

- **Welcome:** the deliberateness of the freshness device. The formula ledger explicitly logs the gala as the "current-tense or legacy-now anchor," meaning the expiry is a design choice, not an accident — and the designer kept it out of the evidence chain. That is the right way to spend a news peg.
- **Jarring:** the arithmetic of that choice. Publication date 2026-08-28; anchor event 2026-09-05. The piece's only future-tense claims have an eight-day validity window, and nothing in the draft or its production pretext schedules the past-tense conversion.
- **Welcome:** a subject dead for 35 years gives the thesis near-total insulation. Even the packet's watch-item (the contested 2024 secret-daughter claim) cannot contradict the draft, because the draft never asserts he had no children — silence was the durable move, and the draft made it.

## Red flags

No blockers. Nothing in the draft is misleading _today_; the findings below are concerns — claims that will curdle within the twelve-month window without intervention.

- **FUTURE-C1 — Year-less gala dating in future/present tense (highest priority).**
  - _Passages:_ intro biography paragraph: "This September 5 he would have turned 80, and his estate is throwing the party in Montreux"; ending: "On September 5 the pretence gets its 80th birthday party: The Great Pretender's Gala... the centerpiece of a five-day festival"; ending: "The statue by Lake Geneva will get its flowers."
  - _Reader effect:_ from Sept 6, 2026 the page visibly hasn't been updated; by mid-2027 "This September 5" actually points at his 81st birthday, making a correct sentence read as an error and taxing trust in the surrounding (accurate) analysis.
  - _Evidence:_ event confirmed as scheduled and sold out (brianmay.com Aug 2026 party guide; freddiemercury.com on-sale page) — so the facts are right at publication; only the tense and the missing year rot.
  - _Minimum viable repair:_ put the absolute year in each passage and phrase so the sentences stay true after the event (an "80th birthday, September 5, 2026" construction; a past-tense-safe ending verb). Alternatively, one scheduled post-event edit converting all three passages.
  - _Expected benefit:_ the page's only decaying passages become evergreen; no reader-facing staleness signal in year one.
  - _Confidence:_ high. _Acceptance test:_ read the intro and ending cold on a simulated date of 2027-08-28; no sentence implies the gala is upcoming or that the next birthday is the 80th.
- **FUTURE-C2 — Undated drifting corpus statistic.**
  - _Passage:_ Rabbit Hole counterarguments: "of the 80 musicians profiled on 9takes, 30 are Type 4s, the most over-represented type in the corpus," with a link to /corpus-stats.
  - _Reader effect:_ the corpus grows continuously; the linked live page will contradict the static numbers, and the draft's best credibility move (base-rate self-suspicion) becomes its most checkable error.
  - _Evidence:_ packet CLM-20 verifies 80/30 today and itself advises a date-stamp; the pipeline adds people weekly.
  - _Minimum viable repair:_ date-stamp the figure ("as of August 2026") or convert to a durable ratio ("more than a third of the musicians profiled here").
  - _Expected benefit:_ the self-audit paragraph stays true indefinitely instead of aging into a mismatch with its own hyperlink.
  - _Confidence:_ high. _Acceptance test:_ the sentence remains accurate if the corpus reads 95 musicians / 36 Fours.
- **FUTURE-C3 — Ending depends on a living person's present-tense status.**
  - _Passages:_ "One woman knows where he is. She has kept it for three decades... the last door he ever asked her to hold shut. / Every audience he ever had is still outside it, singing."
  - _Reader effect:_ Mary Austin is in her mid-70s. If she dies, or ever discloses or leaves an account of the location, the final beat — the emotional payload of the whole piece — becomes factually wrong overnight rather than merely dated.
  - _Evidence:_ the secret has held since ~1993-94 (packet timeline), so the near-term risk is actuarial, not behavioral.
  - _Minimum viable repair:_ none pre-emptively — the present tense is the right artistic choice today. The repair is a refresh trigger: this passage must be first on the check-list of any future edit of this page (see refresh list #4).
  - _Expected benefit:_ the strongest ending in the draft is protected from becoming its most visible error.
  - _Confidence:_ high that the dependency exists; low that it triggers within 12 months. _Acceptance test:_ the refresh checklist for this page names the ending as status-sensitive.
- **FUTURE-C4 — Present-tense external status claim about type databases.**
  - _Passage:_ diagnosis section: "The type databases split between 3w4, 4, and 7w6, and none argue from testimony."
  - _Reader effect:_ database vote distributions shift, especially through anniversary traffic spikes; if PDB consolidates on one type, the sentence misstates the landscape the article claims to be correcting.
  - _Evidence:_ packet S18 establishes the split as of 2026-08; splits of this kind have historically been stable for Mercury, so drift is slow.
  - _Minimum viable repair:_ "have long split" (a perfective construction that stays true regardless of later movement).
  - _Expected benefit:_ removes the only sentence whose truth depends on a third-party site's live state.
  - _Confidence:_ medium. _Acceptance test:_ sentence remains accurate even if PDB's plurality changes.
- **FUTURE-C5 — Unfalsifiable "now" superlative.**
  - _Passage:_ Live Aid section: "His catalog now outlives every act that shared that stage."
  - _Reader effect:_ "outlives" has no stated metric, and Elton John, U2, Madonna, and McCartney shared that stage with very alive catalogs; a skeptical 2027 reader hits an unverifiable boast right before the article's best-sourced statistic, which cheapens it.
  - _Evidence:_ none available in the packet for any metric reading; the adjacent Bo Rhap certification is the claim's defensible core.
  - _Minimum viable repair:_ narrow the claim to what the next sentence proves (the streaming certification) or cut it and let the certified fact carry the paragraph.
  - _Expected benefit:_ removes the paragraph's one challengeable sentence while keeping its force.
  - _Confidence:_ medium. _Acceptance test:_ every comparative claim in the Live Aid section is either dated, sourced, or gone.
