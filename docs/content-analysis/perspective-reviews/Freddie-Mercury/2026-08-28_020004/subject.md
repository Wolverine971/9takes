# Subject-perspective review: Freddie Mercury draft

Central question applied: even if Mercury rejected the typing — and he would have; the draft itself
predicts he'd call the result "a pretence" — has the article represented him fairly?

## Bottom-line verdict

Largely, yes — this is one of the fairest treatments a fiercely private dead man could receive from
a personality-typing article. It quotes his small first-person record accurately in substance,
hosts his advocate's corrections (Freestone's "Freddie loved laughing"), gives Jim Hutton his
factual place with dignity, assigns no sexuality label, closes the Farrokh/Zoroastrian loop, and
ends by _respecting_ the one secret he asked to be kept rather than prying at it. But two trust
breaks require repair before publication: a SERP-surfaced FAQ asserts an intimate deathbed detail
("she was holding his hand when he died") that no checked account supports and that displaces the
partner whose memoir places _himself_ at the bedside; and the Munich section inflates his mild,
self-deprecating gifts anecdote into a betrayal narrative ("pawned by people who'd wanted the
wallet") the record does not contain. A quieter pattern also needs one honest fix: the draft
consistently clips the playful or normalizing codas Mercury attached to his darkest statements,
shifting his register toward pure confession. Verdict: **revise** — the repairs are small, contained,
and none touch the draft's spine.

One correction in the draft's favor: the evidence packet's CLM-08 charge that "Every day is
Fasching for me" is absent from the on-file Wigg transcript is wrong. The sentence is there,
garbled by auto-captions as "I live life to the full world. Every day is _flashing_ for me,"
immediately following his Fasching discussion. The draft's "he told Wigg" attribution is supported.

## What landed

- **SUBJECT-H1 — The anti-sad-clown paragraph.** "The shyness was real and the joy was real. What
  he rationed was access. ... The tragedy people project onto his whole life actually describes
  only the space between the walls and the stage." This is the single most important fairness move
  in the draft: it hosts Freestone's on-record correction of the myth _before_ the analysis needs
  it, and refuses to resolve the ambivalence in the thesis's favor. It must survive every later
  pass untouched.
- **SUBJECT-H2 — The Silence handled without erasure or grovel.** "The judgment deserves an answer
  built from his own stated arithmetic rather than a defense brief" — the section states the
  activist criticism first, answers it from his own vocabulary (scars, barriers, the unsold private
  self), and lets Freestone carry a witness line. It explains the silence without pretending the
  criticism didn't exist. This is exactly how to treat a dead man's most-judged decision.
- **SUBJECT-H3 — The ending keeps his secret.** "The statue is not where he is. One woman knows
  where he is. ... the last door he ever asked her to hold shut." An article about his privacy that
  ends by _declining to violate it_ earns the right to everything before it. Preserve verbatim.
- **SUBJECT-H4 — Jim Hutton's plain dignity.** "they wore matching rings in an era with no legal
  category for what the rings meant." Factual, unlabeled, unsentimental — the draft neither outs,
  names, nor minimizes. Combined with the honored claims-to-avoid list (no sexuality label, no
  secret-daughter claim, hedged diagnosis date), this is the safe-inference boundary working.
- **SUBJECT-H5 — Visible separation of behavior from inference.** "he refused every offer to
  correct them, afraid, _the people who filmed him said_, that fixing the teeth would change the
  voice" — the draft repeatedly shows its attribution seams instead of laundering them, and the
  Rabbit Hole concedes what the type does not explain plus the 30-of-80 musician-Four base rate.
  The closing note ("interpretation, never diagnosis... he'd have likely told us the result was a
  pretence anyway") is epistemic humility in the subject's own vocabulary.
- **SUBJECT-H6 — Mary Austin analyzed, not just quoted.** "She was the one person whose love could
  not be explained by Freddie Mercury, because there was no Freddie Mercury empire when she signed
  up." A genuinely new, fair reading of the most-pasted quote in his file — it explains his loyalty
  without pathologizing hers.

## What missed

- **The Munich section overwrites his own account of himself.** He said, on tape: "I buy a lot of
  shitty things for a lot of people, just most of the times I get them back a few years later" —
  and, asked in the next breath whether that left him disillusioned with mankind, "No, no, not
  really." The draft renders this as extravagant gifts "returned or pawned by people who'd wanted
  the wallet and moved on," an entourage that "took the champagne and treaded exactly where he'd
  said people tread." The wound is real elsewhere in the transcript; _this_ moment was
  self-deprecating and shrugging, and the draft darkens it to feed the stress-arrow argument. See
  SUBJECT-B2.
- **His de-dramatizing codas are systematically trimmed.** The Dolezal pretence confession ended
  "It's fun." The scars passage ended, under Wigg's pressing, with Mercury universalizing: "I think
  everybody has that kind of thing... it doesn't matter what you are, a bricklayer or... somebody
  who's in a sweet shop." The draft quotes the dark halves accurately and omits the light halves
  everywhere. He kept telling interviewers his barriers were ordinary and his act was enjoyable;
  the article's register says otherwise. See SUBJECT-C1.
- **One fabricated intimate fact in the FAQ.** See SUBJECT-B1 — the only outright unsupported
  claim about him on the page, and it sits on the surface most likely to be excerpted by search
  engines.

## What I expected

- **Quote fidelity to a small first-person record** — largely delivered; the Wigg material is
  substance-accurate against the on-file transcript, ellipses compress without distorting (packet
  CLM-03), and the second pass already cut one unpinnable Mercury quote (the teeth line). The two
  breaches are the Munich embellishment and the coda-clipping.
- **His humor** — present and structurally protected (H1), which most treatments of Mercury fail.
- **His ethnicity and faith on the page** — delivered: Parsi childhood, the family kept from the
  machinery, Zoroastrian rites, "The name came all the way back." The draft names what he himself
  kept out of interviews without treating the reticence as shame; Freestone's "he was ashamed of
  nothing" is carried.
- **Criticism explained without erasing responsibility** — Sun City is present in one accurate
  sentence; the AIDS-silence criticism is stated before it is answered. Fair on both.

## What surprised me

- **Welcome:** the funeral as the payoff of the Farrokh thesis — "The last ceremony of Freddie
  Mercury's life belonged to the faith Farrokh Bulsara was born into" — closes the identity loop
  with a verified fact instead of a psychological claim. Also the Elton John Tuke-painting beat,
  which lets his generosity be _flawless production_ rather than pathology.
- **Jarring:** the invented inner monologue of an eight-year-old ("I'll build the boy they'll want
  to know") asserts the article's thesis as the child's own stated intention — interiority no
  source could ever support (SUBJECT-C2). Also "he admitted with a laugh that doesn't quite hold" —
  a tone-reading of an audio moment presented as observation.

## Red flags

### SUBJECT-B1 — Blocker: FAQ asserts Mary Austin "was holding his hand when he died"

- **Passage:** frontmatter FAQ "Was Freddie Mercury married? Who was Mary Austin?": "...and she was
  holding his hand when he died."
- **Effect:** a checkable, SERP-surfaced intimate detail about the most private moment of a man the
  article itself establishes as fiercely private — and the record does not support it. Worse, it
  quietly displaces Jim Hutton, whose memoir places himself present, re-centering the platonic
  female ex-fiancée at the deathbed. That is precisely the heteronormative rewrite of Mercury's
  story that his partner's testimony resists; an article this careful about the rings and the
  labels cannot afford it.
- **Evidence:** packet dispute #1 (S13 sweep: Dave Clark "reportedly at the bedside"; Hutton
  present per _Mercury and Me_; Freestone in the house; multiple accounts say Mary was phoned
  _after_ death). Independent search this run (Smooth Radio, IrishCentral, allthatsinteresting,
  aggregators) found no account of hand-holding at the moment of death; the packet traces the
  claim's likely seed to an unsourced line in the repo research file.
- **Minimum viable repair:** delete the clause, or replace with her attested role, e.g. "...and she
  tended him through his final days."
- **Expected benefit:** removes the page's only fabricated intimate fact from its most-copied
  surface and keeps faith with Hutton's testimony.
- **Confidence:** high.
- **Acceptance test:** no clause anywhere on the page places Mary Austin in the room at the moment
  of death; every Mary sentence in the FAQ traces to a packet-verified claim.

### SUBJECT-B2 — Blocker: Munich gifts passage embellishes his testimony into a betrayal narrative

- **Passage:** Munich section: "He bought extravagant gifts... most of them came back a few years
  later, returned **or pawned** by people who'd **wanted the wallet** and moved on" and "The
  entourage took the champagne and **treaded exactly where he'd said people tread**"; Rabbit Hole
  stress arrow: "an entourage that **pawned the gifts**."
- **Effect:** his actual on-record line is mild and self-mocking ("I buy a lot of shitty things for
  a lot of people, just most of the times I get them back a few years later"), and when Wigg asked
  whether this left him disillusioned with mankind he answered "No, no, not really." The draft
  invents a mechanism (pawning), a motive (wanted the wallet), and splices the anecdote to the
  "tread all over you" quote — which in the transcript is about vulnerability in love and celebrity
  generally, not the entourage. The Four-to-Two stress-arrow argument then leans on the embellished
  version. This puts unsupported venal behavior on real (unnamed) people and makes the subject's
  shrug into a wound he declined to claim.
- **Evidence:** on-file Wigg transcript, re-read this run
  (`youtube-transcripts/1985-freddie-mercury-david-wigg-munich.md`); packet CLM-08.
- **Minimum viable repair:** strip "or pawned" and "who'd wanted the wallet and moved on" (both
  body and Rabbit Hole); let the transcript's own words carry the moment; either cut the
  tread-splice or mark it explicitly as the draft's reading; optionally keep the honest wrinkle
  that he refused Wigg's disillusionment framing.
- **Expected benefit:** the stress-arrow claim survives on the true quote alone, and the section
  stops overwriting the subject's account of his own generosity.
- **Confidence:** high.
- **Acceptance test:** every behavioral specific in the Munich gifts passage and the Rabbit Hole
  stress arrow traces to the transcript or a named source; "pawned" appears nowhere unless newly
  sourced.

### SUBJECT-C1 — Concern: the draft clips Mercury's normalizing codas, darkening his register

- **Passages:** intro and Scene by Scene use "The way I see it is that all this is a pretence"
  without its verified close, "It's fun." (packet CLM-04, Louder transcription). The
  epigraph/diagnosis/shy sections use the scars quote without his follow-up under pressing: "I
  think everybody has that kind of thing... a bricklayer or... somebody who's in a sweet shop"
  (transcript-confirmed this run). Munich omits "No, no, not really" (disillusionment).
- **Effect:** three-for-three, the confession is kept and the deflating close is cut. The draft
  reads his interviews as "a therapy transcript with jokes" — but removing the jokes from the
  quotes is what makes them read as therapy. He repeatedly insisted his barriers were ordinary and
  his act enjoyable; a fair representation lets him say so once.
- **Minimum viable repair:** reinstate "It's fun." where the Dolezal quote does thesis work, and
  add one sentence in the shy or diagnosis section acknowledging he universalized the barriers —
  then argue past it explicitly (the argument survives; withdrawal is still multiply attested).
- **Expected benefit:** inoculates the spine against the strongest subject-side objection: "you
  quoted my dark bars and cut my punchlines."
- **Confidence:** high.
- **Acceptance test:** the Dolezal pretence quote appears at least once with "It's fun."; the draft
  contains Mercury's everybody-has-this claim (quoted or closely paraphrased) before relying on the
  scars quote as discriminating evidence.

### SUBJECT-C2 — Concern: invented childhood interiority asserts the thesis as his intention

- **Passage:** childhood section, `inner-thought` paragraph: "Everyone here arrived knowing
  someone. I arrived knowing nobody. Fine. I'll build the boy they'll want to know."
- **Effect:** fabricated first-person thought of an eight-year-old, stating the article's central
  claim as the child's own plan. No source could support it; if the rendered styling doesn't
  unmistakably mark authorial imagination, readers will take it as reconstruction.
- **Minimum viable repair:** a visible framing cue in the text itself (e.g., "Imagine the first
  morning:") rather than relying on CSS alone.
- **Expected benefit:** keeps the device (it works) while making its epistemic status honest.
- **Confidence:** medium (depends on how the class renders on the live page).
- **Acceptance test:** a first-time reader asked "did Mercury think this?" answers "the writer is
  imagining it" from the text alone.

### SUBJECT-C3 — Concern: "Freestone put it flatly" overstates the attribution

- **Passage:** childhood section: "Freestone put it flatly: he became Freddie at school and never
  went back to Farrokh."
- **Effect:** per the dopeYEAH transcript (packet CLM-02 nuance), the flat phrasing is the
  _interviewer's_; Freestone affirmed and elaborated — and separately noted Farrokh stayed visible
  "till about 1972–73," which softens "the renaming had already happened where it counts."
- **Minimum viable repair:** "Freestone agreed:" or paraphrase without "flatly."
- **Confidence:** high. **Acceptance test:** the attribution matches the transcript's
  question-and-affirmation shape.

### SUBJECT-C4 — Concern: "In 1976 he told her he was bisexual" stated as bare fact

- **Passage:** Mary Austin section.
- **Effect:** the event is known only through Austin's later accounts (packet timeline, attributed
  claim). The draft's no-label discipline is excellent; the highest-sensitivity sentence on the
  page deserves the same visible sourcing the teeth sentence gets.
- **Minimum viable repair:** "In 1976, she later recalled, he told her he was bisexual."
- **Confidence:** high. **Acceptance test:** the sentence carries an attribution cue to Austin.

### SUBJECT-C5 — Concern: Bowie quote pinned to a venue no source establishes

- **Passage:** Live Aid section: "David Bowie said it at the tribute concert in 1992."
- **Effect:** a marquee quote about Mercury given a manufactured-looking occasion (packet CLM-12:
  wording verified, venue unpinned). Low subject-harm, but it is the kind of checkable slip that
  costs the page trust with exactly the readers who knew him best.
- **Minimum viable repair:** "Bowie said of him" (drop the venue) unless a source pins it.
- **Confidence:** high. **Acceptance test:** no unpinned venue or date attaches to the Bowie quote.

## Specific improvements

The B1/B2/C1–C5 repairs above are the improvement list. Two additions:

- **SUBJECT-I1 — Bank the Fasching correction.** The full transcript sentence — "I live life to the
  full... Every day is Fasching for me" (captioned "flashing," directly after his Fasching riff) —
  is attributable to the Wigg session. The draft's existing attribution is safe; the synthesis
  stage should carry this correction so CLM-08's misattribution charge doesn't trigger an
  unnecessary cut. Acceptance test: the verify/synthesis pass records the Fasching sentence as
  transcript-supported rather than unpinned.
- **SUBJECT-I2 — Soften "a laugh that doesn't quite hold."** The draft asserts the emotional
  quality of a laugh on a 40-year-old tape as observed fact. Either attribute it as reading ("a
  laugh that, on the tape, reads as...") or cut. Confidence: medium. Acceptance test: no sentence
  presents the draft's tone-perception of archival audio as observation.

## Follow-on questions

## Limits of this review

- The subject is deceased; this is a fairness proxy built from his interview record and
  advocate-adjacent witnesses (Freestone is protective by disposition; Hutton's memoir framing is
  disputed by parts of the fandom). I did not and cannot obtain the subject's view.
- All transcript verification is capped by auto-caption quality; "shitty things" and "flashing"
  are both caption readings, and my Fasching correction rests on garble-in-context interpretation.
  One human listen to the Wigg video would settle both (SUBJECT-Q2).
- The deathbed check used secondary and aggregator web sources; primary memoirs (Freestone 1998,
  Hutton 1994, the OK! 2000 print original) were not directly accessed (standing packet
  limitation, including Wayback unavailability).
- I did not evaluate SEO, Enneagram-theoretic correctness, or temporal durability — those belong
  to other jury seats. Typing disagreement alone was not treated as a fairness defect.
