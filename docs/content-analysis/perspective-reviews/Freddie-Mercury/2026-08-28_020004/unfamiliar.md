# Unfamiliar-Reader Review: Freddie Mercury draft

Protocol note: per the unfamiliar-reader exception, the frozen draft was read first with no
packet and no web research; initial comprehension was recorded before opening
`evidence-packet.md`. No orientation search was needed — the packet resolved every
omission-vs-ignorance question. The pipeline comments embedded in the snapshot (fresh-eyes,
second-pass, cohesion notes) were not used as anchors; findings below come from the rendered
reader surface only, cross-checked against the packet afterward.

Reader stance simulated: I know "Queen," "Bohemian Rhapsody," and that there was a movie at
some point. I do not know the band members' names, what Live Aid was, who Mary Austin or Jim
Hutton were, or anything about the Enneagram.

## Bottom-line verdict

An unfamiliar reader can finish this article and pass every test the role sets: I can say who
Freddie Mercury is (Queen's frontman, born Farrokh Bulsara in Zanzibar, sent to boarding
school at eight, dead of AIDS at 45 in 1991), why he matters (Live Aid, the most-streamed
20th-century song), why now (his 80th birthday gala on September 5, 2026), and what the
article claims (Enneagram Type 4: he hand-built "Freddie Mercury" as a mask, guarded the
person underneath, and withdrew when hurt — the stage was the only intimacy that couldn't
hurt him). The hook is the best kind for a newcomer: a concrete image (the most famous mouth
in rock, hidden behind its owner's hand) that IS the thesis before any theory arrives.

The revision case is surgical, not structural: one packet-substantiated factual problem on
the FAQ surface (the exact surface an unfamiliar searcher hits first), plus a cluster of
introduce-before-use misses — Jim Hutton, Roger Taylor, Brian May, "the movie," and the
Enneagram notation are all deployed before the draft says who or what they are. Every one is
a one-clause fix.

## What landed

- **UNFAMILIAR-H1 — The cold open.** "Now look at almost any candid photograph of him from
  those years. The most famous mouth in rock is hidden behind his hand." Zero prerequisites,
  instantly visual, and the follow-up line — "They are the same gesture, made in opposite
  directions" — hands a newcomer the entire thesis before a single term of jargon appears. I
  understood the article's project by paragraph five. This is the strongest possible entry
  for my perspective and must survive revision untouched.
- **UNFAMILIAR-H2 — The facts-first paragraph.** "The facts first, for anyone who arrived
  through the movie. Freddie Mercury was born Farrokh Bulsara on September 5, 1946..." One
  paragraph of biography, placed exactly where a disoriented reader needs it, then back to
  the argument. This is the design the role contract hopes for: orientation without
  encyclopedia. (Its one flaw is filed as UNFAMILIAR-C3.)
- **UNFAMILIAR-H3 — The Wigg source card.** "Wigg interviewed Mercury across two decades and
  was one of the few journalists he trusted... It runs 18 minutes and reads like a therapy
  transcript with jokes." A newcomer has no way to weigh sources; this card did the weighing
  for me and correctly set expectations for the quotes that carry the middle of the piece.
  Trust-building at exactly the right moment.
- **UNFAMILIAR-H4 — The Mary Austin insight.** "She was the one person whose love could not
  be explained by Freddie Mercury, because there was no Freddie Mercury empire when she
  signed up." I had never heard of Mary Austin ninety seconds earlier, and I could retell
  this insight at dinner. That is the delight test passing for a reader with no priors.
- **UNFAMILIAR-H5 — The Live Aid reversal and the Bohemian Rhapsody re-read.** "Untouchable
  is the load-bearing word" made me reinterpret the article's own opening scene, and "The
  song is a locked room, and the world sings along to the door" gave me a new relationship
  with the only song of his I actually knew. Both are payoffs a newcomer can cash without
  homework.
- **UNFAMILIAR-H6 — The Rabbit Hole quarantine.** "For the Enneagram nerds. Skip if you're
  not deep into the system. The rest of the analysis stands on its own." Explicit permission
  to skip is exactly right for this perspective, and the promise is true — the analysis does
  stand without it.
- **UNFAMILIAR-H7 — The laughing paragraph.** Freestone's "Freddie loved laughing" beat kept
  the piece from reading as a misery narrative; as a first-time reader I finished with a
  person, not a case study. It must survive.

## What missed

- The diagnosis section's second paragraph promises to arbitrate a dispute ("The type
  databases split between 3w4, 4, and 7w6, and none argue from testimony") before a newcomer
  has been told what a type database is, what the numbers mean, or what "w" notation is.
  The promised effect — "here is the controversy we're settling" — lands as "here is an
  argument already in progress." The functional writing recovers it within two paragraphs
  (the Four/Three/Seven behaviors are defined in plain language, which is why this is a
  concern and not a blocker), but this is the single highest-friction moment in the piece
  and the most likely early-exit point for my reader. Filed as UNFAMILIAR-C4.
- "The facts first, for anyone who arrived through the movie" is written _for_ the least
  oriented readers and yet assumes they know which movie. The sentence's promise —
  meeting the unoriented where they are — is undercut by its own first clause. Filed as
  UNFAMILIAR-C3.
- The Scene-by-Scene section's final beat ("**The timing.** Jim Hutton later dated Mercury's
  AIDS diagnosis to the spring of that same year") is built to land as the emotional peak of
  the middle third, and for me it half-landed: I did not know who Jim Hutton was or why his
  dating of a diagnosis would be authoritative, because his introduction ("an Irish barber,
  his partner from 1985 until his death") arrives a full section later. Filed as
  UNFAMILIAR-C1.

## What I expected

- Every named person to carry an identifying clause at first use. Mary Austin gets one, Jim
  Hutton eventually gets one (too late — C1), Barbara Valentin gets one ("the actress"),
  Peter Freestone gets a good one. Roger Taylor, Brian May, and Peter Straker never get one
  at all (C2).
- One clause, somewhere, saying what the Enneagram is — the article defines Type 4
  behaviorally (well) but never frames the system itself (C4).
- A reason the birthday gala and the statue are in Montreux, Switzerland, when every scene
  in the article happens in Zanzibar, India, London, or Munich (C5).
- A word on what Live Aid actually was. "The biggest concert on Earth" conveys scale but not
  nature; I inferred "charity event" only from the name. One clause ("the global charity
  broadcast") would ground the superlative. Recorded as a preference, not a concern — the
  reading survives without it.
- Not expected, and correctly absent: encyclopedic discography, family tree, or a
  chronological career recap. The role contract says not to penalize those omissions, and
  none were missed.

## What surprised me

- **Welcome:** the cats paragraph ("narrated his day, long-distance, to a calico cat") and
  the Elton John Christmas-gift ending — both delivered character through concrete objects,
  which is precisely what a reader without context can absorb. The Zoroastrian funeral beat
  ("The name came all the way back") closed a loop I had been unconsciously tracking since
  the childhood section; for a first-time reader that landing felt earned, not researched.
- **Jarring (mildly):** the italicized inner-thought paragraph in the childhood section
  ("Everyone here arrived knowing someone... I'll build the boy they'll want to know"). On
  first read I briefly took it for a sourced quotation — a diary, a letter — because every
  other indented or styled block in the piece is sourced testimony. Realizing it was
  authorial invention produced a small trust wobble in an article that otherwise signals
  sourcing discipline hard (C6).

## Red flags

### UNFAMILIAR-R1 (blocker) — FAQ asserts an unsupported deathbed detail on the highest-trust surface

- **Passage:** frontmatter FAQ "Was Freddie Mercury married? Who was Mary Austin?", final
  clause of the answer: "and she was holding his hand when he died."
- **Reader effect:** the FAQ block is the surface an unfamiliar searcher meets first (SERP
  snippet and on-page answer box). A vivid deathbed detail stated as flat fact gets absorbed
  as fact by exactly the reader least equipped to check it. If that reader later encounters
  the standard accounts, the whole article's sourcing credibility — its main trust asset —
  takes the hit.
- **Evidence:** the shared evidence packet (CLM-07, Disputes #1) found no source trail:
  checked accounts place Dave Clark reportedly at the bedside, Hutton present per his
  memoir, Freestone in the house, and several accounts say Mary Austin was _phoned first
  after_ his death. The packet's verdict: "The claim cannot stand as fact." Note the
  packet's location tag says "FAQ 5 (how-did-he-die)"; the clause actually sits in the Mary
  Austin FAQ — the repair should target the right answer.
- **Minimum viable repair:** delete the clause, or replace with a supported closer (e.g.,
  she inherited Garden Lodge and kept the location of his ashes secret — both already in the
  answer's orbit and packet-verified).
- **Expected benefit:** the FAQ surface becomes as defensible as the body, which currently
  out-sources it.
- **Confidence:** high (substantiated by the packet, not by my unaided read — an unfamiliar
  reader cannot detect this, which is exactly why it matters).
- **Acceptance test:** no clause in any FAQ answer asserts a deathbed fact the packet's
  claim inventory classes as disputed; the Mary Austin answer contains only
  attributed/verified claims.

### UNFAMILIAR-C1 (concern) — Jim Hutton used one section before he is introduced

- **Passage:** "The Great Pretender, Scene by Scene," final beat: "**The timing.** Jim
  Hutton later dated Mercury's AIDS diagnosis to the spring of that same year." His
  introduction ("Jim Hutton, an Irish barber, was his partner from 1985 until his death")
  arrives in the following section.
- **Reader effect:** the section's emotional peak asks a newcomer to weigh an unidentified
  person's testimony about the most consequential fact in the article. I stalled on "who?"
  at the precise moment the draft wanted maximum impact.
- **Evidence:** first-read experience; packet confirms the body's first Hutton mention is
  this passage. The role contract names "people... introduced before use" as a core test.
- **Minimum viable repair:** add the identifier at first use ("Jim Hutton, his partner,
  later dated...") and let the Mary section keep the fuller introduction.
- **Expected benefit:** the diagnosis-timing reveal lands at full weight on first read.
- **Confidence:** high.
- **Acceptance test:** reading the body top-to-bottom, every occurrence of "Jim Hutton"
  after the first is preceded by an identifying clause.

### UNFAMILIAR-C2 (concern) — Roger Taylor, Brian May, and Peter Straker are never identified

- **Passages:** "with Roger Taylor and Peter Straker beside him in drag" (Scene by Scene);
  "Roger Taylor called the trip a mistake" (Munich); "Brian May doubted it was physically
  possible" (The Silence); "Roger Taylor recalled the speech to Rolling Stone" (The
  Silence).
- **Reader effect:** I never learned that Taylor is Queen's drummer or May its guitarist;
  "he gathered the other three" let me infer band membership only by The Silence, three
  sections after Taylor's first appearance. Peter Straker remains a complete unknown — I
  briefly wondered if he was staff. Each unidentified name is a small comprehension toll,
  and they compound.
- **Evidence:** first-read experience; the draft nowhere carries a role appositive for any
  of the three (verified by re-scanning the snapshot).
- **Minimum viable repair:** one appositive each at first use: "Queen's drummer Roger
  Taylor," "his friend the singer Peter Straker," "Brian May, Queen's guitarist."
- **Expected benefit:** the band materializes as people, which also quietly strengthens the
  article's own "stayed in one band for 20 years" typing complication.
- **Confidence:** high.
- **Acceptance test:** every named person in the body carries an identifying clause at first
  use.

### UNFAMILIAR-C3 (concern) — "the movie" is never named

- **Passage:** "The facts first, for anyone who arrived through the movie."
- **Reader effect:** a reader who hasn't seen or heard of the biopic hits a puzzled beat in
  the one sentence explicitly addressed to the least-informed readers. It also wastes a free
  orientation asset: naming the film gives movie-arrivals a handhold and non-movie readers a
  fact.
- **Evidence:** first-read experience; the snapshot contains no other mention of the film.
- **Minimum viable repair:** "for anyone who arrived through the 2018 biopic _Bohemian
  Rhapsody_" (title verifiable in seconds by the editor; not packet-covered).
- **Expected benefit:** the orientation paragraph orients everyone it addresses.
- **Confidence:** high.
- **Acceptance test:** a reader who has never heard of the film can parse the sentence
  without inferring missing context.

### UNFAMILIAR-C4 (concern) — Enneagram notation deployed before it is glossed

- **Passage:** diagnosis section: "an Enneagram Type 4 with a 3 wing... The type databases
  split between 3w4, 4, and 7w6, and none argue from testimony."
- **Reader effect:** "3 wing," "3w4," "7w6," and "type databases" arrive within two
  sentences, none defined; the system itself ("the Enneagram" as a nine-type map of core
  motivation) is never framed in the body. The behavioral writing that follows is excellent
  and jargon-free — which is why I kept reading — but this is the piece's highest-friction
  moment and its most plausible abandonment point for a newcomer deciding whether the
  article is "for them."
- **Evidence:** first-read experience; the wing concept is only explained inside the
  explicitly skippable Rabbit Hole; the link to /enneagram-corner/enneagram-type-4 is an
  exit, not an explanation.
- **Minimum viable repair:** one appositive clause at first use (e.g., "the Enneagram, a
  nine-type map of core motivation —" before "Type 4"), plus "personality-typing databases"
  for "type databases." The notation then decodes itself ("with a 3 wing" → 4w3).
- **Expected benefit:** the article's stated ambition — settling the personality question
  for people who arrive from search — works for readers who have never seen the notation.
- **Confidence:** high for the gap; medium for how many readers it actually sheds (the
  surrounding prose does recover most of them).
- **Acceptance test:** a reader with zero Enneagram exposure can read the diagnosis
  section's first two paragraphs without clicking a link or skipping notation they can't
  parse.

### UNFAMILIAR-C5 (concern) — Montreux is never explained

- **Passages:** "his estate is throwing the party in Montreux" (intro); "The Great
  Pretender's Gala, at the Casino Barrière in Montreux... The statue by Lake Geneva" (ending).
- **Reader effect:** every scene in the article happens in Zanzibar, India, London, or
  Munich; then the commemoration — the article's "why now" and its closing image — sits in a
  Swiss lakeside town with no stated connection to him. I finished the article still
  carrying the question, which slightly blurs the ending's otherwise clean geography (public
  statue there, secret ashes somewhere).
- **Evidence:** first-read experience; the packet verifies the gala facts (CLM-17) but
  records no Montreux-connection context anywhere in the draft.
- **Minimum viable repair:** one clause at either mention explaining his Montreux tie (the
  town's role in Queen's recording life / why the statue stands there) — the fact itself
  needs a citable source (the official gala announcement, packet S10, is the natural place
  to look); the draft should not assert it unsourced.
- **Expected benefit:** the ending's statue-versus-ashes contrast closes with no dangling
  "why there?" and the "why now" anchor gains a reason to be where it is.
- **Confidence:** high that the gap exists; the repair depends on a source the editor must
  confirm.
- **Acceptance test:** a reader can say in one sentence why Freddie Mercury's statue and
  birthday gala are in Montreux.

### UNFAMILIAR-C6 (concern) — the invented inner-thought paragraph is not marked as invention

- **Passage:** childhood section: "_Everyone here arrived knowing someone. I arrived knowing
  nobody. Fine. I'll build the boy they'll want to know._"
- **Reader effect:** every other set-off block in the piece is sourced testimony (epigraph,
  pull-quote, source card, block quotes), so on first read I parsed this as a quotation and
  looked for the attribution. Discovering it was authorial imagination produced a small
  trust wobble — the article's discipline elsewhere is what makes the unmarked invention
  conspicuous. It also brushes the role contract's "overconfident before research supplies
  context" test: it scripts a real eight-year-old's private thoughts.
- **Evidence:** first-read experience; the packet's first-person evidence inventory contains
  nothing resembling it, confirming it is invention rather than paraphrase.
- **Minimum viable repair:** either confirm the rendered `.inner-thought` styling visibly
  distinguishes imagination from quotation on the live template, or add a two-word framing
  cue in the preceding sentence (e.g., "Imagine the arithmetic:").
- **Expected benefit:** the device keeps its emotional value without spending sourcing
  credibility.
- **Confidence:** medium (depends on how the class renders on-site, which this review could
  not see).
- **Acceptance test:** a first-time reader shown the rendered paragraph identifies it as the
  writer's imagination, not a quotation, without being asked.
