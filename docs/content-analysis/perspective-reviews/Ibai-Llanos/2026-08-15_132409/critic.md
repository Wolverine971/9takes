## Bottom-line verdict

The article survives the strongest skeptical reading: calm can be a business strategy, generosity can be brand safety, and multi-platform distribution can be commercial optimization. It acknowledges those incentives and answers them with documented private costs and conflict behavior rather than simply asserting sincerity.

## What landed

The Agüero section explicitly states the skeptic's case before testing it. The countertyping section names Type 7 as serious and admits that deliberate record escalation is not naturally predicted by a pure Type 9 account.

## What missed

The sentence “Nobody chooses that price as a marketing strategy” is rhetorically stronger than the evidence warrants; health consequences do not by themselves prove motive. Arrow claims also compress complex anxiety and goal-setting into typology.

## What I expected

I expected commercial incentives, contradictory evidence, and falsifiers. All are present, though some theory language remains more confident than the evidence packet.

## What surprised me

The article uses the CPAP detail as evidence of adaptation to discomfort rather than using body size itself as a personality shortcut.

## Red flags

No P0 blocker. The rhetorical overstatement is local and does not invalidate the evidence-led diagnosis or create a factual safety issue.

## Specific improvements

At maintenance, recast the marketing-price sentence as an inference and soften the arrow section. Publication can proceed because the central thesis does not depend on either absolute formulation.

## Follow-on questions
