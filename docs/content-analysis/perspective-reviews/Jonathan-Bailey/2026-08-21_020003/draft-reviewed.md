> "One thing that we're all born with is the sense of longing. If you long for someone, more often than not you don't think you are worthy of it."
> — Jonathan Bailey, Interview magazine, 2024

<p class="firstLetter">Jonathan Bailey was thirteen, in a leotard, and he had brought a friend to watch.</p>

The plan was a flex. A school friend was staying for the weekend, and before whatever they were actually going to do, Bailey wanted to swing by ballet class. His pitch, as he told CBS Sunday Morning in 2024: "I've just got to pop in and just take one photo with so many girls. It's going to be amazing. You're going to love it."

He had been, by his own count, the only boy in a leotard for fifty miles, and he had never once minded. Then he looked up and found his friend's eyes across the room.

He stopped dancing that year.

Two decades later, Jon M. Chu cast him as Fiyero in _Wicked_. Of that first meeting with his director he told CBS in 2024: "we talked a lot about actually growing up and about dance and what it meant to play a character who literally dances through life now."

He is thirty-eight now. To most of the people reading this he is Anthony Bridgerton, the eldest brother on the biggest costume drama on Netflix, a man whose entire appeal is restraint failing in public. By at least one official measure he is also the most desirable man on the planet. People named him its 2025 Sexiest Man Alive, a title NBC News and CBS News both reported as the first given to an openly gay man. He is the global face of Giorgio Armani's _I Will_, the brand's first new men's fragrance pillar in more than twenty years. Since June he has been The MARTINI Man, hired to sell _sprezzatura_, the Italian art of looking like you never practised.

He practised. Ballet, piano, clarinet, three seasons at the Royal Shakespeare Company before he was a teenager, a university place turned down at fifteen. The effortlessness is a manufactured good and he is the only one who knows the hours.

And he will not tell you who he loves.

## What is Jonathan Bailey's personality type?

### Jonathan Bailey is an Enneagram Type 3

On the Enneagram he reads as a Type 3, the Achiever, most likely a 3w2 with a social-dominant instinct. The type carries one specific injury: the self keeps getting edited to match whatever the room reflects back.

Achievement is the visible symptom of [Type 3](/enneagram-corner/enneagram-type-3). What runs it is a nervous system permanently tuned to how a room is reading you.

Bailey has described that mechanism twice, unprompted, without any idea he was describing a personality pattern.

The first time was the dance studio. His account of it, given to CBS Sunday Morning in 2024, is unusually precise about cause: "I just suddenly became aware of a story and a narrative that was put on something that I was so passionate about." Not that ballet became less fun. That a story arrived from outside and won.

The second was his twenties, closeted, working. "It was like a Tupperware box that had been locked down," he told Attitude in 2020. "It was affecting relationships, friendships, romantic relationships, because I felt like I was withdrawing." Then the line that matters most: "To be able to identify emotion seems to me completely parallel to being honest and authentic." He is describing a period where he could not locate his own feelings. Threes reliably report exactly this, because feelings were replaced early by a performance that worked better.

What settles it is the speed. He did not argue with the reading or wait to see whether the feeling passed. He took one glance as data and amputated inside a second.

Confidence on the core type: high. The evidence is behavioural, sourced, and repeated across three decades. His wing and his dominant instinct are a harder call, and neither one is settled here.

## Jonathan Bailey's Childhood and the Crying Raindrop

Three decades is not a figure of speech here.

His grandmother took him to _Oliver!_ in the West End when he was five. He came home and said that was what he wanted to do. She is ninety-five now, and he has checked the story with her, because he does not quite believe it either.

His first stage role came around the same time, at his village primary school: a raindrop in _Noah's Ark_. He had choreography. The boy playing Noah missed the cue for the rain, so the rain never fell, so the dance never happened. Bailey cried. At the end, once the applause had finished and the parents were reaching for their coats, they let him do it.

He tells this as a comic bit. "A crying raindrop in Noah's Ark," he told Phoebe Waller-Bridge in 2024. "It didn't rain. The bitch that played Noah forgot the cue." He has also described the version of himself who wept over it as "about five going on twenty-nine."

Within a year, the Royal Shakespeare Company had scouted him, and at six he faced a choice between the RSC at the Barbican and playing Nipper in the very show that started it. He took the RSC. He had three seasons there before he was a teenager, and his parents refused to treat any of it as remarkable: "I didn't miss a day of school. My parents were like, get back, tables first."

That household is worth pausing on. He is the youngest of four and the only boy, raised in what he has called a cooperative of four brilliant women and a father with an incredible work ethic. His sisters, he says, "were like my heroes growing up." He wore a head brace for two years, which he remembers as "awkward, and painful actually." He describes himself as "really enthusiastic, probably quite annoying, hard to calm down."

Then comes the line that carries the rest of his life: "I had the same passions as I did when I was sort of five. So I sort of knew who I was." He puts an expiry date on that certainty in a separate interview with British GQ: "I remember at primary school being really clear and then it sort of gets murky as you get older."

Something happened between five and thirty that made a very clear boy murky.

## Three Times Jonathan Bailey Read the Room and Made a Decision

He has spent his working life responding to cues. The first one he ever waited for did not come. Here are three that did.

### Thirteen: the friend in the doorway

_Cue: a school friend looks across a dance studio._

"I just remember clocking his eyes across the room and it was that one moment in me that I went, oh, this isn't right, this isn't actually the right thing I should be doing."

Nobody said anything. No incident, no confrontation, nothing to point at afterwards. He had chosen those classes over basketball, and one silent reading of himself was enough to overturn it. Then he removed the evidence.

<p class="inner-thought">He is seeing what I look like. He is right about what I look like. I did not know until now.</p>

### Thirty: the calls that went to his agent

_Cue: casting directors and publicists telephone his representation._

"Oh, no, you can't come out. You shouldn't really do that."

The people advising silence were, he has said, mostly other gay men in the industry, some of them casting directors who rang his agent to make the point. He described the going rate plainly: "They want you to be gay, but not too gay." This time the reading arrived loudly, from professionals, with a career attached to it. He came out publicly in 2018, the year he turned thirty. Five years later, with _Bridgerton_ making him the most bookable romantic lead on television, he took the role the advice had been built to prevent: Tim Laughlin in _Fellow Travelers_, a devout young Senate staffer working through the McCarthy-era purge of gay employees while in love with a man neither of them can name out loud, opposite Matt Bomer, across eight hours of premium television. He played the Tupperware box and got an Emmy nomination for it.

### Thirty-seven: the backflip he didn't do

_Cue: a magazine asks the world's sexiest man for a party trick._

"I would hate for my back flip to go wrong and it be on TikTok. So I'm just slightly more cautious."

He can still land one, mostly. He said so in the same breath, and then explained the physics of why it sometimes gets away from him, and then noted that as long as your hands hit the floor the crowd goes wild anyway. Twenty-four years after the leotard, the calculation still runs in real time and out loud. He can do the thing. He is pricing what a failed version would look like to strangers.

## Why Jonathan Bailey Will Not Say Who He Is Dating

The same calculation runs on much bigger things than a backflip.

The honest answer to the search query is that it is not publicly established, and the record is openly contradictory. In December 2023 he told the Evening Standard he was seeing "a lovely man" and declined to name him. In July 2025, closing out an episode of _Chicken Shop Date_ with [Amelia Dimoldenberg](/personality-analysis/amelia-dimoldenberg), he said he was available. Both statements are on the record. Neither has been reconciled, and the widely circulated name attached to him for years traces back to a single 2019 awards-show moment and has never been confirmed by him. In that same Evening Standard conversation he had already drawn the distinction he has held ever since, describing the life he keeps back as "private" rather than "secret."

Some people find the whole posture convenient, and they have a point worth stating at full strength. Ambiguity is commercially useful for a romantic lead. It is useful for a fragrance ambassador and it is extremely useful for a Sexiest Man Alive. He has never had to prove he would protect the privacy at a cost to himself, because so far it has cost him nothing. Last November, after a viral press clip with his _Wicked_ co-star [Ariana Grande](/personality-analysis/ariana-grande), a chunk of the internet spent a week speculating about his sexuality anyway, which is roughly what happens when a public figure leaves a shape unfilled.

Here is the part the judgment misses. For the image type, being read by other people is not a social inconvenience. It is the mechanism by which the self gets overwritten. Bailey has one documented, catastrophic experience of it, at thirteen, and a second slower one through his twenties that he describes as a sealed container and a withdrawal from everyone who loved him. When he says a private life is "completely critical," he gives a reason that is operational rather than defensive: "I don't know if I would be able to be as confident to speak out on other things if I felt that my whole life was up for grabs." The fenced room is what pays for the speaking out. Remove it and the advocacy goes with it.

He is not shy about the rest of it. Phoebe Waller-Bridge, who cast him in _Crashing_ and has watched the effect land on audiences for a decade, told him to his face in 2024: "You're like a chemistry machine. There's this incredible erotic energy that people are so excited about." Matt Bomer, who filmed eight episodes of one of the most explicit gay love stories on premium television opposite him, said of the People cover: "I'm glad the world has finally woken up to what we've all known for years, and I could not be more proud and happy for Johnny. I love him dearly, and he is more than deserving of this title. It's long overdue."

He will give you the longing. He has built an entire career out of manufacturing it on demand. The one thing he keeps is the fact of who it is actually for.

## What Critics Said About Jonathan Bailey's Richard II

The fence shows up in the work too.

In February 2025, in the gap between the two _Wicked_ films, Bailey went back to a London stage and did 103 performances of _Richard II_ for Nicholas Hytner at the Bridge Theatre. It is the least commercially necessary thing he has ever done.

The reviews split along one seam, and it is the same seam every time. Time Out's Andrzej Lukowski opened with "Jonathan Bailey is a great stage actor," gave the production three stars, and then landed the knife: "the final passages lose considerable poignancy through Richard's triviality." Matt Wolf, writing in The Arts Desk under the headline "handsomely mounted, emotionally muted," also gave three stars, granted that "Bailey, an infinitely warmer performer, certainly has the toolkit to chart the character's acquaintanceship with humility," and then asked the only question that counts in that play: "will you weep for this Richard? As of my recent visit, I'd say no."

They are describing a real thing. The charm arrives on time, every night, at full wattage. The grief takes longer to get there.

Which is what you would expect from an instrument built this way. A performer trained from the age of five to read a house and give it the thing that lands has one blind spot, and it is precisely the register that cannot be calibrated to a house. Charm is a transaction with a room. Sorrow is not, and neither is love, which is the same border he polices offstage: the room gets what can be given to a room, and the rest stays behind the fence. He has said his method starts with a character's want, that "longing comes before anything else," which is a beautiful way to build a person and also a way of approaching grief from the outside, through appetite, through what someone is reaching for. Wolf himself allowed that the depth might arrive later in the run. That is the whole question about Bailey in one clause: the toolkit is not in doubt, the arrival time is.

## Why Jonathan Bailey Stopped Acting for a Year

Seven months after he opened as Richard, in September 2025, he was cycling between Rome and Pisa on his way to a friend's wedding, on a bad line to a British GQ journalist, and he said this: "I've been working solidly for about three years now, which has been amazing and mind-blowing. But with everything happening in the world right now, I'm going to stop acting for a bit next year and just focus on The Shameless Fund."

It went around the world as Jonathan Bailey quits acting. His sister rang him. "Wow, is everything OK? You've given up?" He spent two months correcting it, finally telling Esquire in November, "I'm not taking the whole of 2026 off." His takeaway from the episode was not indignation: "I learnt at that moment that you can't control it."

The Shameless Fund is a UK grantmaking nonprofit he founded in June 2024, and the reason he gives for it is a single number.

<div class="key-stat">
	<span class="key-stat__number">1p</span>
	<span class="key-stat__label">of every £100 raised in the UK reaches the LGBTQ+ community, by Bailey's own count (British GQ, 2025)</span>
</div>

The figure is his own count rather than a verified statistic, and he repeats it everywhere he goes. He also names his own position in the arrangement without being asked: "I'm fully aware that I'm right at the top of that," he told Out, meaning the privilege, adding, "I'm also a massive benefactor of everyone who came before me."

Then look at what he actually planned to do with the year. No tour. No memoir. No documentary about the work. "Over the next few months, I'll be focusing on staffing and making sure we get all the right people in all the right places." The most bookable man in the industry described his sabbatical as hiring. When People asked what makes him proud, he talked about the people who joined the fund with "expertise way beyond mine," and called the whole thing "twinkly," which is a strange word for a charity and exactly the word a man uses about something he has not yet learned to package.

The commerce got routed the same direction. When MARTINI signed him in June 2026 to embody effortless Italian living, the multi-year deal came with support for the Fund attached. The desirability is being converted into a budget.

<div class="pull-quote">
"The opposite of shame is freedom, right?"
</div>

He said that to Out in November 2025, and it explains the name over the door. He has watched shame do its work up close, in an industry where the advice came from people who had already lost the argument with themselves, and he has been clear about the stakes: "it can literally be a matter of life and death."

## What Is Jonathan Bailey Doing Next? Sondheim at the Barbican

The year of hiring has a date at the far end of it.

In summer 2027 he goes back to the Barbican to play Georges in _Sunday in the Park with George_, directed by Marianne Elliott, who cast him as the gender-swapped Jamie in _Company_ in 2018 and handed him the Olivier that followed.

The venue is not incidental. The Barbican is where the Royal Shakespeare Company put him on a professional stage at seven, in _A Christmas Carol_, after he turned down _Oliver!_ to get there. Thirty-two years later he returns to sing Sondheim's musical about a painter so absorbed in the work that the woman posing for it leaves him.

Then his co-star withdrew. On 2 August 2026, Ariana Grande, cast as Dot, pulled out through a representative who told People she "will be taking a step back from visibility" once the Eternal Sunshine tour ends, citing a break from public-facing work and "endless, ongoing public scrutiny." Empire Street Productions said the decision came with their complete understanding. The show opens as planned. Bailey is still Georges.

The two of them spent the same year being looked at more than almost anyone alive, on the same press tour, for the same film. When the Barbican came round, one of them stepped back from visibility and the other kept the date. Bailey built his fence at thirteen, out of a leotard and a look, and it is the reason he can still stand in the light without it costing him the thing behind it.

### Jonathan Bailey's Wing: 3w2

The 3w2 gets where it is going by way of other people, and counts being liked as part of the score. Bailey's stated acting method is close to a 2 creed: "don't judge your character," because "if you fully love someone the judgment falls out the window." Asked what matters in a relationship he named cheerleading and "watching someone thrive." He was raised by three older sisters he calls his heroes, he founded a fund rather than a production company, and his default public register is high-fives and self-deprecation rather than mystique.

The 3w4 case is real and should be conceded. He keeps going back to Sondheim, twice now for Marianne Elliott, he built a whole theory of character out of longing, he found "a dull ache" in Fiyero, and _Fellow Travelers_ is not a role a purely social operator hunts down. Best read: a 3w2 with a working 4 <a href="/enneagram-corner/enneagram-wings-complete-guide">wing</a> he uses at work and leaves at the stage door.

### Jonathan Bailey's Instinctual Subtype: social-dominant

The social 3 chases standing inside a group and converts personal prestige into group position. Bailey thinks at sector scale: LGBTQ+ funding as a category, visibility as filling spaces he did not have, brand partnerships engineered to spill into a grant fund. That is <a href="/enneagram-corner/enneagram-instinctual-subtypes">social instinct</a> operating in public.

This is the least certain call in the analysis. The sexual 3 wins through one-to-one presence, and Bailey's entire filmography is chemistry, which is why most of the internet reads him sexual-first. Both instincts are clearly loaded. The tiebreak is that when he was handed the single largest one-to-one validation available to a man in his industry, he called it "completely absurd" and told his dog.

### Stress and Growth Arrows

Threes disintegrate toward 9: disengagement, numbness, going quietly missing. His account of his closeted twenties is that arrow in his own words: a sealed box, a withdrawal from the people closest to him, and no reliable access to what he was feeling. He also describes the pattern in miniature in the present tense: confidence, then "a deep spike of fear and self-doubt."

Threes integrate toward 6: loyalty to a group, courage against advice, institution-building instead of self-building. Coming out in 2018 against a chorus of professional counsel is that move. So is spending a year staffing a nonprofit and then deferring to the people he hired.

### Counterarguments: Why Jonathan Bailey Might Not Be Type 3

The prevailing internet reading is 7w8, and the supporting evidence is not nothing. He calls himself "hard to calm down." He signs up for things compulsively and finishes almost none of them. He has done Everest Base Camp, marathons, triathlons, mountaineering and gymnastics. Those are Seven appetites, and the Three read has no use for any of them.

What sinks the Seven read is the shape of his worst moments. A Seven under threat escapes sideways into a new option. Bailey's defining response to threat was self-amputation at thirteen, and his documented low period reads as numbness rather than mania. A Type 2 read is also available given the warmth and the philanthropy, and it fails for a simpler reason: Twos build identity around being needed, and nothing in his record suggests he needs to be needed. He needs the story about him to be one he wrote.

The falsifier: find one occasion where he abandoned finished work because something newer looked more fun, or show that the privacy guards his options rather than his story. Neither is in the public record.

## Jonathan Bailey Is Still Doing the Dance After the Applause

He is on screen this year regardless of the quitting headlines. Anthony sat out the first half of _Bridgerton_'s fourth season in January and returned in February, and the scheduling behind that absence is the argument in miniature: while season four was shooting, the most bookable romantic lead on Netflix was off making _Jurassic World Rebirth_ and doing Shakespeare at the Bridge. Armani is selling a bottle with his face on it and a promise for a name, a project Giorgio Armani personally approved before his death. He has spent his quietest year on record doing the least photogenic work available to a movie star, which is hiring.

A Three learns early that the applause is the proof. What Bailey learned, at five, in a school hall, is that sometimes you do the dance after it stops and there is nobody left to clap.
