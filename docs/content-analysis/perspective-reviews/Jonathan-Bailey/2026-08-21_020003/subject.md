## Bottom-line verdict

Judged on the question this role exists to ask — not "is the typing right" but "has this man been
represented fairly" — the draft is better than most celebrity psychological writing and fails in
three specific, repairable places.

What it gets right is unusual. It refuses to name a partner. It answers the relationship question
once, correctly, as not publicly established, reports both contradictory data points, and adjudicates
neither. It states the most cynical available read of him at full strength before reframing it, and
the reframe does not exonerate. It carries two adverse critics in their own words rather than
paraphrasing the sting out of them. It lets him state his own privilege in his own sentence. It ends
on his curiosity rather than his injury. Those are the choices a subject would notice.

The failures are not tonal. They are three places where the piece asserts something about him that
the record does not support:

1. The epigraph — the first thing any reader sees — takes a passage in which Bailey is explaining
   **how he builds characters** and, by silently deleting the sentences that say so, re-presents it as
   a personal admission that he does not feel worthy of being loved. The draft knows the passage is
   about craft; it quotes the deleted sentence correctly as craft later in the same article.
2. An H2 asserts he "Stopped Acting for a Year." That is the exact formulation Bailey publicly and
   specifically identified as **"an unfortunate misquote"** — and the draft's own body, three
   paragraphs down, reports him correcting it. The article headlines the misquote and debunks it in
   the same section.
3. The article's organizing premise — "he will not tell you who he loves," "the fact of who it is
   actually for" — presupposes a concealed person. The same article says his status is not publicly
   established and quotes him saying "I'm available." If he is single, the piece has converted an
   absence into a concealment and built his profile on it.

None of the three is a hard rewrite. Each is a sentence-level repair. But all three run in the same
direction: they make him slightly more hidden, slightly more wounded, and slightly more knowable than
his own record permits. That is the specific failure mode this perspective is built to catch, so the
verdict is revise rather than pass — and trust is strained rather than broken, because everywhere
else the piece treats him as a whole person with a career, a sense of humour, and reasons of his own.

## What landed

**SUBJ-H1 — The relationship section refuses to adjudicate, and says so.**

> "The honest answer to the search query is that it is not publicly established, and the record is
> openly contradictory."

This is the single most consequential fairness decision in the piece and it is made correctly. Both
data points are named, neither is resolved, no partner is named, and the widely circulated tabloid
link is characterised as unconfirmed rather than repeated. A subject reading a profile of himself
would check this paragraph first. It must survive revision unaltered.

**SUBJ-H2 — The cynical case is stated at full strength before it is answered.**

> "Some people find the whole posture convenient, and they have a point worth stating at full
> strength. Ambiguity is commercially useful for a romantic lead. It is useful for a fragrance
> ambassador and it is extremely useful for a Sexiest Man Alive."

Steelmanning the case against your subject and then declining to demolish it is rare, and it is the
thing that makes the following reframe credible rather than promotional. The reframe also runs
through his own stated reason — "I don't know if I would be able to be as confident to speak out on
other things if I felt that my whole life was up for grabs" — rather than through the writer's
psychology. Preserve the whole two-paragraph movement.

**SUBJ-H3 — Criticism is carried in the critics' words, with the critic's own hedge intact.**

> "will you weep for this Richard? As of my recent visit, I'd say no."

and

> "Wolf himself allowed that the depth might arrive later in the run. That is the whole question
> about Bailey in one clause: the toolkit is not in doubt, the arrival time is."

The section does not euphemise a mixed reception, does not cluster it where it cannot bite, and does
not turn the psychological reading into an excuse. It explains a limitation without denying it, and
it keeps the reviewer's own qualifier — which is more care than the reviewer's editor gave it. It
also respects his craft, which matters: the piece treats him as a working actor with a technique,
not as a specimen.

**SUBJ-H4 — He states his own privilege, in his own sentence.**

> "I'm fully aware that I'm right at the top of that," he told Out, meaning the privilege, adding,
> "I'm also a massive benefactor of everyone who came before me."

The writer does not perform this observation on his behalf. Letting the subject say the
uncomfortable thing himself, unprompted, is the difference between reporting and positioning.

**SUBJ-H5 — The close ends on curiosity rather than on the wound.**

> "Ask him what he still wants and he will tell you, cheerfully, unprompted, in the middle of an
> interview about being the sexiest man on earth, that he wants to know what happens with volcanoes."

After four thousand words of reading a man through an injury, the piece ends by letting him want
something ordinary and unphotographable. This is the passage that most protects his dignity, and it
is the one place the article stops interpreting him. It must survive.

**SUBJ-H6 — The type claim is offered with an exit.**

> "The falsifier: find one occasion where he abandoned finished work because something newer looked
> more fun, or show that the privacy guards his options rather than his story."

Plus the conceded 3w4 case, the conceded sexual-instinct alternative, and "his wing and his dominant
instinct are a harder call, and neither one is settled here." A subject who rejects the typing is at
least told, in the article itself, what would defeat it. That is the minimum condition for typing a
living person who never asked to be typed, and the draft meets it.

**SUBJ-H7 — His own vocabulary carries most of the argument.**

Tupperware box, masking, murky, twinkly, "a deep spike of fear and self-doubt," "the bitch that
played Noah forgot the cue." He is recognisable in his own diction, including the jokes. The comic
register survives the psychological reading, which is what keeps him a person rather than a case.

## What missed

**SUBJ-M1 — The TL;DR renders his life as a list of injuries.**

Five of six bullets are wounds or artifacts of wounds: the leotard, the raindrop, the Tupperware box,
the 1p, the backflip he will not do. Only the sixth ("What the type misses") contains anything he
enjoys. A subject scanning the summary box — the element most likely to be screenshotted and shared
without the article — sees his life reduced to six things that went wrong. The article body is more
generous than its own abstract.

**SUBJ-M2 — The mitigating half of his own sentence is missing from the founding scene.**

In the same breath as "I actually decided not to continue with the dance," Bailey says "but I was
acting and singing." The draft's "He stopped dancing that year" and its later "self-amputation at
thirteen" both omit the clause that turns a total severance into a narrowing. The piece's most
serious psychological claim about him would read differently with six of his own words restored.
Cross-referenced at SUBJ-C10.

**SUBJ-M3 — The one time he corrected the record about himself, the piece repeats the error in a
heading.** Detailed at SUBJ-R2. This is the miss a subject would find hardest to read past, because
it is the one instance in the whole file of him saying, on the record, "that is not what I said."

**SUBJ-M4 — Nothing in the main narrative shows him enjoying anything without it being read as
performance.** Everest, marathons, triathlons, mountaineering, gymnastics and the nine courses all
live in the TL;DR and the Rabbit Hole — the two containers a general reader is least likely to read.
Every scene in the body is metabolised into the thesis. The result is a man who is never simply
having a good time, which the record does not support and which the article's own counterevidence
section quietly contradicts.

## What I expected

**SUBJ-E1 — His own sentence about privacy, not two words extracted from it.**

He said: "It's not secret, but it's private." The draft deliberately carries only the words "private"
and "secret" because the full sentence trips an internal lint gate on contrast constructions. That is
a tooling constraint overriding the subject's sharpest self-description of his own boundary — the one
line in the record where he defines the thing this article is largely about. If a house style rule
cannot accommodate a subject's own defining sentence, the sentence should win. (Verbatim quotation is
the standard exemption everywhere else in the draft; the epigraph is kept past the same gate on
exactly that reasoning.)

**SUBJ-E2 — A visible statement that the childhood scenes have one witness.**

The raindrop, the RSC/Nipper choice, the dance studio, the head brace and the Open University
expulsion are attested only by Bailey, decades later, in promotional interviews. Nothing contradicts
them. But the entire psychological argument descends from them, and the piece presents them with the
confidence of documented events. One clause — that these are his own retellings — would cost nothing
and would let the reader calibrate. A subject is entitled to have his recollections labelled as
recollections, particularly when they are being used to diagnose him.

**SUBJ-E3 — The fuller correction, in his words.**

The draft gives "I'm not taking the whole of 2026 off." He also said "My intention was to say, 'Next
year I'll be back'" and "I'll definitely be back to work next year" (Esquire UK, 9 Nov 2025, via E!
News). The draft carries the smallest available version of his correction while the surrounding
section is built on the framing he was correcting.

**SUBJ-E4 — Some acknowledgement that "not publicly established" cuts both ways.**

The piece treats the unresolved status as withholding. The record equally supports "there is nothing
current to disclose." The article never entertains the second possibility, and its thesis cannot
survive it. See SUBJ-R3.

## What surprised me

**Welcome.** That the piece concedes the commercial convenience of his ambiguity and then does not
use the psychological reading to clear him of it. "He has never had to prove he would protect the
privacy at a cost to himself" is a sentence most profiles of a beloved subject would not write. (It
has a separate problem — SUBJ-C4 — but the willingness is the right instinct.)

**Welcome.** That the Rabbit Hole concedes the prevailing internet read of him is 7w8 and gives its
supporting evidence honestly — "Those are Seven appetites, and the Three read has no use for any of
them" — instead of pretending the alternative is weak.

**Welcome.** The volcanoes ending. It refuses the closing flourish the whole piece has been building
toward and lands on something small and true instead.

**Jarring.** That the article quotes the same Interview magazine passage twice, correctly identifying
it as his acting method in the Richard II section ("He has said his method starts with a character's
want, that 'longing comes before anything else'") while presenting a cut-down version of the very
same passage as a personal confession in the epigraph. The piece demonstrably knows what the quote
is. That makes the epigraph harder to read as an oversight.

**Jarring.** That a section titled "Why Jonathan Bailey Stopped Acting for a Year" contains, three
paragraphs later, the reporting that proves the title wrong.

## Red flags

### Blockers

**SUBJ-R1 — The epigraph strips the frame that makes it a statement about acting, and re-presents it
as a confession about his own worth. (Blocker)**

- **Passage:** the epigraph, lines 88–89, above the cold open:
  > "One thing that we're all born with is the sense of longing. If you long for someone, more often
  > than not you don't think you are worthy of it."
  > — Jonathan Bailey, Interview magazine, 2024
- **What he actually said**, in the same continuous passage, answering Phoebe Waller-Bridge on how he
  finds a way into a character: _"One thing that we're all born with is the sense of longing. Longing
  comes before anything else, doesn't it? Whoever you put on the wall, laminate the poster or
  whatever, it's there. And actually, if you long for someone, more often than not you don't think
  you are worthy of it. **And that, to me, is a way into characters.**"_
- **Reader effect / trust problem:** the draft deletes three intervening clauses and the closing
  sentence — the sentence that identifies the whole observation as a professional technique — joins
  the two survivors without an ellipsis, and capitalises "if" so the seam disappears. Mounted above
  an article arguing that this man hides who he loves, the surviving fragment reads as Bailey
  admitting he does not believe he is worthy of love. That is not a compression, it is a change of
  genre: craft talk presented as unguarded self-disclosure. It is also the first thing every reader
  sees, and it is attributed to him by name and outlet.
- **Evidence:** full passage read this pass at `jbaileyfansite.tumblr.com` (the same reproduction the
  evidence packet used as S-05, read at greater length than CLM-01 records — the packet identifies
  one missing sentence; there are three plus the closing frame). The craft framing is independently
  corroborated by coverage of the same interview summarising it as Bailey answering how he decides
  what makes a character tick. `interviewmagazine.com` returned HTTP 403 to direct fetch this pass,
  as the packet records.
- **Minimum viable repair:** either (a) restore the passage with the closing clause and mark every
  omission with an ellipsis, accepting that it now reads as a statement about acting; or (b) keep the
  epigraph slot and use a line he said about himself — "I remember at primary school being really
  clear and then it sort of gets murky as you get older" (British GQ) does the same structural work
  without reassigning a professional remark to his private life. Do not simply add an ellipsis and
  leave the craft frame deleted; the missing frame, not the missing words, is the fairness problem.
- **Expected benefit:** removes the piece's most prominent misrepresentation of the subject's own
  speech, and removes the internal inconsistency with the Richard II section.
- **Confidence:** high on the omission and the craft framing; medium on canonical wording, since the
  primary is inaccessible and the reproduction is Tier 4. Note that under either wording the silent
  deletion still needs marking.
- **Acceptance test:** place the draft's epigraph beside the reproduction's full passage. Every
  omitted word is marked with an ellipsis, no capitalisation is silently altered, and either the "way
  into characters" clause is present or the quotation is no longer used as an epigraph implying a
  statement about his own worth. Additionally: the FORMULA FINGERPRINT ledger's claim that the
  epigraph is verbatim is corrected, since the contrast-pair waiver currently rests on it.

**SUBJ-R2 — An H2 asserts as fact the exact claim Bailey publicly identified as a misquote, and the
section's own body reports the correction. (Blocker)**

- **Passage:** heading, line 225: `## Why Jonathan Bailey Stopped Acting for a Year`. Reinforced by
  the TL;DR ("sent the world's most bookable actor into a year of hiring staff"), by "The year of
  hiring has a date at the far end of it," and by "He has spent his quietest year on record."
- **What he actually said:** "I then did an interview with a magazine when I was cycling between Rome
  and Pisa on the way to a friend's wedding, and the internet kept cutting out. As a result, there
  was **an unfortunate misquote that said I'd stopped acting for a bit**." / "My intention was to say,
  'Next year I'll be back.'" / "I'm not taking the whole of 2026 off." / "I'll definitely be back to
  work next year." (Esquire UK, 9 Nov 2025, read via E! News this pass.)
- **Reader effect / trust problem:** this is the one documented occasion in the entire record of
  Bailey correcting a characterisation of himself, and the article reproduces the mischaracterisation
  in a heading — escalating "for a bit" to "for a Year." The body then reports him correcting it
  ("He spent two months correcting it"), so the piece contradicts its own headline and leaves the
  false version in the position readers scan, share and quote. The draft also presents the disputed
  GQ sentence as his direct speech — "he said this: 'I've been working solidly for about three years
  now…'" — while using the bad phone line as scene-setting colour rather than as the reason the quote
  needs attribution care. He says the connection cutting out is what produced the misquote.
- **Minimum viable repair:** retitle to something that serves the same query without asserting the
  denied claim — e.g. "Did Jonathan Bailey Quit Acting? What the Year Off Was Actually For." Add the
  words "he says he was misquoted" (or his own "unfortunate misquote" phrasing) to the sentence that
  introduces the GQ line, and add "I'll definitely be back to work next year" to his correction. Fix
  the TL;DR bullet and the "quietest year on record" claim to match.
- **Expected benefit:** the piece stops repeating the one error its subject went on the record to
  correct, and stops asserting an unverified 2026 fact — no 2026 first-person source exists, per the
  packet's own limitation 5.
- **Confidence:** high. The correction is verified verbatim this pass and is corroborated across
  Esquire UK, E! News, People and AOL syndication.
- **Acceptance test:** search the rendered draft for the string "stopped acting for a year" and any
  paraphrase asserting a completed year away from acting. Zero hits outside a passage that attributes
  it to the misreporting. The heading still contains a recognisable form of the search query.

**SUBJ-R3 — The article's premise presupposes a concealed partner the record does not establish.
(Blocker)**

- **Passages:** cold open, line 105: "And he will not tell you who he loves." Heading, line 195:
  "Why Jonathan Bailey Will Not Say Who He Is Dating." Section close, line 207: "The one thing he
  keeps is the fact of who it is actually for."
- **Reader effect / trust problem:** all three sentences require a specific person to exist and be
  hidden. The same section states his status is "not publicly established," reports his December 2023
  "a lovely man" and his July 2025 "I'm available," and correctly declines to reconcile them. If the
  later statement is the current one, the article has taken an ordinary absence of a partner and
  rendered it as deliberate concealment — then made that concealment the spine of his personality
  profile and its H2. For a subject, this is the most invasive move available short of naming
  someone: it insists there is a secret, and the article's whole thesis depends on the insistence.
  It also puts the draft at odds with its own stated discipline in the DISTRIBUTION and
  relationship-status ledgers.
- **Evidence:** internal contradiction between line 105/207 and line 199; packet CLM-03 and Disputes
  item 7 ("The relationship contradiction is not resolvable and must not be resolved").
- **Minimum viable repair:** move the withheld object from a person to the private life. "And he will
  not tell you who he loves" → "And he will not tell you about his private life" (or "…who he loves,
  or whether there is anyone to name"). "The fact of who it is actually for" → "the fact of who it is
  for, if it is for anyone." Retitle the H2 to the question readers actually type without asserting
  the answer — e.g. "Is Jonathan Bailey Dating Anyone? What He Will and Will Not Say."
- **Expected benefit:** the piece keeps its rhetorical engine — a man paid to manufacture desire who
  fences his own — while no longer asserting a private fact about a living person that the public
  record does not support and that the article elsewhere declines to assert.
- **Confidence:** high on the internal contradiction; the underlying fact is by definition unknowable,
  which is the point.
- **Acceptance test:** read every sentence in the draft that refers to his romantic life and ask
  whether it is still true if he is single. No sentence fails. The H2 does not assert that he is
  dating anyone.

### Concerns

**SUBJ-C1 — The invented interior monologue puts appearance-shame in a thirteen-year-old's head that
he never described.**
Line 175: `<p class="inner-thought">He is seeing what I look like. He is right about what I look
like. I did not know until now.</p>` His actual account is "it was that one moment in me that I went,
oh, this isn't right, this isn't actually the right thing I should be doing" — a judgement about the
activity, not about his body. The invented thought converts that into agreement with a shaming read
of his appearance, which is a materially different and more damaging interiority. It renders as an
italic thought-bubble callout (`src/scss/blog.scss:630`) with no label marking it as authorial
invention, sits immediately below a real verbatim quote of his, and is the only such block in the
piece — so a reader has no cue distinguishing it from reported thought. **Repair:** rewrite to stay
inside what he said ("This isn't the right thing for me to be doing. I did not know that until now.")
or drop the middle sentence. **Benefit:** keeps the device without attributing internalised body
shame to a real child. **Confidence:** high. **Acceptance test:** every clause of the inner-thought
block maps to something in the CBS transcript; no clause asserts he agreed with a judgement about how
he looked.

**SUBJ-C2 — "He had never once minded" overwrites what he actually said.**
Line 95: "he had been, by his own count, the only boy in a leotard for fifty miles, and he had never
once minded." His words: "wasn't hyper aware of the fact I was the only boy in, you know, 50 miles
who was wearing a leotard and I loved it." Unawareness is not equanimity, and the substitution
sharpens the before/after reversal the cold open depends on. **Repair:** "and he had never been
especially aware of it" or quote him. **Benefit:** the scene's power survives without improving on
his memory. **Confidence:** high (packet CLM-21). **Acceptance test:** the sentence is defensible
from the transcript without inference.

**SUBJ-C3 — A comic punchline is presented as a relationship-status statement.**
Line 199 places "he said he was available" from _Chicken Shop Date_ beside the Evening Standard
interview as equal-weight evidence: "Both statements are on the record." The format is comic and
flirtatious by construction and the line is delivered as a joke in a bit; the draft gives the reader
no way to know that. Treating it as disclosure is what manufactures the "openly contradictory"
record the section then declines to resolve. **Repair:** one clause naming the register — "closing
out an episode of the comedy interview series _Chicken Shop Date_ … he told Amelia Dimoldenberg,
deadpan, that he was available." **Benefit:** the reader can weigh it; the non-adjudication becomes
honest rather than structural. **Confidence:** high (packet, S-03). **Acceptance test:** a reader who
has never seen the show can tell from the draft that the line is a joke on a comedy show.

**SUBJ-C4 — "It has cost him nothing" asserts an unfalsifiable claim about the private costs of
privacy.**
Line 201: "He has never had to prove he would protect the privacy at a cost to himself, because so
far it has cost him nothing." The costs of guarding a private life are, by construction, private; the
draft states their absence as fact in its own voice. **Repair:** attribute it to the skeptical read
("the case runs that…") or hedge to visible cost ("because no cost has been visible from outside").
**Benefit:** keeps the critique at full strength without the writer claiming knowledge of his
interior ledger. **Confidence:** high. **Acceptance test:** no sentence in the section asserts what
his privacy has or has not cost him personally.

**SUBJ-C5 — Speculation about his sexuality is framed as a consequence of his own choice.**
Line 201: "a chunk of the internet spent a week speculating about his sexuality anyway, which is
roughly what happens when a public figure leaves a shape unfilled." This assigns causal
responsibility to him for other people's speculation about an openly gay man's sexuality. The record
shows he did not participate, and that the larger wave was a backlash calling the speculation
homophobic (Newsweek, Nov 2025). Two factual imprecisions ride along: the clip was an NYT Cooking
appearance, not a press clip, and "a week" is unsourced. **Repair:** cut the causal clause; report the
episode and the backlash. **Benefit:** removes an implicit "he brought it on himself" from a passage
about a gay man's sexuality being litigated by strangers. **Confidence:** high (packet CLM-22, S-20).
**Acceptance test:** the sentence describes what happened without attributing it to a gap he left.

**SUBJ-C6 — A third party's withdrawal is used as the measuring stick for his resilience.**
Line 262: "one of them stepped back from visibility and the other kept the date. Bailey built his
fence at thirteen … and it is the reason he can still stand in the light without it costing him the
thing behind it." This credits Bailey's constitution by contrast with a named peer's decision to step
back from public life under scrutiny — a comparison he never invited and, on his record, would be
unlikely to welcome. It also asserts his present psychological state with no 2026 first-person source
of any kind, and carries only the producer's "complete understanding" line while THR reported the
withdrawal surprised key production members. **Repair:** make the contrast about the situation rather
than the two people's durability, drop the causal "it is the reason," and either note the contested
reporting or drop the amicability characterisation. **Benefit:** keeps the genuinely strong Barbican
bookend without measuring one person's psyche against another's hardest week. **Confidence:** medium-
high (packet CLM-07, S-13, Disputes item 3). **Acceptance test:** the section's closing argument
holds if the reader knows nothing about why the co-star withdrew.

**SUBJ-C7 — His acclaimed performance is read as autobiography.**
Line 183: "He played the Tupperware box and got an Emmy nomination for it." This tells the reader
that his _Fellow Travelers_ performance was him enacting his own closeted years. He has connected
Anthony and Tim through "yearning," and has said his way into characters is longing — he has not said
Tim was him. Collapsing an actor's work into his biography is a specific reduction actors object to,
and it sits oddly beside a piece that elsewhere respects his technique. **Repair:** attribute the
connection or soften to the resemblance ("He took the part of a man who cannot name what he feels").
**Benefit:** credits the craft rather than treating the role as a symptom. **Confidence:** medium-high
(packet CLM-09, S-01). **Acceptance test:** the sentence does not assert he was playing himself
unless he said so.

**SUBJ-C8 — His advocacy statistic is flagged as unverified without the published counter-figure.**
The `key-stat` block (lines 233–236) displays "1p" as the piece's most screenshot-able element, and
the body adds "The figure is his own count rather than a verified statistic, and he repeats it
everywhere he goes." Labelling it is right; the omission is that an independently published UK figure
exists — LGBT+ Consortium / Dr Cat Walker, 27 Mar 2025: 10p in every £100 of voluntary-sector income
— an order of magnitude apart, measuring a different denominator. As written, a reader can only
conclude either that the number is fine or that he inflates it, with no way to tell which; "he repeats
it everywhere he goes" nudges toward the second. **Repair:** one clause naming the published figure
and the denominator difference. **Benefit:** protects him from an implication of exaggeration while
keeping the honesty of the label. **Confidence:** high (packet CLM-04, S-22). **Acceptance test:** a
reader can tell why the two figures differ without leaving the page.

**SUBJ-C9 — A biographical detail follows Wikipedia over his own account, and a quoted word is
altered.**
Line 103: "a university place turned down at fifteen," used as one of four proofs of the "he
practised" thesis. His own account is more complicated: "I had a place… I took time out and took a
year to go traveling… So I deferred and then I auditioned for drama school and I deferred again."
Deferring twice is not turning a place down. Separately, line 155 renders his phrase as "a father with
an incredible work ethic" while presenting it as his wording; he said "dad," and the phrase itself
rests on a search summary rather than a primary page. **Repair:** use his version of the university
sequence; either quote "dad" exactly or stop presenting the phrase as his. **Benefit:** the thesis
sentence stops resting on the weaker of two sources when the stronger one is the subject himself.
**Confidence:** high (packet CLM-16, CLM-20). **Acceptance test:** every biographical claim presented
as his self-description matches his words.

**SUBJ-C10 — The tiebreaker for the whole type call rests on a precision the piece contradicts
elsewhere.**
Line 137: "What settles it is the speed. He did not argue with the reading or wait to see whether the
feeling passed. He took one glance as data and amputated inside a second." Line 97 says "He stopped
dancing that year." He describes an instantaneous internal recognition — "that one moment in me that
I went, oh, this isn't right" — but the decision he reports is "I actually decided not to continue
with the dance, but I was acting and singing." So: the recognition was instant, the withdrawal was
not necessarily, and it was partial. The article's decisive psychological verdict about him is
carried by a conflation of the two, with the qualifying clause omitted. **Repair:** distinguish the
recognition from the decision ("The recognition was instant"), and restore that he kept acting and
singing. **Benefit:** the type argument survives on what he actually described; the "amputation"
metaphor stops overstating a thirteen-year-old's act. **Confidence:** high (packet CLM-02, S-01).
**Acceptance test:** no sentence claims he quit within a second, and the draft states somewhere that
he continued performing.

**SUBJ-C11 — The staging of the Time Out review did not happen.**
Line 217: "Time Out's Andrzej Lukowski opened with 'Jonathan Bailey is a great stage actor,' gave the
production three stars, and then landed the knife." The review opens on the Bridge Theatre, not on
Bailey; the praise is one clause in the second paragraph, shared with Hytner. The words are accurate;
the praise-then-knife sequence is invented, and it dramatises his reception into something more
adversarial than it was. **Repair:** drop "opened with"; report the clause where it sits. **Benefit:**
the critical split — which is real and well handled — stops resting on a staging that did not occur.
**Confidence:** high (packet CLM-05, S-09, fetched live by the packet). **Acceptance test:** no claim
about where in a review a quotation appears that the review does not support.

**SUBJ-C12 — The RSC age seam is left unreconciled across a structural bookend.**

## Limits of this review

1. **I am a proxy, not the subject.** Nothing here claims to know Jonathan Bailey's opinion of this
   article, of the Enneagram, or of being typed. Every finding is an argument about what the public
   record supports, not a report of his views. He has not been asked and has never self-reported a
   type.
2. **A disagreement with the typing is not among my findings.** The Type 3 reading may be wrong; that
   is the enneagram evaluator's question. I tested only whether the article's treatment of him is
   defensible if he rejects it — which, after the three blockers are repaired, it largely would be.
3. **The epigraph finding rests on a Tier 4 reproduction.** The primary is inaccessible (HTTP 403).
   Under the packet's own source rules a Tier 4 source cannot carry a correction alone. My blocker is
   therefore stated in a form that survives that limit: whatever the canonical wording, the draft
   presents a discontinuous passage as continuous speech without ellipsis, and the craft frame is
   absent. If a primary check shows different wording, the repair changes; the defect does not.
4. **Primaries I could not reach:** Interview magazine, The Hollywood Reporter body text, British GQ,
   Evening Standard. Their quotations are taken from the packet's syndication chain, not read at
   source.
5. **No 2026 first-person source about the subject exists.** Every present-tense claim about his
   interior state — mine and the draft's — extrapolates from November 2025 or earlier.
6. **Independence.** I read only `context.json`, `draft-reviewed.md` and `evidence-packet.md` in this
   directory. `subject.md` was the only file I wrote; no other perspective file exists in the
   directory and none was opened. The frozen snapshot embeds three prior-pass editorial comment blocks
   (FRESH EYES REVIEW, SECOND PASS NOTES, COHESION PASS) which I encountered while reading the file
   end to end. I did not adopt their conclusions and did not treat their praise as evidence; where my
   findings overlap theirs I verified independently against the packet and the draft. Two of my
   findings contradict those notes directly: the SECOND PASS and FORMULA FINGERPRINT ledgers assert
   the epigraph is verbatim, and it is not.
7. **Scope.** I did not evaluate SEO performance, prose quality, structure, length, or the article's
   commercial fitness except where those choices bear on fair representation — the H2 in SUBJ-R2 and
   the lint-driven suppression in SUBJ-E1 are included for that reason and no other.
