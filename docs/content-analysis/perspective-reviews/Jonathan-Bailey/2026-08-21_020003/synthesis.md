## Executive verdict

Six perspectives returned `trust: strained`, `delight: clear_hit`, `recommendation: revise`. That
combination is unanimous and it is accurate. The argument is sound, the structure is earned, and the
piece contains at least three sentences no other draft in this corpus has. Nothing in the P0 list
touches the thesis.

What the jury actually found, and what my own re-reading of every cited passage confirms, is a single
recurring defect wearing nine costumes: **the draft improves on its sources at the exact moments the
improvement is most useful to it.** A review's rhetorical order is restaged so the praise comes first.
A craft remark becomes a confession about being unworthy of love. "Wasn't hyper aware" becomes "never
once minded." A comic punchline becomes an on-the-record relationship statement. A room of parents
reaching for their coats becomes "nobody left to clap." A thirteen-year-old's "this isn't the right
thing I should be doing" becomes "he is right about what I look like." Individually each is a clause.
Collectively they are a pattern a careful reader can detect in one pass, and the piece's whole
authority rests on being the kind of article that does not do this.

The second finding is narrower and belongs to one reviewer. The diagnosis section rests its
`Confidence: high` on two episodes of being closeted, which no type-discriminating reading can carry —
and the genuinely Three-specific evidence is already in the draft, sitting in other sections doing
narrative work. This is the one P0 that is a thinking problem rather than a wording problem, and it is
also the one with a fairness edge: unrepaired, the piece can be read as explaining a gay man's
concealment as a personality trait.

Everything else is punctuation-level or a clause. The nine P0s together are roughly one editing
session, and eight of the nine cost fewer than thirty words each.

**Word budget is a hard constraint on this revision.** The frozen body runs ~4,089 words against
blog-lint's 4,050 warn line and a 4,500 ceiling — about 400 words of headroom. The P0/P1 list spends
roughly 250 of them. The fresh-eyes mic-drop trims and the Grande scoreboard cut are the offsetting
withdrawals. Do not fund the P2 gloss list until those land.

## P0 — mandatory red-flag repairs

### P0-01 — The Time Out review's structure is asserted and it did not happen

- **Origin:** CRITIC-R1 (critic, blocker), FAN-B1 (fan, blocker), SUBJ-C11 (subject). Packet CLM-05, S-09.
- **Location:** "What Critics Said About Jonathan Bailey's Richard II," second paragraph.
- **Passage:** "Time Out's Andrzej Lukowski opened with 'Jonathan Bailey is a great stage actor,' gave
  the production three stars, and then landed the knife: 'the final passages lose considerable
  poignancy through Richard's triviality.'"
- **Adjudicated problem:** The words are accurate; the sequence is invented. The review opens on the
  Bridge Theatre ("a bit like excitedly running off to meet an old friend you haven't seen in ages and
  then finding the conversation is… okay but a bit stilted"). The Bailey clause sits in the **second**
  paragraph and is half of a compound sentence shared with Hytner. "Praise, then knife" is the shape of
  a reservation nested inside admiration, and it is not the shape of this review.
- **Evidence and confidence:** High — contradicted, not disputed. Fetched live by the packet and
  re-fetched independently by the critic reviewer. This is the packet's only flatly contradicted claim.
- **Minimum repair:** Delete the sequence assertion. "Lukowski granted in his second paragraph that
  'Jonathan Bailey is a great stage actor,' gave the production three stars, and still concluded…"
  Restoring the Hytner half is optional and improves it — shared praise is a colder compliment, which
  serves the argument.
- **Reader benefit:** The section becomes check-proof. Time Out is one click away, unpaywalled, and the
  check takes fifteen seconds.
- **At risk:** Both critics' verbatim quotes and the three-star ratings (PROTECT-04). Only the staging
  around them is wrong. Do not soften the quotes while fixing the frame.
- **Acceptance test:** No sentence in the draft asserts where in a review a quotation appears unless the
  review supports it. Open timeout.com/london/theatre/richard-ii-10-review: the first sentence is not
  the quoted clause, and the draft no longer says it is.

### P0-02 — The H2 asserts as fact the exact claim Bailey publicly identified as a misquote

- **Origin:** SUBJ-R2 (subject, blocker), UNFAM-R1 (unfamiliar, blocker, caught on the unaided read).
- **Location:** H2 #6 `## Why Jonathan Bailey Stopped Acting for a Year`. Reinforced by the TL;DR bullet
  ("sent the world's most bookable actor into a year of hiring staff"), "The year of hiring has a date
  at the far end of it," and "He has spent his quietest year on record."
- **Adjudicated problem:** The heading states the claim, the body two paragraphs later reports him
  correcting it, and the closer confirms he worked anyway. The draft is one of the "quitting headlines"
  it criticises. The heading also escalates: he was misreported as stopping "for a bit"; the heading
  makes it "for a Year."
- **Evidence and confidence:** High, and I verified this outside the packet. Bailey to Esquire UK, 9 Nov
  2025: the bad connection on the Rome–Pisa call "resulted in an unfortunate misquote that said I'd
  stopped acting for a bit"; his intention was "Next year I'll be back"; "I'm not taking the whole of
  2026 off." Corroborated across Parade, E! News, Geo News and AOL syndication. Separately, packet
  limitation 11 records that **no 2026 first-person source exists**, so "his quietest year on record"
  asserts an unverified 2026 fact in the article's own voice.
- **Minimum repair:** Retitle to a past-tense construction that serves the query without asserting the
  denied claim — e.g. "Did Jonathan Bailey Quit Acting? What the Year Off Was Actually For." Add "he
  says he was misquoted" to the sentence introducing the GQ line, and add "I'll definitely be back to
  work next year" to his correction. Fix the TL;DR bullet and hedge or drop "his quietest year on
  record."
- **Reader benefit:** The correction becomes the section's payoff instead of its self-contradiction.
- **At risk:** FUTURE's preserve list #6 protects this heading as the draft's model of a durable
  past-tense construction. That protection is about **tense**, not about the assertion — the
  replacement must stay past-tense and must not become a perpetual-currency question. See Conflicts.
- **Acceptance test:** Search the rendered draft for "stopped acting for a year" and any paraphrase
  asserting a completed year away from acting. Zero hits outside a passage attributing it to the
  misreporting. A reader who reads only the eight H2s cannot come away believing he stopped acting for a
  year. The heading still contains a recognisable form of the search query.

### P0-03 — The epigraph deletes the frame that makes it craft talk and re-presents it as a confession

- **Origin:** SUBJ-R1 (subject, blocker), CRITIC-C5 (critic, concern). Packet CLM-01, Disputes item 1.
- **Location:** The epigraph, above the cold open — the first thing every reader sees.
- **Passage:** "One thing that we're all born with is the sense of longing. If you long for someone, more
  often than not you don't think you are worthy of it." — Jonathan Bailey, Interview magazine, 2024
- **Adjudicated problem:** In the source, this is Bailey answering Phoebe Waller-Bridge on how he decides
  what makes a character tick, and the answer closes on "And that, to me, is a way into characters." The
  draft deletes the intervening material and the closing frame, joins the survivors with no ellipsis,
  and capitalises "if" so the seam disappears. Mounted above an article arguing this man hides who he
  loves, the surviving fragment reads as Bailey admitting he does not believe he is worthy of love. That
  is not compression; it is a change of genre, attributed to him by name and outlet. The draft knows the
  passage is craft — it quotes the deleted sentence correctly as craft in the Richard II section
  ("longing comes before anything else"). So the piece is using both halves of a passage it has silently
  cut in one place.
- **Evidence and confidence:** High on the craft framing — the two reviewers read the same reproduction
  independently, and my own search independently corroborates that Waller-Bridge's question was how he
  decides what makes a character tick. **Medium on canonical wording:** interviewmagazine.com returned
  HTTP 403 to my direct fetch this pass, as it did to the packet; the only reachable reproduction is Tier
  4; and the two reviewers disagree on the extent of the omission (critic: one intervening sentence;
  subject: three clauses plus the closing frame). The frame deletion, not the word count, is the defect.
- **Minimum repair:** Do **not** simply insert an ellipsis and leave the craft frame deleted. Either
  (a) restore the passage with the "way into characters" clause and mark every omission with an ellipsis,
  accepting that it now reads as a statement about acting; or (b) keep the epigraph slot and use a line
  he said about himself — British GQ's "I remember at primary school being really clear and then it sort
  of gets murky as you get older" does the same structural work and is not borrowed from a craft answer.
  **Option (b) is the recommended path,** because it does not depend on wording that cannot currently be
  established. See RQ-02 if the writer wants to keep the existing quote.
- **Reader benefit:** Removes the piece's most prominent misrepresentation of the subject's own speech,
  and removes the internal inconsistency with the Richard II section.
- **At risk:** The FORMULA FINGERPRINT ledger's contrast-pair waiver currently rests on the epigraph
  being verbatim ("Quotes are never altered, so it stays"). If the epigraph changes or is marked as
  compressed, that ledger line must be corrected — the waiver is currently justified on false grounds.
  Under option (b) the comparative construction leaves with the quote and the count goes to 0/0.
- **Acceptance test:** Place the draft's epigraph beside the source passage. Every omitted word is marked,
  no capitalisation is silently altered, and either the "way into characters" clause is present or the
  quotation is no longer used as an epigraph implying a statement about his own worth. The FORMULA
  FINGERPRINT ledger's verbatim claim matches whatever is on the page.

### P0-04 — The display statistic omits the contradicting published figure, and its magnitude misreads

- **Origin:** CRITIC-R2 (critic, blocker), FAN-C3 (fan), SUBJ-C8 (subject), UNFAM-C6 (unfamiliar, on
  magnitude). Packet CLM-04, S-22, Disputes item 2.
- **Location:** "Why Jonathan Bailey Stopped Acting for a Year," the `key-stat` block.
- **Passage:** **1p** — "of every £100 raised in the UK reaches the LGBTQ+ community, by Bailey's own
  count (British GQ, 2025)."
- **Adjudicated problem:** Two defects in the most screenshot-able element on the page. (i) The only
  published UK figure runs an order of magnitude higher — LGBT+ Consortium research by Dr Cat Walker,
  27 Mar 2025, puts it at **10p in every £100** of voluntary-and-community-sector income — and no
  published source matching 1p was located by the packet or by two reviewers searching independently.
  The denominators differ ("raised" vs voluntary-sector income), so this is not a strict refutation, but
  there is no reading under which 1p is the established number. The draft's hedge tells the reader the
  number is _unverified_; it does not tell them it is _contradicted_, which is a materially different
  disclosure, and "he repeats it everywhere he goes" nudges toward "he inflates it" while withholding
  the means to judge. (ii) Separately, "1p of every £100" reads to a non-UK reader as roughly one
  percent. It is 0.01% — a hundredfold misread of the piece's most quotable number, in the direction
  that drains it.
- **Evidence and confidence:** High on the discrepancy and on the absence of a 1p source (three
  independent searches agree). Medium on whether the denominators reconcile — which is exactly why the
  repair attributes rather than adjudicates. Four perspectives converged, two of them from opposed
  motives: the critic to protect the reader, the subject reviewer to protect Bailey from an implication
  of exaggeration. That convergence is the strongest signal in the jury.
- **Minimum repair:** One sentence in the body under the stat naming the LGBT+ Consortium figure, noting
  the denominators differ, and saying that 1p is the figure he campaigns on. Add the plain-English
  magnitude inside the stat label. Keep the stat and keep the existing hedge — extend it, do not delete
  it. "He repeats a number nobody can source, everywhere he goes" is itself on-thesis.
- **Reader benefit:** Removes the piece's most amplifiable factual exposure and converts a liability into
  evidence. The philanthropy section gains its only moment of skeptical pressure.
- **At risk:** PROTECT-08, the existing hedge sentence, which all four reviewers say to extend rather than
  remove. Do not let the repair become a deletion of the stat — his repetition of it is real evidence.
- **Acceptance test:** A US reader states the correct order of magnitude after reading only the stat
  block. No `key-stat` block in the draft displays a number for which the piece knows a contradicting
  published figure it does not name.

### P0-05 — The diagnosis rests `Confidence: high` on two episodes of being closeted

- **Origin:** ENNEAGRAM-R1 (enneagram, blocker). Lone reviewer, squarely in domain — accepted under
  rule 4.
- **Location:** "What is Jonathan Bailey's personality type?" — "Bailey has described that mechanism
  twice, unprompted…", the dance-studio paragraph, the Tupperware paragraph, closing on "Threes reliably
  report exactly this." Recurs in the dating section ("one documented, catastrophic experience of it, at
  thirteen, and a second slower one through his twenties") and in the Rabbit Hole's stress arrow.
- **Adjudicated problem:** Both exhibits are minority-stress accounts, not type signatures. A gay
  thirteen-year-old who stops ballet the instant a male friend sees him in a leotard, and a closeted man
  in his twenties who cannot locate his own feelings, are describing concealment. Any of the nine types
  produces both accounts under those conditions. The draft never names the confound and asserts high
  confidence on that base. Two costs follow: an Enneagram-literate reader discounts every downstream
  claim that leans on it, and a general reader gets a piece that appears to explain a gay man's
  concealment as a personality trait. I ran the reviewer's acceptance test on the frozen text and it
  fails: delete those two paragraphs and what remains supporting `Confidence: high` is one assertion and
  one unsourced population claim.
- **Evidence and confidence:** High. This is a methodology defect, not a typing disagreement — the
  reviewer lands on Type 3 as well, and so do I. The fix is already in the draft: the backflip
  calculation under zero external threat, "Yeah, I've got this" followed by "a deep spike of fear and
  self-doubt," the manufactured-effortlessness composite, "twinkly," and the routing of brand deals into
  a grant fund are all image-calibration with nothing pressing on him. They sit in the prompt book, the
  sabbatical section and the closer doing narrative work.
- **Minimum repair:** Two changes inside the diagnosis section, ~40 words. (1) One sentence before the
  two exhibits naming and disposing of the confound in the piece's own register: the closet explains a
  great deal of what follows, and a Three is not the only type it does this to, which is why the case
  cannot rest here. (2) Cite at least one no-threat calibration datum as the third exhibit — the backflip
  is the cleanest. Keep the two closeting episodes as context.
- **Reader benefit:** The confidence claim becomes supportable and the diagnosis gains the one evidence
  class it lacks.
- **At risk:** **Cite the backflip; do not move it.** The backflip entry is protected by fan preserve #7
  and unfamiliar preserve #12 (the prompt-book cue form). Also at risk: the DISTRIBUTION ledger's
  two-type-paragraph cap outside the diagnosis and the Rabbit Hole quarantine — the enneagram reviewer
  explicitly protects that accessibility discipline (PROTECT-10) and says not to trade it away to fix
  this. The repair adds to the diagnosis section, where type theory already lives.
- **Acceptance test:** Delete the ballet paragraph and the Tupperware paragraph from the diagnosis
  section. What remains still supports "Confidence on the core type: high."

### P0-06 — The piece presupposes a concealed partner its own section says is not established

- **Origin:** SUBJ-R3 (subject, blocker). Lone reviewer, in domain — accepted under rule 4.
- **Location:** Cold open ("And he will not tell you who he loves."); H2 #4 ("Why Jonathan Bailey Will Not
  Say Who He Is Dating"); section close ("The one thing he keeps is the fact of who it is actually for.").
- **Adjudicated problem:** All three require a specific person to exist and be hidden. Nineteen lines
  below the first of them, the same section states his status is "not publicly established," reports the
  Dec 2023 "a lovely man" and the Jul 2025 "I'm available," and correctly declines to reconcile them. If
  the later statement is the current one, the article has taken an ordinary absence of a partner and
  rendered it as deliberate concealment — then built its spine and an H2 on the insistence. This is an
  internal contradiction on the highest-liability fact in the piece, and it puts the draft at odds with
  its own relationship-status ledger.
- **Evidence and confidence:** High on the internal contradiction — it is textual and I verified it in the
  frozen draft. The underlying fact is unknowable by construction, which is the point. Packet CLM-03,
  Disputes item 7.
- **Minimum repair:** Move the withheld object from a person to the private life. Three sentence-level
  edits: "who he loves" → "who he loves, or whether there is anyone to name"; "the fact of who it is
  actually for" → "the fact of who it is for, if it is for anyone"; retitle the H2 to the query readers
  type without asserting the answer — "Is Jonathan Bailey Dating Anyone? What He Will and Will Not Say"
  keeps every SERP term the HEADING MIX ledger cites.
- **Reader benefit:** The rhetorical engine survives intact — a man paid to manufacture desire who fences
  his own — while the piece stops asserting a private fact about a living person that it elsewhere
  declines to assert.
- **At risk:** The cold open's final beat is one of the piece's best lines and the H2 is its largest
  search-intent asset. The repair must not soften the withholding, only de-presuppose its object. Do not
  let this edit touch PROTECT-01 or PROTECT-05.
- **Acceptance test:** Read every sentence referring to his romantic life and ask whether it is still true
  if he is single. No sentence fails. The H2 does not assert that he is dating anyone.

### P0-07 — The invented interior monologue puts appearance-shame in a real thirteen-year-old's head

- **Origin:** SUBJ-C1 (subject). Lone reviewer, in domain — accepted under rule 4 and elevated above the

- **Passage:** `<p class="inner-thought">He is seeing what I look like. He is right about what I look
like. I did not know until now.</p>`
- **Adjudicated problem:** His actual account is "it was that one moment in me that I went, oh, this isn't
  right, this isn't actually the right thing I should be doing" — a judgement about the activity. The
  invented thought converts that into agreement with a shaming read of his body, which is a materially

- **Reader benefit:** Keeps the device, which works, without attributing internalised body shame to a real

- **At risk:** The device itself (PROTECT-11: the decision to hold interiority to one instance). Repair
  the block; do not delete it and do not add a second one elsewhere.
- **Acceptance test:** Every clause of the inner-thought block maps to something in the CBS transcript. No

### P1-02 — The sexuality-speculation sentence assigns the cause to Bailey and omits the backlash

### P1-07 — "What settles it is the speed" adjudicates against an alternative the reader never sees

- **Origin:** ENNEAGRAM-C1 (enneagram), UNFAM-C3 (unfamiliar), SUBJ-C10 (subject, on the underlying
