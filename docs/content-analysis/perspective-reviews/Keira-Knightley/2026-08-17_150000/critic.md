## Bottom-line verdict

The strongest skeptical case is that the article selects anxiety-compatible episodes around a principled Type 1 or ambitious Type 3. It survives because it names those alternatives, uses cross-domain evidence, and preserves moments that do not flatter the thesis.

## What landed

Carney's criticism is not dismissed, public advocacy is not automatically treated as personality proof, and the breakdown testimony establishes fear independently of the author's reading. The argument also differentiates seeking safety from merely appearing brave.

## What missed

“Counterphobic” can become unfalsifiable if both retreat and confrontation count as Six. The instinct-stack claim is much less secure than the core-type claim, and some elegant lines outrun what public evidence can prove.

## What I expected

Contrary testimony, alternative types, source limits, and evidence that is not simply career success. All four are present.

## What surprised me

The article's best evidence is not the lawsuit; it is asking agents to withhold the role she wanted most, followed by long trust once Wright was vetted.

## Red flags

No blocker. The inner monologue is literary reconstruction, and readers should not mistake it for a quotation; its styling and surrounding attribution make that distinction adequate.

## Specific improvements

At the next substantive revision, reduce certainty in the instinct paragraph and seek direct craft testimony rather than adding more confrontation examples.
