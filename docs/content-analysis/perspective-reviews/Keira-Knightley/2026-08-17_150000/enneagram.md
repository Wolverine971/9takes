## Bottom-line verdict

Type 6 is argued through threat anticipation, costly trust, doubt around desired exposure, loyalty after vetting, and achievement used to preserve permission or safety. The 6w7 reading is plausible; sx/sp is more tentative.

## What landed

The diagnosis does not rest on self-reported anxiety alone. Asking not to be considered for a desired role, attaching to Wright after a hostile first meeting, using hypnotherapy to approach the feared arena, and curating a bounded home/work perimeter establish a coherent motivational pattern.

## What missed

Counterphobic theory risks absorbing contradictory behavior. The 7-wing evidence is public humor under safe conditions, while the 5-wing alternative and sexual-instinct assignment lack equally discriminating evidence.

## What I expected

Core strategy, phobic/counterphobic movement, wing, instincts, stress and security, alternative types, and a statement of what would change the conclusion. Each is present.

## What surprised me

The acting-for-grades bargain provides stronger developmental evidence than the more visible public fights because it connects love, permission, vigilance, and achievement before fame.

## Red flags

None requiring repair. The article keeps theory concentrated and names Type 1 as the strongest alternative.

## Specific improvements

Future editions should treat instinct order as low-confidence and look for first-person evidence about belonging, resources, and one-to-one intensity before strengthening it.
