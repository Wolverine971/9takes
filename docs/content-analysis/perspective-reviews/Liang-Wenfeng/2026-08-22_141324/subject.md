## Bottom-line verdict

Standpoint: a proxy for how Liang Wenfeng, or someone who has read everything he has actually said, would judge this article's fairness. Not whether he would accept "Type 5."

The draft is unusually careful with him in the places where careless articles are cruel: it labels every 2026 line "purported," refuses to invent a wife, gives the year of birth and no more, uses his own vocabulary (originality vs. imitation, free-riding, watermelons and sesame seeds, "hypothesis") instead of a stock founder story, and lets his strongest critics speak before it answers them. He would recognize himself in it.

Two passages break that fairness and both sit in load-bearing slots. First, the TL;DR and the critics section say he has "never published" how many GPUs he owns, placed directly beside an export-control accusation, when the draft's own timeline reproduces the counts he gave 36Kr in 2023 and the 10,000-A100 figure in a paper he submitted. Second, the "fifth point" in the signature section quotes "what has the greatest return for me to do at this moment?" as evidence that money may have changed his motive, and omits the very next sentence in the same source, where "return" is the reason he chooses AGI over products. Both are cheap to repair. Until they are, the article makes him look more secretive and more mercenary than the record it cites.

**SUBJECT-R1. Blocker. "Never published" the GPU count, beside an export-control accusation.**

- Passage: TL;DR bullet "Gives away the map, hides the engine: ... The one number he has never published is how many GPUs he owns." and critics section, "What he has never published is the number of cards in the building, the only asset that cannot be bought back once it is taken away. He publishes the map and hides the engine."
- Reader effect: placed two paragraphs after Alexandr Wang's "They can't talk about it obviously because it's against the export controls," the claim reads as corroboration that he conceals his hardware. It frames the subject as evasive on the exact point where he is accused of illegality.
- Evidence: the draft's own timeline lists his disclosed counts; 36Kr 2023 (S01) has him reciting "a single card ... 100 cards in 2015, 1,000 cards in 2019, and then 10,000 cards"; the Fire-Flyer AI-HPC paper (arXiv 2408.14158, submitter Wenfeng Liang, S31) states 10,000 PCIe A100s; the purported transcript gives "roughly 20,000 H-equivalent ... basically all Nvidia" and, separately, "Huawei gives us 16,000 cards, this should be publicly stateable." The packet flags this as CLM-40, "Contested ... defensible only as 'no audited current inventory'." The honest version of the "hides" half exists in the same transcript: the moderator's closing warning, "please absolutely don't spread some numbers or situations externally, including card quantities."
- Minimum viable repair: replace "never published" in both places with a true statement of the gap, e.g., that no audited or current count has ever been released and that the one document that gives a 2026 figure is purported and was, per its own moderator, meant to keep card quantities private. Keep "publishes the map" as is.
- Expected benefit: the split survives on an accurate footing and the subject is no longer implied to be hiding something he put in a paper.
- Confidence: high (the contradiction is internal to the draft).
- Acceptance test: grep the body and TL;DR for "never published"; zero hits, or every hit is qualified to "audited" / "current" / "outside a purported document." The timeline component and the critics-section sentence must no longer contradict each other.
