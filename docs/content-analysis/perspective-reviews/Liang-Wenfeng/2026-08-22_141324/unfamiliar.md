## Bottom-line verdict

Read cold, with no packet and no prior knowledge beyond "DeepSeek was some Chinese AI thing in the news," this article works. By the end I can say who Liang Wenfeng is (a quant-fund billionaire who built a frontier AI lab and refuses to charge what it is worth), why he matters (richest AI-model founder alive, a leaked transcript that froze his own funding round), and what the article claims: he is an Enneagram Type 5, a person whose security is understanding, who treats every other appetite (money, products, staff, attention) as a leak, and whose famous restraint is how he protects the one thing he cannot buy back, his team. The article also tells me what would prove it wrong (a post-IPO price hike). That is more than most profiles of anyone give a newcomer.

The problems are comprehension gaps, not trust breaks. The single most important event in his public life, the day his model knocked $589 billion off Nvidia, is stated as an effect with no cause, and the article calls a "$6 million training figure" famous before it has told me what it is. A key-stat box puts a 545% margin beside "priced to cost" and does not explain why those are not a contradiction. The article's own timeline prints two GPU counts and then says he has never published the number of GPUs. Ten yuan figures have no dollar equivalents, and two different "50 billions" in different currencies sit in adjacent sentences. Sixteen mentions of "36Kr" never say what 36Kr is, and seven source names appear only in Chinese script. All six are fixable in a sentence or a clause each, and none touches the spine.

Zero blockers. Revise.
