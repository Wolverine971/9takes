## Bottom-line verdict

Snapshot audited: `draft-reviewed.md`. Its SHA-256 (`80ae5a3c…c411d`, computed locally) matches `context.json` and the supplied value. The live draft was not read. No other perspective review was read.

Did the writer understand her well enough to show an informed fan something new? Mostly yes. This is not the public-image Madonna. The writer has Flynn, Burgoyne, Liz Rosenberg, Stein's cardiac ward, Joan Lader, the three mother songs, and the 2018 retelling of the 1984 story. One section, on aging, gives a fan a connection they have not made before.

Trust is strained, not broken. A fan stops to correct the page in six places: a dropped word in the thesis quote, a rule about "every backlash" that her own catalogue contradicts, a claim that critics withheld respect until 1998, a stale sales superlative, a Kabbalah date, and a loyalty claim. Each is a one-line fix. None touches the thesis, and the largest one (FAN-R2) makes the Type 3 case stronger once it is narrowed.

The biggest gap is the work itself. The page argues that she performs for a dead mother, frames itself on the Celebration Tour, and never notes that she put her mother's face above that stage (FAN-R8).

Line numbers below refer to `draft-reviewed.md`. The draft's own notes put it at 4,379 words against a 3,200 to 3,900 band, so every repair here is sized and the adds are paid for.

## What landed

**FAN-H1. The tense reading and its 2026 echo (lines 126–128).**
> "Notice the tense in that 1991 Vanity Fair sentence, too: 'I still have to prove.' Present tense, from the top of the mountain."

Fans know the 1991 line by heart. They have not seen it set beside "I'm going to be somebody" from 2026. Thirty-five years, same word. It must survive because it is the only place the thesis is shown in her grammar instead of asserted.

**FAN-H2. Beatty's line turned around (line 215).**
> "He thought he was describing a flaw. He was describing the operating system. She kept his complaint in her own final cut."

Every fan can recite the Beatty line. Few have noticed that she was the executive producer who left it in. The packet's saved Wikipedia copy of the film article (S42, citing Taraborrelli) adds that Beatty's lawyer got other scenes removed, which makes the survival of this one more pointed. Keep it.

**FAN-H3. The aging turn (lines 291–295).**
> "It asks her to become less visible on purpose, and to accept that the songs stop being played on purpose."

> "She built a career on controlling the frame, and she has lost control of the close-up."

This is the delight hit. Fans are tired of both the face jokes and the stan defenses. The section does neither. It ties "you will definitely not be played on the radio" to the silence after 1963, then refuses to let her off: "None of this makes the choice free." It passes the test of "I knew those moments, but I had never connected them that way."

**FAN-H4. Admiration and criticism side by side (lines 229–235).**
> "None of that cancels hooks."

Flynn and Burgoyne are the informed route into the gay-icon question, and the page places them next to hooks without using them as a rebuttal. A fan reads this as written by someone who knows both the debt and the charge.

**FAN-H5. Christopher turned on the page itself (line 219).**
> "nearly everything known about her mother's death comes from Madonna, and Madonna films things."

Fan-facing writing almost never aims the skeptic's case at its own thesis. This must survive any trim of the graveside paragraph.

**FAN-H6. The callback that proves command of the record (line 319).**
> "It was at least the second time she had told that story from a VMA stage."

Only someone who knows 2018 happened writes that sentence. It is also the best evidence on the page for "no win stays won." See FAN-I2 for a clause that sharpens it.

**FAN-R2 (concern, highest priority). "After every backlash she changes the product" is not what her record shows.**
- Passage: line 136; restated at line 241 ("Provoke, absorb the hit, recalibrate, win") and line 270 ("a six-year respectability campaign").
- Reader effect: this is the tiebreaker against Type 8. A fan can refute "every" from memory, and the page refutes it too. Its own 1984 beat (line 195) is followed by a first No. 1, and its own 1989 beat is followed by 1992.
- Evidence:
  - Toronto, 1990: police threatened arrest over the "Like a Virgin" staging and "she refuses to change the show" (S42, *Truth or Dare* plot summary, the film cited at lines 211–217).
  - After *Sex* she doubled down for about eighteen months: the Girlie Show as a dominatrix in September 1993, then the Letterman appearance in March 1994 (S42). The softening followed.
  - "Human Nature," on Bedtime Stories itself, is "an answer song to the backlash." She said of it "I'm not sorry," and its video ends on "Absolutely no regrets" (Wikipedia, "Human Nature (Madonna song)," citing *Folha de S. Paulo*, Nov 21, 1994).
  - Sales, per Wikipedia's albums discography: Like a Prayer 15 million, Erotica 6 million, Bedtime Stories 8 million, American Life 5 million.
- Reasoning: she holds position when outrage sells and changes the product when the numbers fall. Erotica and American Life were each her lowest seller to that date, and those are the two recalibrations the draft documents. That pattern is more Type 3 than the draft's version. It is also the connection a fan has not made: she does not flinch at outrage, she flinches at the scoreboard.
- Minimum repair: narrow "every backlash" to the two that cost her sales. Add one clause that Bedtime Stories also carried "Human Nature." Change "six-year respectability campaign" to match the 1994 start. Net about 25 words.
- Benefit: the tiebreaker stops being refutable and gains its sharpest evidence. The Rabbit Hole's falsifier (line 272) gets a real test.
- Confidence: high on the facts. Medium on intent, which no source documents (packet CLM-10).
- Acceptance test: no sentence claims she changed course after every backlash; 1984 and 1989 are not contradicted by the rule; "Human Nature" or Toronto appears once.
