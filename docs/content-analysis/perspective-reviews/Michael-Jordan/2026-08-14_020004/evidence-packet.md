# Evidence Packet: Michael Jordan (draft snapshot 2026-08-14_020004)

This packet is common evidence for six independent evaluators. Entries are labeled **verified fact**, **attributed claim**, **interpretation**, **disputed claim**, or **unresolved question**. The draft's thesis (Enneagram Type 3, "manufactured grievance engine") is a hypothesis throughout, never a fact.

## Identity and scope

- **verified fact** — Subject is Michael Jeffrey Jordan, basketball player, b. 1963-02-17 in Brooklyn, NY, raised Wilmington, NC; six NBA championships with the Chicago Bulls, six Finals MVPs, five MVPs; later majority owner of the Charlotte Bobcats/Hornets (2010-2023), co-owner of NASCAR's 23XI Racing, NBC special contributor (2025-26). Age 63 at draft date.
- **verified fact** — He is a distinct entity from actor Michael B. Jordan, whom 9takes already covers at `/personality-analysis/michael-b-jordan`. The entity-gap packet (S-22) documents that the personality-type SERP currently conflates the two; the draft's first-hundred-words disambiguation responds to that documented confusion.
- **verified fact** — The article exists on the backlog's evergreen-demand + personality-intent rationale, not an entity-gap win: the entity-gap audit scored him 32/100 PASS and restricts the winnable lane to personality-type intent only (S-22).
- **Knowledge cutoff of this packet:** compiled 2026-08-14. Events through February-March 2026 (Daytona 500, Gayle King interview) were verified by live web search at compile time; the single August 2026 item (Wilbon/HoopsHype) rests on the repo research file only.

## Dated timeline

Event dates separated from publication dates where they differ.

- **1963-02-17** — Born, Brooklyn, NY. **verified fact**
- **~1970s, Wilmington NC** — Father James Jordan builds a backyard court; Michael loses to older brother Larry in daily one-on-one. **attributed claim** (Jordan's own retellings; the specific quote "When you see me play, you see Larry play" circulates widely but is not pinned to a primary transcript — see Disputes).
- **1978** — Sophomore year, Laney High. Coach Clifton "Pop" Herring keeps 5'10" Jordan on JV; the lone underclassman varsity slot goes to 6'7" Leroy Smith. Jordan retold this for decades as being "cut." Correction published by TIME (2012-01) and Forbes (2012-01). **verified fact** (the correction); the "cut" framing is Jordan's own compression. His workout-motivation quote ("close my eyes and see that list...") is **attributed** — widely quoted, carried in the TIME debunk, no primary transcript.
- **1982-03-29** — As a 19-year-old freshman, hits a 16-foot jumper with 15 seconds left to win UNC the NCAA title 63-62 over Georgetown (S-17). **verified fact**. The "15 seconds" figure is the dominant citation (History.com, Fox, Newsweek); a few accounts say 17.
- **1986-04-20** — Scores 63 in a double-OT playoff **loss** (135-131) at Boston Garden; Bird's "God disguised" quote follows postgame (S-16). **verified fact** (event); quote wording varies (see Disputes).
- **1993-03-19 / 03-20** — LaBradford Smith scores a career-high 37 (15-of-20) vs. the Bulls; the teams play again the next night and Jordan scores 36 by halftime (47 total). Jordan told teammates Smith had said "Nice game, Mike"; Smith later said "I didn't see Jordan after the game," and Jordan admitted in The Last Dance (aired 2020) that he invented the exchange. **verified fact** (games, dates, scoring) + **attributed claim** (the invention, on Jordan's own on-camera admission, S-03; CBS Sports retrospective per S-21).
- **1993-05** — Atlantic City casino visit the night before a playoff game vs. New York, reported contemporaneously by The New York Times. Richard Esquinas's 1993 book claims $1.25M in golf gambling debts; Jordan disputed the figure; settlement reported at a fraction (~$300k per contemporary reporting). **attributed claims** (S-21, S-24).
- **1993-07-23** — James Jordan murdered at a North Carolina highway rest stop. **verified fact**
- **1993-10-06** — First retirement ("I just feel that I don't have anything else to prove"). **verified fact**. The gambling-suspension conspiracy theory is debunked and excluded per the entity-gap packet (S-22).
- **1994** — Minor-league baseball, Birmingham Barons. Bryon Russell's "Why'd you quit? I could guard you" gym remark — known **only through Jordan's own 2009 HOF retelling** (S-01); Russell has not materially disputed it. **attributed claim**
- **1995-03** — Returns to the NBA; second three-peat 1996-98.
- **1995-10 (training camp)** — Punches Steve Kerr in a practice scrap; black eye; same-day apology call. Kerr later: standing up to Jordan was "the best thing that I ever did" (2020 press cycle). Jordan's shower quote from The Last Dance. **attributed claims**, consistently retold by both parties (S-03, S-21).
- **1996-06-16 (Father's Day)** — Bulls beat Seattle 87-75 for title #4, the first after his father's death; Jordan collapses sobbing in the locker room clutching the game ball. **verified fact** (broadcast footage). The on-air Rashad quote wording is **attributed** (see Disputes).
- **1998** — Ends the Finals with the last shot over Bryon Russell. **verified fact**
- **2003-02-21** — Scores 43 vs. New Jersey days after turning 40 (first 40-year-old with a 40-point game). **verified fact**
- **2003-05-07** — Fired by Wizards owner Abe Pollin weeks after his final game; statement: "I am shocked by this decision, and by the callous refusal to offer me any justification for it" (ESPN, 2003-05-07, S-07). **verified fact**, exact wording confirmed.
- **2005-10** — 60 Minutes interview with Ed Bradley; both gambling quotes in the draft match the widely published transcript excerpts (S-04). **verified fact** (that he said them; the self-assessment inside them is his own).
- **2009-09-11** — Hall of Fame induction, ~23-minute speech (video 23:23, S-01). Tears before and during; Leroy Smith present; grievance catalog. **verified fact**, checked against the full transcript on file.
- **2009-09** — Wojnarowski's Yahoo column: "he was petty and punitive"; "This wasn't a Hall of Fame induction speech, but a bully tripping nerds with lunch trays in the school cafeteria" (S-09). **verified fact** (that he wrote it), wording confirmed.
- **2010-03** — NBA Board of Governors approves his $275M purchase of the Charlotte Bobcats; first former player to become majority owner of an NBA franchise (S-18). **verified fact**
- **2011-12 season** — Bobcats go 7-59 (.106), the worst winning percentage in NBA history, ending the season on a 23-game losing streak. **verified fact** (league record books; no single URL captured — see Research limitations).
- **2020-02-24** — Kobe Bryant memorial eulogy; the crying-meme joke (S-15). **verified fact**; the "Now he's got me" prefix wording — see Disputes.
- **2020 (April-May)** — The Last Dance airs: "Winning has a price..." / "Break" walk-off (Ep 7); Vancil "completely present" quote; "Republicans buy sneakers, too" addressed by Jordan as said "in jest" (Ep 5). **verified facts** (that these appear in the doc as described).
- **2020-06-05** — Jordan + Jordan Brand pledge $100M over 10 years; joint statement opens "Black lives matter. This isn't a controversial statement." (Nike newsroom, S-08). **verified fact**, first-party source. Draft cites Forbes; the statement text is confirmed regardless of outlet.
- **2023-08** — Sells majority stake in the Hornets after 13 years; franchise never won a playoff series under his ownership. **verified fact**
- **2024-10** — 23XI Racing + Front Row Motorsports file a federal antitrust suit against NASCAR over the charter system. **verified fact**
- **2025-10-21** — NBC "MJ: Insights to Excellence" debuts (announced May 2025; segments with Mike Tirico, pre-taped from one before-season interview) (S-14). **verified fact**
- **2025 (fall)** — Ryder Cup rental-house free-throw story, told by Jordan in the NBC interview (S-02). **verified fact** that he told it; the events inside it are his own account (**attributed**).
- **2025-12-01 → 12-11** — Antitrust trial begins; settles on day nine-plus; joint statement 2025-12-11; teams' six charters restored and charters made effectively permanent ("evergreen") (S-10). **verified fact**. Dismissal with prejudice 2026-02-03 is carried by the repo research file citing ESPN/Sportico (**attributed**, not independently re-verified).
- **2026-02-15** — Tyler Reddick wins the Daytona 500 for 23XI (S-12). **verified fact**
- **2026-03-27** — Gayle King / CBS interview: lawsuit "was personal"; prepared to be ousted from the sport (S-13, S-27). **verified fact** that the interview occurred and carried this tenor; exact phrasing — see Disputes.
- **2026-08-11** — Wilbon: people at ESPN "got panicky" over the NBC move (HoopsHype per S-21). **attributed claim**, repo-sourced only.

## First-person evidence

Primary transcripts on file in `youtube-transcripts/` were read in full for this packet.

1. **2009 HOF speech (S-01, full transcript on file).** Supports: the grievance catalog structure (Leroy Smith "he's here tonight... you made a mistake dude"; Buzz Peterson; freeze-out; Krause; Bryon Russell re-enactment); "that fire started with my parents... people added wood to that fire"; "that puts so much wood on that fire that it kept me each and every day trying to get better"; the closing "refuge" passage; "playing the game at 50 oh don't laugh." **Cannot support:** the claim that Jordan believed every grievance — on the freeze-out he says on the podium, "no matter what people may have said if it was a rumor I never took it as truth," i.e., he cites it as fuel while disclaiming certainty. Evaluators should weigh that nuance against the draft's ledger row.
2. **NBC "Insights to Excellence" segments, 2025-26 (S-02, transcript on file).** Supports verbatim: "it's the most nervous I've been in years"; "those kids heard the stories from the parents about what I did 30 years ago..."; "I haven't picked up a ball in years"; the magic-pill / "that's who I am... what I live for" passage; "the most valuable asset I have is time"; "I have an obligation to the game of basketball... pass on messages of success and dedication"; "I was able to please that kid not knowing if I could." **Cannot support:** interior states beyond what he says; also note the segments are pre-taped from one interview (S-14), so "he told Mike Tirico on NBC in October 2025" is accurate as to airing, not necessarily to when he said it.
3. **60 Minutes, 2005 (S-04).** Supports both gambling quotes as quoted in the draft ("...I don't look at that line. I step over that line."). It is self-assessment, not clinical evidence.
4. **The Last Dance, 2020 (S-03).** Supports: the LaBradford Smith admission; "Winning has a price..." and the "Break" walk-off; the shower quote about Kerr; "Republicans buy sneakers, too" acknowledged as a joke made in jest.
5. **Sager interview, April 2016 (S-06).** Supports: "the birth of Michael Jordan" framing — Vice renders it "Before then, I was 'Mike' Jordan. All of a sudden I make that shot, I'm 'Michael' Jordan." Draft's rendering adds an "and"; substance matches.

## Public record, accomplishments, failures, and controversies

- **Accomplishments (verified facts):** six titles/six Finals MVPs/five MVPs; 1982 NCAA title shot; 63-point playoff game; first former player to hold an NBA majority stake (2010); Forbes-tracked billionaire (draft avoids a static number, consistent with packet rules); 23XI's Daytona 500 win (2026) and the charter settlement.
- **Failures (verified facts, needed for balance):** the Bobcats' 7-59 season (worst win-percentage in league history) and zero playoff series won across 13 years of ownership; a winless-in-the-playoffs Wizards front-office/playing tenure ending in the 2003 firing; the 2025 season's 23XI charter limbo preceding the settlement.
- **Controversies:** gambling episodes (Atlantic City 1993, Esquinas debts) — documented as reported events with named sources; the draft follows the packet rule of no totals and no addiction claims, with 60 Minutes self-description carrying the interpretive weight. The 1985 freeze-out is **disputed** (participants deny; Jordan himself hedged on the podium). The 2009 speech's reception as "petty" is **attributed criticism** the draft engages rather than hides. Teammate violence (Kerr, Perdue punches) is acknowledged by all parties. Excluded per prior packet rules and honored by the draft: the sister's memoir allegations (contested) and the 1993 suspension conspiracy (debunked).
- **Political neutrality arc:** "Republicans buy sneakers, too" (1990, on-record as a jest per Jordan in TLD) → June 2020 statement and $100M pledge (first-party, S-08). Both endpoints verified; the draft's framing of the thirty-year gap is **interpretation**.

## Enneagram hypothesis evidence

The draft's typing (Type 3, the Achiever; wing 3w4; so/sp; stress→9; growth→6) is a **hypothesis**. External typing-community consensus is 3w4 (personality-database/Boo tier — expectation-setting only, per S-22; never a factual authority).

**Evidence FOR Type 3 (each item's factual base labeled above):**

- Audience-indexed worth across five decades: the 1978 roster fixation (verified correction + his own quotes), the HOF grievance catalog (verified transcript), the 2025 free-throw nerves before children (his own account, on file).
- The type's characteristic vice (deceit-as-self-story, standard Enneagram theory) literalized in the invented LaBradford Smith insult — an unusually direct fit, and the draft's strongest single datum (verified admission).
- Heart-triad shame surfacing at unguarded moments: HOF tears, "Break" walk-off, Father's Day floor (all on footage).
- Stress-to-Nine reading: gambling as numbed autopilot (60 Minutes self-description) and the 1994 withdrawal year. **interpretation** — the facts support "he gambled and he withdrew"; the arrow mapping is theory.
- Growth-to-Six reading: the 23XI fight benefiting all teams' charters; "obligation to the game" framing at NBC. **interpretation** on verified events.

**Evidence AGAINST / for the closest alternative (Type 8, the Challenger):**

- Physical domination and punches (verified/attributed), room control, willingness to sue his own sanctioning body at 61 and risk expulsion (verified event; his own "personal" framing).
- **Discriminating evidence the draft leans on:** (a) 1990 sneaker-neutrality — an 8 keeps the conviction, Jordan kept the sponsor (verified event; the tiebreaker logic is **interpretation**); (b) audience-dependence of the free-throw nerves (his own account; interpretation that an 8 wouldn't feel this).
- **Complication evaluators should know:** the 2020 statement and $100M pledge show the conviction eventually going public at massive scale — the draft handles this as a growth arc; whether it weakens the 1990 tiebreaker is a fair line of challenge.
- A Type 1 read (punishing standards) is dismissed quickly in the draft; the factual base (discipline aimed at winning, not moral correctness) is consistent with the record but the dismissal is **interpretation**.

**Behavior the hypothesis does not cleanly explain (draft concedes):** the baseball year as tribute to his father's first dream, and the same-day Kerr apology — tenderness with no audience payoff. Also note the HOF freeze-out hedge ("if it was a rumor I never took it as truth"): a wrinkle in the "believed his own inventions" reading; the draft's ledger frames output as indifferent to truth, which the hedge partially supports and partially complicates.

## Disputes and unresolved questions

1. **The coin-toss shrug (CLM-10) — contradicted detail.** The only documented shrug in the security-guard gambling record belongs to Wozniak, who beat Jordan on a $20 quarters bet and mimicked Jordan's shrug at him (The Last Dance Ep 6; S-19, S-25). No source was found for Jordan shrugging at guards when _he_ won. The draft's sentence inverts the documented scene. Evaluators should treat this as a factual error unless a source surfaces.
2. **The 1985 All-Star freeze-out — disputed claim.** Participants (Isiah Thomas foremost) have denied it for decades; Jordan's own podium framing hedges ("if it was a rumor I never took it as truth"). The draft's ledger labels it "Alleged; participants have denied it" — accurate — but the row's "Cited it from the Hall of Fame podium" omits his hedge. Whether the hedge strengthens or weakens the "truth was never the input variable" thesis is open to argument.
3. **Unpinned emotional-anchor quotes — unresolved.** "I know he's watching. This is for Daddy" (1996, on-air), "When you see me play, you see Larry play," and the "close my eyes and see that list" workout quote all circulate in consistent forms across decades of coverage but were not pinned to a primary transcript or dated first publication. Substance is uncontested; verbatim renderings should stay qualified.
4. **"Now he's got me" (CLM-06) — unresolved wording.** The crying-meme joke and its context are verified; the exact prefix words were not confirmed in the text sources checked (the memorial video would settle it).
5. **Bird quote wording (CLM-20) — variance, not dispute.** At least three renderings exist in reputable sources; all contain "God disguised as Michael Jordan."
6. **August 2026 Wilbon item — single-source.** Repo research cites HoopsHype 2026-08-11; not independently verified at compile time.
7. **Whether Jordan personally invited Leroy Smith to the induction** — widely reported and consistent with "he's here tonight," but the invitation mechanics are not on the record from either man. Low stakes.
8. **What must stay qualified:** all interior-state claims (what Jordan felt at the free-throw line, what the tears meant), every stress/growth-arrow mapping, and the 3-vs-8 tiebreaker logic. The factual skeleton beneath them is strong; the psychological readings are the article's argument, not its evidence.

## Research limitations

- **No long-form self-revealing corpus exists.** Jordan does no podcasts and few unscripted long interviews; first-person evidence concentrates in one prepared speech (2009), one cooperative documentary (2020), one 2005 news interview, and pre-taped NBC segments (2025-26). Every "interiority" claim in any review must survive on this thin, largely performance-adjacent base.
- **Auto-caption transcripts.** Both on-file transcripts are YouTube auto-captions; they establish substance and phrasing but not punctuation-grade verbatim text. Small wording deltas between draft and transcript (documented above) are within caption noise.
- **Not independently verified at compile time:** the 2026-02-03 dismissal-with-prejudice date; the Wilbon/HoopsHype item (2026-08-11); the exact on-air Rashad exchange (1996); the memorial-eulogy prefix "Now he's got me"; the original publication venue of "When you see me play, you see Larry play" and the "close my eyes and see that list" quote; the Esquinas settlement figure (reported ~$300k, never confirmed by either party).
- **Could not be fully accessed:** original 1993 NYT Atlantic City article and 2012 TIME piece (paywalled/archival; substance corroborated by multiple secondary carriers); The Last Dance itself (streaming only; quotes checked against multiple contemporaneous recaps).
- **Deliberately excluded per standing rules (S-22):** the sister's memoir allegations (contested), the 1993 suspension conspiracy (debunked), tabloid marriage material, static net-worth figures, and clinical/diagnostic language. The draft honors these exclusions; evaluators should not penalize their absence.
- **What this packet cannot settle:** whether the Type 3 hypothesis is correct. It records the strongest evidence on each side; the typing, the wing, the subtype, and both arrow mappings remain interpretations for the evaluators to pressure-test.

**Largest unresolved evidence gap:** the emotional anchor quotes ("This is for Daddy"; "When you see me play, you see Larry play"; the memorial "Now he's got me" prefix) are consistent across decades of coverage but none is pinned to a primary transcript — and one checkable detail (the coin-toss shrug, CLM-10) is affirmatively contradicted by the documented Last Dance scene.
