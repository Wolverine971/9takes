## Bottom-line verdict

Read as a proxy for Michael Jordan's fair representation, this draft is one confirmed factual error and a handful of overclaims away from being something the subject could not reasonably call unfair. The portrait is recognizable, built overwhelmingly from his own words, and it repeatedly does the thing subjects almost never get: it explains his cruelest behavior without excusing it and lets him grow past his worst decade. The sympathetic reframe of the Hall of Fame speech even matches Jordan's own on-record self-account ("If you understood it from my perspective then you would understand" — Rashad/NBA-TV), which the draft apparently arrived at independently. But one load-bearing anecdote is affirmatively inverted against him (the coin-pitching shrug — the documented scene has the security guard beating Jordan and shrugging at _him_), the piece's "manufactured grievances" engine is pluralized from a single admitted instance, and the ledger omits Jordan's own on-podium epistemic hedge about the freeze-out — three moves that each make him look slightly worse than the record supports, all in the same direction. Trust: **strained**, repairable. Recommendation: **revise**.

## What landed

**SUBJECT-H1 — The empathy turn explains without exonerating.** "If your name is only as safe as the last scoreboard, a teammate coasting through practice is a hand reaching for your name. … The cruelty was real, and Perdue's word for him stands." This is the fairness test passed at its hardest point: the draft gives the punches a comprehensible interior logic while explicitly refusing to launder them. Perdue's "a**hole" survives; so does Jordan's humanity. Must survive revision — it is the piece's ethical spine.

**SUBJECT-H2 — The Hall of Fame reframe is Jordan's own reading, independently reached.** "He was not taking a victory lap. He was disclosing his fuel source, in front of the fire marshals, while crying." My research confirms Jordan told Ahmad Rashad essentially this: critics saw the worst speech, but "if you understood it from my perspective then you would understand," and "I'll go to my grave thinking, I said what I wanted to say" (CBS Chicago coverage of the NBA-TV interview). The draft grants him the interpretation he publicly asked for _while keeping Wojnarowski's criticism standing_ ("The criticism holds up… Leroy Smith gained nothing"). That is exactly what fair looks like. Preserve.

**SUBJECT-H3 — The 1978 correction is precision, not gotcha.** "The first entry in his catalog of injustices was edited by its own author." The draft corrects the "cut" myth via the TIME record, names the defensible coaching logic (Leroy Smith's height), and then explains _why_ his memory made the edit — without mocking him for it. A subject can live with having his self-mythology corrected this carefully.

**SUBJECT-H4 — He speaks in his own vocabulary throughout.** Fire and wood, "refuge," "that's who I am," "it was personal," the 60 Minutes self-assessment carrying the entire gambling section's interpretive weight. The thesis is repeatedly built out of sentences Jordan actually said rather than sentences said about him. Recognizability is high.

**SUBJECT-H5 — The piece lets him change.** "It took thirty years for the conviction to outrank the sponsor, and when it finally did, the sponsor was named Jordan." Plus the NASCAR generosity note ("litigating other people's security into existence") and the NBC motive quoted with the honest observation that it "had no scoreboard in it at all." The draft does not freeze him at 1993. Preserve all three beats.

**SUBJECT-H6 — The exclusions are honored.** No sister's-memoir allegations, no 1993-suspension conspiracy, no marriage tabloid material, no addiction diagnosis, no static net-worth number. The gambling section stays inside what he himself told Ed Bradley. This is public-record discipline the subject would notice.

## What missed

**The coin-pitching scene is inverted against him** (blocker SUBJECT-B1 below). The one documented shrug in that record was aimed _at Jordan_, by a guard who had just beaten him.

**His on-podium honesty about the freeze-out is erased** (SUBJECT-C1). On the Hall of Fame stage Jordan said of the freeze-out, "no matter what people may have said, if it was a rumor I never took it as truth" (per the packet's transcript, S-01). The draft's ledger row and speech section cite the freeze-out as a grievance he "filed" without the hedge — making him look like a man who believed a rumor, when his actual words show him burning a rumor for fuel while disclaiming belief. The omitted hedge _supports_ the draft's own thesis (truth was never the input variable) while giving him more epistemic credit. Leaving it out is the unfair version of the same point.

**The "invention" pattern is pluralized from one admitted case** (SUBJECT-C2). "He told teammates about insults that were never spoken. He avenged compliments that never happened" — plural, in the intro; "some of the slights were composed from nothing" — plural, in the diagnosis; "Why He Invented Enemies" and "keep inventing insults" in the metadata. The record contains exactly one admitted invention (LaBradford Smith). The freeze-out was other people's rumor, hedged; the Russell taunt is undisputed as real. The single case is spectacular and thesis-carrying on its own; the pluralization is the draft quietly claiming a documented _habit_ where it has a documented _instance_.

## What surprised me

- **The Bobcats failure handled as the thesis's hardest test, with dignity.** "He owned the wall the list hung on" and "the one scoreboard he finally owned measured him nightly and refused him the only lever he ever trusted, himself" — the worst season in NBA history is presented as tragedy with structure, not as a punchline. Subjects rarely get their failures treated as evidence of their machinery rather than their inadequacy.
- **"Praise has no shelf in the archive."** The Bird-compliment paragraph is a genuinely fresh observation about his memory, and it is fair — it describes what the record shows (he never built a game around a compliment) without diagnosing what he felt.
- **The restraint on gambling.** The draft lets his own 60 Minutes sentences carry the entire interpretive load and adds no totals and no clinical language. Unexpected, and correct.

## Red flags

**SUBJECT-B1 (BLOCKER) — The coin-toss shrug is a documented inversion.**

- **Passage:** "Security guards roped into pitching coins for twenty-dollar bills, Jordan hitting them with the famous shrug when he won." ("What happened when Michael Jordan ran out of scoreboards")
- **Reader effect / trust problem:** Paints Jordan taunting his own employees after taking their money — petty-tyrant flavor, in the paragraph establishing his compulsive pattern. A reader who has seen The Last Dance Ep 6 knows the scene runs the other way.
- **Evidence:** Packet Disputes §1, S-19 (NBC Sports), S-25 (Action Network): guard John Michael Wozniak _won_ the $20 quarters bet and did Jordan's shrug _at Jordan_. The packet found no source for the draft's version and instructs evaluators to treat it as a factual error.
- **Minimum viable repair:** Rewrite the sentence to the documented scene — e.g., quarters pitched with his security guards for twenty-dollar bills, one guard beating him and hitting him with his own shrug. Drop "roped into" (coercion flavor with no source). The corrected version _serves the thesis better_: he built stakes so real that losing $20 to his own security guard was an event worth filming.
- **Expected benefit:** Removes the draft's only affirmatively contradicted claim; converts a fairness liability into a stronger beat.
- **Confidence:** High.
- **Acceptance test:** The sentence matches S-19/S-25 (Wozniak wins; shrug aimed at Jordan); no remaining claim that Jordan shrugged at guards he beat; no unsourced coercion framing.

**SUBJECT-C1 (concern) — The freeze-out hedge is omitted.**

- **Passage:** Ledger row "1985 | All-Star 'freeze-out' … | Cited it from the Hall of Fame podium 24 years later"; also the HOF section's "The freeze-out." list.
- **Trust problem:** His actual podium words — "no matter what people may have said, if it was a rumor I never took it as truth" (S-01 transcript) — show him disclaiming belief while using the rumor as fuel. Omitting the hedge makes him look credulous where the record shows him precise, and the ledger's own "What the record shows" column is the natural home for it.
- **Minimum viable repair:** Add the hedge to the 1985 row's record column (e.g., "Participants deny it; Jordan himself said on the podium he 'never took it as truth'") or one clause in the HOF section.
- **Expected benefit:** More accurate portrait _and_ a sharper thesis — he cites fuel he doesn't even claim is true.
- **Confidence:** High (transcript on file per packet).
- **Acceptance test:** The hedge appears wherever the freeze-out is used as a grievance exhibit.

**SUBJECT-C2 (concern) — Pluralized invention claims exceed the documented record.**

- **Passages:** Intro: "He told teammates about insults that were never spoken. He avenged compliments that never happened." Diagnosis: "some of the slights were composed from nothing"; "on nights the league failed to supply one, he handled the sourcing himself." Frontmatter: meta_title "Why He Invented Enemies"; description "keep inventing insults."
- **Trust problem:** One invention is admitted and documented (LaBradford Smith). "Some," "insults" (plural), "enemies" (plural), and "kept inventing" assert an iterated habit the public record supports once. The subject could fairly say: I admitted one.
- **Minimum viable repair:** Either ground the iteration honestly (e.g., "at least once, by his own admission, from nothing") and let the LaBradford case carry the engine, or add a second sourced instance of invention. Soften the metadata plurals or accept them knowingly as the one place the packaging outruns the proof.
- **Expected benefit:** The thesis engine keeps its power and becomes unimpeachable.
- **Confidence:** High on the documented count; medium on severity (Jordan grinned through the admission and has never disputed the "manufactured slights" characterization of himself).
- **Acceptance test:** Every plural or iterative invention claim in body and metadata is either supported by ≥2 sourced instances or reworded to the admitted single case.

**SUBJECT-C3 (concern) — Invented first-person interiority at the grief apex.**

- **Passage:** `<p class="inner-thought">Three years of box scores he never saw. Every basket tonight went up on the one scoreboard I can't check.</p>` (Father's Day section)
- **Trust problem:** The draft authors thoughts inside Jordan's head about his murdered father, and the authored thought converts his grief into the article's scoreboard metaphor — at the one moment the surrounding prose says the scoreboard turned _off_. Even rendered as a marked device, this is the piece's most invasive move: the subject's dead father recast in the article's vocabulary, not his own. His actual on-air words ("I know he's watching. This is for Daddy") already do this work.
- **Minimum viable repair:** Rebuild the inner-thought line from his own recorded language (watching, "for Daddy"), or move the scoreboard observation into the author's voice in the following paragraph, where it already half-exists ("the one day the calendar names for fathers, the bill came due").
- **Expected benefit:** Keeps the interior beat the piece needs without fabricating cognition about a murdered parent.
- **Confidence:** Medium (the device may be house style and visually marked; the fabrication concern stands regardless).
- **Acceptance test:** No first-person sentence in Jordan's voice asserts thoughts the record doesn't contain; the grief section's metaphors are attributable to the author or to Jordan's actual quotes.

**SUBJECT-C4 (concern) — Totalizing absolutes the article itself contradicts.**

- **Passages:** "a man who cannot feel his own worth unless a witness is watching him prove it" (TL;DR); "Jordan registered nothing else" (diagnosis); "his has always been crowd-issued" (Rabbit Hole); "the only unrehearsed thing he had done in years" (Father's Day).
- **Trust problem:** The draft documents its own counterexamples — the baseball year as private tribute, the same-day Kerr apology, the NBC motive with "no scoreboard in it at all." Absolute interior claims ("cannot," "nothing else," "always," "only") assert a certainty the public record cannot support and that the piece's own evidence undercuts.
- **Minimum viable repair:** Soften two or three of the absolutes to near-absolutes, or explicitly attribute them to the type lens ("the Three's claim would be…"). The rhetoric survives; the overreach doesn't.
- **Expected benefit:** The typing reads as an argued hypothesis (which the packet requires) rather than a verdict on his psyche.
- **Confidence:** Medium.
- **Acceptance test:** Interior-state absolutes are either hedged, attributed to the framework, or supported by his own words in the same passage.

**SUBJECT-C5 (concern) — The packaging is harsher than the piece.**

- **Passages:** persona_title "Basketball's Grievance Machine"; meta_title "Why He Invented Enemies."
- **Trust problem:** The body earns complexity — grief, growth, tribute, apology; the front-facing labels reduce him to a mechanism and a fabricator. A subject who read only what search and social surfaces show would encounter the totalizing version the body carefully avoids.
- **Minimum viable repair:** Keep "grievance" if desired but drop "machine" for something the body supports (the ledger frame is available: e.g., a "grievance ledger" title), and align the meta_title with SUBJECT-C2's repair.
- **Expected benefit:** The article's fairness extends to the surfaces most people actually see.
- **Confidence:** Medium (SEO conventions are real; this is a tradeoff, not an error).
- **Acceptance test:** Persona/meta titles make no claim the body wouldn't defend under SUBJECT-C2's standard.
