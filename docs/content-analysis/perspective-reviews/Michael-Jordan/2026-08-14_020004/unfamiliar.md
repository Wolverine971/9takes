## Bottom-line verdict

A reader who knows only "famous basketball player, the shoe guy" can finish this article oriented, engaged, and able to restate the thesis in one sentence: Michael Jordan is an Enneagram Type 3, a man whose sense of worth only exists while an audience watches him prove it, so when the world stopped supplying doubters he manufactured his own. The cold open (a 62-year-old legend shaking over one backyard free throw) is the best kind of hook for an outsider because it inverts the one thing even a non-fan "knows" about Jordan — fearlessness — in the first two paragraphs. Jargon discipline is excellent: every Enneagram term used in the main body is translated into plain language at first use, and the deep-theory material is quarantined in a clearly labeled skippable accordion.

## Red flags

All six are concerns; none rises to a blocker.

- **UNFAMILIAR-R1 — "Freeze-out" is used twice and never defined.**
  - _Passage:_ grievance ledger, 1985 row: "All-Star 'freeze-out' by jealous veterans"; and the HOF section: "The freeze-out. The doubting reporters. Jerry Krause."
  - _Reader effect:_ I cannot evaluate the row at all — I don't know what the alleged offense _was_ (Was he benched? Excluded from the team?). A grievance table whose entry can't be pictured teaches nothing.
  - _Evidence:_ the shared packet also never defines it; my one orientation search confirmed it is disputed NBA-insider lore (veteran All-Stars, allegedly led by Isiah Thomas, denying rookie Jordan the ball at the 1985 All-Star Game; he scored 7 points), not general knowledge.
  - _Minimum viable repair:_ one defining clause at first use, in the table row or a footnote-style addition — enough that the alleged act is picturable.
  - _Expected benefit:_ the 1985 row becomes evaluable and the HOF grievance list stops leaking readers.
  - _Confidence:_ high. _Acceptance test:_ a reader who has never watched an NBA game can say what allegedly happened at the 1985 All-Star Game after reading only this article.

- **UNFAMILIAR-R2 — Jerry Krause is named with zero identification.**
  - _Passage:_ HOF section: "The freeze-out. The doubting reporters. Jerry Krause. Bryon Russell's gym taunt, re-enacted."
  - _Reader effect:_ every other item in that grievance list is either explained elsewhere or self-describing; "Jerry Krause" is a bare name. It creates the question "who is this and what did he do to Jordan?" and the article never answers it — the exact failure mode the unfamiliar-reader lane exists to catch.
  - _Evidence:_ the name appears exactly once in the reader-visible draft; no identifying clause anywhere.
  - _Minimum viable repair:_ a two-to-five-word appositive (the Bulls general manager he feuded with) or cut the name from the list — the list survives without it.
  - _Expected benefit:_ no dangling proper noun in the article's most emotionally important section.
  - _Confidence:_ high. _Acceptance test:_ every proper noun in the HOF grievance list is either identified in-line or established earlier in the article.

- **UNFAMILIAR-R3 — The 1994 ledger row depends on information delivered three sections later.**
  - _Passage:_ grievance ledger, 1994 row: "'Why'd you quit? I could guard you,' from Bryon Russell | Said to a retired baseball player at a Chicago gym."

  - _Expected benefit:_ the table becomes fully self-contained, which matters because tables are precisely what skimmers read out of order.
  - _Confidence:_ high. _Acceptance test:_ a first-time reader encountering the table before any later section can identify who Russell taunted and why "quit" was the word.
