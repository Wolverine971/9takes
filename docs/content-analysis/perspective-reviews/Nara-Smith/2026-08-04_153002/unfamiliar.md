## Bottom-line verdict

I came in knowing nothing about Nara Smith and left able to state who she is, why she matters, what the article claims, and what the type answer is. That is the core test and the draft passes it. The orientation machinery — the cold open, the TL;DR accordion, the key-stat block, the in-frame/out-of-frame table, the question-shaped H2s — is unusually good at carrying a stranger through 4,500 words.

Two things strained my trust, and both are cheap to fix. The article asserts a reporting relationship with Forbes that did not happen, and it stages invented first-person interior monologue about a real woman during her child's cancer diagnosis without any signal that it is authored rather than documented.

The comprehension gap that cost me the most is different from the ones the draft seems to anticipate. The Enneagram material is handled well for a newcomer. What is missing is the political charge of the word "tradwife." The draft only ever conveys the label through the three neutral-sounding clauses she rebuts (homemaking, financial dependence, dishes), and never tells me the term is bound up with an ultraconservative and far-right-adjacent movement. Without that, I could not understand why being called it is an injury rather than a quibble, and I could not parse the single strongest criticism in the piece: Tia Levings' "Your own content has bolstered that oppression" points at an antecedent the article never supplies.

Revise. None of this requires new research or restructuring. The blockers are a two-word attribution fix and one clause of invented fact; the top concern is roughly one sentence placed early.

## Red flags

### UNFAM-B1 — Invented interior monologue is presented as documented experience (BLOCKER)

**Passage.** L385: "She has cancer. I know she has. Nobody has said it. The room has said it. Now I carry it for a week, put dinner on the table, and say nothing to anyone, including the person beside me who would hear it in my voice." And L316: "They handed me a word invented for a picture I am not in..."

**Reader effect.** The `.inner-thought` styling (thought-bubble icon, italic, tinted panel — `src/scss/blog.scss:592`) tells me I am reading a thought rather than speech. It does not tell me whose composition it is. In an article this dense with genuine quotation, a first-person italic panel attached to a named living person reads as her interiority, reported. I took the L385 passage as a claim about what actually happened in that week.

**Evidence.** The passage asserts a specific unsourced fact: that she told no one, including her husband, for a week. Nothing in the evidence packet supports it. The only nearby evidence runs the other way in tone — she reports telling Lucky "This is it. Let's just brace ourselves" at the point the call came (packet, "On the eight months"). "Put dinner on the table" has no source at all. Separately, L316 sits immediately after the Wicker paragraph about Black women and exclusion, so "a picture I am not in" reads as Nara answering the race argument; the packet is explicit that "No located interview has Nara herself discussing being biracial in Germany or racism she experienced. Any first-person framing of this would be unsupported."

**Minimum viable repair.** Two changes, no restructuring. (1) Cut the unevidenced clause at L385 — end the beat at "The room has said it." (2) Add a four-to-six-word authorial signal to the first inner-thought a reader meets, e.g. opening it with "Read it from where she stood:" so the device is keyed once. Alternatively move L316 ahead of the Wicker paragraph so it cannot be read as her reply to it.

**Expected benefit.** Removes the only place where a stranger cannot distinguish reporting from authorship, in the two most sensitive passages in the article.

**Confidence.** High on L385 (the clause states an unsourced fact). Medium-high on L316 (placement implication).

**Acceptance test.** A reader who has never seen a 9takes article can, on first read, correctly say which sentences in these two panels Nara Smith is on record as having said or thought — and no sentence in either panel asserts a private action that the packet does not source.
