## Identity and scope

**Nate Bargatze** (born **March 25, 1979**, Nashville, Tennessee; raised in the Old Hickory
neighborhood). Age 47 as of the draft date. Stand-up comedian, actor, writer, producer. Founder of
Nateland Entertainment (2023). Known for deadpan, monotone delivery and clean material. _(verified
fact — Wikipedia, corroborated by Biography.com and his two official bios.)_

No aliases. "Bargatze" is a rare surname with no real-world collision, though the query
`nate bargatze personality type` pulls in Big Nate, Nate Archibald, and Nate Jacobs, and
RogerEbert.com's own review URL misspells him "bergatze."

### _This Past Weekend w/ Theo Von_ #660 — 2026-05-27 (Tier 1, pulled fresh 2026-08-25)

Video `xQ2UheBJgME`, uploader "Theo Von," duration 7,149 s. Auto-transcript; speaker turns marked
`>>` and merged, so attributions were disambiguated by context. **This is a two-Southern-male-comedian
podcast — the attribution trap documented in the repo applies.** Timestamps are auto-transcript
approximations.

- **On funding the park (~81:23):** Von asks if he is putting his own money in with someone else.
  "No, just my money." Then: "This doesn't go anywhere. I will be in big trouble. No, I mean I would
  be touring for the rest of — but it's like **right now** it's on. It's just me."
  _What it supports:_ that he described himself as the sole funder as of May 2026.
  _What it cannot support:_ that he is the sole funder permanently, or that no investors are
  planned. **He hedges it himself with "right now,"** and trade coverage six months earlier said the
  team would approach investors after the feasibility study (S-12).
- **On the cost (~76:43):** "it's all budgeted in like... we're looking it's like **roughly going to
  be like $350 million**." _This is his own pre-construction estimate, hedged twice._ It was
  independently restated by WSMV in May 2026 (S-11), which is what makes the figure citable.
- **On Opryland (~81:59):** "my first job was a theme park. I worked at Opryland here in Nashville
  and I was 15... then they got rid of that **my senior year in high school** and the whole city kind
  of was like why would you do that? Like it was a profitable theme park. Everybody kind of liked it."
  _Note:_ the "senior year" detail is **his recollection and it does not match the record** — see
  CLM-06.
- **On the rides (~83:00):** Von asks if he remembers a coaster's name. **"I know them all."** He
  names the Wabash Cannonball and the "screaming delta demon."
- **On his Opryland jobs (~83:27):** "I was a sweeper and I worked in the dog kennel and my dad did
  magic there."
- **On the Bridgestone goal (~83:46–84:01):** "my big daydreaming goal when I was starting comedy was
  to headline Bridgestone... when I did it for the first time I remember that night I really was like
  what am I supposed to do now because I just thought about that for 20 years."
- **The ticket count (~84:13–84:21), full exchange as transcribed:**
  - [84:04, Bargatze] "which is crazy. It was 20 years pretty much I think exactly for me to get there
    to headline Bridgestone" — [84:12, Von] "congratulations on that dude."
  - [84:13] **"And we sold 19,365 tickets which is 20 years. Isn't that crazy?"**
  - [84:19, Von] "Dang."
  - [84:19] **"If you're into — I don't know. I'm not even into numbers."**
  - [84:21, Von] "You sold 19,365 tickets." Then the 19 + 365 decomposition is worked out **in
    dialogue, across both speakers**, _after_ the disclaimer.
    _What it supports:_ he volunteered the count, glossed it as "20 years," and disclaimed interest in
    numbers seconds later. _What it cannot support:_ a precise elapsed-time claim — see CLM-01. The
    draft's "Four seconds" is a rhetorical rounding; by the transcript clock the gap from "Isn't that
    crazy?" to "not even into numbers" is ~2–3 s, and from the start of the ticket count ~6–8 s. Neither
    equals four, and auto-transcript timestamps are not precise enough to settle it.
- **On purpose (~84:52):** "what's the purpose of me... and I thought it can't be about me, right?
  **This life cannot be about me or I'm going to go crazy**, right? And so from that moment is when I
  started Nateland the company."
- **On the park not being a monument (~85:20):** "This theme park is not going to be about me. It's
  about the state of Tennessee... it's called Nateland and there's going to be Easter-eggish stuff,
  but **people think it's going to be like a shrine to me. It's not.**"
- **On the name (~85:51–86:18):** "I think it's the trust. The reason it's named after me is the
  trust. If I can keep **trust** with my audience... if they can **trust** that they know that they
  can see what I'm doing..." then "the responsibility of that park thriving is on me. I need to sell
  the tickets. I need to keep the **trust** that I have with this audience. That's on me. That's not
  anybody else's problem. I'll do it. That's what I feel has been bestowed upon me. I'm just doing
  what I feel I'm being asked to do. **Called to.**"
  _Note on the Rabbit Hole's count:_ "trust" occurs **five** times, and the passage is **interrupted
  by Von** ("I see. So, you're putting yourself behind"). The draft's "four times in a single answer"
  is close but not exact on either half.

**Verified failures and pressure points.**

- _**The Breadwinner**_ (2026-05-29, TriStar/Sony; dir. Eric Appel; written by Bargatze and Dan
  Lagana). **RT 20% from 41 critics, avg 4.1/10; Metacritic 33/100 from 13 critics; CinemaScore A−;
