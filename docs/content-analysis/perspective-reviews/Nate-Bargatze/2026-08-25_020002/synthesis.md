## Executive verdict

The argument is right and the evidence base is not. Every perspective independently rated the piece a
`clear_hit` on delight, and the reason is the same in all six: the draft noticed that the
self-erasure and the empire are one behavior rather than a contradiction, and it proved that before
naming it. Nothing below asks for that to change. The Emmys running-balance section, the ADHD
concession, and the counterargument section's admission of its own ceiling are better than the genre
and must survive intact.

What has failed is the layer underneath. Twelve claims are wrong, misattributed, unsourced, or
shaped in the subject's favor against the record — and the pattern the subject evaluator identified
is the thing that converts them from correctable errors into a fairness problem: **every numeric
error in the piece is wrong in the direction that makes the sentence land better.** Nineteen years
and 365 days (it is twenty-one). Four seconds (two to three, or six to eight). Nine words (fourteen).
Eleven days (ten). Four mentions of "trust" (five). Four specials (five). A writer whose thesis is
that this man notices arithmetic is not noticing arithmetic, and a reader can catch four of those six
without leaving the page.

Three failures are structural rather than incidental. The closing sentence asserts the park is rising
on the old Opryland ground; the same article says a mall is there, and the record says the site is
undisclosed — the piece spends its entire accumulated credit on a sentence a first-time reader
falsifies from the article's own text. The cold open, the piece's showcase, is built on a coincidence
that does not exist. And the tour clause is wrong three ways while omitting the Guinness world record
set nine days before the draft date — the single most on-thesis fact available about the subject, and
the only one that measures the ambition the article says is hidden. Five of six perspectives found
that omission independently. That is the strongest convergence in the jury.

Two perspectives (critic, subject) recommended `hold_for_research` over `revise`, on the grounds that
the memoir and The Atlantic cannot be settled at the desk. I am adjudicating this as **revise, with
two publication gates**. The critic and subject are right that neither RQ-01 nor RQ-02 can be closed
by editing — but both have honest desk repairs available that are not improvisation: stop asserting
what is not sourced. P0-12 removes the assertion of an unverified childhood event without waiting on
the book, and RQ-02's fallback narrows what the unverifiable quotes are asked to carry. The piece can
be revised now. It cannot be published with the assertive versions restored until those two
questions close.

One packet finding is overturned. CLM-07 grades the alcoholism claim "UNSUPPORTED, and contradicted
in emphasis," with the parent's gender "apparently transposed." The subject evaluator found Kirkus
reporting from the memoir itself, and I verified it directly: "his paternal grandparents were
alcoholics, the grandmother 'basically what these days they'd call abusive,' unforgiving of his
father's speech impediment." The bare fact is supported by his own book. The draft's real offense is
narrower and different from what the packet described — see P0-07.

The binding operational constraint: the reader-visible body is **4,497 words against a hard 4,500
ceiling** (reproduced with `blog-lint.sh`'s own counting method). Three words. Every repair below
that adds text must be funded by a cut, and the second pass already declined one fix for exactly this
reason. Banking headroom is step zero of the revision, not an optional cleanup.
