## Bottom-line verdict

The Type 6 call is well supported and I would not overturn it. The draft does the three things most
public-figure typing refuses to do: it gives the strongest alternative a real hearing, it marks its
own decisive move as a reading rather than a proof, and it states a falsifier out loud. It also
refuses, in the text, to convert two social-worker parents into a childhood mechanism. That paragraph
buys more credibility than any single piece of evidence in the piece.

What holds it back is not the verdict but the machinery underneath it. The argument runs almost
entirely at the level of behaviour — what he does, repeatedly, in public — and never names the motive
that would make the behaviour a type rather than a habit. The Six's actual engine, in this site's own
material, is a person who cannot ratify their own read and goes outside for the ratification. The
draft describes that engine beautifully in its closing paragraph and never says it. Meanwhile the
discriminator it does lean on is the one most exposed to the shape of the available sources, its
stated falsifier cannot be met by any evidence the piece can reach, and the one growth-line paragraph
reads the same event in the opposite direction from the close.

None of that is a trust break. All of it is repairable without new reporting, mostly by using house
material the draft already links to or already has. Zero blockers. Nine concerns, and the first two
are cheap fixes with disproportionate payoff.

## What landed

**ENNEAGRAM-H1 — The persona reframe (L168–170).**

> "Over-preparing is not what he does instead of being socially uneasy. It is one of the things
> producing it, and then the camera finds it."

This is the piece's best type-work and it never uses a type word. It takes the internet's binary — is
the awkward guy real or a bit — and replaces it with a causal loop in which the preparation and the
awkwardness are the same phenomenon seen twice. That is exactly what a motivational typology is
supposed to buy you over a trait list, and a reader who has never heard of the Enneagram gets the
insight anyway. Must survive revision verbatim.

**ENNEAGRAM-H2 — The empathy turn (L228–230).**

> "The apparatus is not a trap he builds for other people. It is the thing he built to get through a
> room, and he hands it out because it is what he has."

Motive without exculpation. The very next sentence declines to settle the account, which is the part
that makes it honest rather than a defence brief. This is how a type lens should touch an ethical
charge: it explains the origin of a behaviour and explicitly does not convert the explanation into a
verdict about its effects.

**ENNEAGRAM-H3 — The refusal to manufacture a wound (L146).**

> "Nothing in the public record establishes that his parents' profession caused anything… It is
> texture. It is not a mechanism, and a profile that pretends otherwise is inventing a childhood wound
> because the shape of the story wants one."

My contract asks me to catch trauma being smuggled in as type proof. The draft catches it first, in
public. Preserve.

**ENNEAGRAM-H4 — A falsifier exists at all (L248).** Stating what would change the call is rare and
correct, even though the specific falsifier chosen has a problem (ENNEAGRAM-C4).

**ENNEAGRAM-H5 — Phobic/counterphobic handled as a mode, not a phase (L188).**

> "Then he did the counterphobic thing, which is where Sixes stop looking anxious and start looking
> reckless."

I checked this against house material expecting an error and did not find one.
`src/blog/enneagram/enneagram-type-6.md:226` says most Sixes carry both tendencies and shift between
them by domain. Treating the FAA confrontation as a counterphobic move by a man read as otherwise
guarded is house-consistent, not a contradiction.

**ENNEAGRAM-H6 — Type 5 gets a real hearing (L136–138), and the concession is genuine:** flat affect,
encyclopedic preparation, observational remove, difficulty with unstructured time, plus Stone's
"observer of human behavior" conceded as "a Five sentence." Most drafts strawman the alternative.
This one hands it the best evidence in the file before answering it.

## What missed

**The type argument stays outside the person.** The diagnosis section opens "The Type 6 pattern is
anticipatory" (L132) and everything downstream is a catalogue of anticipatory acts: a degree, a
licence, a replica bar, twenty dollars. That is a description of a coping strategy. It is not a
motive, and Six is not the only type that prepares. The reason the pattern is Six-shaped — that he
does not trust his own read of whether a thing is settled, and so needs a person outside himself to
close it — is the piece's actual thesis and appears only as narration, never as the claim. See
ENNEAGRAM-C6 and ENNEAGRAM-I1.

**The decisive move is the most fragile one.** The 5-vs-6 tiebreaker (L138) is carried by two
instances, both drawn from promotional Q&As about public projects. The house corpus contains a
sturdier discriminator that the draft does not use. ENNEAGRAM-C3, ENNEAGRAM-C5.

**The argument has no residue.** Every fact in the piece is absorbed by the thesis, including the
facts that resist it. "The entire operating system" (L210) and "the last move has not changed" (L265)
are totalizing phrasings, and the packet is holding a first-person statement that would have
complicated them. ENNEAGRAM-C7.

## What I expected

- **Core fear and core desire, named once.** A Type 6 piece that never says what a Six is afraid of.
  House material states it plainly — "Prevent being unsafe, unsupported, or unprepared"
  (`enneagram-types-in-stress.md:239`) and "seeking guidance while scrutinizing the guide"
  (`enneagram-type-6.md:73`). Neither appears.
- **The head centre, in one clause.** Six sits in the fear/head triad with Five and Seven. That single
  fact is why Type 5 is the serious alternative rather than an arbitrary one, and it would let a lay
  reader understand the shape of the contest instead of taking the writer's word that Five is the
  competitor. Absent. ENNEAGRAM-I3.
- **The house discriminators, used.** Both exist and both are better than what the draft substituted:
  5-vs-6 at `enneagram-types-in-stress.md:230`, 1-vs-6 at `enneagram-type-6.md:494`. The draft
  reinvented both. Its 1-vs-6 replacement (rightness-held-unwitnessed vs believability-to-a-room) is
  compatible with house and defensible on evidence — I have no objection to it. Its 5-vs-6
  replacement is the weaker of the two swaps.
- **A "what this does not explain" line.** The Rabbit Hole has a case-against and a falsifier, which is
  most of the way there, but every listed alternative is answered. Nothing is left standing.
- **Instinct hedged.** Delivered: "Public material does not support a confident full stacking" (L242).
  Correct and welcome.

## What surprised me

**Welcome:** the Dumb Starbucks reading (L210) — pre-armouring against the fight he expected and being
taken out by the credential he had not thought to acquire — is the single best *structural* argument
for the type in the piece, because it is the pattern failing on its own terms rather than the writer
asserting it. It is also drawn from public record rather than self-report, which makes it immune to
the source problem that weakens the tiebreaker. It is currently doing less work than it could.

**Jarring:** the growth-line paragraph (L244) cites the release of *You Can See Everything* as
movement toward Nine, and the close (L265) cites the same release as proof the pattern has not moved
in twenty years. I read both twice to be sure. ENNEAGRAM-C1.

**ENNEAGRAM-C1 — Concern — The same event is read as growth and as unbroken compulsion, ~600 words
apart.**

- **Passage:** Rabbit Hole, L244: "That is Fielder abandoning staged material for John Wilson, building
  a character around what a performer actually did in the room, and releasing a film he says he still
  cannot fully account for." Close, L263–265: "what he was excited about was the film coming out, so
  that people could help him understand what he had been through… the last move has not changed: find
  somebody who was there, hand them something, and ask them to say it out loud."
- **Reader effect:** An Enneagram-literate reader who reads the Rabbit Hole before the close is told
  the film release is evidence of growth, then told it is the purest instance of the ungrown pattern.
  The piece's whole thesis is the second reading. The first quietly refutes it.
- **Evidence:** House growth doctrine makes these mutually exclusive on this exact axis.
  `enneagram-connecting-lines.md:180`: growth toward Nine means "They consult their own judgment,
  **stop polling everyone for certainty**, and take the next step before every 'what if' has an
  answer." The close is a portrait of polling for certainty, scaled up to a theatre. The release
  cannot be both.
- **Minimum viable repair:** Drop the film clause from the growth list at L244. The other two items —
  backing Wilson on limited proof of concept, building Cara around the performer — are clean
  growth-line evidence and carry the paragraph alone. If the writer wants to keep the double reading,
  it must be made explicit as a double reading (the act of releasing is Nine-ward; the reason he gives
  for wanting it released is not), not left for the reader to collide with.
- **Expected benefit:** Removes the only place where the piece argues against its own thesis, and
  sharpens the close by leaving it uncontested.
- **Confidence:** High.
- **Acceptance test:** *You Can See Everything* is cited as evidence for exactly one connecting-line
  reading, or the tension between the two readings is stated in the text.

**ENNEAGRAM-C3 — Concern — The 5-vs-6 tiebreaker rests on a source pool that guarantees the answer.**

- **Passage:** L138: "The tiebreaker is direction, and it is a reading rather than a proof. A Five's
  preparation tends toward self-sufficiency and needs no witness. Fielder's keeps arriving with an
  audience attached." Supported by two instances: the pilot motive and the twenty dollars.
- **Reader effect:** For a reader who has typed people before, this is the moment the argument is won
  or lost, and it is won on two anecdotes both told by the subject while promoting a public project.
  The hedge ("a reading rather than a proof") is present and correct, but it is generic — it does not
  name the specific reason to doubt.
- **Evidence:** Both instances come from promotional Q&A captions (packet F1, F2). The packet preserves
  the research memo's objection verbatim and this session's re-verification of it: the audience-facing
  quality of the evidence "may be an artefact of *where the evidence comes from* — promotional
  interviews about public projects — rather than a property of the man." A source pool composed of
  public talk about public work will over-produce witness-attached preparation for any subject,
  including an actual Five.
- **Minimum viable repair:** One sentence after the tiebreaker naming the limitation, and one anchor
  that does not depend on self-report. The Dumb Starbucks FAQ taped inside the store (L206–208) is
  already in the piece and is public-record: an argument written down in advance, addressed to an
  adversary who had not arrived, so that a stranger could read it. That is witness-directed
  preparation evidenced by an artifact rather than by an interview.
- **Expected benefit:** The tiebreaker stops being vulnerable to the single strongest objection
  available against it, and the piece models the epistemics it claims.
- **Confidence:** High that the objection is live; medium that a reader notices unaided.
- **Acceptance test:** The tiebreaker is supported by at least one instance that is not the subject's
  own retrospective account, and the source-pool limitation is named in the text.

**ENNEAGRAM-C4 — Concern — The stated falsifier cannot be satisfied by any evidence the piece can
reach.**

- **Passage:** L248: "sustained evidence that he prepares equally hard for things nobody will ever see
  or grade, with no audience to be credible to, would push this toward 5 or 1. The record available
  here keeps pointing the preparation outward, at a room."
- **Reader effect:** It reads as a genuine test that was run and passed. It is closer to a test that
  cannot be run. Public reporting is, by construction, reporting about public things; preparation with
  no audience is the category least likely to be documented about anyone. "The record keeps pointing
  outward" may describe the record rather than the man.
- **Evidence:** I searched for exactly this (see Research log). Two sources, no instance found — which
  is the point: absence here is uninformative, not confirming.
- **Minimum viable repair:** Restate the falsifier as something the evidence base could actually
  deliver. Two candidates, both reachable: (a) collaborator testimony describing him abandoning a
  prepared plan without needing anyone to sign off on the abandonment; (b) a documented instance of him
  seeking reassurance in a *private* domain, which would confirm rather than refute Six and can be
  sought from named collaborators. Then say plainly that unwitnessed preparation is not observable
  from public sources, so its absence is not evidence.
- **Expected benefit:** Converts a rhetorical gesture into a real commitment, which is the whole reason
  to include a falsifier.
- **Confidence:** High.
- **Acceptance test:** The falsifier names a class of evidence that a named source type could plausibly
  produce, and the piece does not treat its absence as support.

**ENNEAGRAM-C5 — Concern — The house 5-vs-6 discriminator is unused, and it is both stronger and
immune to ENNEAGRAM-C3.**

- **Passage:** L138, the invented tiebreaker, in place of the house one.
- **Evidence:** `enneagram-types-in-stress.md:230` — "Type 5 asks, 'Do I understand enough to handle
  this?' Type 6 asks, 'Have **we** found the danger and made this safe?'" The plural is the
  discriminator. Fielder does not prepare alone and never has: the rehearsal method is a company of
  people hired to run the check with him, replica sets are built by a crew he credits explicitly
  (packet F5), the aviation case was taken to a former NTSB member on television, and the film is
  handed to a theatre to be interpreted. A Five's preparation is characteristically solitary; his is
  institutional in its very form.
- **Reader effect (of the miss):** The piece leaves its best argument on the table and substitutes a
  weaker one. Worse, it already has all the evidence for the better one — it is the subject matter of
  four of the seven sections.
- **Minimum viable repair:** Add the "we" discriminator to L138 alongside the direction argument, drawn
  from the structure of the work rather than from interviews.
- **Expected benefit:** The tiebreaker becomes grounded in public record — what he built — instead of
  self-report, which neutralizes ENNEAGRAM-C3 at its root.
- **Confidence:** High.
- **Acceptance test:** The 5-vs-6 section contains at least one discriminator evidenced by the
  structure of his work rather than by something he said in an interview.

**ENNEAGRAM-C6 — Concern — The motive is never stated, only enacted.**

- **Passage:** L132, the diagnosis: "The Type 6 pattern is anticipatory. You run the bad version in
  advance so the real version cannot ambush you."
- **Reader effect:** A reader finishes able to say *what* Sixes do and unable to say *why*, which means
  the type has explained a habit rather than a person. It also leaves the piece weaker against Five,
  Three and One, all of which prepare.
- **Evidence:** House material states the motive repeatedly and the draft's own close describes it
  without naming it. `enneagram-type-6.md:73`: "seeking guidance while scrutinizing the guide."
  `enneagram-type-6.md:484`: "When you trust your own inner authority, you stop seeking external
  validation for internal knowing." `enneagram-types-in-stress.md:247`: "The deeper fear is facing
  danger without reliable support or confidence in one's own judgment." Compare the draft's close at
  L265: "find somebody who was there, hand them something, and ask them to say it out loud." That is
  the motive, rendered as image.
- **Minimum viable repair:** One or two sentences in the diagnosis section, in plain language, before
  the evidence list: the Six's difficulty is not danger, it is that their own read of the danger never
  feels authoritative, so the settlement has to come from outside. Then the twenty dollars, the licence
  and the theatre are three instances of one motive rather than three instances of a habit.
- **Expected benefit:** Turns a behaviour catalogue into a motivational argument, which is the only
  kind that can survive an alternative-type challenge. It also makes the close land harder, because
  the reader now recognizes what they are looking at.
- **Confidence:** High.
- **Acceptance test:** A reader who has finished the diagnosis section can state the Six's motive in
  one sentence without using the word "prepare."

**ENNEAGRAM-C7 — Concern — The strongest first-person disconfirmer is available and unused, and the
argument reads as totalizing without it.**

- **Passage:** No location — an omission. Contributing passages: L210, "the thing the entire operating
  system is trying not to be true"; L265, "the last move has not changed."
- **Evidence:** Packet F6 (caption-verified, Nolan Q&A 4:11–5:16): he says he does not really want to
  do any of the things he does and just ends up stumbling into them, that a project starts from an idea
  he got excited about and grows, and that going into *The Curse* he was most insecure about his

  anticipatory-planning reading" and records it as unused. Five other windows from the same caption

- **Reader effect:** Not a factual problem — a selection problem, and a knowledgeable reader who has
  seen the same Q&A will feel it. It is also what produces the frictionless quality of the argument:
  nothing in the piece resists the type.
- **Minimum viable repair:** Two to three sentences, best placed in the Rabbit Hole's case-against.
  Note that the reading is not obviously fatal — chronic uncertainty about one's own agency, and
  describing oneself as carried along rather than driving, is itself Six-consistent per the
  self-doubt material above — but say that it is the piece's live counterweight and that the
  self-deprecating promotional register cuts both ways. Paraphrase only: caption sources remain barred

- **Expected benefit:** The argument acquires residue, which is what distinguishes a diagnosis from a
  frame. It also converts the disconfirmer into a motivational data point rather than hiding it.
- **Confidence:** High.
- **Acceptance test:** The Rabbit Hole contains at least one first-person self-report that cuts against
  the anticipatory-planning thesis, engaged rather than absorbed.

**ENNEAGRAM-C8 — Concern — Wing certainty exceeds what public evidence supports, and the 6w7 rebuttal
does not address the obvious objection.**

- **Passage:** L240: "The wing is 6w5, and it is not a close call. A 6w7 discharges anxiety outward
  through humour, motion and company; Fielder's comedy is researched, structural and delivered at a
  remove."
- **Reader effect:** A reader who knows the system will notice two things. First, the sentence dismisses
  6w7 on the grounds that it "discharges anxiety outward through humour" while describing a man whose
  entire vocation is comedy, and the rebuttal is a claim about *style* of humour that is asserted, not
  evidenced. Second, the evidence offered for the 5 wing — analytic frame, willingness to sit and
  watch, difficulty with unstructured time — is the identical evidence the main body conceded as the
  Type 5 case at L136. Using one pool to argue "the 5-ness is a wing, not a core" and then "the wing
  is not a close call" reads as circular.
- **Evidence:** Wings are among the least determinable constructs from public material; nothing in the
  packet supports a wing call at higher confidence than the core call, and the core call is
  appropriately hedged while the wing call is not.
- **Minimum viable repair:** Delete "and it is not a close call," or replace it with the certainty the
  evidence supports ("the better fit is 6w5"). Add one clause acknowledging that his career is comedy
  and stating why that is not itself a 7-wing argument — the discriminator is whether humour is used to
  *discharge* discomfort or to *examine* it, and his is built to sit inside discomfort rather than
  release it.
- **Expected benefit:** Certainty calibrated to evidence, and the one objection every informed reader
  will raise is answered in advance.
- **Confidence:** Medium-high.
- **Acceptance test:** No wing claim in the piece carries higher stated certainty than the core-type
  claim.

**ENNEAGRAM-C9 — Concern — The main body presents a fact that appears to satisfy the Rabbit Hole's
falsifier, and the two are never connected.**

- **Passages:** L166: "His set-dressing standards reach details no camera will ever find." L248:
  "sustained evidence that he prepares equally hard for things nobody will ever see or grade… would
  push this toward 5 or 1."
- **Reader effect:** Read in sequence, the piece states a falsifier and appears to have already met it
  1,200 words earlier. The two *are* reconcilable — the camera is not the audience; the participant in
  the room is — and the Rabbit Hole's Type 1 paragraph immediately before the falsifier does the
  reconciling work. But it never names the collision, so a reader has to do it unaided, and a fast
  reader will not.
- **Evidence:** Packet F5 is the source of both. Two qualifications matter. First, he attributes the
  standard to participant experience, not correctness — which is what saves the falsifier. Second, he
  frames the labour as the crew's and credits the production designer and team explicitly; the draft
  renders it as "His set-dressing standards," which over-attributes a collective practice to an
  individual disposition and is the version that makes the collision look worse than it is.
- **Minimum viable repair:** One clause in the falsifier distinguishing camera-invisible from
  witness-free ("not hidden from the camera — hidden from everyone, with no room to be believed by"),
  and at L166 restore the participant-facing rationale so the fact arrives already reconciled.
- **Expected benefit:** Closes the piece's only apparent self-contradiction on the evidence, and fixes
  a small attribution overreach at the same time.
- **Confidence:** Medium-high.
- **Acceptance test:** The falsifier's wording cannot be satisfied by any fact already asserted in the
  main body.

## Specific improvements

Repairs for C1, C2, C8 and C9 are stated in full above and are not repeated. The four below are
constructive builds rather than fixes.

**ENNEAGRAM-I1 — Name the motive once, in the diagnosis section (repairs C6).** Insert before the
"Look at what he does when a stake appears" paragraph at L134. Plain language, no jargon: the Six's
problem is not that danger is everywhere, it is that their own verdict on the danger never feels
final, so someone else has to sign it. Benefit: every subsequent section stops being a separate
exhibit and becomes the same act repeated. Acceptance test as C6. Confidence: high.

**ENNEAGRAM-I2 — Add the collective discriminator to the 5-vs-6 contest (repairs C3 and C5).** At
L138, alongside "direction," add "company": a Five checks alone, and Fielder has never checked alone —
the method *is* hiring people to run the check with him. Evidence is the shape of the work, so it is
untouched by the promotional-source objection. Benefit: moves the decisive argument from two anecdotes
to four sections of public record. Confidence: high. Acceptance test as C5.

**ENNEAGRAM-I3 — One clause of centre framing, in the Type 5 paragraph (L136).** Say that Five, Six and
Seven are the three fear-centred types, which is why Five is the live alternative rather than an
arbitrary one. Reader effect now: the reader takes on faith that Five is the competitor. After: they
understand why, and the concession that follows reads as rigour rather than throat-clearing. Cost: one
sentence. Benefit: accessibility for the unfamiliar reader and rigour for the informed one, from the
same clause. Confidence: medium-high. Acceptance test: a reader can say why Type 5 and not, say, Type
2 is the alternative being tested.

**ENNEAGRAM-I4 — Promote Dumb Starbucks to a type argument, not only a narrative punchline (L206–210).**
It is currently the best evidence in the piece and is doing only narrative work. The FAQ taped inside
the store is a written argument prepared for an adversary who never came, addressed to strangers — a
public artifact, not a self-report — and the closure by a health permit is the pattern failing on its
own terms. One added sentence tying it to the motive named in I1 gives the piece an anchor that no
source-pool objection can touch. Benefit: the type call gains a leg that does not depend on anything
Fielder said about himself. Confidence: medium-high. Acceptance test: at least one type argument in
the piece rests entirely on a public artifact.
