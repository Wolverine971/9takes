the winnable editorial ground is the *persona-versus-person* interpretive question, not biography of record.
Evaluators should note this is the strategic frame the draft was commissioned under; it is a repository
position, not an external fact.

**Knowledge cutoff for this packet:** 2026-09-09. The documentary had premiered but had not opened
theatrically; no evaluator here has seen the film. Nothing in this packet supports a review of it.

**Named private third parties are out of bounds.** The entity-gap packet positively identified the

### F1 — The curse anecdote (SHOWTIME/Nolan Q&A, `etoG2I6gaHE`, 2:36–3:40)

**Status: attributed claim** (his own retrospective account in a promotional Q&A), **caption-verified as
paraphrase**.

The caption at 2:36 contains, in his narration: he had to get a cell phone because he had moved from Canada,
he was in **Westwood** going to the store, a woman asked for money, he had none, and she said she was
cursing him. At 3:08: he went into the store, could not stop thinking about it, states **he does not believe
in this stuff** but it was bothering him, then afterward went to an ATM, took out **$20**, returned, gave it
to her and asked if the curse was gone; she smiled and said yes. At 3:40: "And thus, the curse was born."

**What it supports:** the store→ATM→return sequence; the disbelief-plus-rumination pair; that the anecdote is
the stated origin of _The Curse_. The Westwood and cell-phone details — which are not recorded in any prior
repository research note — **are present in the caption** and are therefore supported, not invented.

**What it cannot support:** any date (no year is given); any claim about how the reassurance functioned
afterward; that the episode is representative of his behaviour generally. It is a story he tells about
himself while promoting a show whose premise it became.

**Speaker ambiguity (unresolved question).** The observation that he was lucky she was still there — "because
if she wasn't there, then you would never know if that was real or not… this curse would be essentially over
you forever. Anything that happened to you, you could potentially go back to that moment and use that as your
excuse" — appears inside the same unlabeled caption block, introduced by "Well, and I was like." Prior
supervisory audits placed it with **Safdie**, and the draft attributes it to Safdie. The caption alone does
not settle the speaker. Evaluators should treat the Safdie attribution as reasonable-but-unconfirmed rather
than as caption-verified.

### F2 — Why he trained as a pilot (Jack Black Q&A, `70xdc3T3oqk`, 18:21–19:54)

**Status: attributed claim, caption-verified as paraphrase. Directly re-checked for this packet.**

The caption has Fielder saying that when he started — "right when I had the basic idea" — he thought nobody
would take him seriously as someone investigating aviation, and that if they had to get pilots involved they
would assume it was unserious. The caption contains the fragment "I felt insecure." He then says he wanted to
become a pilot **"both to learn really what it's like and so I can understand it, but also it'll help me when
I'm talking to these pilots to be like, hey, I'm a pilot, too."** He adds that reaching commercial-pilot level
"takes a lot of work" and would "really help me like go through the mindset, but also sort of show that I'm
like trying to take this seriously."

**What it supports:** two motives — understanding and credibility — given **together and unranked**, in one
breath, in his own words. The draft's second-pass repair on this point (both motives preserved, neither
ranked, psychological weight marked as a reading) is **accurate and verified**.

**What it cannot support:** which motive weighed more; any equivalent motive for the 2005 commerce degree
(the aviation account cannot be applied retroactively); precise licence-issuance dates.

**Chronology this window establishes (material — see CLM-02).** The credential was pursued **from the point
he had the basic idea for the aviation season**, not before the project existed. At 17:20 Black says the
training took two years and infers Fielder must have known where the season was headed from the beginning;
Fielder does not dispute it. Reporting independently describes a ~2–2.5-year progression (private →
instrument → multi-engine → commercial → 737 type rating) completed during production and revealed in the
season 2 finale (May 2025).

### Dumb Starbucks (2014)

**Verified facts:** opened February 7, 2014 at 1802 Hillhurst Avenue, Los Feliz; replicated the Starbucks logo,
interior, uniforms and menu with "dumb" prefixed ("dumb tall, dumb grande and dumb venti"); an in-store FAQ made
the parody argument — "By adding the word 'dumb,' we are technically 'making fun' of Starbucks, which allows us
to use their trademarks under a law known as 'fair use'"; it drew queues and news crews; on February 10 Fielder
revealed himself as the operator.

**Attributed claim / disputed:** Starbucks Corporation said "We are evaluating next steps and while we
appreciate the humor, they cannot use our name, which is a protected trademark," and **never sued**. Legal
opinion was sceptical of the defence — Notre Dame law professor Mark McKenna: "My gut tells me a court would be
bothered by how much of the Starbucks trademark was used. It's not just the word but they also made the store
look just like it."

**The trademark question was never adjudicated.** No court tested the fair-use argument. This bears directly on
the draft's table cell "Survived the law" (CLM-06).

**Disputed claim — the closure.** Contemporaneous reporting is uniform that the LA County health department shut
it the same day for operating without a permit. Wikipedia additionally records that the Los Angeles County
Department of Health Services "has no records of an action against Dumb Starbucks." Evaluators should treat the
closure as well-reported but not administratively confirmed.

**Note on "not-for-profit":** VHEC and Wikipedia both use that phrase. Whether Summit Ice is a registered
non-profit entity, as opposed to a business donating 100% of profits, is not established here.

5. **The stated set-dressing standard** (attributed claim, F5) is *believability to the participant* — a

### Evidence against, and the closest alternative

**Type 5 is the serious alternative** and the research packet's type-challenge memo flags the draft's tiebreaker
as contestable:

  social time all read as Investigator competence-before-engagement.

  learning and credibility together, and public interviews disproportionately expose public work." The
  audience-facing quality of the evidence may be an artefact of *where the evidence comes from* — promotional
  interviews about public projects — rather than a property of the man. **This packet re-verified that the two
  motives are given together and unranked (F2), so the objection stands against the frozen draft.**

**Type 1** has some pull via the perfectionism, but the stated standard is participant-believability, not
correctness (F5). **Type 3** explains credential-collecting but not work built to risk looking foolish with
failures left in the edit. The draft's Type 3 counter was rebuilt in the second pass after the earlier version
rested on an unsupported stereotype; the current version is evidence-based.

### Behaviour the type does not explain

**How readily he gives authorship away** (verified/attributed): backing John Wilson on limited proof of concept

growth-to-Nine line covers some of this. The research memo's judgment — preserved, not endorsed — is that it does
not cover **the ease of it**.

### Discriminating evidence that would move the reading

The draft states its own falsifier: sustained evidence that he prepares equally hard for things nobody will ever
see or grade, with no audience to be credible to, would push toward 5 or 1. Evaluators should note that F5 is
partial evidence *of that kind* — details "no one is seeing" — but he attributes the standard to participant
experience and the labour to his crew, which cuts both ways.
