## Bottom-line verdict

The core call survives scrutiny. Type 6 is the best-supported motivational read of the public record, and the draft argues it the right way: from what Mahomes says is running in his head ("if this doesn't work right here"), from what he was afraid of losing on draft night (the coach, not the platform), and from what it took for him to believe his own knee (a trusted authority trying to break it). That is motivation-first typing, and it produces the piece's one genuinely non-obvious insight: the improvisation is the rehearsed part.

Two things break trust for an Enneagram-literate reader, which is exactly the reader this lane serves. First, the draft labels him **counterphobic** in the H3 and TL;DR while (a) the house Type 6 page it links defines counterphobic as moving toward fear and challenging authority, (b) the Chestnut subtype model the Rabbit Hole uses makes the counterphobic Six the _Sexual_ Six, not the self-preservation Six the draft assigns, and (c) the draft's own evidence and its own sentence ("a sexual Six attacks the threat head-on; Mahomes organizes around staying intact") describe a phobic Six. The TL;DR also redefines "counterphobic" to mean "calm when the worst case arrives," which is the prepared-phobic mechanism, not counterphobia. Second, the "specific dread" of Reid is illustrated with a sentence the packet verifies belongs to Travis Kelce.

Both repairs are cheap. The sp-3 alternative is named but not actually tested (its two tiebreakers are defined away inside the same paragraph), and the 6→3 stress arrow is used to absorb the 2025 collapse without a discriminator. Fix the label, fix the quote, give the Three case one honest paragraph, and this is a strong Six profile.

## What landed

**ENNEAGRAM-H1 — The Kelce trap as motivational evidence.** "In the back of my head all I think about is, if this doesn't work right here... I just make sure, if I do throw the no-look pass or the sidearm pass, that I make sure it's completed." Followed by: "That is the sentence the Seven reading cannot survive." Why it must survive revision: this is the single best piece of discriminating first-person evidence in the piece. It locates anticipatory worst-case ideation _inside_ the exact behavior the crowd reads as Seven spontaneity. A Seven's inner monologue during a flashy play is not "if this doesn't work." The draft found the motivation under the behavior instead of typing the behavior.

**ENNEAGRAM-H2 — The Six's crisis-competence, stated accessibly.** "Its signature is that the most anxious person in the room becomes the steadiest one when the crisis is real, because the crisis has been rehearsed." And the 13-second payoff: "The situation was new to the stadium. It was not new to him." Why it must survive: this is correct type theory (Sixes are often the calmest people in a genuine emergency) delivered in plain language with an on-field proof. It is the paragraph a non-Enneagram reader will remember.

**ENNEAGRAM-H3 — The object of draft-night fear.** "He had a second or third-round grade from the draft advisory board. He went tenth. And the thing he was afraid of losing was the coach." Why it must survive: this is the strongest 6-versus-3 discriminator in the entire piece, and the draft does not even flag it as one. A Three's draft-night fear is about the platform, the slot, the money, the spotlight. Mahomes's stated fear was about losing a specific guide. The Rabbit Hole should point back to this sentence instead of re-arguing the helmet.

**ENNEAGRAM-H4 — The Cooper test.** "The most gifted quarterback of his generation did not believe in his own knee until a doctor he trusted tried to break it and failed." Why it must survive: this is the Six's relationship to self-trust in one sentence. The house page's growth line is "Learning to trust myself was like discovering I'd been carrying the map all along while frantically asking others for directions." Mahomes's own words ("it wasn't like he was just going to clear me to clear me") are that pattern in the wild, and the draft pays it off against its own diagnosis line ("only trusts himself once someone he respects has tested him").

**ENNEAGRAM-H5 — Anxiety located correctly in the head center.** "The dread belongs to the waiting. It leaves the moment he has something to do." Why it must survive: this distinguishes Six anticipatory anxiety from ordinary performance anxiety. The fear is about what he cannot control yet, not about executing. That is the right distinction and the draft makes it without jargon.

**ENNEAGRAM-H6 — Leadership as reliability.** "He leads the way he learned, by being the man whose routine you can set a clock by." Why it must survive: an accurate, person-first rendering of how healthy Sixes lead (consistency, not charisma), sourced to a teammate who was not trying to make an Enneagram point.

**ENNEAGRAM-H7 — Disclosure gated on trust.** "He did not hide it. He waited until he trusted the structure." Why it must survive: the father's-arrest section reads a timeline (eighteen months of silence, then disclosure inside a controlled production) as a Six behavior without over-claiming interiority. It is the right inference from public facts.

## What missed

**ENNEAGRAM-M1 — The TL;DR's promised explanation lands under the wrong label.** Bullet 4: "Enneagram Type 6, counterphobic: the most anxious type is often the calmest one when the worst case finally arrives. He has already run it a thousand times." The promised effect is "here is why a nervous person is calm in the clutch." What is delivered is a correct mechanism (rehearsal) attached to a term that means the opposite (charging at fear instead of preparing for it). An informed reader stops trusting the type vocabulary here, in the first 150 words of the type claim.

**ENNEAGRAM-M2 — The Rabbit Hole's sp-3 test does not test.** "Two things push back, both made above: a Three banks applause, while Mahomes treats Brady's sentence as permission and a mentor's routine as law; and a Three with any image instinct picks the cooler helmet." The paragraph has just defined the sp-3 as "the least image-driven Three, who ... wants to be good rather than look good." So the helmet tiebreaker is conceded away in the same breath, and "validated by the GOAT" is exactly what a Three wants from the one person whose opinion counts. The promised effect, a serious alternative taken seriously, does not land.

**ENNEAGRAM-M3 — The stress-arrow worked example is asserted, not worked.** "The man who never takes a hit was taking them. That is what a Six under stress looks like, performing certainty he does not have." The house 6→3 mechanism is image-as-armor, shift from "we" to "me," going solo. The only evidence offered is "I think I ran a little bit too much." A quarterback running more behind a failing offense is also just a quarterback running more. The sentence promises a diagnosis and delivers a gloss.

**ENNEAGRAM-M4 — "Every Six makes some version of that trade."** The trade is "rather be mocked than unprotected." Counterphobic Sixes, the subtype the draft says he is, characteristically make the opposite trade (the house page: "dismisses their own fear as weakness"). The totalizing "every" is both an overgeneralization and a second internal contradiction of the label.

## Red flags

### ENNEAGRAM-R1 — "Counterphobic" is the wrong label for the evidence presented, and the draft contradicts it internally (BLOCKER)

- **Passage:** H3 under "What is Patrick Mahomes's personality type?": "Patrick Mahomes is an Enneagram Type 6, the Loyalist, in its counterphobic form, with a Seven wing that supplies the grin." TL;DR bullet 4: "Enneagram Type 6, counterphobic: the most anxious type is often the calmest one when the worst case finally arrives." Rabbit Hole subtype paragraph: "a sexual Six attacks the threat head-on; Mahomes organizes around staying intact and the people who make him feel covered." Helmet section: "He would rather be mocked than unprotected. Every Six makes some version of that trade."
- **Reader effect:** the lane is "patrick mahomes enneagram." That reader knows what counterphobic means. They read the label, then read a man who wears the safest helmet, slides early, compliments pass rushers so they hit softer, will not ask his coach for a favor, and says "it's up to Coach Reid." The label and the portrait are opposites, and the reader's trust in every other type term in the piece drops.
- **Evidence:** (1) The linked house page `src/blog/enneagram/enneagram-type-6.md` defines phobic Sixes as those who "prepare, plan, seek backup" and "seek allies, build coalitions, look for protective structures," and counterphobic Sixes as those who "challenge authority publicly," "pick fights," and "dismiss their own fear as weakness." Nearly every behavior the draft cites as type evidence is on the phobic list. (2) The Rabbit Hole uses the Chestnut subtype model (sp = warmth/safety, so = group norms, sx = attacks the threat head-on). In that model the counterphobic Six _is_ the Sexual Six, the countertype; the Self-Preservation Six is the most phobic subtype. "Counterphobic, sp/so" is a contradiction inside the framework the draft chose. (3) The TL;DR's gloss ("calmest when the worst case arrives... already run it a thousand times") describes the prepared-phobic mechanism, not counterphobia; counterphobia is moving toward the fear, not having rehearsed it away. (4) Real counterphobic evidence exists and the draft does not use it: "if you're not toeing that line I feel like you're not playing aggressive enough," "I've never been a big completion percentage guy, I'm someone that likes to push it" (New Heights EP15, verified in `youtube-transcripts/mahomes/new-heights-ep15-mahomes.md`), wanting a brace to finish the game on a torn ACL, rehab "they hold me back because I always want to go a little bit further," the Tyreek cuss-out, the helmet slam. The honest picture is the one the house page says is most common: "Most Sixes contain both tendencies, shifting between them depending on domain." Phobic about the body, counterphobic about the throw.
- **Minimum viable repair:** either (a) delete "in its counterphobic form" from the H3 and rewrite TL;DR bullet 4 without the word, or (b) keep the word and earn it: one sentence in the diagnosis section defining it, placing him as phobic off the clock and counterphobic on it, and citing the "toeing that line" / "likes to push it" lines as the counterphobic evidence. Either way, fix "Every Six makes some version of that trade" (it is false for counterphobic Sixes) and reconcile the Rabbit Hole sentence that says he is _not_ the attack-the-threat subtype. Option (b) is the better article; option (a) is the safer one.
- **Expected benefit:** the type vocabulary becomes trustworthy for the reader the lane targets, and the link to the house page stops contradicting the draft.
- **Confidence:** high that the current usage is wrong; high that the evidence supports "both modes, domain-dependent"; medium on whether the editor should keep or cut the word.
- **Acceptance test:** read the H3, TL;DR bullet 4, the helmet section's "Every Six" sentence, and the Rabbit Hole subtype paragraph in sequence. An Enneagram-literate reader should find no sentence in which the draft describes behavior that the house page assigns to the opposite mode without the draft acknowledging it. If "counterphobic" remains, the article must contain at least one verified quote of him moving toward a risk rather than away from it.

### ENNEAGRAM-R2 — The "specific dread" of Reid is illustrated with Travis Kelce's sentence (BLOCKER)

- **Expected benefit:** the section's type claim rests on his words, and the replacement quote is more discriminating than the one it replaces.
- **Confidence:** high (transcript-verified by the packet).
- **Acceptance test:** every quotation in the "specific dread" paragraph can be located in the EP15 transcript in a speaker turn that is contextually Mahomes, not Kelce.

### ENNEAGRAM-R4 — The 6→3 stress arrow absorbs the 2025 collapse without a discriminator (CONCERN)

And Rabbit Hole: "Under stress a Six moves toward Three: performance as armor, certainty as a mask. The 2025 season, covered in the final section, is the worked example."

- **Evidence:** the house mechanism is specific: "shift from 'we' to 'me' thinking," "the loyal team player suddenly goes solo, proving they can handle everything alone," "image becomes armor." Running more is consistent with that mechanism (taking it on himself) but is also consistent with a degraded receiving corps and a failing offensive line, which are the conventional explanations. If (b) cannot be sourced, (a).
- **Expected benefit:** the stress arrow stops being a universal solvent and the section's honesty about 2025 reads as honesty rather than as type spin.
- **Confidence:** medium-high.
- **Acceptance test:** the article states, in one sentence, what 2025 evidence would have looked like if he were _not_ a Six under stress. If no such sentence can be written, the arrow claim should go.
