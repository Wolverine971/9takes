## First-person evidence

### Preparation vs. improvisation (core tension)

- **Jason Kelce → confession** (New Heights EP15, 2022-12-01) [S01]. Jason: "for someone that doesn't care how it looks you manage all right, arm behind the back…" Mahomes: "I've for sure practiced. I try to put myself in those situations during the offseason and practice the sidearm throws, no-look throws and stuff like that, not the behind the back even though I'm scared to do it… when I'm doing some of the stuff though, in the back of my head all I think about is if this [expletive] doesn't work right here… I just make sure if I do throw the no-look pass or if I throw the sidearm pass that I make sure it's completed." **Verified fact** (he said it). Supports: the improvisation is rehearsed and worst-case-framed. Cannot support on its own: that this is a _type_ marker rather than ordinary professional diligence.
- "Sometimes I do stuff on the field and I don't even remember doing that, just kind of black out." (EP15) **Verified.**
- "It's how can you be focused in chaos, how can you not let the moment be bigger than what it is… Coach Reid puts us in those situations all training camp, all during the season… when you've been put in those situations you go back to those fundamentals." (EP15) **Verified.**
- "I played every sport growing up and I just wanted to win. I didn't care how it looked. I didn't care how it got done." (EP15) **Verified.** Supports the Seven/Three readings as much as the Six reading.
- **CBS Mornings, 2023-07-17** [S05]: Burleson's actual question was about being "in the zone" ("the game slows down… what is that like"), not "where the highlights come from." Answer verbatim: "I think it's all from preparation. I watch a lot of Kobe videos, and if you ever watch anything about Kobe he always talks about the shots that he practices thousands and thousands of times to take once, to take twice. And so whenever those moments do come up during the game it's not like it's the first time I've done it. I've prepared for that. And I think that's why it slows down. You see the highlights and everybody's like, oh, it's a highlight reel, it's being Superman out there, but they don't see that preparation." **Verified fact**; the draft's framing of the question is an **interpretation**.
- Chris Simms Unbuttoned via Yahoo, 2023 (research file, not re-fetched): "I've always believed that if I'm gonna do it in the game, I have to practice it." **Attributed claim** (secondary).

- The house Type 6 page's stress arrow is 6→3 ("performing success… rigidly certain"); the draft uses this to absorb the 2025 season. Evaluators should note that this makes Three-like behavior count _for_ the Six read, which is unfalsifiable unless a discriminator is stated.

### Closest alternative and discriminating evidence

- **Self-preservation Three** (draft's named alternative). Discriminators the draft offers: (a) what he does with praise (permission vs. applause); (b) the uncool helmet. Counter-discriminator not addressed in the body: a Three who wants to be seen as "doing it the right way" and as a role model may also choose the safe helmet and defer publicly to a beloved coach; the villain quotes and "right way" vocabulary are compatible with either read.
- **Type 7** (crowd read). Discriminator offered: Sevens do not keep an unchanged film week for nine years. Counter: the 7-wing and sp-instinct claims absorb most Seven evidence, so the distinction rests on the preparation evidence alone.

### Phobic vs. counterphobic (unresolved)

The house page defines **phobic** Sixes as those who "seek allies, build coalitions, look for protective structures… prepare, plan, seek backup," and **counterphobic** Sixes as those who "move toward what scares them… challenge authority publicly… pick fights." Most of the draft's evidence (helmet, early slide, compliments to rushers, "I would not ask him," "it's up to Coach Reid") matches the house _phobic_ description; the counterphobic evidence is the 13-second composure, "I'm a competitor," wanting to finish the game in a brace, and pushing rehab limits. The draft does not define "counterphobic" or show him challenging authority. **Unresolved question** for evaluators: does the evidence support the "counterphobic" label as the house defines it, or "phobic with a 7 wing"?

### Behavior the hypothesis does not explain well (per draft/research file)

- Genuine ease with ridicule and the absence of visible anxiety in public affect; assigned by the draft to wing/instinct rather than core. **Interpretation.**
- Alex Smith's "a thousand questions" vs. Mahomes's "I would just follow him": if Smith is right, the "shadowing, not asking" motif is weaker, though asking is also Six-consistent. **Disputed.**

## Disputes and unresolved questions

1. **Counterphobic vs. phobic.** The draft's label is "counterphobic," but the house Type 6 page's counterphobic profile (moves toward fear, challenges authority, picks fights) is contradicted by most of the draft's own evidence (safest helmet, early slide, will not ask Reid for a favor, "it's up to Coach Reid"). The 13-second composure and "I'm a competitor" support it. Conclusions about the _subtype of fear_ must stay qualified.
2. **Six vs. SP-Three.** The villain quotes ("we believe we play the game the right way"), "I want to be where Tom is," "if we don't win it, it's failure," trophy visualization, and the role-model vocabulary are all compatible with a Three. The draft's tiebreakers (permission vs. applause; the helmet) are arguments, not evidence; the 6→3 stress arrow makes Three-like 2025 behavior count for the Six read. Unresolved.
3. **Did he ask Alex Smith questions or shadow him?** Mahomes: "I wasn't going around asking… I would just follow him." Smith: "It's a thousand questions." Both in the record; the draft uses only the first.
4. **Brady's exact words.** Only Mahomes's relay supports "you're doing it the right way"; Reid later recalled "this is your league now." The visit itself is independently confirmed (Reid, Jan 21, 2019).
5. **Was he unbothered by nine teams passing?** Mahomes: "didn't think about it at all." Pat Sr.: "it kind of hurt him. It really did." Randi: nervous about the phone. The draft uses the mother but not the father.
6. **The "Coach Reid is gonna kill me" quote** belongs to Travis Kelce. Whether Mahomes felt the equivalent is supported by his own words ("no chance I'm walking through the defense late… feel like an idiot"), but the printed sentence is not his.
7. **Pat Sr.'s sobriety.** On camera: "I haven't had a drink since then." Feb 2026: jailed on an ankle-monitor reading; tests clean; probation extended. The draft's "He said he made a vow" is within the evidence; any stronger claim either way is not.
8. **Which _Kingdom_ episode says what.** E1 = draft night, Alex Smith, success/failure; E5 = father's arrest (Aug 20, 2025 linear; Aug 14 streaming); E6 = "lowest points." The draft cites "_The Kingdom_ (ESPN, 2025)" generically; the E5/E6 quotes are known only through secondary transcriptions.
9. **The "18" recovery.** The host's phrase "a 16 recovery, 18 recovery against the Bills" leaves open whether 16 and 18 were different nights. The draft's "18… the morning of" is a reasonable but not textual reading.
10. **Week 1 status** is a forecast. Schefter (Aug 20, 2026): "on track." Reid has made no promise; the draft's "nobody in the building will promise out loud" is consistent with the record as of Aug 22.
11. **"Lakeside restaurant in Tyler."** Lago del Pino is a private-lake property west of Tyler; his hometown is Whitehouse. Cosmetic, but "in Tyler" vs "outside Tyler" is checkable.
12. **1-9 in one-score games, "ESPN, Jul 2026."** The number is corroborated; the cited ESPN article was not located.
