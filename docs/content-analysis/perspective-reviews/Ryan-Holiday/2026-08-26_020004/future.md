## Bottom-line verdict

Read from August 2027, this page holds up better than most current-events-anchored profiles, and it
holds up for a structural reason rather than a lucky one: the argument is carried by a 2011 document,
a 2018 filmed conversation, and a mechanism demonstrated three separate times across eleven years.
Delete the newest event entirely and the diagnosis still stands. That is the durability test, and the
draft passes it.

The date discipline is also unusually good. I swept the reader-visible body for relative time
language and found essentially none outside quotations: no "recently," no "currently," no bare "last
year." Every load-bearing event carries an absolute date, and the contested Ivanka video date is
correctly softened to "April 2026" rather than picking a disputed day.

One thing breaks the review, and it is not a slow decay — it is already wrong. The `key-stat` block
presents the Naval Academy's 381 removed books as a standing fact. Most of those books went back on
the shelves in May 2025, roughly five weeks after the cancellation and sixteen months before this
draft's date. The body sentence around it is defensible past-perfect; the pull-out display element is
not, and a pull-out number is the single most-scanned object in that section. The repair is one
clause and it does not touch the thesis — the reversal is favorable context, not a correction.

Beyond that, the failure mode over twelve months is maintenance drift rather than collapse: an age
that goes false on a known date, a present-tense tour status, two present-tense claims about live
third-party web pages, and a hook fastened to the freshest and most thinly-sourced event on the page.
All are cheap to fix now and expensive to notice later.

## What landed

**FUTURE-H1 — Absolute dates everywhere, including where a disputed date invited a guess.**

> "Two years before that video he posted his most-quoted line from Book 5 of _Meditations_ to the
> Daily Stoic account, in February 2024"

> "ESPN traced the route in a May 2020 feature"

This is the draft's largest durability asset and it is worth naming explicitly because it is
invisible when done right. A twelve-month-later reader is never asked to compute what "recently"
meant. The packet records a genuine dispute over the video's day (Bates says April 27, Mediaite cites
an April 30 timestamp); the draft says only "April 2026" and never resolves what the record does not
resolve. **This must survive revision** — including through any future refresh, where the temptation
will be to sharpen "April 2026" into a specific day now that the event feels historical.

**FUTURE-H2 — Elapsed time anchored at both ends, not to the present.**

> "Eight years after writing that sentence, he published a book called _Stillness Is the Key_."

> "Eleven years later he stuck out his tongue."

These constructions are permanently correct because both endpoints are fixed. Contrast them with
"he left it up for fifteen years," which silently means "fifteen years as of the moment you are
reading," and goes wrong on its own. The draft mostly uses the durable form. **The both-ends form
must survive**; it is the pattern the refresh list below asks the two exceptions to adopt.

**FUTURE-H3 — The mechanism is demonstrated three times across eleven years, so no single event is
load-bearing for the diagnosis.**

> "Same trigger both times. Someone else, in public, holding his philosophy. In 2015 he aimed the
> correction at himself and handed the other man a microphone. Eleven years later he stuck out his
> tongue."

The 2015 Pigliucci control case is the most future-proof structural decision in the piece. It
converts the April 2026 video from _the evidence_ into _one instance of a pattern with a
counter-instance_, which is exactly what "current events used as appendices rather than as proof"
looks like when done properly. It also makes the argument falsifiable rather than merely
sympathetic. **Do not cut this to make room for anything on the refresh list.**

**FUTURE-H4 — The central contradiction is explicitly left open, which makes it unfalsifiable by
future news.**

> "Whether that counts as restitution or as laundering is not something the record settles, and he
> is still living inside the question."

A resolved verdict here would have been the piece's biggest twelve-month liability, because any
future development would date it. An openly-held question absorbs new events instead of being
contradicted by them. If he publishes a mea culpa in 2027, this sentence still reads correctly; if
he doubles down, it still reads correctly. **This must survive revision intact.**

**FUTURE-H5 — The primary evidence is a document that cannot go stale.**

> "And yet I think they are both equal parts fear, the fear of simply _just being_."
> ... "Eight years after writing that sentence, he published a book called _Stillness Is the Key_."

A 2011 blog post gets _more_ valuable with time, not less. Building the strongest section of the page
on a fifteen-year-old fixed text rather than on this quarter's news is the reason this page will
still be worth reading in 2027. See FUTURE-C4 for the one present-tense claim attached to it that
does need repair.

## What missed

**The Naval Academy section stops at the moment of maximum injustice and never says what happened
next.** The section ends on Friedman's "tragic new low," which is the correct rhetorical peak, but
the story's actual resolution — most of the books returned within weeks, partly under public pressure
— is absent. A 2027 reader who half-remembers the episode as resolved will read the page as either
uninformed or as quietly withholding. This is the promised effect that does not land: the section
promises the definitive account of why Holiday lost that stage, and delivers everything except the
epilogue that a reader can look up in one search. Full finding at FUTURE-R1.

**The closer stacks four present-tense status claims in one sentence.**

> "He is thirty-nine years old, with thirteen books behind him, a bookshop on a small-town main
> street, two sons, and a standing invitation from most stages in the country."

The closing line of the piece is the place a reader is most likely to trust literally and least
likely to date-check. One of those four claims is guaranteed to be false within ten months, one is a
counter that his own publishing cadence threatens, and one is an unsourced reputational assertion
sitting three sections after a stage was withdrawn from him. Findings at FUTURE-C1, C6, C7.

## Red flags

### FUTURE-R1 — BLOCKER: the 381-book removal is displayed as a standing fact; it was substantially reversed in May 2025

**Exact passage.** The `key-stat` block in "Why the Naval Academy canceled Ryan Holiday":

> **381**
> books removed from the Nimitz Library. He was asked to not mention them for one hour.

**Reader effect / trust problem.** A `key-stat` pull-out is a decontextualized display object: it
carries no date and is read as a present-tense fact about the institution. The removal was reversed
roughly five weeks after the April 14, 2025 cancellation and sixteen months before this draft's
2026-08-26 date. A reader who knows the books came back reads the number as either stale or as
selectively withheld, and the surrounding section is the piece's most precisely-sourced passage —
so the damage lands on the draft's strongest sourcing, not its weakest. This is not decay over
twelve months; it is already misleading at publication, which is why I am filing it as a blocker
rather than as a refresh item.

**Evidence.** The 19th News, May 2025: "Hundreds of 'DEI' books are back at the Naval Academy."
CBS Baltimore, reporting the same reversal, states that "all but a few of the nearly 400 books" were
"back on the shelves after the newest Pentagon-ordered review," with about 20 held aside for further
checking. Two independent outlets converge on the substance. The packet does not carry this — its
Naval Academy sources (S-10 Scholars at Risk, S-11 PEN America, S-12 WTOP) are all April 2025
incident-time records that predate the reversal, so this is a gap in the packet, not a claim the
draft contradicted.

**Note on scope.** The body sentence — "the slide referencing the 381 books that had been pulled from
the shelves" — is past-perfect and remains accurate as of the lecture date. I am not asking for it to
change. The defect is specifically the undated display element.

**Minimum viable repair.** Add a date and an outcome clause to the `key-stat` label only. It needs to
convey: 381 pulled in April 2025; nearly all restored weeks later after public pressure. No other
sentence in the section needs to move.

**Expected benefit.** Removes an already-false present-tense implication; costs roughly a dozen
words; and the epilogue is _favorable_ to the draft's argument — he refused to drop a slide about a
removal that was itself reversed under exactly the kind of public naming he could not stop doing.
The fix strengthens the section it repairs.

**Confidence.** High on the reversal (two converging independent outlets). Medium on the precise
residual count (~20), which the CBS piece itself hedges; the repair should therefore say "nearly all"
rather than commit to a number.

**Acceptance test.** A reader who has never heard of this episode can state, after reading the
key-stat block alone, both that 381 books were removed in April 2025 _and_ that the removal did not
stand — without consulting another source. A second test: the block contains at least one absolute
date.

### FUTURE-C2 — "he is still touring" is a present-tense status on the freshest event in the piece

publication") and convert "he is still touring" to a past-tense observation about August 2026. The
insight survives intact; only the vantage point changes.

**Expected benefit.** The piece's closing analytical beat stops depending on the reader's calendar.

**Confidence.** High.

**Acceptance test.** Read the sentence as if today were 2027-08-26; it states nothing false.

### FUTURE-C4 — two present-tense claims about live third-party web pages, one rendered to readers

**Exact passages.**

1. Visible `source-card` in "Two Sides": "Source: 'Two Sides,' ryanholiday.net, July 5, 2011.

2. Morning section: "The Painted Porch stocks what **its own site calls** 'a small collection of only

the correct path is `/pages/about-us`), which is direct evidence that this site's URLs move.

**Evidence.** Packet S-02 (verified live 2026-08-26), S-23 (fetched 2026-08-26, "marketing copy"),
and the packet's "Blocked this session" list recording the `/pages/about` 404.

**Minimum viable repair.** Change "Still live at the original URL" to a retrieval-dated form ("as
retrieved August 2026") — that claim is permanently true regardless of what happens to the page.

both-ends form the draft already uses elsewhere ("left it up for the fifteen years since," or

**Expected benefit.** Three self-invalidating claims become permanently true, at a cost of a few
words. This is the cheapest durability fix on the list.

**Confidence.** High.

**Acceptance test.** No reader-visible sentence asserts the current state of a third-party web page
