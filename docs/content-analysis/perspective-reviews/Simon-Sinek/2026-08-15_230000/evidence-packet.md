## Identity and scope

Simon Sinek is a British-born author and speaker known for _Start With Why_, _Leaders Eat Last_, and leadership work centered on purpose and belonging. The frozen article interprets his public record as Enneagram Type 2, likely 2w1. It uses public books, interviews, talks, and criticism and makes no diagnostic claim.

## Dated timeline

- **Childhood:** Moved repeatedly across countries and attended multiple schools.
- **2005:** Experienced a personal and professional breakdown while his business appeared successful, then developed the “why” framework.
- **2009:** Published _Start With Why_.
- **2014:** Published _Leaders Eat Last_, using the Marines and “officers eat last” as a leadership model.
- **2016:** A talk about millennials became a major viral success and attracted criticism for broad generalization.
- **2019:** Published _The Infinite Game_.
- **2025:** Discussed loneliness, optimism, care protocols, friendship, ADHD, and relationship history in several public conversations.

## First-person evidence

- Sinek frames the 2005 crisis as a loss of service and meaning rather than a loss of revenue.
- He describes being lonely and optimistic simultaneously.
- He reports a late ADHD diagnosis that clarified how others experienced his attention and expression.
- He uses explicit friendship protocols, including “no crying alone,” short emergency calls, and saying “I love you.”
- He has reframed decades of anxiety about marriage around the strength of his friendships.

## Third-party testimony

- Lieutenant General George Flynn supplied the “officers eat last” account that became a central leadership image.
- Friends including Maria Shriver and Christina Tosi appear in Sinek's descriptions of deliberate connection practices.
- Neuroscientist Paul Middlebrooks disputes the Golden Circle's limbic-brain explanation.
- Military-leadership criticism argues that Sinek reduces leadership to emotional appeal.

## Disputes and unresolved questions

- Named sources support the load-bearing quotations, but several tags are contextual rather than same-sentence inline.
- Recent relationship claims derive from a small number of 2025 interviews.
- Sinek's own frameworks may shape how he narrates earlier events.
- Commercial success, expertise branding, Type 1 morality, and Type 3 achievement provide non-Type-2 explanations.
- Public friendship practices do not reveal all private relationships.
