## Bottom-line verdict

The article mostly earns its sympathetic reading. It concedes the strongest non-partisan objection
to the Tokyo withdrawal before answering it, it refuses the flattering Osaka equivalence, it lets
Biles overrule the article's own sense of drama about her lowest point, and it deliberately runs
its thesis against Paris 2024 — the one result that could have falsified it. That is more
adversarial self-testing than this genre normally attempts, and the best-sourced claim on the page
(the Senate testimony) is also the claim doing the most argumentative work. Those are the marks of
a piece that argued rather than advocated.

What strains trust is not the argument. It is that the article's sourcing errors are **not randomly
distributed**. Three of the four blockers below move in the same direction: each one makes Biles
sound more certain, more self-aware, or more purely psychological than the record supports, and
each sits at a load-bearing position (cold open, thesis-test section, thesis-test section). A
skeptical reader who catches one will assume the rest of the page tilts too. The fourth blocker is
direction-neutral but corrodes the page's specific competitive promise — "Nobody shows their work.
Here is the work" — because the single most-cited source on the page is named wrongly in three
reader-facing places.

The type argument itself survives everything I could throw at it, with one real dent: the
half-point spotter, which the piece treats as its cleanest compressed proof, has an on-record
in-sport counter-explanation the article never mentions.

Fix the four blockers and this passes. None of the repairs requires new open-ended research.

## What landed

**CRITIC-H1 — the concession before the rebuttal (L332).**

> "Give them their strongest version, because they had one. An Olympic team is four other women who
> trained five years, and one was going home without a medal because of a decision Biles made in a
> hallway. That cost landed on real people, and Biles has never argued otherwise."

Must survive revision. This is the article inviting the reader to hold the cost against her before
it offers any mitigation, and it names the harmed party rather than gesturing at "critics." A
sympathetic piece that will not do this is advocacy; one that does has bought the right to argue.

**CRITIC-H2 — refusing the easy solidarity (L340).**

> "Osaka removed herself from a room that was extracting something from her; Biles stayed, cheered
> through the final, and carried the failure as a debt."

Must survive. The Osaka pairing was the 2021 consensus and it would have cost the article nothing
to keep it. Breaking it is a distinction the piece makes _against_ its own convenience, and it is
the single sharpest sentence for a reader who came in skeptical of mental-health framing.

**CRITIC-H3 — letting the subject overrule the article's drama (L366).**

> "Ask her for the lowest point of the Tokyo era and she does not name Tokyo."

Must survive. Tokyo is the article's most valuable narrative asset and the reason anyone clicks.
Subordinating it to her own account is the opposite of cherry-picking, and it is the moment I
stopped reading the piece as a case being built.

**CRITIC-H4 — the thesis run against its hardest case (L406).**

> "Now hold that against 2015. A third consecutive World title produced 'did I do it?' Three Olympic
> golds produced a woman who, two years later, still cannot tell you whether she is finished."

Must survive. Paris was the result that could have retired the thesis; the article goes and gets it
rather than stopping at the flattering data. This is the structural reason the piece reads as an
argument rather than a profile. (Note that CRITIC-R1 partly undermines the payoff — see below.)

**CRITIC-H5 — a stated falsifier (L427).**

> "This analysis is overturned by evidence that the reassurance-seeking is performance rather than
> need."

Must survive, with a caveat. Naming what would kill the reading is rare in this genre and it is the
correct falsifier. The caveat is that, as the packet observes, it cannot be tested against any
available source — so it functions as intellectual honesty rather than as a live check. Keep it;
don't oversell it.

## What missed

**The empathy turn answers an argument its named critics never made (L330–L332).** The article
introduces Charlie Kirk ("sociopath," "shame to the country") and Piers Morgan ("What a joke"),
then writes "Give them their strongest version, because they had one" — and supplies the
teammates-cost argument, which is not Kirk's or Morgan's argument and is considerably better than
either. The effect is that the piece gets credit for facing criticism while the only critics the
reader meets remain cartoons. My own search confirms the packet's picture: the reputable criticism
genuinely was thin and mostly social-media, so this is partly a limit of the record. But the
sentence attributes to two named people a case they did not make, which is its own small unfairness
running the other way.

**The loyalty inference does not follow (L338).** "The reason to read this as loyalty rather than
self-preservation is what it cost her afterward, which she has never let herself off for." Guilt
afterward is fully compatible with self-protection in the moment — people feel terrible about
decisions that also saved them. The article has a much stronger argument sitting one sentence away
(she stayed in the arena and cheered through a final she was free to leave), and it rests the claim
on the weaker one.

**The strongest disconfirming behaviour never appears reader-facing as a list.** The Type 3 case is
named and rebutted in the diagnosis section, which is fair. But the four things the typing genuinely
does _not_ explain — the appetite for the celebrity machine, the clapback register, a decade of
self-initiated record-chasing, and returning to the site of her worst public failure to do it again
— are absorbed wholesale by "under strain a Six borrows the Three" (L419) and never presented to
the reader as unresolved. The article tells the reader it has a soft seam; it never lets them see
its dimensions.

- **The Chiles medal reallocation**, in a section whose title promises "what it didn't settle."
- **An in-sport reading of the spotter deduction.** Chellsie Memmel, USA Gymnastics' high-performance
  technical lead, is on record in the same Washington Post piece the draft cites for the rule.
- **Biles's own physical account of why 2028 is open.** She has given one, repeatedly, in an
  interview the article cites for a different sentence.
- **Some acknowledgment that the age-three memory is an age-three memory**, given the article's
  otherwise careful epistemic hygiene.
- Reasonably: the twisties framed as proprioceptive rather than psychiatric. The article does this,
  in the FAQ (`What are the twisties?`), and it is the right call. Credited, not expected-and-missing.

## What surprised me

**Welcome.** That the piece breaks the Osaka pairing (H2), and that it names its own softest seam
(L417–L419) rather than burying it. Also that it declines to date the two-up two-count reasoning
(L336) — the caution is real, and as it happens the record is _friendlier_ than the article assumes
(Biles's 2021 response to critics already names the team medal: "I simply got so lost my safety was
at risk as well as a team medal"). The article is more careful than it had to be, then spoils it —
see CRITIC-C3.

**Unwelcome.** The direction-consistency of the errors. Individually, a misnamed podcast, an
unlocatable Netflix quote, an "I think" swapped for "obviously," and an absolute claim about her
body are four small things. Together they describe a draft whose slips all favour its own thesis.
That pattern is what a critic is for, and it is why trust is `strained` rather than `intact`.

## Red flags

### CRITIC-R1 — BLOCKER — Cold open asserts a claim the draft's own cited source contradicts

- **Location:** L213, cold open: "She is 29, she has not said whether she is going to Los Angeles in
  2028, and **the reason she gives for not knowing has nothing to do with her body.**" Compounded at
  L438 and FAQ L86.
- **Reader effect / trust problem:** This sentence installs the article's entire frame — that what
  is unresolved in Biles is psychological, that "the signal cut out" — in the load-bearing first
  screen. It is built on one CNN quote ("Mental health plays a big role in it because, physically,
  my coaches will get me in shape") while suppressing the same subject's physical account from an
  interview the article itself cites 225 lines later.
- **Evidence:** In the L'Équipe interview the draft cites at L438 for "whether on the apparatus or in
  the stands," Biles also says: **"But 2028 seems so far away, and my body is aging. I felt it in
  Paris,"** and **"I went back to the village, I took the elevator and my body literally collapsed.
  I was sick for 10 days."** Independently confirmed this pass via US syndication of the L'Équipe
  interview (wkyc/NBC News). The draft additionally reports at L440 a June 2026 hospitalisation she
  called one of the scariest experiences of her life; the packet records a further procedure on
  22 July 2026. An absolute "nothing to do with her body" cannot stand against the article's own
  page.
- **Minimum viable repair:** Drop the absolute. Say the reason she _names first_ is mental rather
  than physical. Add one clause in H2 9 carrying the L'Équipe body sentences.
- **Expected benefit:** The Paris payoff at L406 gets stronger, not weaker: a woman with a
  documented physical case for stopping who still cannot say she is finished is a _better_ example
  of the article's pattern than one for whom the body is silent.
- **Confidence:** High. Quotes verified against syndication this pass.
- **Acceptance test:** No sentence in the piece claims her indecision is exclusively non-physical,
  and a reader who searches "Biles 2028" and lands on the L'Équipe body quotes cannot find a reason
  the article withheld from an interview it cites.

### CRITIC-R2 — BLOCKER — Unlocatable quotation, attributed to a named production, in the thesis-test section

- **Location:** L394: "Her framing in _Simone Biles Rising_: 'It wasn't at a point of weakness, it
  was a point of strength.'"
- **Reader effect / trust problem:** The sentence converts a therapy appointment into a strength
  narrative _in her own voice_, at the exact point where the article is testing its thesis against
  Paris. On a page whose stated differentiator is "Nobody shows their work. Here is the work," a
  quotation attributed to a specific Netflix production that cannot be located is the most damaging
  single error available.
- **Evidence:** Packet CLM-16: unverified as verbatim; the substance (a therapy session the morning
  of the all-around final, and her unease about disclosing it) is well documented, but no located
  source carries this sentence, and it appears nowhere in the repository research file — it entered
  at second-pass drafting with no evidence trail. The closest documented wordings ("I think we used
  to think of therapy as a weakness, and now I think of it as a strength"; "This was more of a
  strength thing for me") are from a Hoda Kotb interview, not the docuseries.
- **Minimum viable repair:** Swap in a documented wording and correct the venue to the Kotb
  interview, or cut the quotation marks and paraphrase the well-documented substance. Do not keep
  the current sentence pending a Netflix audit.
- **Expected benefit:** Removes the one item on the page that a hostile reader could characterise as
  fabricated.
- **Confidence:** High on the defect (packet did the verification); high that the repair is cheap.
- **Acceptance test:** Every quotation on the page resolves to a locatable source with the correct
  venue named.

### CRITIC-R3 — BLOCKER — Quotation altered in the direction the argument benefits from

- **Location:** L396: "There was always a part of me that thought what if? But that was
  **obviously** my anxiety talking."
- **Reader effect / trust problem:** Published wording is "…And **I think** that was my anxiety
  talking." The draft deletes her hedge and inserts a certainty marker, inside quotation marks. The
  article's growth arc needs her to name her own fear with confidence; the alteration supplies
  exactly that. This is the specific failure mode a critic exists to catch, and it is not
  recoverable as a typo.
- **Evidence:** Packet CLM-17, against S-16 (Olympics.com, August 2024).
- **Minimum viable repair:** Restore verbatim. If the hedge reads weakly, move the sentence out of
  quotation marks and paraphrase.
- **Expected benefit:** Removes a demonstrable quote distortion favouring the thesis.
- **Confidence:** High.
- **Acceptance test:** String-match the quoted sentence against S-16.

### CRITIC-R4 — BLOCKER — The page's most-cited source is named wrongly, three times, reader-facing

- **Location:** L202, L206, L238 — "<em>Beyond Medals and Perfection</em> podcast."
- **Reader effect / trust problem:** No podcast of that name exists. The show is _The Art of
  Excellence_ (Glenn Zweig), Ep. 122; "Coaching Simone Biles beyond medals and perfection" is the
  episode title. This source carries the cold open, the `key-stat` "2" that is also the meta
  description, and the article's load-bearing "did I do it?" quote. A reader who tries to verify the
  page's best material — precisely the reader this page is courting with "nobody shows their work" —
  searches an italicised title and finds nothing.
- **Evidence:** Packet CLM-01, verified by yt-dlp metadata on the source video and by the host's own
  episode page (S-01, S-02). The packet also notes the wrong name has been committed to
  `scripts/blog-source-audit.mjs` OUTLETS, so the audit tool will keep grading the misattribution as
  clean — the error will not self-correct.
- **Minimum viable repair:** Rename all three instances to _The Art of Excellence_ with the episode
  title as descriptor, and fix the OUTLETS entry so the tool stops certifying it.
- **Expected benefit:** The page's central verifiability claim becomes true.
- **Confidence:** High.
- **Acceptance test:** Searching each italicised source name in the article returns the actual
  publication; `blog-source-audit.mjs` no longer contains the phantom outlet.

## Specific improvements

### CRITIC-C1 — The Chiles reallocation, omitted from a section titled "what it didn't settle"

- **Location:** L398 ("The three of them made the first all-Black gymnastics podium in Olympic
  history"), FAQ L80.
- **Problem:** True of the ceremony as it happened, and the pipeline's decision to leave CAS out as
  "not Biles's story" is defensible. But the section's own promise is _what it didn't settle_, and
  the most literally unsettled thing about that podium is whether it stands. Days later CAS voided
  the inquiry that lifted Chiles to third and awarded the bronze to Ana Bărbosu; in January 2026 the
  Swiss Federal Supreme Court referred it back to CAS. A reader who knows the medal history reads
  the silence as either tact or ignorance, and cannot tell which.
- **Repair:** One subordinate clause noting the bronze was reallocated days later and remains
  disputed. Nothing in the article's argument changes — the bow, "absolutely," and "She's queen" are
  unaffected.
- **Benefit:** Removes the only place where a knowledgeable reader can catch the article being
  quieter than its own section title.
- **Confidence:** High on the facts, medium that it belongs in the piece.
- **Acceptance test:** A gymnastics-literate reader finishes the Paris section without knowing a
  fact about that podium the article does not.

### CRITIC-C2 — The spotter argument has an unmentioned in-sport counter-explanation

- **Location:** L384–L386, TL;DR bullet L224.
- **Problem:** The article treats the standing −0.5 as its cleanest compressed proof. Three things
  weaken it and none appear: (a) Cécile Landi's stated reason is consequence severity on the hardest
  vault any woman has competed — "one mistake can have serious consequences" — which is ordinary
  risk management for a novel skill with catastrophic failure modes, not a temperament tell;
  (b) Chellsie Memmel, USAG's high-performance technical lead, is on record in the _same_ Washington
  Post article the draft cites that the deduction is a Code problem — "You can stay [on the mat] for
  Pak salto on bars… Why can't you stay for a double-flipping [element] on vault" — which reframes
  the half point as the sport's arbitrariness rather than a price she uniquely elects to pay;
  (c) "a vault she can land" (L386, L224) is the article's own framing and it presupposes the
  spotter is surplus, which is the very thing in dispute.
- **Repair:** One clause conceding Memmel's reading, and replace "a vault she can land" with wording
  that does not assume the spotter is unnecessary.
- **Benefit:** The argument survives in honest form — she still absorbs a measured cost, and she is
  the only woman competing the skill — instead of being answerable by a fact the article never
  mentions.
- **Confidence:** High. Corroborated this pass in general coverage of the deduction as a safety
  provision on an objectively dangerous vault.
- **Acceptance test:** A reader who knows Memmel's position cannot rebut the article's best
  compressed argument with information the article withheld.

### CRITIC-C3 — The article hedges the post-hoc question, then cancels its own hedge

- **Location:** L336: "Whether she ran that in the hallway or assembled it three years later is not
  recoverable, **and barely changes the finding, because building a load-bearing justification after
  the fact does the same work.**"
- **Problem:** "Barely changes the finding" is asserted, not argued, and it does heavy lifting.
  Real-time tactical loyalty and retrospective self-justification are different psychological events
  with different implications; collapsing them lets the loyalty reading stand no matter which is
  true. In the one section where falsifiability matters most, the article makes itself unfalsifiable
  in a single clause.
- **Repair:** Either (a) cite Biles's contemporaneous 2021 response to critics — "I simply got so
  lost my safety was at risk as well as a team medal" — which puts the team-medal reasoning far
  closer to the event than the hedge allows, and then drop the hedge; or (b) keep the hedge and say
  in one sentence what actually changes under each reading.
- **Benefit:** Option (a) is strictly better than what is there now and costs one citation.
- **Confidence:** High that the current clause is a dodge; high that (a) is available.
- **Acceptance test:** The paragraph no longer contains a claim that the finding holds regardless of
  which factual account is true.

### CRITIC-C4 — Motive laundering: remorse used as proof of motive

- **Location:** L338.
- **Problem:** "The reason to read this as loyalty rather than self-preservation is what it cost her
  afterward" does not follow. Subsequent guilt is compatible with self-protective action.
- **Repair:** Rest the claim on behaviour already in the paragraph — she stayed in the arena and
  cheered through a final she was free to leave — and demote the remorse to corroboration.
- **Benefit:** Converts the section's weakest inference into its strongest, using material already
  present.
- **Confidence:** High.
- **Acceptance test:** The loyalty claim is supported by an action, not by a feeling reported later.

### CRITIC-C5 — Straw critics fronting a steelman they did not make

- **Location:** L330–L332.
- **Problem:** See "What missed." The named opposition is the two most discreditable critics
  available; the argument answered is better than anything either said and is credited to them by
  "because they had one."
- **Repair:** Split the move. Name Kirk and Morgan as the noise, then introduce the teammates-cost
  objection in the article's own voice as the version worth answering, without attributing it to
  them.
- **Benefit:** Keeps the section's real virtue (the concession) while removing an unfairness in the
  critics' direction.
- **Confidence:** Medium-high. My search this pass confirms the reputable-criticism record really is
  thin, so this is a framing fix, not a research gap.
- **Acceptance test:** No sentence credits Kirk or Morgan with an argument they did not advance.

### CRITIC-C6 — The article's spine gets its least epistemic care

- **Location:** L256–L266, closing callback L444, and the meta_title.
- **Problem:** An uncorroborated age-three memory carries the origin story, the ending's entire
  payload, and the SEO title. The packet is explicit that corroboration is unavailable in principle
  — records sealed, witnesses family-only — and that age-three autobiographical memory is
  reconstructive. The prose ("Sit with what that child did") forecloses doubt rather than
  acknowledging it. The asymmetry is the objection: the best-sourced claim on the page (Senate
  testimony, verbatim) is handled with more caution than the least-sourced one.
- **Repair:** One clause marking it as her own account of being three. Nothing else changes.
- **Benefit:** Costs the scene nothing and inoculates the ending against the obvious attack.
- **Confidence:** High.
- **Acceptance test:** A reader can tell, from the page alone, that the hallway scene is
  self-reported and uncorroborated.

### CRITIC-C7 — The stress line explains the whole public career

- **Location:** L419, and the unlisted disconfirmers throughout.
- **Problem:** Everything Type 6 does not cover — the celebrity machine, the clapback register, a
  decade of self-initiated record-chasing, returning to the site of her worst public failure — is
  absorbed by "under strain a Six borrows the Three." As written, no achievement-oriented behaviour
  could count against the typing. Naming this as "the softest seam" is honest but does not repair
  it.
- **Repair:** Add one sentence stating what achievement behaviour the stress line would _not_
  explain — i.e., what a Three would do here that Biles does not.
- **Benefit:** Converts a named seam into an actual constraint.
- **Confidence:** Medium-high.
- **Acceptance test:** The Rabbit Hole contains at least one observable behaviour the 6→3 reading

### CRITIC-C8 — Unsourced amplification on a page selling source discipline

- **Location:** L286: "a bronze on beam that **America told her** was a disgrace."
- **Problem:** Her documented statement is "people made me so ashamed of it in Rio." "America told
  her was a disgrace" is a larger claim with no instance cited behind it — the exact move the page's
  own pitch invites scrutiny of.
- **Repair:** Scale to the evidence, or cite one instance.
- **Confidence:** High.
- **Acceptance test:** No collective-actor claim on the page lacks a named instance.

### CRITIC-C9 — Two habitual presents that a following reader will read as stale

- **Location:** L384: "When Biles performs it, Laurent Landi stands on the landing mat." Also L440:
  "Nothing further."
- **Problem:** Both Landis left World Champions Centre in December 2024 and Biles has not competed
  since Paris 2024, so the present tense describes an arrangement that no longer exists. "Nothing
  further" is false as of the compile date: she posted about a 22 July 2026 procedure and has said
  she will explain "sooner or later." What remains undisclosed is the _cause_. This matters to me as
  critic and not just as a currency issue, because "Nothing further" is the evidence the article
  uses to prove its "open book about the facts, layers about the fear" reading — a reading that
  weakens if she has in fact kept updating.
- **Repair:** Past-tense the spotter arrangement and date it to Antwerp 2023. Change "Nothing
  further" to name what is withheld (the cause), not the updates.
- **Confidence:** High.
- **Acceptance test:** No sentence describes a coaching or disclosure arrangement in the present
  tense that ended before the publish date.
