## Bottom-line verdict

Type 6 is very likely the right call, and this draft does better Enneagram work than almost
anything on the typology sub-SERP it is targeting. The authority-paradox passage (L360) welded to
sworn Senate testimony is the strongest type-evidence pairing I have seen in this corpus: a
motive stated by the subject herself, under oath, that a Six framework explains and a behavioural
description does not.

The problem is that the sentence the whole 3-vs-6 call turns on is wrong about Type 3, and the
anecdote it turns on has not been established to mean what the draft says it means. **"An Achiever
trusts the scoreboard, because the scoreboard is the point" (L246) is a mischaracterisation of
Type 3** — it is contradicted by 9takes' own published Type 3 page, which is built on the premise
that the scoreboard _never_ satisfies a Three. And **the referent of "did I do it?" is asserted,
not established**: Aimee Boorman's own surrounding sentences on the same podcast point away from
the title and toward a process goal, which would dissolve the "result already settled" premise the
tiebreaker needs.

Neither of those overturns the typing. The second pillar ("are we sure? … cuz it can't happen
again") is genuinely discriminating and survives untouched. But the page's competitive pitch is
"Nobody shows their work. Here is the work," and right now the work has a broken load-bearing beam
in the one paragraph that has to hold. That is a repair, not a retraction — and the repaired
version is a _stronger_ argument than the current one, because the real discriminator is sitting
in the draft already, unused.

Third blocker: the half-point spotter, which the draft's own ledger calls "the best compressed
argument on the page," is surface behaviour read as motivation with an on-record competing
explanation omitted and no comparison class available.

**Recommendation: revise.** Every blocker is fixable from evidence already in the packet or the
local transcripts. None requires new research.

## What landed

**ENN-H1 — The Six formulation in the diagnosis (L244).** "A Type 6 does not doubt the evidence.
She doubts whether it covers everything, because what she is really asking is whether she is safe
now, and no scoreboard has ever answered that."

This is the single best sentence of Enneagram writing on the page. It does the one thing most
celebrity typing never does: it separates _doubt about facts_ from _doubt about safety_, which is
precisely the distinction that makes Six legible and stops the type collapsing into "anxious
person." It is also person-first and jargon-free — no "head centre," no "fixation," no
"cowardice." Must survive revision verbatim.

**ENN-H2 — The authority paradox tied to the Senate testimony (L354–L360).** "A Six's relationship
to institutions is a standing argument between wanting a protector and knowing protectors fail,
which is why Sixes so often stay inside organisations they have stopped believing in. Leaving ends
the surveillance, and the surveillance is the entire point."

This is the article's strongest type argument and it is not the one the article thinks is
strongest. The evidence class is the best available anywhere in the packet — sworn congressional
testimony stating a motive in her own words, requiring no inference: "I worked incredibly hard to
make sure that my presence could maintain a connection between the failures and the competition at
Tokyo 2020." The theory is exactly right and matches 9takes' own Type 6 page, which has a section
literally titled "The Authority Paradox" ("you simultaneously crave and question authority").
Nothing else in the piece pairs a verified primary-source motive with a type mechanic this
cleanly. **If the revision has to choose which pillar to lead on, this one is evidentially
stronger than "did I do it?" and nobody has noticed.**

**ENN-H3 — The growth line read as self-trust, not sedation (L378, L419).** "That is what growth
looks like on a Six, and it does not look like confidence. It looks like a woman who has stopped
requiring certainty before she moves."

Correct 6→9 integration theory and correctly _de-glamorised_. It also matches 9takes' own
integration section on the Type 6 page ("When you trust your own inner authority, you stop seeking
external validation for internal knowing"). The "rather than sedation" qualifier pre-empts the
most common misreading of the 9 line. Keep.

**ENN-H4 — The counterphobic charge located in the instinct, not the type (L413–L415).**
"social-dominant, with a counterphobic charge… Best read: so/sx, the least certain call here."

Most popular Enneagram writing treats counterphobic as a binary property of the whole type. Siting
the counterphobic charge in a sexual/one-to-one second instinct while keeping the organising
instinct social is the more sophisticated read, it is internally coherent, and the uncertainty is
declared. This is the most technically careful paragraph in the Rabbit Hole.

**ENN-H5 — The twisties are not typed (FAQ L74, and the body's restraint).** "It is a documented
proprioceptive phenomenon in gymnastics, not a psychiatric diagnosis."

The draft uses her _response_ to the twisties as type evidence and never claims the type caused
them. On a page about a Type 6 whose most famous moment is a mental-health event, this restraint is
the difference between analysis and astrology. Explicitly preserve — a later refresh will be
tempted to blur it.

**ENN-H6 — Naming the softest seam in the pre-write memo and the falsifier in the body.** The
instinct to state a falsifier at all (L427) puts this above the entire competing SERP. The
execution has a problem (ENN-C2) but the move is right and should not be dropped in response to
criticism of its wording.

## What missed

**ENN-M1 — The discriminator is the wrong one, and the right one is already in the draft.**
The diagnosis argues 6-over-3 on _who she asked_ (a private trusted individual, no audience). That
is a weak axis: 9takes' own Type 3 page describes SP-3s who "hate being seen as show-offs and
actively hide accomplishments," and every Three has a specific significant person whose read
counts. The strong axis is _what she asked for_. "Are we sure? Are we sure? cuz it can't happen
again" is a question about **recurrence of danger**. No Three asks that. A Three's version of
post-victory unease is about **sufficiency of worth** — "was that enough, am I still the one." The
draft has the discriminating quote and argues from the weaker one.

**ENN-M2 — Nothing is left over.** The 7 wing absorbs the appetite and the comedy; the 3 stress
line absorbs the competence-costume years; the 9 growth line absorbs the deference, the therapy
and the bow. The pre-write memo promised the softest seam would be "flagged in the Rabbit Hole,
not hidden" — reader-facing, it is _resolved_ there instead. A reader finishes the Rabbit Hole
with the impression that Six explains one hundred percent of Simone Biles, which is the exact
property that makes typology unfalsifiable and is the thing this page claims to be better than.

**ENN-M3 — Type 3's actual best form is never engaged.** The draft argues against generic "Type
3." The public typing on the board is **3w2** (So Syncd). The 2 wing is what makes that typing
coherent: it explains the warmth, the team-first accounting, the cheering for rivals before her own
routine, and the "letting somebody down" vocabulary — all of which the draft treats as
uncomplicated social-Six evidence. Beating 3w2 is a harder and more interesting fight than beating
3, and the draft never has it.

**ENN-M4 — The best available first-person Six evidence is not on the page.** In the same _Call
Her Daddy_ transcript the draft already quotes, Biles describes pre-Tokyo dread _and the
countermeasure_: "Something's coming up. It feels like I don't know what it is, but like I cannot
control this. It is out of my control. So we're going to control what we can control," including
asking her doctor about a temporary dose change. Anticipatory threat with no object, followed
immediately by concrete preparation, is the Six engine in one passage — first-person,
contemporaneous to the event, and evidentially far stronger than a reconstructed age-three memory.
It is sitting in a transcript the draft mined for four other quotes.

## Red flags

### Blockers

**ENN-R1 — The Type 3 premise the whole call turns on is wrong.** `confidence: high`

- **Passage:** L246, diagnosis section: "An Achiever trusts the scoreboard, because the scoreboard
  is the point. Biles trusts Boorman, and Cecile, and her therapist on Thursdays. She checks with
  them."
- **Reader effect / trust problem:** This is the sentence that converts two anecdotes into a
  typing. An Enneagram-literate reader stops here, because it inverts the best-known feature of
  Type 3. If the discriminator is wrong, the Type 3 reading is not refuted — it is merely asserted
  against — and the page's entire "here is the work" claim fails in the paragraph that carries it.
- **Evidence / reasoning:** 9takes' own `src/blog/enneagram/enneagram-type-3.md` states the
  opposite as the core Three mechanic: "each win satisfies a little less than the last. You need
  bigger achievements to feel the same validation" (L44); "**The moving finish line.** Each win
  satisfies less than the last" (L199); and its FAQ, "The achievement was supposed to answer the
  question 'Am I valuable?' but accomplishments can never resolve that question" (L553). By 9takes'
  own published theory, a Three who wins a third consecutive World title and then cannot feel that
  it landed is _textbook Three_, not counter-evidence to Three. The draft's tiebreaker currently
  argues for the alternative it is trying to defeat.
- **Minimum viable repair:** Replace the audience-based discriminator with a content-based one. The
  Three's post-victory question is about the _sufficiency of the result_ ("was that enough"); the
  Six's is about the _recurrence of the danger_ ("can it happen again"). Then let the second quote
  do the work it is already able to do: "Are we sure? Are we sure? cuz it can't happen again" is a
  question about a future threat, and no Achiever reading explains why the threat, not the verdict,
  is what she cannot close. Three to four sentences; no new research.
- **Expected benefit:** The 6-over-3 call goes from asserted to argued, on an axis that is
  defensible against a reader who knows Type 3, and the page stops contradicting its own pillar
  content.
- **Acceptance test:** Read L246 alongside `src/blog/enneagram/enneagram-type-3.md` L44 and L199. No
  sentence in the people draft may assert that achievement satisfies a Three, and the stated
  discriminator must be testable against both quoted questions rather than against the setting in
  which they were asked.

**ENN-R2 — "Did I do it?" is given a referent the source does not support.** `confidence: high`

- **Passage:** L240: "There were judges, a scoreboard, a medal, and a stadium of people who had
  watched. She walked up to the one adult whose read she trusted and asked her to confirm the
  result." Propagates to the H2 title (L232), FAQ 1 (L68), the TL;DR bullet ("Asks for confirmation
  of settled facts"), and the Rabbit Hole falsifier (L427, "about results already settled").
- **Reader effect / trust problem:** The tiebreaker requires that the thing she asked about was
  _already objectively settled_. If "it" refers to something the scoreboard did not measure, the
  premise evaporates and the quote becomes an ordinary coach-athlete debrief — which is exactly
  what the packet flags as unsettleable (Disputes §2).
- **Evidence / reasoning:** In the source transcript (`/tmp/biles-transcripts/-6Z_whbdk-U.txt`,
  _The Art of Excellence_ Ep. 122), Boorman's own framing runs: "**yes she did win that allaround
  but** at the end of it she came up to me and you know said did I do it **and it was about how she
  managed all of the stress and the pressure to be this three repeat world champion and the growth
  that she had to show and the trust that she had to put in the people around her**… **But it
  wasn't about the gold medal around her neck. It was about the process that it took to get
  there.**" The contrastive "but," and Boorman's explicit exclusion of the medal, both point to "it"
  meaning the year-long process goal — pressure management — not the title. That is a goal no
  scoreboard measured, which is precisely why she would have to ask a person. The draft asserts the
  opposite referent and never tells the reader the source's own gloss competes with it.
- **Minimum viable repair:** Two options, both cheap. (a) Stop asserting the referent: cut "asked
  her to confirm the result" and write the ambiguity into the argument — she asked the one adult
  whose read she trusted whether she had done it, and Boorman's own account says the "it" was not
  the medal. This is _not_ a weakening: a Six asking whether she held together under pressure, on a
  goal only a trusted authority can score, is a cleaner Six than a Six re-checking a scoreboard.
  (b) If the referent is to be kept, it needs a second source that dates and defines it; none
  exists in the packet.
- **Expected benefit:** Removes the article's most attackable inference, and converts it into a
  better one. Also protects the falsifier at L427, which currently depends on the same unestablished
  premise.
- **Acceptance test:** No reader-facing sentence — body, H2, FAQ, or TL;DR — may state or imply
  that the thing she asked Boorman to confirm was the title, the score, or a "settled result,"
  unless a cited source establishes the referent. The Boorman gloss ("it wasn't about the gold
  medal") must be visible to the reader wherever the quote carries argumentative weight.

**ENN-R3 — The spotter deduction is surface behaviour read as motivation, with the competing
explanation omitted and no comparison class.** `confidence: medium-high`

- **Passage:** L384–L386, and the TL;DR bullet "Pays for the spotter: half a point, every time, on
  a vault she can land."
- **Reader effect / trust problem:** This is the page's most concrete, most quotable type argument —
  the ledger calls it "one number that says the whole type." For a reader who follows gymnastics it
  is the weakest, because paying 0.5 for a spotter on the hardest vault any woman has competed is
  what a rational person does with a catastrophic downside, not a personality signature.
- **Evidence / reasoning:** Three problems compound. (1) The draft's own quoted rationale — Cécile
  Landi, "one mistake can have serious consequences" — is a statement about _objective physical
  danger_, i.e. the prudential explanation, offered one line before the psychological reading. (2)
  The packet carries an on-record insider reading the draft does not surface: Chellsie Memmel,
  USAG high-performance technical lead, arguing the deduction is a Code problem — "You can stay [on
  the mat] for Pak salto on bars… Why can't you stay for a double-flipping [element] on vault"
  (Washington Post, Oct 2023). Under that reading, taking the spotter is normal safety practice
  that the rules wrongly penalise. (3) The inference silently requires a comparison class — other
  gymnasts who would _not_ pay — and there is none, because she is the only woman to have competed
  the skill internationally.
- **Minimum viable repair:** Keep the beat; demote the claim. One clause conceding the rules
  dispute, and a narrower type reading: the type evidence is not that she takes the spotter, it is
  that she _budgets_ for the failure case in a sport where nobody else has to price that decision.
  Roughly: "Insiders think the rule itself is wrong — USA Gymnastics' own technical lead has said
  so. Which is the point: she is the only person who has ever had to decide, and she decided in
  favour of the thing that goes wrong." Approximately two sentences.
- **Expected benefit:** Converts the page's most attackable psychological claim into one that
  survives a gymnastics-literate reader, without losing the image.
- **Acceptance test:** The passage must (i) acknowledge that the deduction is contested inside the
  sport as a rules problem, and (ii) not assert or imply that other gymnasts would decline the
  spotter. Separately, "When Biles performs it, Laurent Landi stands on the landing mat" and the
  TL;DR's "every time" are habitual presents describing an arrangement that ended when Landi left
  World Champions Centre on 10 December 2024; they must be put in the past tense or dated to
  Antwerp 2023.

### Concerns

**ENN-C1 — "Confidence: high" no longer survives its own supports.** `confidence: high`
L248. With ENN-R1 and ENN-R2 open, the stated confidence rests on: one coach's decade-old
recollection whose referent is disputed by the coach's own gloss, one self-reported therapy
exchange, and one costly behaviour with a competing non-psychological explanation. That is a solid
case for Type 6 and a _moderate_ case for the tiebreaker over Type 3. **Repair:** after fixing
R1–R3, re-set the label, and consider splitting it — high confidence on Six over Three; moderate
on the specific discriminator. **Benefit:** the confidence label becomes information rather than
decoration, which is the differentiator the page is selling. **Acceptance test:** the confidence
statement must name what it is confident _about_, and every evidence item it leans on must still be
standing after the R-fixes.

**ENN-C2 — The falsifier as written cannot be operated.** `confidence: high`
L427: "This analysis is overturned by evidence that the reassurance-seeking is performance rather
than need." No obtainable evidence can settle whether a private question was performed or needed —
the packet says so directly ("the falsifier as written cannot be tested against the available
sources, only argued"). An untestable falsifier is a rhetorical gesture wearing a scientific
costume. **Repair:** state at least one _behavioural_ falsifier that future evidence could actually
satisfy. Candidates already implied by the piece: she rebuilds without a trusted circle rather than
inside one; she competes the YDP without a spotter when the deduction still applies; she resolves
LA 2028 without consulting the people she consults. **Benefit:** the page becomes the only one on
the sub-SERP that can be proven wrong, which is the whole pitch. **Acceptance test:** a reader can
name one observable future event that would falsify the typing.

**ENN-C3 — The strongest competing typing (3w2) is never engaged as such.** `confidence: medium`
L236 and L427. The board's typing is 3w2; the draft argues against Type 3. The 2 wing is what makes
that reading coherent — it absorbs the warmth, the cheering for rivals, the team-first accounting
and the "let somebody down" vocabulary that the draft claims for social Six. **Repair:** one to two
sentences in the Rabbit Hole counterarguments distinguishing a 3w2's other-orientation (image
maintained _through_ being valued by people) from a social Six's (safety maintained _through_
obligation to people). **Benefit:** the page beats the typing the reader actually arrived with.
**Acceptance test:** the counterargument section names 3w2, not just 3, and offers a discriminator
that is not "she was scared."

**ENN-C4 — Types 2 and 8 are dismissed with a non-discriminating quote.** `confidence: high`
L429: "Type 2 has a smaller case built on the warmth, and Type 8 one built on the clapbacks.
Neither explains 'come pick me up, I'm scared.'" Acute terror after losing air awareness at an
Olympics is a universal human response; it discriminates no type at all, and a Two in distress
asking to be retrieved by her people is entirely ordinary. **Repair:** use 9takes' own published
discriminator instead — `enneagram-type-6.md` L498: "If you're aggressive because you genuinely
don't feel fear, you might be an 8. If you're aggressive _because_ you feel fear and refuse to
submit to it, you're likely a counterphobic 6." Her own line — "I was always the kid that if you
said I couldn't do something, I'm doing it" — is already on the page and fits the second. For Two,
the discriminator is direction: a Two's worst outcome is not being needed; hers is letting people
down. **Benefit:** removes the one place the article uses raw distress as type proof, which is the
error its whole genre is accused of. **Acceptance test:** neither alternative type is dismissed
using an expression of fear or distress as the discriminating fact.

**ENN-C5 — The type explains everything, and the reader never sees the residue.**
`confidence: medium-high` L415, L419, L427 collectively. Wing absorbs the appetite and comedy;
stress line absorbs 2018–21; growth line absorbs the deference and the bow. The pre-write memo
identified "behavior the type does NOT explain" and said it would be "flagged in the Rabbit Hole,
not hidden"; reader-facing, it is explained away instead. The one item the memo listed that nothing
absorbs — sustained, self-initiated record-chasing across a decade, and returning to the site of
her worst public failure to do it again — never appears. **Repair:** two sentences at the end of
the counterarguments naming what Six does not account for, without resolving it. **Benefit:** the
page demonstrates the falsifiability it claims; it is also the single cheapest credibility gain
available. **Acceptance test:** a reader can finish the Rabbit Hole and state one thing about
Simone Biles that the Type 6 reading does not explain.

**ENN-C6 — The spine reads as etiology, and the obvious control is unaddressed.**
`confidence: medium` L264 ("Verification was the only power on offer"), L272 ("The girl who checked
the room at night grew into a woman who still checks"), L360 ("the hallway at three years old,
scaled up to a federal hearing room"), L444 (the closing callback). Read charitably this is
_recurrence_, not causation, and the draft never says the foster home made her a Six. Read the way
most readers will read it, it is a childhood-trauma-causes-type story — a claim the Enneagram does
not make and 9takes has explicitly stopped making. The available control is inside the article:
**Adria went into the same placement and was adopted at the same time.** **Repair:** one clause
converting the frame from origin to first appearance — the hallway is the earliest place the
pattern is visible, not the reason it exists. Adria does not need to be typed; naming that the same
house produced different people is enough. **Benefit:** keeps the strongest scene and the ending
intact while removing the piece's most legitimate theoretical objection. **Acceptance test:** no
sentence states or implies that the foster placement produced the type; the closing callback reads
as pattern continuity rather than cause.

**ENN-C7 — Anxiety and type are never separated.** `confidence: medium` Throughout, but sharpest
at L246 and in the Rabbit Hole. The piece runs on fear-flavoured evidence — dread, therapy, "my
anxiety talking," the twisties — about a subject who has publicly discussed anxiety and an ADHD
diagnosis. It never tells the reader that anxiety is not a type, that all nine types produce
anxious people, and that what makes this Six is the _shape_ of the fear (specific, anticipatory,
relational, resolved by verification) rather than its presence. On a site whose highest-traffic
page is about the Enneagram and mental illness, that clause is owed. **Repair:** one sentence,
best placed in the diagnosis section after L244. **Benefit:** inoculates the page against its most
common and most damaging reader objection ("so anyone anxious is a Six"). **Acceptance test:** a
reader who has an anxiety disorder and is not a Six can find the sentence that tells them so.

## Follow-on questions

**ENN-Q1 — What is the referent of "it" in "did I do it?"**
_What would change:_ everything about ENN-R2 and the framing of the article's most-repeated quote.
If a source establishes she meant the title, the current framing stands as written and the
tiebreaker is restored. If it confirms Boorman's process reading, the repair in R2(a) is required —
and produces a better argument. _Best source to pursue:_ the full audio of _The Art of Excellence_
Ep. 122 (2025-10-12) around the timestamp of the passage, listened to rather than grepped; secondly
Boorman's 2024 memoir _The Balance Point_, which may narrate the same 2015 moment with a referent.

**ENN-Q2 — Has Biles ever described her own motivation in security terms rather than achievement
terms, unprompted?**
_What would change:_ the confidence label. A single unprompted first-person statement organised
around safety rather than worth would move the typing from well-argued inference to
subject-corroborated, and would make ENN-R1 much cheaper to repair. _Best source to pursue:_
_Simone Biles Rising_ Parts 1–2 (Netflix, Jul/Oct 2024), which the packet could not audit; the
Hoda Kotb long-form interviews.

**ENN-Q3 — Does any other elite gymnast decline a spotter where the 0.5 would apply?**
_What would change:_ ENN-R3. A documented case of a comparable athlete refusing the deduction
restores the comparison class and makes the spotter genuinely discriminating rather than
prudential. _Best source to pursue:_ FIG Code of Points neutral-deduction records for spotted
vaults, and gymnastics-technical coverage of the Memmel rules argument (Washington Post, Oct 2023;
Gymnastics Now).

## Limits of this review

- **I evaluated the framework application, not the typing's truth.** The Enneagram is a clustering
  hypothesis, not a diagnostic instrument. Nothing here establishes that Simone Biles is a Type 6;
  it establishes which arguments in this draft would survive a reader who knows the system and which
  would not.
- **ENN-R2 rests on transcript reading, not audio.** The quote and Boorman's surrounding sentences
  were grep-verified against a YouTube auto-transcript that is unpunctuated. The contrastive "but"
  and the explicit exclusion of the medal are unambiguous in the text, but tone and emphasis are not
  recoverable from it, and an auto-transcript can drop clause boundaries. ENN-Q1 is the correct
  resolution path; until then the finding supports "stop asserting the referent," not "the referent
  is definitely the process."
- **I could not audit _Simone Biles Rising_.** Two open items (ENN-Q2, and the packet's own CLM-16
  quote-provenance gap) sit behind a Netflix session this pass could not open.
- **ENN-R1 is grounded in 9takes' own Type 3 material.** That is the right standard for internal
  consistency and it happens to match mainstream Riso-Hudson and Naranjo treatments of Three. If the
  site later revises its Type 3 page, this finding should be re-checked rather than assumed.
- **I did not assess factual accuracy, chronology, SEO, structure, or prose** except where they
  bear on the type argument. The temporal problems I touched (the Landi habitual present, "every
  time") are noted only because the article's behavioural evidence is written in a tense that is no
  longer true.
- **Confidence is stated per finding.** The three blockers are the ones I would defend against
  disagreement; the concerns are meaningful but a reasonable evaluator could rank several of them
  lower.
- **No other perspective's review was read**, and none existed in the directory at review time.
