# Fan Perspective Review: Simone Biles

Standpoint: someone who has watched this sport through three quads, knows the Tokyo team
final rotation by rotation, and can name the five skills — but is not a stan and did not come
here to have her defended.

## Bottom-line verdict

This writer did the reading, and it shows in the places that matter most: the Boorman
material is the freshest Biles reporting I have seen aggregated anywhere, and the half-point
spotter is the best single-number argument about her I have encountered. I would share this
piece. That is the test, and it passes.

Then it loses my trust three times on things a gymnastics fan checks reflexively — and one of
them is a rule this article explains to me, in its own voice, incorrectly. The draft tells me
"Olympic team finals run two-up two-count: two gymnasts per apparatus, both scores count."
That is not the Tokyo format and never has been an Olympic team-final format. It was
three-up, three-count. The draft's whole empathy turn — the section it is proudest of, the
one that answers Kirk and Morgan — is built on a correctly-reasoned argument resting on a
misstated rule, and the ellipsis in the Biles quote sits exactly where her own misstatement
was, which means the article lifted her error out of quotation marks and adopted it.

Second: the draft describes Laurent Landi standing on the landing mat in the habitual
present. Both Landis left World Champions Centre in December 2024 and are now running the
Georgia program together. Every gymnastics fan alive knows this. Reading it in a piece
current to August 2026 is the tell that the writer stopped following the sport at Paris.

Third: the Boorman podcast — the source of this article's best original material — does not
exist under the name printed three times reader-facing. When I went to go listen to it, which
is precisely what a delighted fan does, I could not find it.

None of these break the thesis. All three are cheap to fix. But this is a page whose stated
competitive pitch is "Nobody shows their work. Here is the work," and right now a reader who
knows the sport catches the work being shown wrong. Revise.

## What landed

**FAN-H1 — The Boorman twelve-year testimony is genuinely new material.**

> "I think only twice in the 12 years that I coached her did I ever see her get lost in the
> air."

I have read a great deal about Biles and I had never seen this quantified by the person best
placed to quantify it. Pairing it with the twisties — the faculty that failed her — is the
reason this piece exists. It must survive revision because it is the only thing on the page
that no other Biles article has.

**FAN-H2 — "She never looked. She just always knew where it was."**

Four sentences into the piece and I have a fact about her body I did not have. The
five-word paragraph placement is right. Do not touch it.

**FAN-H3 — The half-point spotter.**

> "Half a point, in a sport decided by hundredths, on a vault she can land, bought against
> what happens if she cannot."

This is the best compressed argument on the page and the one thing here I would actually
repeat to another gymnastics fan. I knew the Antwerp deduction; I had filed it as a safety
footnote. Reading it as a recurring, voluntary, quantified purchase of reassurance is the
"I had never connected them that way" moment. It survives revision at all costs — though see
FAN-R2, because the sentence three lines above it is currently out of date.

**FAN-H4 — "Did I do it?" after a third consecutive world title.**

The choice to anchor the typing on the 2015 Glasgow moment rather than on Tokyo is the
correct read and an unobvious one. Every other Biles personality piece starts at the
withdrawal. Starting at the moment she won something nobody had ever won and immediately
went looking for a person to confirm it is a better argument and a better story.

**FAN-H5 — The two-up two-count section's _reasoning_, separate from its rule.**

> "Take one more vault, catch the floor with an ankle, and four women go home with nothing."

The substitution logic is the single most under-reported fact about the withdrawal and the
draft is right that the critics did not know it. The argument is sound. Only the rule
statement carrying it is wrong, which is why FAN-R1 is a repair and not a demolition.

**FAN-H6 — Conceding Kirk and Morgan's strongest version before answering it.**

> "An Olympic team is four other women who trained five years, and one was going home
> without a medal because of a decision Biles made in a hallway."

Fan pieces about Biles almost never do this. It buys the answer its credibility.

## What missed

**The proprioception connection is set up and then not made.** The draft establishes that
she navigates by a non-visual signal ("more of a whoosh that I hear in my ears") and that in
Tokyo "the signal cut out." It stops one inference short of the thing that would have made
this piece unforgettable to me: a gymnast who spots the landing visually has a second
instrument to fall back on when air sense fails. Biles, by her own account, never used the
visual one. She had no backup. The draft owns both halves of that and never closes it.
See FAN-C6.

**The hook's power is invisible to anyone who does not already coach.** "She never looked"
only lands as extraordinary if you know that finding the landing visually is standard
technique. The draft has Boorman _teasing_ her about it without ever saying why it was
tease-worthy. See FAN-C5.

**Paris is a medal ledger, not a competition.** The section correctly asks whether winning
settled anything, which is the right question. But "the last two days are the ones worth
watching" flattens the actual drama: beam and floor were the same afternoon, roughly ninety
minutes apart. She fell, and then took silver, in one session. That is a better fact than
the one printed. See FAN-C2.

**The bow is described without the rivalry that gives it meaning.** Andrade appears as a
name that beat her. See FAN-C3.

## What I expected

- **The Chiles medal.** Any piece that prints "the first all-Black gymnastics podium in
  Olympic history" and stops is going to be read by fans as either uninformed or evasive,
  because that podium was undone within days and the dispute is still live as of January 2026. FAN-C1.
- **Who is coaching her.** The article prints her own April 2026 line — "physically, my
  coaches will get me in shape" — and never tells me that the coaches who got her in shape
  for eight years both left in December 2024 and now run Georgia together. For a piece whose
  ending is "will she come back," the coaching vacancy is the most concrete open variable
  there is, and it is absent. Folded into FAN-R2.
- **A plain statement that she has not competed since Paris 2024.** The draft circles this
  ("Not a comeback. Not a retirement.") without saying it. Two years out of competition at
  29 is the fact the 50-50 hangs on.
- **Suni Lee.** The most famous downstream consequence of the withdrawal is that Lee won the
  all-around gold Biles vacated. The draft says "The United States won silver" and moves on.
  FAN-C4.

## What surprised me

**Welcome:** the 2013 "safe space to fail" stretch. I did not know Boorman deliberately let
a teenage Biles bottom out, and the draft is right that it reframes why Boorman specifically
was the adult she walked up to in Glasgow. That is real reporting doing real argumentative
work.

**Welcome:** "Do I get new coaches?" followed immediately by "oh, you're right." I had never
seen that exchange. It is funnier and more revealing than anything in the standard Biles
profile.

**Jarring:** the article explaining an Olympic scoring format to me and getting it wrong. In
a piece this carefully sourced, hitting a rule error in the writer's own voice was the single
most disorienting moment of the read, because it retroactively made me start checking things
I had been taking on trust.

**Jarring, minor:** "nine thousand kilometres from Spring, Texas." Houston to Tokyo is
roughly 10,700 km. Small, but it is in the emotional climax of the piece and it is the kind
of number a fan idly checks.

## Red flags

### FAN-R1 — BLOCKER — The Tokyo team-final format is stated incorrectly, in the article's own voice, twice reader-facing

- **Passage:** L334, body: "Olympic team finals run two-up two-count: two gymnasts per
  apparatus, both scores count, nothing thrown away." Also L71, FAQ answer: "under the
  two-up two-count format she was entered on every event."
- **Reader effect / trust problem:** This is the article teaching me a rule I already know,
  incorrectly, in the section built to defeat Kirk and Morgan. A gymnastics reader stops
  reading and starts auditing. It also undermines the page's entire competitive claim
  ("Nobody shows their work. Here is the work"), because the work shown is wrong.
- **Evidence:** The Tokyo 2020 women's artistic team **final** was three-up, three-count —
  four-member teams, three gymnasts per apparatus, all three scores counting, no drop score.
  Wikipedia's event record states it verbatim: "In the final, each team selected three
  gymnasts to compete on each apparatus. All scores on each apparatus were summed to give a
  final team score." (Confirmed this pass; the 4-up/3-count figure that circulates belongs to
  _qualification_, not the final.) No Olympic team final has ever run two-up two-count.
  **Note:** the shared evidence packet asserts "Two-up two-count is the correct Olympic
  team-final format" (CLM-09). That is also wrong, so this cannot be resolved by deferring to
  the packet.
- **Compounding problem:** the draft's rendering of the Biles quote at L334 places its
  ellipsis exactly where her own words "since I'm on every event, it's two up two count"
  appear in the raw transcript. She misspoke; the draft elided her misstatement and then
  restated it as fact unquoted. The article now owns an error it could have simply reported.
- **Minimum viable repair:** In both L334 and L71, replace with the correct format — three
  gymnasts per apparatus in the final, every score counting, no drop score. The argument
  needs no other change: with no throwaway score and Biles slated on all four events, a
  mid-final injury is exactly as catastrophic as the draft says, and withdrawing between
  rotations is what allowed Chiles to be added. Optionally restore the elided clause and let
  her misstatement stand inside the quote where it belongs.
- **Expected benefit:** The section's argument becomes checkable and survives contact with a
  reader who knows the sport, at zero cost to its rhetorical force.
- **Confidence:** High. Verified against the primary event record this pass.
- **Acceptance test:** No sentence in the draft states a per-apparatus count of two for an
  Olympic team final; the stated format matches the Tokyo 2020 team-final record; the
  substitution argument still reads as sound without the word "two."

### FAN-R2 — BLOCKER — The coaching arrangement is written in a present tense that ended in December 2024

- **Passage:** L384: "When Biles performs it, Laurent Landi stands on the landing mat and
  does not touch her." Compounded at L382 ("On the vault, her coach's") and at L438, which
  prints her line "physically, my coaches will get me in shape" with no context.
- **Reader effect / trust problem:** This is the fan tell. Both Landis left World Champions
  Centre in December 2024 — Cécile to Georgia as head coach, Laurent following as associate
  head coach — and Biles has not competed since Paris 2024. A habitual present describing an
  arrangement two years dead, inside a piece whose current-tense window is April–August 2026,
  reads as a writer working from 2024 clips. It lands on the page's single best argument
  (FAN-H3), so the damage is concentrated exactly where the piece can least afford it.
- **Evidence:** Packet CLM-12 and S-11 establish both departures (December 2024). Confirmed
  and extended this pass: Laurent Landi was hired as associate head coach at Georgia in 2026,
  reuniting with Cécile as head coach; no replacement coach for Biles has been publicly
  identified.
- **Minimum viable repair:** Two changes. (1) Move L384 into past tense anchored to Antwerp
  2023 — "At Antwerp in 2023, Laurent Landi stood on the landing mat and did not touch her" —
  which costs nothing, since the deduction is already sourced to that meet. (2) Add one
  clause at L438 noting that the coaches she refers to left WCC in December 2024 and now run
  Georgia. That single clause also converts the section's weakest sentence into its most
  concrete open question.
- **Expected benefit:** Removes the strongest signal that the writer does not follow the
  sport, and turns a currency liability into the most substantive unknown in the comeback
  story.
- **Confidence:** High on the departures (two independent confirmations). Medium on "no
  named replacement" — that is an absence of reporting, so the repair should state what is
  known and not assert a vacancy.
- **Acceptance test:** No sentence describes a current coaching arrangement at WCC; a fan
  reading the 2026 section can tell who coached her through Paris and that they have gone.

### FAN-R3 — BLOCKER — The podcast carrying this article's best original material is named wrongly, three times, reader-facing

- **Passage:** L202, L206, L238 — "_Beyond Medals and Perfection_ podcast," italicised as a
  publication in all three.
- **Reader effect / trust problem:** This is a fan-specific failure and it is the sharpest

  plus the host's own episode page). The quotes themselves are verbatim and October 2025 is
  the correct date, so only the name is at fault.
- **Minimum viable repair:** Replace all three with "_The Art of Excellence_" and, on first
  use only, the episode title. Also correct `scripts/blog-source-audit.mjs` OUTLETS, which
  has the wrong name registered and will otherwise keep grading the misattribution clean on
  every future pass.
- **Expected benefit:** The article's most shareable material becomes findable, and the audit
  tool stops certifying the error.
- **Confidence:** High.
- **Acceptance test:** Searching every italicised publication name in the draft returns a
  real, locatable source; the source-audit script no longer contains "Beyond Medals and
  Perfection" as an outlet.

## Specific improvements

### FAN-C1 — Carry the Chiles medal reallocation in one clause (highest priority concern)

- **Location:** L398 body ("the first all-Black gymnastics podium in Olympic history") and
  L80 FAQ.
- **Problem:** Among gymnastics fans this is the single most litigated fact of Paris 2024.
  CAS voided the inquiry that lifted Chiles to bronze (filed four seconds late), reallocated
  it to Ana Bărbosu, and in January 2026 the Swiss Federal Supreme Court sent the matter back
  to CAS. A fan reading an unqualified "first all-Black podium in Olympic history" in a piece
  dated August 2026 concludes the writer either does not know or is protecting the moment.
  Both readings cost more than the clause would.
- **Evidence:** Packet CLM-18, S-18, S-19. The claim is true _of the ceremony as it happened_
  and GMA described it that way at the time — so the fix is qualification, not retraction.
- **Repair:** One subordinate clause fixing the claim to the ceremony and noting the bronze
  was later reallocated and remains disputed. Do not expand it — the research file's call
  that the CAS fight is not Biles's story is right, and a fan does not need it relitigated
  here, only acknowledged.
- **Benefit:** Converts the passage from a fan credibility risk into evidence the writer was
  actually watching. The bow also reads _better_ with it: the gesture survives the medal.
- **Confidence:** High on the facts; medium on placement, since the section is tight on the
  4,500-word ceiling.
- **Acceptance test:** A reader who knows the Bărbosu decision finds nothing in the passage
  they would have to correct.

### FAN-C2 — Fix "the last two days"; beam and floor were the same afternoon

- **Location:** L398, "The last two days are the ones worth watching."
- **Problem:** Both event finals were 5 August 2024, in the same session block. As written it
  is either loose or wrong, and it discards a stronger fact.
- **Evidence:** Packet CLM-14, S-12, S-13.
- **Repair:** "The last afternoon is the one worth watching" — then let the compression do
  the work: she fell off beam into fifth, and roughly ninety minutes later took floor silver
  and bowed. The article's thesis is better served by the whiplash of one session than by two
  vague days.
- **Benefit:** Corrects a date a gymnastics reader will catch, and buys a sharper beat for
  free.
- **Confidence:** High.
- **Acceptance test:** No sentence implies beam and floor finals fell on different days.

### FAN-C3 — Introduce Andrade in one clause so the bow means something

- **Location:** L398, "Then the floor final, silver behind Rebeca Andrade."
- **Problem:** For a reader who watched the quad, Andrade is not a name that beat her — she
  is the only gymnast who ever consistently pushed Biles, and the Biles–Andrade rivalry was
  the defining one of the era. Without that, the bow reads as generic good sportsmanship
  rather than the specific, loaded gesture it was, and "She's queen" loses its weight.
- **Evidence:** Packet S-17 carries the bow and the quotes; the rivalry framing is standard
  in Paris coverage.
- **Repair:** One clause identifying Andrade as the Brazilian who had become her closest
  rival of the quad, placed at first mention.
- **Benefit:** The bow becomes the best fan-facing moment in the Paris section instead of a
  procedural detail. Costs roughly eight words — and the cohesion notes already flag "Owens
  is in Indianapolis now, ninth NFL season" as the cheapest eight words on the page.
- **Confidence:** High.
- **Acceptance test:** A reader who has never watched gymnastics can say why bowing to _this_
  competitor was notable.

### FAN-C4 — Acknowledge that Suni Lee won the all-around gold

- **Location:** L338–L340, the Tokyo aftermath.
- **Problem:** The most cited downstream fact of the withdrawal is missing. Its absence is
  conspicuous to a fan precisely because it cuts slightly against the section's grief note —
  something good came out of the hallway decision, for a teammate.
- **Evidence:** Olympic record; uncontested.
- **Repair:** One clause in the aftermath, noting Lee took the all-around title.
- **Benefit:** Signals the writer is reporting the event and not only Biles's interior
  experience of it, which is the difference between an informed piece and a sympathetic one.
- **Confidence:** High on the fact; medium on whether the section has room.
- **Acceptance test:** The Tokyo aftermath names what happened to the competition Biles left.

### FAN-C5 — Tell the reader that spotting the landing is standard technique

- **Location:** L202, the cold open; Boorman "used to tease her about the ground."
- **Problem:** The hook depends entirely on the reader understanding that gymnasts are
  coached to find the landing visually. Without that, "she never looked at the ground" reads
  as an unremarkable quirk, and the piece spends its single best asset on a reader who cannot
  see why it matters. It also leaves Boorman's teasing unmotivated.
- **Evidence:** Reasoning from the passage itself — Boorman's teasing only makes sense as a
  response to something unusual. Not independently sourced this pass.
- **Repair:** One clause establishing that finding the floor on the way down is what gymnasts
  are taught to do, placed before or inside the Boorman question.
- **Benefit:** Converts the hook from a quirk into an anomaly, which is what the rest of the
  article needs it to be — and sets up FAN-C6.
- **Confidence:** High that the clause helps; medium on exact phrasing, which should be
  checked against a coaching source rather than asserted, since the draft would be making a
  technique claim in its own voice.
- **Acceptance test:** A reader with no gymnastics background can state why not looking is
  remarkable.

### FAN-C6 — Close the proprioception loop; this is the piece's biggest missed delight

- **Location:** Between L206 ("more of a whoosh that I hear in my ears") and L213 ("the
  signal cut out"), and again available at the close, L446.
- **Problem:** The draft owns both halves of an insight I have not seen anywhere and never
  joins them. She navigated by a non-visual signal. The twisties are the failure of exactly
  that signal. A gymnast who relies on visual spotting has a second instrument when air sense
  goes; by her own account she did not use the visual one. The connection is right there, and
  the article walks past it into metaphor ("the signal cut out").
- **Evidence:** The draft's own two facts, plus packet S-30 (the twisties as a loss of air
  sense / proprioceptive feedback, not a psychiatric condition). This is an _inference_ the
  draft's evidence licenses, not a documented finding.
- **Repair:** One or two sentences making the connection explicit, written as inference and
  hedged accordingly — the gift and the failure were the same faculty, and she had no
  fallback. Do not state it as established sports science.
- **Benefit:** This is the "I knew those moments, but I had never connected them that way"
  payload. It is the one thing in this article I would lead with when sharing it, and right
  now the reader has to assemble it themselves.
- **Confidence:** High that it is the strongest available improvement; medium on the claim
  itself, which is why it must be hedged and must not be asserted as fact.
- **Acceptance test:** The connection appears once, explicitly, in hedged language, and is
  not restated at the close (the close already works — see Preserve list).

### FAN-C8 — Check the Tokyo–Texas distance

- **Location:** L324, "nine thousand kilometres from Spring, Texas."
- **Problem:** Houston–Tokyo is roughly 10,700 km. If that is right, the figure understates

## Follow-on questions

**FAN-Q3 — Who is coaching Biles now, and has she said anything about it?**
_What would change:_ If a coach has been publicly named, FAN-R2's repair becomes a
one-clause factual update and the 2026 section gains its most concrete comeback detail. If
she has publicly said she has no coach, that is a far stronger ending than the locker photo,
because it makes the 50-50 practical rather than psychological. _Best source:_ Biles's own
Instagram; USA Gymnastics; Gymnastics Now / GymCastic, which cover WCC staffing closely.

**FAN-Q4 — Did Biles ever correct or repeat the "two up two count" description elsewhere?**
_What would change:_ If she has used the phrase repeatedly, the draft could report the
misstatement as hers — which is itself mildly interesting about how she narrates the
withdrawal — instead of silently adopting it. _Best source:_ the _Call Her Daddy_ transcript
already in hand, plus _Simone Biles Rising_ Part 1, which covers Tokyo.

**FAN-Q5 — Where does the "point of strength" therapy line actually come from?**
_What would change:_ Packet CLM-16 flags this as attributed to _Simone Biles Rising_ with no
locatable trail, and the closest documented wordings come from a Hoda Kotb interview. If it
cannot be located, it must be re-attributed or cut — it sits in the section built to test the
thesis. A fan will not catch this; a journalist will. _Best source:_ the docuseries itself
(needs an authenticated session), or the Kotb interview transcript.

**FAN-Q6 — Is the beam/floor same-day framing better served by naming the session?**
_What would change:_ Minor. If the two finals were in a single session with a defined gap, a
specific interval ("inside two hours") is stronger than "the last afternoon." _Best source:_
the Paris 2024 competition schedule.
