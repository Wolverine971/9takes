## Bottom-line verdict

Read as of August 2027, this article's argument still works and its reporting does not.

That split is the whole review. The thesis is built almost entirely on fixed past evidence — a
2000 foster placement, a 2015 question to a coach, a 2021 Senate hearing, a 2024 podcast — none
of which can decay. Strip every 2026 fact off the page and the Type 6 case stands untouched. That
is rare in this format and it is the piece's largest durability asset.

The perishable material is the problem, and it is concentrated exactly where it does the most
damage: the meta description, an H2 heading with a hard-coded age baked into its anchor slug, and
the final section, which is the ending. Two claims are already wrong on the day of publication —
"Nothing further" about her health (contradicted by her own 22 July 2026 post) and a habitual
present tense describing a coach who left in December 2024. Neither is an argument failure; both
are currency failures in sentences the article leans on.

The timing pressure is sharper than the draft assumes. The 2026 World Championships open in
Rotterdam on 17 October 2026 — ten weeks after publication — and the 2027 Worlds (6 October 2027)
sit on the LA28 qualification pathway. The "still-open 50-50" is not a twelve-month framing. It
is a ten-week framing.

Revise. Every repair is a dated clause, not new research.

## What landed

**FUTURE-H1 — The bookend has zero temporal dependency.**

> "She never looked. She just always knew where it was."

and the close:

> "She always knew where the ground was, and she never once looked at it. She was too scared to
> look."

Nothing here is indexed to a news cycle, a roster, an age, or an undecided comeback. This survives
her retiring, competing, winning, or never appearing again. It is the one part of the page that
reads identically in 2027, 2030 and 2040. **Must survive revision untouched** — and note that if
the perishable material in H2 9 is ever cut wholesale in a later refresh, this close is what makes
that cut survivable.

**FUTURE-H2 — The spine is dated, past, and closed.**

> "Two sentences, eight years apart, running the same errand."

The 2015 "did I do it?" and the 2024 "are we sure? are we sure?" are both fixed, both dated, both
about events already concluded. The article's central claim rests on them rather than on anything
current. Checked the arithmetic because relative spans usually rot here: 29 October 2015 to the
17 April 2024 _Call Her Daddy_ release is eight years and six months, so "eight years apart" is
right, and it is measured between two past points rather than from the present, which is why it
never ages. This is the correct way to build a durable claim and it should be the model for the
repairs elsewhere.

**FUTURE-H3 — The Tokyo timeline is built from procedure, not from news.**

> "An Olympic team final is a procedure: event order, warm-up length, touches on the apparatus,
> who can replace whom, all fixed before anyone walks in."

Two-up two-count, the one-touch warm-up, the substitution rule — competition format changes across
quads but the 2021 format is a closed historical fact, and the section explicitly scopes itself to
that final. It cannot be overtaken by events. The same is true of the Senate testimony section,
which is the best-sourced and most permanent material on the page.

**FUTURE-H4 — The hallway scene and its callback.**

> "walking down a hall in the dark to see whether the room was still the way she left it."

A 2000 memory and a 2026 photograph, joined. The 2000 half is permanent. The 2026 half is the
fragile one (see FUTURE-C2 and FUTURE-C8), but the _structure_ survives substitution — any future
checking behaviour slots into the same sentence. Preserve the construction even if the locker
photo eventually has to be swapped for a newer instance.

**FUTURE-H5 — The Paris section's closing comparison.**

> "A third consecutive World title produced 'did I do it?' Three Olympic golds produced a woman
> who, two years later, still cannot tell you whether she is finished."

The comparison itself is durable — it is a claim about what winning did and did not settle at two
fixed moments. Only the trailing "still cannot tell you" is a now-claim. Fix the tense (see
FUTURE-C2) and the whole move keeps working under any 2028 outcome, including a retirement
announcement, which would arguably strengthen it.

## What missed

**The page never tells a 2027 reader when it was written.** `lastmod` is frontmatter; nothing
reader-facing carries an as-of date. The final section is an explicit live status report — Laureus
in April, hospital in June, locker in August — presented in a continuous present with no anchor
telling the reader how old that present is. A reader arriving in 2027 from a comeback headline
cannot tell whether this page predates or postdates the news that brought them.

**The most perishable material occupies the most load-bearing position.** H2 9 is the ending. The
future contract's failure mode — current events used as the enduring argument rather than as an
appendix — is present here structurally rather than logically: the _reasoning_ correctly treats
2026 as illustration, but the _architecture_ makes 2026 the last thing the reader reads and the
note the piece closes on.

**The description promises an open question as the page's subject.** `description` frontmatter
ends "and a still-open 50-50." That is the SERP-facing sentence. It is a status claim with a
ten-week shelf life sitting in the one place that is expensive to keep current and most visible
when stale.

## What I expected

Given the review date and how much 2026 material the piece carries, I expected three things a
durability-conscious draft would have:

1. **A visible as-of stamp** on the section that reports live status. Absent.
2. **Dated attribution on every quote in the current-events section.** The Laureus/CNN quote is
   dated April 2026. The L'Équipe quote sitting beside it is undated and, per the packet's
   internal dating evidence, is one to two years older (see FUTURE-C5).
3. **Age and roster facts kept out of headings and slugs**, where they are cheap to write and
   expensive to change. Both appear (see FUTURE-C1, FUTURE-C3).

What I did _not_ expect and do not require: encyclopedic hedging, or a refusal to use 2026 news.
Using current events is correct here. Anchoring them is the missing step.

## What surprised me

**Welcome:** how little of the argument depends on anything recent. I ran the removal test — delete
H2 9 entirely and the article still opens, argues, evidences, tests itself against Paris, and
closes on the air-awareness reversal. Most people-profiles built around an unresolved comeback
fail that test badly. This one passes. The draft has bought itself durability it does not seem to
know it has, which is why the fixes below are cheap.

**Jarring:** the ending's emotional payload rests on the single most ephemeral artifact on the
page. The locker photo is an Instagram Story from 13 August 2026 — three days before compile — and
per the packet the originals have already expired. The most load-bearing image in the close is
also the least verifiable, and it is one of a series of Stories rather than the single decisive
gesture the prose implies.

## Red flags

**FUTURE-R1 — "Nothing further" is false at publication and is a negative claim she has said she
will break. (BLOCKER)**

_Location:_ H2 9, "Simone Biles is 29 and the answer is still 50-50," second paragraph.

> "In June 2026 Biles was hospitalised for something she has never named, posted a photo of her
> wristbands from bed, called it one of the scariest experiences of her life, and thanked 'my
> close circle who reached out, checked in, visited & or sent flowers.' Nothing further. An open
> book about the facts, layers about the fear."

_Reader effect / trust problem:_ "Nothing further" is contradicted six weeks before publication.
On 22 July 2026 she posted about undergoing a medical procedure with Owens present ("His birthday
but he takes me to get my procedure"), reported by Forbes (23 July), Olympics.com and TheGrio (25
July). She has also said she plans to explain "sooner or later." So the sentence is wrong now, and
the thing it asserts — silence — is something the subject has publicly committed to ending. A
reader who follows her sees a page that stopped paying attention in June. Worse, this sentence is
the _proof_ for the "open book about the facts, layers about the fear" reading, so a currency
error is carrying interpretive weight.

_Evidence:_ Evidence packet CLM-22 (status: "false as of the compile date"), dated timeline entry
2026-07-22, sources S-22, S-23.

_Minimum viable repair:_ Replace the absolute with a dated, bounded statement. Add the July
procedure as a second beat, and date-stamp the non-disclosure: what is undisclosed is the _cause_,
not the existence of updates. Something with the shape of "…and in July she posted from a
procedure she has still not explained, saying she would 'sooner or later.'"

_Expected benefit:_ The paragraph becomes accurate, stays accurate when she does explain, and the
"open book about facts, layers about fear" reading gets _stronger_ evidence — two disclosures with
the cause withheld both times is a better demonstration of the pattern than one silence.

_Confidence:_ High. Directly evidenced in the packet.

_Acceptance test:_ No sentence in H2 9 asserts an open-ended "never" or "nothing further" about
her health. Every non-disclosure claim carries an as-of date. The paragraph reads correctly if she
publishes a full explanation tomorrow.
