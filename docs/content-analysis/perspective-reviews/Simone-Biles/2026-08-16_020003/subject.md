## Bottom-line verdict

Judged on the only question this role asks — _even if she rejects the Type 6 reading, has she been
represented fairly?_ — the article gets the hard things right and the easy things wrong.

The hard things: it lets her name her own worst moment against the grain of its own structure, it
refuses to claim access to her mind at the moment it would most benefit from claiming it, it
concedes her critics' strongest point before answering it, it protects her from the specific
misreading that got her called a sociopath in 2021, and it states what would overturn its own
thesis. Those are not small. Most celebrity-typing writing fails every one of them.

The easy things are sourcing and fidelity, and there the page breaks its own promise. It says
"Nobody shows their work. Here is the work," then sends the reader to a podcast that does not
exist under the name given, attributes a quotation to a Netflix docuseries that does not appear
to carry it, changes a word inside her quotation marks in the direction the argument needs, and
asserts a silence about her health that she had already broken three weeks before the draft's own
date — while reading that silence as a symptom.

None of this is malice, and the piece is warm about her throughout. But a fairness review cannot
wave through altered quotations and an invasive claim that is factually untrue. Four blockers,
all with bounded repairs. `revise`.

## What landed

**SUBJ-H1 — Her own definition of her low point, kept against the article's structure.** (L366–368)

> "Ask her for the lowest point of the Tokyo era and she does not name Tokyo."
> "There were so many days I would come back in the gym and it was like one step forward, five
> steps back."

The whole piece is built around Tokyo. This paragraph tells the reader that the woman it is about
does not agree that Tokyo was the worst of it. Letting the subject overrule the article's own
centre of gravity, in her own words, in the article, is the most subject-respecting move on the
page. **Must survive:** it is the single clearest evidence that she was listened to rather than
arranged.

**SUBJ-H2 — The twisties defined as physiology, not pathology.** (FAQ, L74)

> "It is a documented proprioceptive phenomenon in gymnastics, not a psychiatric diagnosis."

One sentence that forecloses the exact misreading that produced "she's totally a sociopath." It
sits in an FAQ, which is where a reader arriving cold from search will hit it first.
**Must survive:** it is the most protective sentence in the document and it costs nothing.

**SUBJ-H3 — The critics given their real case, with her responsibility left intact.** (L332)

> "Give them their strongest version, because they had one. An Olympic team is four other women
> who trained five years, and one was going home without a medal because of a decision Biles made
> in a hallway. That cost landed on real people, and Biles has never argued otherwise."

My contract asks whether criticism is explained without erasing responsibility. This does both at
once and, crucially, credits _her_ with acknowledging the cost rather than defending her from it.
**Must survive:** removing it would convert the section from an argument into an apologia.

**SUBJ-H4 — Refusing to claim the inside of her head at the one moment it would pay.** (L336)

> "Whether she ran that in the hallway or assembled it three years later is not recoverable…"

The evidence is a retrospective interview given ~33 months later in a comeback year. The draft
says so. **Must survive.** One wrinkle worth naming: the clause that follows — "barely changes the
finding, because building a load-bearing justification after the fact does the same work" — leaves
her no available position in which she is simply telling the truth about her reasoning. The
epistemics are right; the framing quietly makes her sincerity unfalsifiable. A repair is in
SUBJ-I9.

**SUBJ-H5 — Her interior monologue is actually hers.** (L312)

> `<p class="inner-thought">America hates me. The world is going to hate me. And I can only see
what they're saying on Twitter right now.</p>`

Verbatim from her own account — "That was my first thought." In a piece that infers motive
constantly, the one place styled as direct access to her mind is the one place it did not invent
anything. **Must survive**, and the technique is worth keeping as a house rule.

**SUBJ-H6 — Adoption handled in her vocabulary, not the outsider's.** (L266–268)

> "Biles calls them mom and dad without qualification… 'Well, yeah, aren't you? Where'd you come
> from?'"

The one well-documented occasion on which Biles publicly corrected a misrepresentation of herself
was an NBC analyst insisting her grandparents were not her parents. This passage lands on the
right side of that correction without ever having to cite it, and Nellie's "I knew I had my own
barriers" keeps the family complicated rather than sentimental. **Must survive.**

**SUBJ-H7 — A stated falsifier and a refusal to let the type explain everything.** (L427, L429)

> "This analysis is overturned by evidence that the reassurance-seeking is performance rather than
> need." / "Type 2 has a smaller case built on the warmth, and Type 8 one built on the clapbacks."

Naming the condition under which the reading collapses is what separates a typing argument from a
verdict about a person. **Must survive.**

## What missed

**The page's differentiating promise is the promise it breaks.** L236 stakes the entire
competitive claim on "Nobody shows their work. Here is the work." The work is then shown to a
podcast that does not exist under that name (SUBJ-R4), a docuseries that does not appear to carry
the quote attributed to it (SUBJ-R3), and a quotation altered inside its own quotation marks
(SUBJ-R2). A reader who takes the article's invitation and checks two of its citations finds two
dead ends. That is not a sourcing footnote; it is the thesis of the page failing in public.

**The "current-tense anchor" is stale in the one place currency is a boundary.** The piece
deliberately closes on 2026 material. Its most sensitive 2026 claim — that she has said nothing
further about her health — was already false when the draft was dated (SUBJ-R1).

**The setup sentence is wrong about its own sources.** "So Syncd says 3w2. Personality Database
crowd-votes ESFJ. One Enneagram site says Six." So Syncd lists ESFJ _and_ 3w2, so the two
aggregators agree on the MBTI axis and differ only on the Enneagram. The "three answers" framing
that justifies the whole article is looser than the record.

- **Her privacy treated as a boundary rather than as data** (SUBJ-R1).

## What surprised me

**Welcome.** That the article puts her disagreement with its own premise in the text (SUBJ-H1). I
did not expect a typing piece to hand the subject a veto over its structure and then keep writing.

**Welcome.** That the hardest interiority beat on the page turned out to be verbatim (SUBJ-H5).

**Jarring.** "An open book about the facts, layers about the fear" (L440), applied to an
undisclosed medical emergency. She supplied the onion/layers metaphor about _trauma_ disclosure
(L372); the draft extends it to a hospitalisation she has never characterised, and uses her
non-disclosure as type evidence.

**Jarring.** "obviously" replacing "I think" inside her quotation marks (L396). Small, one word,
and it moves her stated confidence in exactly the direction the argument profits from.

## Red flags

### SUBJ-R1 — BLOCKER. Her health silence is asserted as fact, is untrue, and is read as a symptom

- **Passage:** L440. "In June 2026 Biles was hospitalised for something she has never named…
  **Nothing further. An open book about the facts, layers about the fear.**"
- **Trust problem:** the most invasive paragraph in the piece takes the one area where she has
  drawn an explicit boundary, states that she went silent, and converts the silence into evidence
  for the thesis. She is characterised as withholding at the moment she was in fact disclosing.
  Being told your privacy is a symptom is the sharpest misread available; here it also rests on a
  false fact.
- **Evidence:** on **22 July 2026** she publicly posted about undergoing a medical procedure with
  Owens present ("His birthday but he takes me to get my procedure"), and has said she will
  explain "sooner or later." Packet CLM-22 (`status: false as of the compile date`), sources S-22
  (Forbes, 2026-06-06) and S-23 (Forbes / Olympics.com / TheGrio, 2026-07-23→25). The _cause_
  remains undisclosed; the _silence_ does not.
- **Minimum viable repair:** correct to the record — June hospitalisation, 22 July procedure, cause
  still undisclosed, she has said she will explain. Delete "Nothing further." and delete the
  diagnostic gloss. If the layers frame is kept, apply it only where she applied it (L372, trauma).
- **Expected benefit:** removes a false statement, removes the only passage that pathologises a
  medical boundary, and the paragraph gets _more_ current, not less.
- **Confidence:** high.
- **Acceptance test:** the paragraph contains no claim that she stopped speaking about the episode,
  and no sentence treating non-disclosure of a medical cause as evidence for Type 6. A reader aware
  of the 22 July post finds nothing in the paragraph that contradicts it.

### SUBJ-R2 — BLOCKER. A quotation is altered inside quotation marks, in the argument's favour

- **Passage:** L396. Draft: _"There was always a part of me that thought what if? **But that was
  obviously my anxiety talking.**"_
- **Trust problem:** published wording is "…**And I think** that was my anxiety talking." Swapping a
  hedge for "obviously" converts a tentative self-assessment into a dismissive certainty, making
  her sound like someone who has filed her own anxiety away as self-evidently irrational. That is a
  harder claim about her inner state than she made, and it is the claim the thesis benefits from.
  Regardless of size, changing a subject's words inside quotation marks is the one thing this
  review cannot pass.
- **Evidence:** packet CLM-17; S-16, Olympics.com (rightsholder), 2024-08.
- **Minimum viable repair:** restore the published wording verbatim.
- **Expected benefit:** eliminates the clearest fidelity breach on the page at zero argumentative
  cost — her hedged version is, if anything, more useful to a Type 6 reading.
- **Confidence:** high.
- **Acceptance test:** string-match every reader-facing sentence inside quotation marks against its
  cited source; each matches verbatim or marks elision with an ellipsis.
