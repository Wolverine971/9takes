## Identity and scope

Tate Rosner McRae is a Canadian singer, songwriter, and dancer born July 1, 2003. This packet audits a personality-analysis draft arguing that her public evidence best fits Enneagram Type 3w4. The type, wing, instinct, and motives are interpretations; her biography, quotations, chart history, and reported behavior are verifiable or attributable. Knowledge cutoff: August 27, 2026.

## Dated timeline

- **Childhood:** McRae trained at her mother's dance studio after the family returned to Calgary from Oman. In a 2025 Apple Music interview she recalled setting a 6 a.m. alarm and doing 500 crunches at age eight.
- **2016:** At 13, she placed third on _So You Think You Can Dance: The Next Generation_; Paula Abdul praised her performance on the judging panel.
- **October 20, 2017:** A booked dance studio led McRae to upload “one day,” written and recorded in about 20 minutes. The Line of Best Fit later reported 31 million views.
- **2019:** RCA signed her.
- **2020:** “you broke me first” became her first global music breakout.
- **September 2023:** “greedy” began its run to No. 3 on the U.S. Billboard Hot 100.
- **February 21, 2025:** _So Close to What_ was released and entered the Billboard 200 at No. 1 with 177,000 equivalent album units and 137.3 million U.S. on-demand streams, per Billboard/Luminate.
- **May 2025:** Morgan Wallen's “What I Want,” featuring McRae, became her first U.S. Hot 100 No. 1.
- **October 31, 2025:** An upside-down-microphone clip prompted lip-sync allegations. Variety later called the circulating edit deceptive and reported McRae's rebuttal.
- **December 11, 2025:** Rolling Stone published the interview in which McRae described Tatiana, the 15,000-person stage versus four-person dinner split, sexualization, and the Wallen backlash.
- **August 1-2, 2026:** She headlined Osheaga and Lollapalooza. Billboard Canada/Luminate reported 38.3 million U.S. on-demand streams over the next five days, an 18 percent increase from the comparable prior window.

## First-person evidence

- **GRAMMY.com, 2022:** McRae described severe impostor syndrome and a recurring vision that she is not supposed to be in the room.
- **V Magazine:** She described work and results as “black and white,” supporting the draft's effort-to-proof thesis.
- **Apple Music/Zane Lowe, February 2025:** She connected milestones with persistent dissatisfaction; recalled the childhood crunch routine; called songwriting necessary to understand her own thoughts; and described performance pressure and anxiety.
- **Variety Hitmakers, 2025:** She described an extreme split between a shy private self and a fearless onstage self.
- **Rolling Stone, December 2025:** She explained why she named the onstage persona Tatiana, described feeling safer before 15,000 people than at a dinner for four, addressed sexualization, and explained her Wallen decision.
- **Variety, August 2026:** She described post-tour insomnia, mirror fatigue, the need to relearn her preferences, her guarded circle, and the small-room/arena split.

## Third-party testimony

- **Paula Abdul, 2016:** On _So You Think You Can Dance_, Abdul told McRae that her performance was “perfection.” This is named judge testimony from McRae's early public career.
- **Hailey Bieber, Variety, 2026:** Bieber described McRae as distinctive, compelling to watch, and exceptionally hardworking. This is named peer testimony.
- **Variety, December 2025:** The outlet's report characterized the viral microphone clip as deceptively edited while noting McRae's strong denial. This does not establish how much of a full show is live, and the draft now preserves that limit.

## Disputes and unresolved questions

- McRae has not publicly confirmed an Enneagram type. The article must remain an interpretation of public evidence.
- The viral microphone clip cannot establish how much of an 88-date tour was sung live. Any percentage claim is unsupported.
- The self-preservation-first instinct call is less secure than the core Type 3 argument and belongs inside the technical accordion.
- McRae's psychic-dream claim is her stated belief, not a verified paranormal event; the draft uses it only to mark the frame's limit.
- Future album-four timing and creative direction are not announced and should not be predicted.
