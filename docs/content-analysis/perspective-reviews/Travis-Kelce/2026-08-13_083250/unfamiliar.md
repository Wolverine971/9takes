## Bottom-line verdict

Read cold, with no prior knowledge of Travis Kelce beyond "football player married to Taylor Swift," this article works. Within roughly 250 words I knew who he was; by the end of the first section I could state both the type answer (Enneagram Type 7, the Enthusiast — appetite-driven, novelty-seeking) and the thesis (his greatness came from discipline other people installed around his appetite, and the test of his life is staying in a room he can't leave). The cold open requires zero football literacy, the Enneagram claim is delivered in plain English, the counter-typing material is quarantined into a clearly skippable accordion, and the ending leaves a real question rather than a cliffhanger. No blocker: comprehension never breaks, and nothing read as invasive or unearned on the unaided pass.

Five concerns, all repairable at clause level. The highest-priority one is packet-supplied rather than felt on the cold read: the No. 87 section — the passage a new reader is most likely to retell — states as flat fact an origin story its co-protagonist has publicly disputed, which sets up a delayed trust break for exactly the reader this piece will attract. The other four are comprehension snags I hit live: a bare "Jones" acting in the timeline before Butch Jones is ever introduced (my one orientation search confirmed the draft omits that Jones is the coach who suspended him, which also leaves "the man who took everything from him" as a guess), an "At 36" pinned to 2024 quotes that the article's own dates contradict, "New Heights" used three sections before it's identified as his podcast, and unglossed type jargon in a FAQ answer built for search landers.

Verdict: revise — small, targeted fixes; the architecture is sound.

## Red flags

- **UNFAMILIAR-R1 — The No. 87 origin is stated as settled fact while publicly disputed by its co-protagonist.** _Passage:_ "His jersey number is 87, and it has nothing to do with luck or the draft. It is the year his brother was born" … "The number on his back is a receipt" (plus FAQ 3: "The number has nothing to do with luck or the draft"). _Reader effect:_ this is the section a new reader retells — and the evidence packet (CLM-04, S8b) records Jason Kelce on their own podcast in Nov 2025 calling the origin story "the biggest load of bull****," a retroactive myth. The unfamiliar reader who Googles "why does Travis Kelce wear 87" after reading — the single most natural follow-up search this article invites — hits that clip and concludes the article printed family legend as fact, which retroactively poisons trust in everything else. _Evidence:_ packet claim inventory CLM-04; dispute S8b; the NFL Films moment itself is real. _Minimum viable repair:_ attribute the story as Travis's own telling and add one clause acknowledging Jason disputes it — which, note, costs the thesis nothing, because a Seven retrofitting a redemptive meaning onto his own number is _stronger_ Type 7 evidence than the fairy-tale version. _Expected benefit:_ converts a latent trust break into corroborating material. _Confidence:_ high (dispute is documented; how a given reader reacts is probabilistic). _Acceptance test:_ a reader who reads the section and then watches Jason's Nov 2025 clip should feel the article anticipated them, not that it hid something.
- **UNFAMILIAR-R2 — Butch Jones is never introduced, so the section's pivot sentence doesn't parse on first read.** _Passage:_ timeline entry "Jones requires a 3.0 semester GPA; Kelce posts a 3.2," and later "The man who took everything from him became a father figure." _Reader effect:_ a first-time reader meets a surname with no role, then must guess who "the man who took everything from him" is; the emotional payoff of the Kansas City Star jersey anecdote lands as a puzzle. _Evidence:_ orientation search confirmed Jones was the newly arrived head coach who issued the suspension and revoked the scholarship — a fact the draft omits entirely (A to Z Sports, CBS Sports, SI; see Research log). _Minimum viable repair:_ one clause at first mention — e.g., name Jones in the timeline as the new head coach, or state in the expulsion paragraph that the incoming coach who suspended him was Butch Jones. _Expected benefit:_ the "second chance… love ya like a father" inscription and the "took everything from him" line snap into focus, and the section's forgiveness arc becomes legible without rereading. _Confidence:_ high. _Acceptance test:_ a cold reader can answer "who kicked Kelce off the team, and who is 'the man who took everything from him'?" without leaving the article.
- **UNFAMILIAR-R3 — "At 36" is contradicted by the article's own dates.** _Passage:_ "At 36, a full decade past the age most players start counting down, he still described the game through its buzz. Asked on _The Rich Eisen Show_ in 2024…" _Reader effect:_ the ending establishes he is 36 in 2026, so the 2024 quotes were at 34–35; an attentive naive reader catches the smear inside the diagnosis section, precisely where the writer is asking to be trusted on finer inferences. _Evidence:_ internal to the draft; independently noted in the evidence packet ("current-age framing rests on 2024 evidence"). _Minimum viable repair:_ re-anchor the age to the quote's date ("At 34, already past the age…") or decouple ("Deep into his thirties, he still…"). _Expected benefit:_ removes the one arithmetic stumble in the article's most argumentative section. _Confidence:_ high. _Acceptance test:_ every age stated in the piece is consistent with the birth year and the dated quote it accompanies.
- **UNFAMILIAR-R4 — First-use gaps: "New Heights" and "the family documentary."** _Passage:_ "Talking it over on New Heights in 2025…" (diagnosis section, first body use); "he said in the family documentary" (No. 87 section, one sentence before "_the 2023 family documentary_ Kelce" is introduced). _Reader effect:_ small comprehension taxes at two points, and — for New Heights — a genuinely load-bearing fact withheld: these confessions happen on _his own show, with the brother who saved him_, which is why they exist on the record at all. _Evidence:_ draft text order. _Minimum viable repair:_ at first use, "on New Heights, the podcast he hosts with his brother"; swap the documentary's two mentions so the named introduction comes first. _Expected benefit:_ zero-cost removal of two re-read moments; the New Heights gloss also quietly strengthens the Jason throughline. _Confidence:_ high. _Acceptance test:_ no proper noun in the body is used before a reader can say what it is.
- **UNFAMILIAR-R5 — Type-system jargon in a search-facing FAQ answer.** _Passage:_ FAQ 4: "…when the reframe fails the Enthusiast goes rigid and hot (the stress move toward Type 1)." _Reader effect:_ FAQ answers are the most likely first contact for a search lander (and for snippet display); "the stress move toward Type 1" is meaningless without the system and undercuts the body's otherwise excellent jargon discipline. _Evidence:_ draft FAQ text; the body version of the same idea ("This is the stress pattern") needs no jargon. _Minimum viable repair:_ replace the parenthetical with plain words ("the pattern where a frustrated Seven turns rigid and critical") or drop it; the rabbit hole already covers the arrow for insiders. _Expected benefit:_ the FAQ block reads as cleanly as the body for the reader most likely to arrive through it. _Confidence:_ medium-high (depends on FAQ rendering placement; see Q2). _Acceptance test:_ every FAQ answer is comprehensible to a reader who has never heard of the Enneagram.
