## Bottom-line verdict

The skeptical case is that the profile romanticizes founder opacity as Type 5 depth and treats product strategy as autobiography. It remains publishable because it names the strongest Type 3 case, preserves the advertising anomaly, and concedes concrete harm in the investor dispute.

## What landed

The dialogue autopsy shows a repeatable response style across several questions. The lawsuit section uses an adversarial source instead of letting the founder's procedural answer control the record.

## What missed

Stress-to-7 and growth-to-8 can make almost any strategic reversal fit the chosen type. The Type 3 rebuttal says the case “dies” when it more accurately remains a serious alternative.

## What I expected

A named rival type, contrary evidence, a source-limit note, and behavior that would weaken the thesis. The article supplies all four.

## What surprised me

The article's best argument is not Yang's reclusiveness but the consistency with which he converts subjective prompts into quantities and systems.

## Red flags

No release blocker. Reported valuation and IPO timing are clearly framed as reports and plans, and the unresolved arbitration remains unresolved in the text.

## Specific improvements

During a later substantive revision, replace “the Achiever case dies” with a more calibrated conclusion and identify a behavior that would genuinely falsify the Type 5 reading.
