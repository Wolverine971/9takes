## Identity and scope

Yang Zhilin is a Chinese AI researcher and entrepreneur associated with Transformer-XL, XLNet, Recurrent AI, and Moonshot AI. The frozen article interprets his public record as Enneagram Type 5, likely 5w4 with a social-first instinct. That is an editorial interpretation of attributed public behavior and testimony, not a diagnosis or a claim to know his private motives.

## Dated timeline

- **1992:** Born in Shantou, Guangdong.
- **2010s:** Studies at Tsinghua and Carnegie Mellon; contributes to Transformer-XL and XLNet; co-founds Recurrent AI.
- **March 2023:** Co-founds Moonshot AI, whose Chinese name references Pink Floyd's _The Dark Side of the Moon_.
- **2024:** Gives the translated long-form interviews that supply most of the article's first-person evidence; Moonshot spends heavily on consumer acquisition; Recurrent investors begin arbitration proceedings.
- **2025:** Kimi loses users after DeepSeek's rise; Moonshot pivots toward open-weight models with Kimi K2.
- **January–June 2026:** K2.5 and subsequent coding models expand Moonshot's developer position; Cursor identifies Kimi K2.5 as the base selected for Composer 2.
- **July 16, 2026:** Moonshot launches Kimi K3 through its products.
- **July 20, 2026:** Moonshot pauses new subscriptions after demand strains capacity.
- **July 27, 2026:** Moonshot releases the full Kimi K3 weights and technical report.
- **August 2026:** Reporting places the latest financing discussion as high as a $50 billion valuation and describes preparations for a possible Hong Kong listing.

## First-person evidence

- Yang calls scaling law the only approach in AI that actually works.
- He defines lossless compression as learning certainty in a chaotic environment.
- He describes desire as something that can be tested against fear of missing out.
- He converts questions about anxiety, loneliness, and failure into probabilities, headcounts, and systems.
- He says open weights can place frontier intelligence in many people's hands at very low marginal cost.
- He says he is not anxious about finding a soft landing for the company.

## Third-party testimony

- Adviser Ruslan Salakhutdinov describes Yang as an exceptional student and praises the open-weight release.
- Investor Zhu Xiaohu criticizes Yang's arbitration response, then acknowledges K3's capabilities.
- Jian Ma describes Yang as sharp, open-minded, and curious.
- Anastasios Angelopoulos calls K3 a major release.
- Reporting characterizes Yang as reclusive while documenting the scale of Moonshot's funding, demand, and planned listing.

## Draft claim inventory

1. Yang repeatedly turns emotion and conflict into information problems.
2. His privacy is paired with unusually public distribution of technical work.
3. The Type 5 argument rests on depth, withdrawal, compression, and protection of the work rather than simple introversion.
4. The 2024 advertising blitz is the strongest anomaly and supports a serious Type 3 counter-reading.
5. The arbitration sequence is ethically mixed: investor grievances are conceded, while Yang's response style remains personality evidence.
6. The Kimi recovery gives the lossless-compression thesis a behavior-level payoff.
7. K3's product launch and full-weight release are separate dated events.

## Disputes and unresolved questions

- Public evidence cannot establish a private core type, wing, instinct, or connecting-line movement.
- Most substantive Yang quotations are English translations of Mandarin interviews.
- The public co-founder roster varies by source and the article avoids making that dispute central.
- No attributable public resolution of the Recurrent AI arbitration was found during this release check.
- Financing valuations and IPO timing remain reported plans rather than completed transactions.
- Company scale, publicity strategy, and model-release choices may reflect institutional constraints rather than Yang's personality alone.
