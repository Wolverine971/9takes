The star of 2026's biggest horror breakout could not scream. Inde Navarrette told Collider's Perri Nemiroff that when she first met writer-director Curry Barker and the _Obsession_ team, "I was in a really shy place in my life." So she told Barker exactly that: "I said, 'I don't know how to scream, and I'm feeling very shy.'" ([Collider Ladies Night, May 16, 2026](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=zEB4C4SIAbY))

By her account, Barker answered with a schedule. He booked days where it was just the two of them, and only once she was comfortable would she scream in front of anyone else. He screamed alongside her: "he would scream with me. And then he would stop and then I would stop. And he was like, 'No, what are you doing? Come on, go go go go on.'" He also got the cast playing charades, which she said "created a really safe space." Four months later, on _Hot Ones Versus_ with co-star Michael Johnston, she compressed the story into one line: "The beginning of 'Obsession' was charades, because I was too scared to do shit." ([First We Feast, September 8, 2026](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=mlwD5SdMM7I))

The film she was too scared to start was made for a reported $750,000 ([NBC News, May 25, 2026](https://www.nbcnews.com/pop-culture/pop-culture-news/obsession-curry-barker-box-office-hit-focus-features-rcna346824)) and had grossed more than $500 million worldwide by September 9, 2026 ([The Numbers](https://www.the-numbers.com/movie/Obsession-%282026%29)). The scream sessions show the order her choices tend to follow. She names the fear. She finds a person or a structure that makes it safe. Then she commits all the way. That order is the core of the argument for Enneagram Type 6, the type organized around security and trust. It is also where the argument meets resistance, because the same woman went skydiving and got her nipples pierced on her 18th birthday, both on the same day.

## What is Inde Navarrette's personality type?

Inde Navarrette fits Enneagram Type 6 best, probably the counterphobic version that walks toward its fears. Confidence is medium. The key clue is how she handles bad outcomes that have not happened yet: she secures against them early and borrows certainty from people she trusts. Type 7 is the serious alternative. She has not named her type.

Type 6 gets flattened into "anxious" or "loyal." The more useful question is what a Six does with doubt. [Counterphobic Sixes](/enneagram-corner/enneagram-type-6#two-flavors-of-fear-phobic-vs-counterphobic) go looking for the threat and test themselves against it. That fits a woman who says she has been "so sensitive" her whole life, "especially towards horror movies," and then took a horror lead ([Variety's Up Next, July 31, 2026](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=pnvlSfoB0Fw)). It also fits smaller moves that recur in her interviews from 2021 to 2026: pacts with herself before auditions, deference to a trusted director, and a habit of guarding against disappointment before it arrives.

The limits are real. Most of this evidence is her own account, given largely during a four-month press run for a film about a woman who loses control of herself. Promotion rewards a humble, fearless story. The reading below treats her statements as evidence of what she says and does, which is not proof of what she feels.

## "Mommy, I do"

Navarrette was born Danielle Fabiola Navarrette in 2001; profiles place her birth in Tucson, Arizona ([HOLA! USA, June 11, 2026](https://www.hola.com/us/celebrities/20260611906877/inde-navarrette-real-name-things-you-didnt-know/); [South China Morning Post, May 19, 2026](https://www.scmp.com/magazines/style/people/celebrities/article/3354079/meet-inde-navarrette-who-plays-nikki-curry-barkers-hit-horror-film-obsession)). "My dad's a Marine, so I grew up moving around a lot," she told Variety. Her parents divorced, and she says her mother raised the children mostly in California's Central Valley, around Fresno, Visalia and Bakersfield. She is the second-oldest in a large blended family, though she admits she loses count: "It's like six or seven." The family has, as she put it on _Jimmy Kimmel Live_, an Australian side and a Mexican side ([June 6, 2026](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=IFUny5cZAdk)).

A Six story would be easier to tell if the moving had hurt. She says it didn't. Other military kids she knew hated leaving their friends. "Me, I was like new story, new place." That is a genuine point for the Type 7 reading, and it belongs on the scale.

The nickname is her own origin story. Asked by the Substack _mixed feelings_ for an anecdote about herself as a kid, she said: "I would always say, 'Mommy, I do.' That's how I got my nickname, Inde, because I was very independent." Her mother, a hairstylist, offered to do her hair, blow her bubbles, help her dress. "Nope. Mommy, I do." Her mother's reply, in Navarrette's telling: "Okay. You're my first daughter. I would like to dress you." ([mixed feelings, June 18, 2026](https://www.mixedfeelings.earth/p/inde-navarrette-interview-obsession))

Toddlers of every type insist on doing it themselves, so the anecdote proves little on its own. What matters more is how the independence got built later, and it was built with a partner. In 2021, years before fame, she told CBR: "I created this mindset with my mom when I was 13 about going into auditions." The rule was that "if we don't get something, that's not what we're supposed to do." The two would make a whole day of each audition, so a bad one faded by the time they got home. Once she could drive herself, that buffer was gone, and she replaced it with a rule of her own: "I needed to kind of make a pact with myself of, 'You know what? I'm going to give it 110%.' That way, whenever I leave, there's no room for doubt, because doubt can really just weed its way in." ([CBR, March 15, 2021](https://www.cbr.com/superman-lois-inde-navarrette-sarah-cushing-interview/)) Doubt is the named enemy, and the defense is a structure set up in advance. Five years later she was still quoting her mother's version: "my mom really instilled in me to um not take no until no's a for sure" (Collider).

She has wanted to act since she was very small, after watching Russell Crowe in _Gladiator_. Yet she would not act in front of her own parents. She and a cousin did dance battles for the family, she told Variety, but "I would never act in front of them. That to me felt like I would rather jump off of a bridge."

The goal came at a price. "I left high school early to work at a coffee shop because headshots were really expensive and my mom couldn't afford them," she said. Sales work at an Orangetheory gym followed. Her father, she says, "really really loves to push education."

## _Superman & Lois_ as college

After a supporting role in the final season of _13 Reasons Why_ in 2020, Navarrette joined The CW's _Superman & Lois_ in 2021 as Sarah Cushing ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Inde_Navarrette)). With her father's emphasis on schooling in mind, she said, "I treated it like college." When she was hit by a car during production and couldn't walk between takes, she sat beside the directors of photography, who "always scared me," and started asking about lenses. "So even though I didn't have the opportunity to go to college I felt like I did." (Variety)

The 2021 CBR interview holds the earliest detailed account of how she handles pressure. At her final screen test, surrounded by the other finalists, she felt "so uncomfortable" with the competition, so she brought a zodiac book and wrote out the signs "from Aries all the way to Pisces." She kept to herself on purpose: "I wasn't talking to these girls, because I knew once I did, I would just want to be their friends." She called the approach "survival mode": "going in, getting it done, and then leaving and letting it go."

The show also taught her how little in the business is certain. In April 2023, with renewal in doubt, she told Just Jared Jr. that even a public statement from DC's James Gunn did not settle it for her: "it's interesting in our industry, it's not a definitive, so we still have to wait for that answer." ([Just Jared Jr., April 7, 2023](https://www.justjaredjr.com/2023/04/07/inde-navarrette-talks-end-of-superman-lois-season-3-and-possibility-of-season-4/)) Two months later, budget cuts ended her run as a series regular, along with several castmates ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Inde_Navarrette)).

Three years on, after _Obsession_ had succeeded, she described the loss in the brightest terms available. "Honestly, I kind of felt like it was a blessing, not necessarily in disguise, but kind of just right in front of my face," she told Collider. "As soon as they were kind of like, 'Dog, you're free.' I was like, 'Dude.'" She added that "that was hard to hear cuz I miss everybody." That quick turn toward freedom is the kind of reframe Type 7 is known for, and it counts for the alternative. It is also hindsight; her reaction at the time could not be checked for this profile.

She has her own image for those years. "Artistically, I felt like a boiling pot that kept being taken off the stove right before boiling over," she told Numero Netherlands in May 2026 ([Numero Netherlands](https://www.numeronetherlands.com/in-conversation-with/in-conversation-with-inde-navarrette)).

## The worst audition she had ever done

_Obsession_ began with a false start. Navarrette sent in a tape, got a Zoom callback, and "it was awful," she told Complex News in June 2026, as quoted by NME. "I think I was in a space personally where I was so shut off from my own emotions, and with Nikki, you need all of them." ([NME, June 25, 2026](https://www.nme.com/news/film/inde-navarrette-couldnt-get-a-job-for-one-and-a-half-years-after-obsession-wrapped-3953098)) On GoldDerby's podcast she called it "the worst audition I had ever done." ([GoldDerby, June 12, 2026](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=T1hlZg2YyjU))

She got a third shot, a chemistry read. "If I have this opportunity, I'm doing everything that I possibly can," she told Complex ([Complex, June 24, 2026](https://www.complex.com/pop-culture/a/alex-ocho/inde-navarrette-obsession-acting-drought-streaming)). In the room she locked eyes with Cooper Tomlinson, who would play Ian, and thought, "how can I make him utterly uncomfortable right now?" (Collider)

Barker, speaking at the Toronto International Film Festival before anyone knew the film would be a hit, said actors could usually manage either the natural material or the frightening material, "and that was a journey to find someone that could nail both those worlds, and Inde just nailed it." ([Moveable Fest, September 6, 2025](https://moveablefest.com/curry-barker-obsession/))

The shape returns later: shut down and frightened, then total commitment once the chance is clearly finite. Comeback-audition stories are also a press-tour staple, so this one counts as a single data point.

## Borrowed certainty

On set, Navarrette guarded her preparation. "I just needed to keep her private. I've really kept Nikki close to my chest," she told GoldDerby. Johnston remembered it on _Hot Ones_: "When you did the smile, 'I'm your freaky Nikki,' remember I was like, 'Show me what you're going to do.' And you were like, 'No.'"

With Barker, she did the reverse. "I'm quite sensitive and so I didn't want to take anything home. So, I kind of used Curry as a mirror where I put full trust in him," she told GoldDerby. She would show him faces, he would adjust them, and "whenever I heard, you know, that's perfect. No, keep it going. Keep it, keep it. That I loved." When an interviewer's question turned on Barker's memory of how a sequence came together, she deferred to it: "if that's what Curry said, then that's definitely what happened."

The physical work followed the same route. "Fun fact, I have a torn ACL," she told GoldDerby. When a scene called for Nikki to move backward, she tried and tried, coaching herself by her birth name: "Danielle, hold it together. You can do this." Then she took the problem to Barker, who asked, "Wait, what if you go forwards and we reverse it?" That is what ended up in the film.

Deference did not make her passive. The script ended with Nikki's death. Barker told an Alamo Drafthouse audience that the studio, Capstone, wanted "a version where you live," and he planned a single throwaway take. "Her performance was so crazy with the with the take where she lives, and so disturbing, um that I had people basically talk me into it." ([Alamo Drafthouse Q&A, June 8, 2026](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=1CvhgXByrtM)) Navarrette remembers the change as a same-day creative call: "last minute we decided to change be like no she's a final girl she wouldn't do that." (Collider) The accounts differ on who pushed. Both agree her take changed the ending. Barker has also said she brought "this like bro-y type of... sassiness to Nikki that I didn't anticipate" ([CinemaBlend, May 29, 2026](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Op0b77nTmTg)).

Taken together, the set stories show where she keeps her certainty. She protects her own preparation from exposure, then hands final judgment to one trusted person and says she loved doing it. For a Six, trust works like infrastructure: once it is in place, the risky work can begin. A Type 4 reading struggles here, since Fours tend to guard their own read of a character against outside correction, and she describes giving hers away gladly.

## The streams she deleted

After wrapping _Obsession_, Navarrette went about a year and a half without another acting job. "Between Obsession and the film after that, I didn't work. I was walking dogs. I was streaming. I was doing a lot of these things to make money," she told Nylon. "I didn't have a lot of confidence because you just keep hearing no, and that can be debilitating." ([Nylon, July 1, 2026](https://www.nylon.com/entertainment/inde-navarrette-encounter-obsession-interview))

The streaming ran on a gaming PC she built herself, Complex reported. "I love playing video games by myself. I'm not getting any work. I know that other people do this," she told Variety. She liked the small audience, which she called "nice and like intimate."

Then she deleted the archive. Asked by Variety whether the streams were still on Twitch, she said, "I deleted all of them just cuz I felt like I was I got scared." She explained the fear: "what if people saw that I switched and now they don't take me seriously." And she was candid that it was only a fear: "it's not based on fact, but it I just panicked and I deleted a lot of them."

Fans had clipped and reposted the streams anyway. "Now seeing that people are so lovingly holding on to it my fear was for nothing," she said, adding that she had loved streaming and that "it also just helped me out with like financials." Nylon noted that old videos from her streamer channel have resurfaced, as they did for [Timothée Chalamet](/personality-analysis/timothee-chalamet) before her.

This is the clearest unforced case in the record, and it separates the leading readings. The bad outcome was hypothetical. She acted before it arrived, at real cost: the archive was something she loved and something that had helped pay bills. A Seven's usual reflex runs the other way, toward keeping the fun and moving on. A Three protecting an image might delete the clips too, but would be less likely to describe the move on a Variety podcast as panic and call the fear baseless. What she describes is the Six sequence: secure against the worst case first, then find out whether it was real.

She does something similar with awards talk. Asked about Oscar buzz, she told Variety that "there's a part of my brain that is um just kind of having a safety net of if it doesn't happen, it doesn't mean that I didn't do a good job." She did not pretend indifference. On GoldDerby she said she "would be lying to you if I said that that's not a dream and that isn't extremely validating." The wanting is real, and the net goes up before any nomination exists.

Asked how she decompresses after emotional scenes, she used the vocabulary directly: "whenever it comes to what I do have control over in order to keep myself secure um it'll be my drives. It'll be my music. It'll be my video games." She was promoting a film about losing control, and control language ran through every interview. The word she chose for what those rituals give her, though, was "secure."

## The case for Type 7, and where it breaks

The strongest rival reading deserves its full weight. On _Jimmy Kimmel Live_, with her mother and brothers in the audience, Navarrette announced, "On my 18th birthday, I went skydiving." Then: "I got my nipples pierced all in one day." She said she loves "getting random tattoos." Asked whether she started streaming with a career in mind, she told Variety, "I don't think that far ahead." She has described a good audition as a high that "feels like drugs," adding, "It's dopamine." Add the freedom reframe, the happy childhood moves and a spread of enthusiasms from gaming to a wish to write and direct. On the [Type 7](/enneagram-corner/enneagram-type-7) reading, the fear talk is junket modesty and the Twitch purge was a one-off impulse.

The Seven case describes her energy well. It fits her stated motives less well. Her own description of the high puts fear first. Asked by GoldDerby what she wants to carry into her next role, she said: "Kind of chasing that high to challenge myself. I think obviously whenever you're really scared of something, but you really want to do it justice and do it correctly and then you get that high of uh accomplishing it and feeling proud of it." The reward she describes is fear faced and a job done correctly. A Seven's high more typically comes from options, novelty and escape from limits. The skydive can be read either way. The Twitch deletion fits the Seven reading poorly.

What the Six reading does not explain matters just as much. It struggles with the piercing on the same day as the skydive, with the "random" tattoos, and with "I don't think that far ahead," which cuts against the anticipation Sixes usually show. It does not explain her apparent pleasure in constant moves as a child. Those stay in the unexplained column.

Two other readings have partial claims. Her most emotional statements lean toward Type 4. Talking about films that show a person's "ugliness," she told Collider, "I felt seen, I felt heard," and that with the _Obsession_ script she thought, "I know exactly how to play this." Showing that side is hard, she added, because "you're constantly told, 'No, don't show that or don't be that or it's uncomfortable.'" Against a Four core: she hands her read of a character to the director, and she talks about belonging far more than about being different. The horror community, she told Variety, "has been the most welcoming out of anyone that I've ever been a part of and I don't ever want to leave that."

Type 9 has a narrower case. The boiling pot image describes expression that kept getting interrupted, and she told Variety that playing Nikki taught her something: "Nikki stood up for herself in so many different ways... I felt like that was something I needed to learn." That fits a Nine finding her voice. It also fits a shy Six gaining nerve, and the public record cannot separate the two. The same line counts against a Type 8 reading of her tough exterior, the actor who told a _Superman & Lois_ castmate "you've got to slap me harder" during a slap scene ([KryptonSite, June 6, 2023](https://www.kryptonsite.com/superman-lois-full-interview-with-inde-navarrette-sarah/)). Eights seldom describe assertiveness as something they had to learn.

## What the pattern costs

The Six pattern has a price, and she names it herself. As _Obsession_ broke out, strangers began stopping her on the street and her Instagram following passed 2.2 million, Nylon reported. Her response: "I don't care how much I'm not sleeping, because I don't know if I'll get this opportunity again." That is scarcity thinking, the same anticipation of loss behind the deleted streams and the Oscar safety net, now driving overwork. It is also a common first-breakout mindset, so it adds weight without settling anything.

Privacy is the most durable trait in the record. In 2021 she told CBR, "I feel as though my stuff is my own and I'm very private about that." In 2026 she told Nylon, "I'm a very private person." Asked about strangers filming her in public, she described a deliberate narrowing of attention: "I kind of put it as like it's none of my business." (Variety) She has described acting itself, borrowing an idea she credits to the actor Milly Alcock, as "a place to hide and to be another person." Privacy is common to several types and simply sensible for someone who became famous at 25, so it supports the portrait more than the type.

Even her career strategy is phrased in terms of trust. She wants to be "very discerning" with her next projects, she told Variety, because "I don't want to lose the audience's trust in me."

## Falling on her face, on purpose

The most interesting line from her 2026 press run cuts against the pattern. Over corned beef on rye with Nylon, she said she wants to prove that _Obsession_ "wasn't just lightning in a bottle." Then she added: "I also want to fall on my face publicly and have something that people don't like [but] maybe I do." She laughed. "So, I'm looking forward to that."

When she moved into her new chapter, the fear that people would not take her seriously was enough to make her erase work she loved. Now she was saying out loud that she wants to fail in public at something she believes in. That could be growth, press-tour bravado, or the Seven in her getting a vote. The Six reading says what to watch for: whether she can keep taking risks without first building the safe room. On the _Obsession_ set, that room took a schedule, a charades game and a director willing to scream first. Her next roles will show how much of it she now carries on her own.
