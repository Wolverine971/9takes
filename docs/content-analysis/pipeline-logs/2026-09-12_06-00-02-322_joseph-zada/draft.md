The call came at 7 a.m. in Sydney, and Joseph Zada knew what it meant before anyone said it. His entire team was on the line. He had won the role of young Haymitch Abernathy in _The Hunger Games: Sunrise on the Reaping_. He told Elle Fanning what came out of his mouth: "I just started swearing, like, 'Now I have to do this and it's going to be really hard.'" Fanning laughed and asked, "You were more scared after finding out?" His answer was one word: "Yes." ([Interview Magazine, August 10, 2026](https://www.interviewmagazine.com/film/joseph-zada-elle-fanning-hunger-games))

He had made the process harder on purpose. While he was still filming Netflix's _East of Eden_ in New Zealand, the Haymitch team asked him for one improvised scene. He told them, "I'll have it to you in three weeks," because "I had a plan to shoot the scenes properly and edit them." Then he left the country to make it: "I flew back to Australia from New Zealand just so I could work with my older brother. He helped me, and so did my dad and a couple of friends."

Set those two moments side by side and a pattern appears that runs through most of what Zada, an Australian actor born in 2005, has said in public. Rising stakes send him to gather his people. Good news registers first as an obligation he could fail. The judges he fears most are the people he admires, and his remedy is getting inside their circle. That sequence is the case for Enneagram Type 6, the type organized around security, trust and the question of who can be relied on. One friend saw something simpler. Emily Alyn Lind, his _We Were Liars_ co-star, spoke to him on FaceTime during the callbacks and summed him up this way: "He just gets what he wants." ([The Hollywood Reporter, relaying People, June 26, 2025](https://www.hollywoodreporter.com/movies/movie-news/joseph-zada-against-big-names-hunger-games-prequel-1236300634/))

## What is Joseph Zada's personality type?

Joseph Zada looks most like Enneagram Type 6, at medium confidence. The strongest clue is what success does to him: it raises his alarm, and he answers by preparing with trusted people and seeking acceptance from admired collaborators. Type 9 is the serious alternative. He has not publicly named a type.

[Type 6](/enneagram-corner/enneagram-type-6) is often flattened into "anxious." A more useful description is a person who scans for what could go wrong and then looks for solid ground, usually in people and structures that have earned trust. In Zada's case the worry has a specific target: how people he respects will judge him, and whether he belongs among them. The [Type 9](/enneagram-corner/enneagram-type-9) reading also has real support, since he describes himself as easygoing and keeps sets light. It gets a full hearing further down.

A limit shapes everything below. Almost every substantial source on Zada is an interview given between February 2025 and August 2026, inside the promotional windows of four projects. Nervousness, gratitude and "the cast became a family" are the house style of those formats, and a newcomer sharing a cast list with Glenn Close and Ralph Fiennes would produce them whatever his type. So this reading leans on what he did, especially when it cost him something, and on statements that repeat across years and interviewers. This research found nothing substantial from before he was 19, and as of September 2026 neither of his two biggest performances has been released.

## A family trade, learned in pairs

Acting was the household business. His father, Jeremy Cumpston, is a director, actor and doctor, and his mother, Jessica Brentnall, is a film producer ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Joseph_Zada)). "My whole family and every family friend was somewhat connected," he told Fanning. "We grew up watching movies every single day. We did acting courses on our school holidays." That upbringing is the plainest explanation for his access and his ease on a set. In this house, acting was the path of least resistance.

He says it did not grab him until his mid-teens. Before that he wanted to be a footballer and, as Maddy Brown reported in a [Flaunt profile](https://www.flaunt.com/post/joseph-zada) published around the June 2025 release of _We Were Liars_, routinely got up at two in the morning to watch Arsenal. "It only properly appealed to me at about 15, when I started helping my older brother with auditions when he'd come back from America," he told Fanning. "Then I wanted to do it myself." He had been on camera before then: his first notable role was in _Bilched_ (2019), directed by his father and starring his brother Hal Cumpston, whom Wikipedia lists as a half-brother. The stories fit if that early part was family participation more than pursuit.

What he found at 15 was a method built with a partner. "We'd work up to eight hours on auditions," he said of the sessions with his brother. "We'd drive each other to the proper limits of a scene and try to do it 100 different ways just for fun." Those are conversational numbers, told warmly. The shape is what matters: from the start, his preparation happened with someone beside him.

The name followed the same shape. "My original name is Cumpston, which my older brother still goes by," he told Fanning. Zada is his grandmother's surname. "When I was starting out, about to go for my big break, I was on my last callback and I'd been thinking about changing my name." He traces it to a great-grandfather he describes as an Afghan cameleer and says "our family built the first mosque in Australia," a claim this analysis could not independently verify. Wikipedia places his Afghan-Aboriginal heritage through a paternal great-grandmother, so the lineage here is his account. What he took from the name sounds like company: "now I feel like I've got my great-grandfather and my grandmother guiding me as my lucky stars." A new credit name is ordinary when a sibling already works under the family one, and both reasons can be true. Still, at the most uncertain point of his career, he reached for ancestors.

## Two accounts of leaving school

The sharpest solo act in his record is also the least settled. In February 2025, on the Perth set of the Stan series _Invisible Boys_, he told Variety Australia's Julie Hosking: "I had a lot of stuff going on when I was younger and some schools just don't know how to handle that, and I ended up being kicked out." He added: "I was going to an all-boys school and there was a bit of a stigma around acting. I just thought it was a very vulnerable place to be." ([Variety Australia, February 13, 2025](https://au.variety.com/2025/tv/features/invisible-boys-shines-light-on-queer-stories-20235))

Wikipedia relays a different version from a December 2025 GQ Australia interview: "My attendance was too low. They said, 'If you do that audition, you're not coming back.' So I dropped out to do an audition." The GQ interview itself could not be opened for this analysis. Both accounts can be partly true, since an attendance problem can end in an ultimatum and an exit. They still place the decision on opposite sides, and the second is the more flattering telling. This profile does not choose between them or build a psychological claim on either.

The Variety account does show the setting he remembers: a school where acting carried stigma and exposure felt risky. That was a real and common constraint, so it should not be read as a formative wound. It does share a structure with what comes later, where the hard part is how a group will see him.

The work that followed barely paused. "As I turned eighteen, I got the lead role in _Invisible Boys_, and then I jumped straight to _We Were Liars_, and then straight to _East of Eden_, and then straight to _Hunger Games_," he told Mckenna Grace ([HERO, March 23, 2026](https://hero-magazine.com/article/294797/inside-heroine-24-mckenna-grace-in-conversation-with-joseph-zada)). By June 2025 he said, "I haven't stopped working in a year and a half" ([Polyester, June 18, 2025](https://www.polyesterzine.com/features/joseph-zada-on-the-hunger-games-playing-tennis-and-becoming-johnny-sinclairnbsp)). Being cast repeatedly explains that schedule as much as appetite does.

One scene from the run shows him under a deadline. He had two days to turn around the _We Were Liars_ tape in the middle of shooting _Invisible Boys_. "I was on set at my lunch breaks reading over my lines and secretly getting other people in the cast to read it with me, which is probably not very appropriate, but man's got to work," he told Polyester. Grabbing readers for an urgent tape is common. The telling part is that his instinct was to recruit, and that he knew he was bending the rules.

## The three-week audition

The Haymitch search began for him in January 2025, when he met casting director Deb Zane, he told Fanning. The audition arrived while he was on _East of Eden_, which began filming in New Zealand in October 2024 and wrapped in March 2025 ([Wikipedia](<https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/East_of_Eden_(2026_miniseries)>)). Asked for one scene, he built a three-week production with family and friends. "It's a crazy audition and I really went all out."

No part of the brief required an international flight. His phrasing, "just so I could work with my older brother," states a purpose, though it cannot rule out that the trip also fit a break in the schedule. Even so, the move repeats the eight-hour sessions from his teens. When the stakes rose, he gathered his people and turned a solo test into a joint project.

The decision-makers noticed. In the casting announcement, producer Nina Jacobson said: "Jo prepared like crazy and stole our hearts." ([RUSSH, November 21, 2025](https://www.russh.com/who-is-joseph-zada/)) Casting statements are marketing meant to justify an unknown, so this confirms how hard he prepared without explaining why.

After _East of Eden_ wrapped, the process moved to Los Angeles for chemistry reads and callbacks. That is when Lind reached him on FaceTime at his hotel. In her telling he said, "You can't tell anyone but it's down to me and a couple [other] people for this _Hunger Games_ thing," and "he's naming names that are really big that he's up against."

Then came the call. He was back in Sydney, and by his account only his younger sister was home: "I walked upstairs and we jumped up and down and cried together and I gave her a big hug." The swearing, and the admission that he was more frightened than before, belong to the same morning. The announcement followed a day later, and "It was pretty full-on."

Read quickly, the swearing is a joke, and it was told as one, more than a year later, to a friendly co-star after the film was safely shot. Self-deprecation about a big break is a familiar interview move. Its value here is its fit with the rest of the record, where good news keeps arriving as a new way to be found wanting.

## Afraid of the people he admires

In July 2025, in Berlin preparing for _Sunrise on the Reaping_, he spoke with his friend and _We Were Liars_ co-star Esther McGregor for Behind The Blinds. Asked about _East of Eden_, whose lead role James Dean played in the 1955 film, he passed over the obvious answer: "funny enough, it wasn't so much the legacy of the story that scared me; it was the cast and the filmmakers. These are people I really admire. I was just afraid of how they would perceive me." Then he added: "But I got over that quickly." ([Behind The Blinds, July 9, 2025](https://www.behindtheblinds.be/love/sharing-the-ride))

The fear has a precise address: admired authorities, looking at him. That precision separates it from generic newcomer humility, which it also resembles. It was said to a close friend, in a warm register, and he says it passed.

His handling of those authorities follows an order. In his first _East of Eden_ callback, he told Fanning, he was asked to promise not to watch the 1955 film. "I said, 'Yeah, sure. I guess I won't watch it.'" He waited about eight months. "I didn't necessarily feel pressure until I finished the job and watched the film. Then I was like, 'Oh, shit.'" For Haymitch, he worked on the voice until "I sounded exactly like him," meaning Woody Harrelson, "and from there I stripped it completely back." He did not meet Harrelson until after shooting: "For my nerves and for me to really attack it, I had to not think about that at all. There was already too much of a task at hand." When they did meet, "I had his blessing." Avoiding a predecessor's performance is standard craft advice. What repeats is the sequence: keep the looming authority out of the room while working, then receive its approval afterward.

Before that shoot he gave Polyester a starker image. "It feels like I am a tribute and the capital is all the viewers watching to see whether I'm ever going to act again." It is a neat line built from the franchise he was promoting. It still casts the audience as a judge with power over his survival as an actor.

The counterevidence belongs here too. After _East of Eden_, he told Flaunt his confidence had grown: "I just learned that these people are actually normal people and they're doing the same thing that I'm doing. I have it in me to go toe to toe with Florence [Pugh], I think. I feel like I can go toe to toe with anyone now." That is a young actor finding his footing, and a reader could fairly stop there. The mechanism he names is still worth noticing. His confidence arrived once the people he feared had been reclassified as peers.

## Getting inside the circle

When Zada explains how nerves resolve, other people are usually part of the answer. Of the start of a shoot, he told McGregor: "I worked myself into a state of so much anxiety and put too much pressure on myself. But by the time I got into the flow, I found my footing." He credited "being more confident in myself, and in the people around me." In Berlin, before _Sunrise on the Reaping_ began, he said he was "phenomenally nervous," then offered his usual fix: "like every other project I've done, I think once I step on set and meet everyone, I'll be fine."

Asked about the gruelling Haymitch auditions in an interview with Swooon, as relayed by CinemaBlend, he said: "they put me through the ringer because it was very important. And it was important for me to go through that process as well not just to learn about the character, but to meet all the people I was working with and feel comfortable." ([CinemaBlend, June 12, 2025](https://www.cinemablend.com/movies/sunrise-on-the-reapings-joseph-zada-put-through-ringer-auditioning-haymitch-why-important)) The Swooon original could not be opened, so that wording rests on the relay. Asked about an ordeal, he described its value in terms of introductions.

He also describes company as protection from his own thoughts. Of nights in Halifax during _We Were Liars_, he told McGregor, "I feel both of us get lonely; we need someone around." Remembering the _Sunrise on the Reaping_ shoot with Grace, he was grateful for "being forced to spend family time so I wasn't chilling in my own head and going crazy by myself." Any 20-year-old living abroad for months gets lonely. What carries weight is the direction, repeated in two interviews fourteen months apart: company keeps him out of his own head, and he welcomed having it imposed.

Family shows up at the edge of each big job. From Berlin he said: "I'm here with my older brother, and I just brought over my dad and my little brother. We're going to Portugal tomorrow for the weekend, which will be really fun, just a little boys' trip right before I jump into the deep end with this." Any one of these trips is ordinary. Across three thresholds, the teenage sessions with Hal, the flight home for the Haymitch tape and Berlin, the same people appear right before the plunge.

The reward he names from the biggest job of his life is acceptance. "This is a perfectly curated team. It's also a family. I got to walk in and be completely accepted, and not just while we were at work. I was always invited to come out with the crew and go to dinners and be a part of it," he told Fanning. "It's a family" is junket boilerplate. The specifics after it are what count.

The same thread runs through smaller answers. Of Glenn Close, he told People: "It means so much to have someone of such stature support you, and Glenn in particular made it clear she was always there for me." ([People via Yahoo, July 7, 2026](https://www.yahoo.com/entertainment/movies/articles/joseph-zada-teases-young-haymitch-110000632.html)) Of _Invisible Boys_ director Nicholas Verso: "I don't think I'd be half the actor today if I hadn't been given that opportunity by Nic." ([Concrete Playground, February 21, 2025](https://concreteplayground.com/brisbane/arts-entertainment/joseph-zada-aydan-calafiore-zach-blampied-pia-miranda-interview-invisible-boys)) Asked in July 2025 about a _Sunrise_ cast he had barely met, he told Brit + Co, "There should be [a group chat], maybe I need to start that. I will." ([Brit + Co, July 23, 2025](https://www.brit.co/joseph-zada-sunrise-on-the-reaping-we-were-liars-interview/)) Each is an expected press courtesy on its own. Together they route his growth through people who backed him.

## The case for Type 9

The Type 9 reading deserves full weight, because much of Zada's own language supports it. He told Flaunt he is "really happy, actually," and said of industry pressure, "You've just got to roll with it." The writer credited him with "an uncommonly zen mentality." On public noise: "I try to block it all out and stay in my own lane." When Fanning asked, "Who are you when you're not a character, Joe?" he answered: "I'm chilling."

He also manages hard material through group mood. "It was never very serious, which kept us loose," he told McGregor of _We Were Liars_. "It made those tough days, when you had to be upset or stressed out, easier, because we filled the rest of the days with lightness." On _Invisible Boys_, he credited friendship for the chemistry: "So if you're really good friends, it shines through." And he agreed without argument not to watch the 1955 _East of Eden_.

The strongest Nine clue is emotional distance. Visiting the _Invisible Boys_ set, Hosking found him "remarkably upbeat for someone who has spent much of the past week in tears." He told her, "I don't tend to wear my emotions on my sleeve like Charlie does," and said the role had "taught me to be vulnerable. I have more access to my emotions, and I think that's been a very big gift." Read as a Nine, that describes a habitual distance from his own feelings that a role had to crack open. On that reading the family trips are nesting, the company is comfort, and the nerves are press idiom laid over an unruffled person.

Three observations resist it. The first is the win itself. A Nine's steadiness would be expected to absorb good news, yet Zada's most consequential good news made him more afraid. The second is how precisely he locates the fear, in the judgment of specific admired collaborators, and how consistently he treats contact with them as the cure. A vaguer unease about disruption would fit a Nine better.

The third is that he does not drift. The Nine reading would gain from evidence of him losing the thread of his own agenda when nobody was pushing, and the record points the other way. Grace said of the shoot, "you kept the most intricate notes. Then you and I sat and watched all of _The Hunger Games_ movies and took notes," a habit she credits with restarting her own journaling. On two days off, he said, "I think I went through like six movies in two days during my time off." He even qualifies his own calm. When Fanning followed up on "I'm chilling," he added: "There hasn't been a whole lot of chill until very recently. But yeah, Australia is a safe place."

One smaller detail should be held lightly. Chilling is also the word he used for what company protects him from: "chilling in my own head and going crazy by myself." This suggests his easygoing surface and his need for company are more connected than the Nine reading allows. Two uses of a casual word cannot prove it.

## "He just gets what he wants"

A Type 3 reading, organized around achievement, is weaker than the Nine case and still worth testing. Lind's line comes from the least performative setting in the record, a private call in the middle of the callbacks, and what she saw was appetite: secrecy about a shortlist and close attention to the competition. Add the GQ account of leaving school for an audition, the unbroken run of jobs, and a dated ambition: "I want to direct a film before I'm 30, that's my goal," he told McGregor in July 2025.

The Three reading stumbles on the same morning the Nine reading does. Winning made him more afraid, which points to fear of falling short more than a need for validation. It also misses how little credit he takes; he routes his growth to Verso, to Jacobson's search, to his brother and his father. And he hedges competence often. "I have a lot of ways of preparing but I'm also very new to the game, so I don't want to set anything in stone right now," he told Polyester. To Flaunt: "I'm just constantly reminding myself that I'm still learning and not to be afraid of whether it's good or not." Even the directing goal came with a disclaimer: "I'm not saying I'll be good at it, but I want to try."

Those hedges carry a serious caveat. They are exactly what a well-advised teenager says in his first press cycle opposite Hollywood veterans, and all three come from about a month in mid-2025. If he speaks with settled authority about his method once his work is released, the hedging was career position more than temperament.

The Six reading does not fully answer Lind either. A Six can want something intensely, and this reading accounts for the manner of his pursuit: tracking the competition quietly, confiding in a friend, preparing with family. It does not fully explain the appetite she saw, and that tension stays open.

## What the record cannot settle yet

Some things resist every reading here. He told Flaunt: "I'm kind of in a weird place right now. I don't want anyone to know anything about me. But now I'm in these situations where I do have to talk about it." In the same passage: "I just hope that nobody thinks that I'm trying to force any ideas on them over time. I'm just here to act and be in movies." He has since given long, open conversations about his family name, his nerves and his mother's wishes. Of his plan to live in Los Angeles, New York or London, he told Fanning, "My mom doesn't really like that idea. She'd rather I just stay home." The stated reticence and the actual openness do not match, and nothing he has said explains how he squares them.

Then there is a line he gave Grace, at 20, about performers younger than himself: "Child actors are just so free, so loose. It's unattainable." In context it was a light remark about a talented young cast, and "unattainable" may simply mean hard to fake as an adult. It is still the one place where he describes a kind of freedom as closed to him, and no type in this analysis explains why.

The public picture of him is also running ahead of the evidence. His released leads so far are _Invisible Boys_, which premiered on Stan on February 13, 2025, and _We Were Liars_, released in June 2025. GQ Australia named him its Breakthrough Actor of the Year for 2025. Grace told Variety Australia he is "genuinely about to become one of the biggest stars ever" ([Variety Australia, June 19, 2026](https://au.variety.com/2026/film/news/joseph-zada-the-hunger-games-sunrise-on-the-reaping-mckenna-grace-interview-37823/)). As of September 2026, all seven episodes of _East of Eden_ are scheduled for Netflix on October 1, 2026, and _Sunrise on the Reaping_ is scheduled for US theaters on November 20, 2026 ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Hunger_Games:_Sunrise_on_the_Reaping)). Those releases will test the portrait more than any interview has. The Six reading predicts that fame will not settle him, and that at the next threshold he will again bring his people with him before he jumps into the deep end.
