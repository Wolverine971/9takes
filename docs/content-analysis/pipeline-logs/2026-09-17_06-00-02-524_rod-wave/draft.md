On September 11, 2021, Rod Wave brought his SoulFly tour to an amphitheatre in Tampa, across the bay from the St. Petersburg streets where he grew up. His fans recite every line, and DJ Fizzumfade has a name for them: "We call it the choir." Rod, the Tampa Bay Times reported, had arrived "quietly at the last minute." He had wanted a smaller room, the 2,000-capacity Jannus Live back home. "I had to tell him," said his booking agent, Andrew Lieber, "Rod, I'm sorry, you're too big." ([Tampa Bay Times, September 22, 2021](https://www.tampabay.com/life-culture/music/2021/09/22/how-rod-wave-made-it-from-st-petersburg-to-music-stardom/))

After the show, in the private parking area, he posed for photos with Julius, a 15-year-old with cancer whose wish was to meet him. Rod asked the boy a question. "Is there anything I could have done better up there tonight? I don't really know what to say between the songs."

That is the puzzle of Rod Wave. Few artists of his generation have put more private feeling on record: his father and then "Uncle Dee" going to prison, a friend dead of cancer, being scared to love. Speaking as himself, to a crowd or to one person, is the part of the job he keeps avoiding. Five years after Tampa, Charlamagne Tha God asked whether he had ever gone to therapy. "Nah," he said. "Just straight music?" Charlamagne asked. "Straight music, man." ([The Breakfast Club, August 10, 2026](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=PdHRAVarHm8))

This profile reads that pattern as Enneagram Type 9, with Type 4 as a serious rival. Two limits matter. He rarely gives interviews, and his hometown paper wrote its best account of him without his cooperation: "He did not speak with the Tampa Bay Times and does not do many interviews." Most of his long first-person talk comes from three video interviews recorded within three weeks in August 2026 to promote his seventh album, _Don't Look Down_, so their settled tone may partly be the album's. Quotations from those videos were checked against YouTube's automatically generated transcripts, not the audio, so sources from 2018 to 2023 carry the most weight below.

## What is Rod Wave's personality type?

Rod Wave most likely fits Enneagram Type 9, at medium confidence. His repeated move is to take himself out of the frame: he lets a public image he calls wrong stand, declines grievances, and puts feelings into songs instead of conversations. Type 4 is the strongest alternative, and parts of his record fit neither type.

[Type 9](/enneagram-corner/enneagram-type-9) gets summarized as "the peacemaker," which makes it sound passive. A more useful description is someone who keeps the peace by turning down the volume on their own presence: their wants, their grievances, their claim to the center of the room. Nines can work very hard. What they avoid is the friction of insisting on themselves.

[Type 4](/enneagram-corner/enneagram-type-4) looks similar in his catalogue, because both types withdraw. A Four's withdrawal usually guards the significance of their own feelings and the sting of being misunderstood. The evidence below suggests Rod guards something plainer, an undisturbed room. It also shows a drive, a lawsuit and a temper that the Nine reading struggles to explain.

## The car outside the club

Rodarius Marcell Green was born on August 27, 1998, in St. Petersburg, Florida. Just before he started middle school, his father, Rodney "Fatz" Green, was sentenced to six years in state prison. Dereck "Uncle Dee" Lane, a close friend of his father's, stepped in as the central man in his life, then went to federal prison himself a year later. Lane's daughter Daijha, who was close to Rod, died of cancer at 17 when he was 14. He later wrote, "I lost Daijha to cancer, I just got over it. I'm scared to love, so scared to get close again."

None of that tells us his type. It is the ordinary explanation for a young man who keeps people at a distance, and any type argument has to beat it.

The voice came early. He made All-State Elementary Chorus twice, and at Lakewood High he sang so constantly, a former teammate told the Times, that the football coach put a timer in the locker room to get players out to practice.

Being seen was harder. Even after he and a classmate, Elijah Simmons, started recording songs, the Times reported, Rod was "shy and uncomfortable with sharing anything publicly." Billboard wrote that "Rod feared rejection" when Simmons posted a video of him singing in a hallway to Facebook ([Billboard, December 8, 2023](https://www.billboard.com/music/features/rod-wave-billboard-number-ones-interview-1235545448/)). Simmons also gave him his stage name: "People are going to ride your wave."

The Tampa Bay Times dates his first show to a school night in August 2016, at a Clearwater strip club called the Menage Lounge, when he was 17. (Billboard dates his first performance of any kind to a teammate's birthday party in 2015.) At the clubs that booked him then, he was too young to wait inside. He told Shannon Sharpe on Club Shay Shay in August 2026 that he sat in the car until it was time to rap, walked in, performed and left. Then he connected that rule to his career. "That ended up being like my formula to stardom to being a star," he said. "Like you never really get to see me." ([Club Shay Shay, August 26, 2026](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=6jKmwaSeY-o))

That is a man about to turn 28 interpreting what happened at 17, and people often recast accidents as strategy. It still shows how he understands his distance today, as a rule he chose to keep. The Tampa Bay Times reported an early instance: after graduation, when he worked at Krispy Kreme, a drive-thru customer asked, "Are you that Rod Wave guy?" He smiled and shook his head no.

## A recording booth built for one

His working method has barely changed in six years. "Making music for me is personal," he told Complex in April 2020, at 21. "When I do my music, I don't even go to the studio. It's just me and my engineer in a dark room somewhere." Complex reported that he skipped outside guest features for the same reason, and he named a limit: "If I feel like it was too personal, I ain't going to drop it." ([Complex, April 3, 2020](https://www.complex.com/music/a/j-mckinney/rod-wave-interview))

The next year his engineer described the routine to the Tampa Bay Times. He travels everywhere in case Rod wants to record at 4 a.m. in an Airbnb closet or an airport lounge. Rod cut the vocals for the gold-certified "Brace Face" with a hotel comforter over his head. The engineer has never seen him write down a lyric.

The booth is where the feelings come out, and by his account almost nowhere else. "I don't really discuss what I go through or how I feel," he told Complex. "So by the time I sit down, it all comes up." In 2026, asked by Sharpe whether his family made room for him to talk about emotions, he said his mother tried, but "I can't really talk about that kind of stuff," and "people probably heard stuff in songs like that they never heard me say in real life." To Charlamagne he borrowed a line he credited to Lil Wayne: saying things to someone's face is hard, "so I do it over snares and bass."

He calls the studio "my hobby, my therapy." When Sharpe noted that Kevin Gates talks openly about mental illness, Rod explained why he does not. "I don't be looking for no sympathy," he said. "I'm going just deal with it myself because I feel like nobody really care about that kind of stuff cuz everybody dealing with their own problems."

"His shows are like going to karaoke with 15,000 people," Todd Moscowitz, CEO of his label Alamo Records, told Billboard in 2023. The feelings Rod says he cannot say to one person get sung back by thousands at once. This suggests he reaches his own feelings most easily when they are aimed at no one in particular. A plainer reading also holds: some people are better writers than talkers.

## The image he won't correct

Asked by Charlamagne about the biggest misconception about him, Rod said the image people have of him "is totally different," then declined to do anything about it. "It's just not natural for me to push that on people or try to prove a point of who I am," he said. "I just don't ever feel the need to do that when I wake up in the morning." It costs him something, he admitted: "I wish people didn't ... take my image and run with it."

He even treats the public figure as a part-time role. Roughly 300 days a year go to business and ordinary life, he said, and 65 to "straight rock and roll." Album releases, tours and press runs are when "I remember who I am."

He also refuses the word nearly everyone uses for his music. Charlamagne asked how someone carrying so much pain became one of America's biggest artists without it destroying him. Rod rejected the premise: "people always taking and saying pain like I'm still not even like that word." Everybody goes through things, he said, and "it's not pain, it's not sad ... it's normal. It's reality." He accepts sadness and rejects depression as a label, though when pressed he allowed, "Yeah, been there." Billboard reported in 2023 that he "has struggled in the past with depression and anxiety and has always been open about having had suicidal thoughts." Both statements belong on the record, with their dates.

The ordinary-guy stance predates the current album. "I understand I'm a rapper, but I am a human. Just like you," he told Complex in 2020. "Don't look at me like a rapper, because I don't want to be treated like that." Five years later he told Billboard, "I'm not like a superstar or a fairy tale. We are going through the same thing." ([Billboard, September 8, 2025](https://www.billboard.com/music/rb-hip-hop/rod-wave-interview-2025-sinners-touring-album-1236057705/))

Relatability is also good business, so the real test is whether he holds the ordinary position when it costs him. On awards, it does. _Don't Look Down_ is his fourth No. 1 album on the Billboard 200 and, as of September 2026, his eighth top-10 project, every title he has charted ([Billboard, September 6, 2026](https://www.billboard.com/music/chart-beat/rod-wave-dont-look-down-number-one-billboard-200-chart-1236335185/)). When Sharpe raised a viral fan complaint that he had never won a BET Award or a Grammy, Rod split the blame, then took more of it. "It might be a 50/50 problem," he said, "to where like maybe I'm not interacting enough or maybe I'm not putting myself out there enough or networking enough." Later: "I've been under a rock for like five years. I'm just not coming up out my shell." And: "Take responsibility for it."

He calls it lifelong temperament. "I've never been the center of attention type of person," he said, recalling that even as a kid, "I ain't never want no birthday parties." He was unsure whether that was "a ego thing or if it's a confident thing."

## The case for Type 4

The obvious reading of Rod Wave is Type 4, and it deserves full weight. His catalogue runs on heartbreak and betrayal. He released a mixtape titled _PTSD_ with a track called "Popular Loner," and his forearms are tattooed "Hard Times." At Barclays Center in November 2023 he climbed a ladder on his stage set while fans screamed "Don't do it, Rod!" and then staged a fall, which he told Billboard came "from a dream I had."

His own words about his younger self read like a Four's. "When I was younger I was so dramatic," he told Charlamagne. "I felt like the world owed me something. I felt like I was all mad at these people, these people what they did to me, how could they do that to me?" He says betrayal marked him more than poverty, because poverty was unchosen: "betrayal is like a decision." And when criticism piled up after his Last Lap tour collapsed in early 2025, his hiatus post was wounded, aimed at those who "drag my name talk about me like a dog constantly speaking on me bashing me and what make me happy I dont fw you" ([HipHopDX, January 31, 2025](https://hiphopdx.com/news/rod-wave-steps-back-music-bashed-by-fans/)).

A Four reading explains a lot of this. The dark rooms protect an inner life too personal for collaborators. Indifference to awards is disdain for a system that cannot see him. The career is a wound turned into an identity. It covers much of the same evidence the Nine reading does.

## Why the breakup answer points to Type 9

Three observations separate the readings.

The first is what he does with being misread. Being seen inaccurately is close to the core injury for a Four, and Fours tend to answer it by explaining themselves more precisely. Rod says his public image is wrong and, as a matter of temperament, lets it stand.

The second is what he does with specialness. His label CEO calls his lane "struggle rap." Rod calls his material "normal," asked in 2020 to be seen as "one of us making music," and now treats his early self-importance as immaturity. Of music he made at 19, he told Apple Music's The Plug Radio, "Damn, I was a little dramatic" ([Apple Music, August 28, 2026](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=91tx_ndYsfE)). A man promoting a happier album has reason to say that, which is why the 2020 remarks matter more.

The third, and sharpest, is heartbreak. Sharpe asked what Rod does when a partner tells him she is unhappy and wants to leave. "I don't know, dog. I ain't never been in that situation," he said. He conceded, "I done been heartbroke," then reconsidered aloud. "Well, let me think back. I ain't never been in a situation to where it was like we didn't mutually agree to go our separate ways." If she is ready to go, he said, "that's going to bring me up to speed. It's time to go." And: "It's always just your turn, dog."

He said he built that response on purpose. "I used to take it real hard," he explained, and "I used to let it affect my work ethic." So "that was the way I had to tell myself and train my brain like ain't nothing or nobody never yours."

Longing for what is gone sits near the center of a Four's emotional world, and his songs are full of it. In conversation, he describes a trained readiness that forms in response to the other person's decision, so that a loss cannot pull him out of his work. That looks more like a Nine restoring calm. It is one answer, given on camera by a man in a relationship, and "let me think back" may mean he preferred not to revisit a particular breakup.

His description of peace points the same way. Asked what it looks like, he named his own bed, a freshly cleaned house, the "AC on 65," an Apple TV show with his partner, and "good meetings at the office" the next morning. Music, recognition and being understood did not come up.

## Where the Type 9 reading strains

The Nine reading has real trouble in three places, and none of them should be explained away with Enneagram vocabulary.

### The engine

Rod has released seven studio albums from _Ghetto Gospel_ in 2019 to _Don't Look Down_ in 2026, and after Alamo signed him in 2018 he played more than 100 shows, mostly at small Florida clubs. He calls himself "over creative": "I like things done a certain way." After the Last Lap tour collapsed he founded Mainstay Touring. "I just didn't want to be the pawn no more," he told Sharpe. "Like if somebody gonna mess this thing up, it's going to be me." Mainstay then promoted Ye's shows at SoFi Stadium, partly, he said, because "I didn't want other people to say maybe that only worked for you" ([DancehallMag, August 29, 2026](https://www.dancehallmag.com/2026/08/29/features/interviews/rod-wave-promoted-the-biggest-hip-hop-show-in-us-history-and-is-launching-a-sports-agency.html)). Nine theory does not explain that drive.

### The countersuit

The Last Lap tour fell apart in January 2025, when a Nashville show was called off moments before he was due on stage and the remaining dates were cancelled. On September 29, 2025, promoter Grizzly Touring sued him in federal court, alleging he owed $27 million of a $57 million advance and, in Billboard's summary, that he raised the scale of his production without warning ([Billboard, October 1, 2025](https://www.billboard.com/pro/rod-wave-last-lap-tour-lawsuit-promoter-sues-advance/)).

His October 12 countersuit blamed Grizzly's scheduling, saying "only 12 of the 35 scheduled [shows] occurred at the date and venue originally announced." It also alleged that Grizzly member company AG Entertainment paid his former agent, Beau Williams, at least $1.8 million in "under-the-table" payments to slip an exclusivity clause into his contract ([Billboard, October 13, 2025](https://www.billboard.com/pro/rod-wave-countersues-tour-lawsuit-canceled-concerts/)). All of this is allegation, and the dispute was unresolved in the reporting reviewed as of September 2026.

In his own voice, he tells it differently. "If it mess up, man, I'm going to be blaming myself," he told Sharpe, "cuz I just ain't with blaming people and because at the end of the day I brought y'all in." The charitable explanation is division of labor, since lawyers litigate and a countersuit is a defensive tool. That is probably most of the story. The filing still carries his name, and nothing public closes the gap. Grizzly's production claim, from an adverse party with money at stake, also resembles his own "I like things done a certain way." That link is an inference.

Avoided conflict can land on other people. In 2024, Ari Lennox, who opened his 2023 Nostalgia Tour and was hit by a bottle thrown from the crowd, wrote about "evil" and "racist" treatment from his fans and said "what made it even worse is Rod saying nothing in my defense. Privately or publicly." She added, "The only reason we met was because we ran into each [other] in a Hotel Lobby after constant efforts of me trying to share my gratefulness for the opportunity." She also said her "abandonment issues" make rejection hit her harder, and that "half of the mess I end up in I'm solely responsible for." Asked about that tour in August 2026, Rod praised her, said "There's some things I just don't got control over," and wished "her experience would have been better." He did not address her charge that he stayed silent.

He has described that distance himself. Discussing Lil Poppa, a younger rapper on the Last Lap tour who died in February 2026 ([NBC News](https://www.nbcnews.com/pop-culture/pop-culture-news/florida-rapper-lil-poppa-dies-25-rcna259758)), Rod told Sharpe, "I never really was, you know what I'm saying, interacting with the people on tour." Poppa had sought him out a few times, "and, you know, I never do that with people." His account and Lennox's agree about the distance, if not about its meaning.

### The temper

Rod says his father still warns him: "bro your temper you got to work on that." He describes his teenage anger as having nowhere to go. "I had a lot of built-up anger," he told Charlamagne, and "I didn't know how to express it." Others his age "violently expressed it," he said. "And I just kind of expressed it through music."

The record shows music did not absorb all of it. In 2014, at 15, he was arrested after a break-in. Police said he admitted taking a .357 revolver from the house and shooting another teen in the foot, and the charge was dropped after the victim called it an accident. A counselor at the juvenile detention center told him he had post-traumatic stress disorder, a remark made to a teenager in custody rather than a diagnosis of record.

As an adult, he turned himself in on a misdemeanor battery charge in Atlanta in 2021, after a promoter said Rod hit him following a 2020 show where a stage collapsed; this research did not find the outcome ([Los Angeles Times, May 21, 2025](https://www.latimes.com/entertainment-arts/music/story/2025-05-21/rod-wave-arrested-atlanta-gun-charges)). In May 2022 he was charged in Florida with felony domestic battery by strangulation. He pleaded not guilty, the charge was dropped within weeks, and his attorney called it "a misunderstanding between a girlfriend and boyfriend" ([Rolling Stone, May 2022](https://www.rollingstone.com/music/music-news/rod-wave-choked-ex-domestic-battery-charge-1352058/)). In 2025 he was arrested twice in Fulton County, Georgia. The first booking, in May, listed 14 charges including pointing a gun at another person; his lawyers said he "was a victim of a burglary and committed no crimes." On November 7, the day he received his first Grammy nomination, for "Sinners," he was booked on drug and weapons charges, and his lawyers said he was "unjustly profiled and unlawfully arrested" ([Rolling Stone, November 10, 2025](https://www.rollingstone.com/music/music-news/rod-wave-arrested-drugs-weapons-charges-grammys-1235462608/)). Both 2025 cases were unresolved in the reporting reviewed as of September 2026.

None of these allegations is proven, and no psychological conclusion should rest on them. They do mean that a portrait of a man who never disturbs a room would be false. The public record does not show why these incidents happened, and this profile does not claim to know.

## The turtle and the choir

Rod's chosen emblem is the turtle. He signed off his Club Shay Shay interview with "Turtle race continue," and a 2025 documentary about him is titled _Don't Look Down: The Turtle Race Continues_. He explains his success as endurance more than brilliance. "I was right there in the room and I was in last place," he told Charlamagne, and some of the trophies went to "whoever could last the longest." Talent, he told Apple Music, is "only like about 40%." That self-image fits a Nine. His pace does not.

When the criticism peaked in January 2025, he did not argue with anyone. He left, telling the fans who stayed, "this ain't goodbye it's simply see u later," and saying he planned to "travel, see the world, find love, raise kids." His next album, _Don't Look Down_, arrived nineteen months later and debuted at No. 1 with 99,000 equivalent album units.

Several things remain open: where the engine comes from, how "I just ain't with blaming people" squares with his countersuit, and what lies behind the weapons cases. The calm, business-minded voice of August 2026 may be growth, or it may be the voice an album about growth requires. When he sounded similarly settled to Billboard in September 2025, the Grizzly lawsuit arrived three weeks later.

What has held since at least 2020 is narrower. The feelings come out when he sits down to record, and rarely in conversation. A wrong public image is left to stand. And on a night in 2021 when the choir sang every word back to him near home, the one thing he asked a 15-year-old fan to critique was the part of the show where he had to talk.
