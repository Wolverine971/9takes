To find Dani's grief in Ari Aster's _Midsommar_ (2019), Florence Pugh imagined her family dying. By the middle of the shoot, "I actually needed to imagine the coffins." By the end, "I actually was going to my whole family's funeral." ([Variety, November 11, 2025](https://variety.com/2025/film/news/florence-pugh-midsommar-abuse-depression-six-months-1236574037/)) In December 2019 she summed up the method: "unfortunately, I can't cry on cue. So, I had to really just destroy myself for an entire three months, but I was happy with the work." ([Deadline, December 5, 2019](https://deadline.com/2019/12/florence-pugh-little-women-black-widow-midsommar-fighting-with-my-family-interview-1202799412/))

From there she went almost straight to Greta Gerwig's _Little Women_, where she played Amy March. "Coming off Midsommar, Amy March in Little Women was the best therapy for me," she told Deadline. She got to "prance around in petticoats" and flirt on camera with [Timothée Chalamet](/personality-analysis/timothee-chalamet). Being on that set, "playing and cuddling everyone," was "the perfect way to balance who I was two weeks previous." ([TheWrap, December 25, 2019](https://www.thewrap.com/florence-pugh-little-women-midsommar-paige-wwe-fighting-with-family/))

Six years later, on _The Louis Theroux Podcast_, she told the same story with a different ending. _Little Women_ "was such a fun experience and obviously a completely different tone from 'Midsommar,' so I think shelved all of that. And then when I got home for Christmas, I was so depressed." Looking back, she said, "I think ['Midsommar'] made me sad for like six months after and I didn't know why I was depressed." (Variety, November 11, 2025)

Her directors describe someone nothing can dent. "There is something about her that can't be knocked down or overtaken," Gerwig said in 2019 (TheWrap). That is the Florence Pugh most people know: loud, funny, impossible to body-shame into silence. Her own stories, told over a decade, keep showing a quieter habit. When pain arrives, she moves toward something lighter and faster, and names the cost later.

Pugh was born on January 3, 1996, in Oxford, England ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Florence_Pugh)). She broke through in _Lady Macbeth_ (2016), earned Academy Award and BAFTA nominations for _Little Women_, plays Yelena Belova for Marvel, and appeared in _Oppenheimer_ (2023) and _Dune: Part Two_ (2024). She is also an executive producer of _East of Eden_, Zoe Kazan's 2026 Netflix series, in which she plays Cathy Ames.

Nearly everything public about her comes from press tours and cover stories, where candor and warmth are part of the brand. Theroux quotations here come from print coverage or an official clip of the episode; where only an automated transcript exists, the article says so.

## What is Florence Pugh's personality type?

Florence Pugh most likely fits Enneagram Type 7, the Enthusiast, at medium confidence. Across a decade of interviews, her own accounts repeat one move: when pain arrives, she reaches for motion, play and a lighter room, and names the cost later. Type 8 is the strongest alternative. Type 2 is a live third reading.

[Type 7](/enneagram-corner/enneagram-type-7) is organized around keeping life open, stimulating and free of pain and limits. Boldness can't settle her type; a bold actress can look like a Seven, an [Eight](/enneagram-corner/enneagram-type-8) or a Three. Two questions do more work: what does she do with her own pain, and what is her pushback defending? An Eight guards vulnerability so as not to be controlled or seen as weak. A Seven moves away from pain to keep things light and moving, and resists anything that boxes her in. Pugh's record fits the second description more often than the first, though not every time.

This is a reading of public interviews, not access to her private life, and the evidence against it gets its own sections below.

## "That annoying kid on every stage"

Her mother, Deborah, is a dancer, and her father, Clinton Pugh, a restaurateur. She has an older brother, the actor and musician Toby Sebastian, an older sister, the actor Arabella Gibbins, and a younger sister, Rafaela. As a small child she had tracheomalacia, a weak windpipe, and was in and out of hospital; the family moved to southern Spain when she was about three and came back to Oxford when she was about six.

She refuses to make the illness a story about her. "I never want this to be a sob story," she told Vogue in 2023, "because it's never been a story in my life." ([Vogue, January 12, 2023](https://www.vogue.com/article/florence-pugh-winter-cover-2023-interview)) This profile takes her at her word.

The household ran on food and company. "We're a big eating family," she told Vogue in 2020. "It's all big and loving and homey." ([Vogue, January 9, 2020](https://www.vogue.com/article/florence-pugh-cover-february-2020))

A school nativity play gave her a feeling she still recognizes. "It was the first time I knew the power of being on stage. I remember thinking, 'Oh God, they're waiting for me, they're listening to everything I say and I have complete control.' I still feel that now," she told [The Guardian in 2018](https://www.theguardian.com/fashion/tvandradioblog/2018/oct/06/florence-pugh-lady-macbeth-king-lear-le-carre). At 18 she called herself "that annoying kid on every stage at school, in every talent contest." ([The Observer, December 28, 2014](https://www.theguardian.com/culture/2014/dec/28/rising-stars-2015-actor-florence-pugh))

"Complete control" sounds as much like an Eight, or a Three holding an audience, as a Seven chasing the thrill of the live moment. The childhood record shows a performer, not yet a motive.

## The tape she almost didn't send

At 17 she heard about an open audition for _The Falling_ and nearly let it pass. Her brother's career had taught her caution. "My brother was in the industry, and I knew that's not how you're going to get a part, handing in a three-minute tape." Her mother talked her into it: "We know you aren't going to get it, but this is something you want to do with your life, so it's kind of a free go." ([W, July 12, 2017](https://www.wmagazine.com/story/florence-pugh-lady-macbeth-movie)) She got the part.

Her earliest interview on record, at 18, already struck two notes. "I do like a bit of danger," she told The Observer. "Guns, cars, running, bullets. I'm up for it." And of her break: "It's a bit of a fluke."

Then came the setback she points to as a turning point. At 19 she was cast as the lead in _Studio City_, a Fox pilot. In 2022 she told The Telegraph, as relayed by Variety, that the people behind it tried to change "my weight, my look, the shape of my face, the shape of my eyebrows," and that "I felt I'd made a massive mistake." Fox passed on the show. Two weeks later she auditioned for _Lady Macbeth_. "That made me fall back in love with cinema – the kind of cinema that was a space where you could be opinionated, and loud, and I've stuck by that," she said. "I think it's far too easy for people in this industry to push you left and right." ([Variety, November 1, 2022](https://variety.com/2022/film/news/florence-pugh-body-shamed-studio-execs-change-face-1235419750/))

She never had to refuse anything; the show simply died. The story was told six years later, by which point candor was part of her public image. But its central value was in her vocabulary by 21. "I don't like being told how to do things," she told Grazia in 2017, describing scenes where the men around her _Lady Macbeth_ character confine her: "it makes Katherine angry, and it made me angry." ([Grazia, April 27, 2017](https://graziadaily.co.uk/celebrity/news/florence-pugh-lady-macbeth-interview/))

What her defiance names, from 19 on, is being told, pushed and boxed in. That matters for the type call. An Eight's pushback is usually about strength and who holds power. Hers keeps coming back to freedom.

## The thrill first, the cost later

At the time, _Midsommar_ was a high. "To get it perfect, I knew, would be so exhilarating and the payoff would be a fantastic feeling — which it was," she told the Associated Press in July 2019 ([AP via Gulf News](https://gulfnews.com/entertainment/hollywood/florence-pugh-brings-the-chills-in-horror-film-midsommar-1.64917847)). In March 2023, on the _Off Menu_ podcast, she said she "most definitely abused my own self in order to get that performance" ([Variety, March 30, 2023](https://variety.com/2023/film/news/florence-pugh-midsommar-abuse-1235569340/)). In November 2025: "But I don't do that anymore. It really fucked me up." (Variety, November 11, 2025) By 2026 the lesson had become a rule: "I now know how to protect my space." ([Vanity Fair, September 25, 2026](https://www.vanityfair.com/story/cover-story-florence-pugh)) She has also said she is proud of the performance, "so I don't regret it" ([Reign with Josh Smith, January 2, 2025](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=7vyExn--DtA)).

The Valentino dress follows the same order. On July 8, 2022, at Valentino's couture show in Rome, she wore a sheer pink gown; she and designer Pierpaolo Piccioli had removed the lining ([Harper's Bazaar, August 16, 2022](https://www.harpersbazaar.com/culture/film-tv/a40772062/florence-pugh-dont-worry-darling-interview/)). Her Instagram reply to the body-shaming led with nerve. "I was excited to wear it, not a wink of me was nervous. I wasn't before, during or even now after." Then the punchline: "Why are you so scared of breasts? Small? Large? Left? Right? Only one? Maybe none? What. Is. So. Terrifying." ([TODAY, July 11, 2022](https://www.today.com/style/style/florence-pugh-see-through-sheer-gown-rcna37548)) She later gave Harper's Bazaar the final word: "They were so angry that I was confident, and they wanted to let me know that they would never wank over me. Well, don't."

In 2023 she framed it as politics: "It's the freedom that people are scared of; the fact I'm comfortable and happy." ([ELLE UK, August 29, 2023](https://www.elle.com/uk/life-and-culture/culture/a44863369/florence-pugh-interview-elle-style-awards/)) Only in September 2024 did she name the hurt: "[The internet's] a very mean place. It's really painful to read people being nasty about my confidence or nasty about my weight. It never feels good." ([British Vogue, September 18, 2024](https://www.vogue.co.uk/article/florence-pugh-british-vogue-interview))

People process hurt over time, and a reflective cover interview invites admissions. None of this proves she was hiding pain in 2022. What it shows is the order in which she tells her own stories. In two unrelated episodes, three years apart, the thrill and the comeback came first and the cost arrived years later.

## "The last person that I look after"

The Theroux episode contains her most direct account of the habit. Discussing a past relationship she has not named, one she described as "causing you pain," she said: "I think when you are such a bubbly person and you fill in a lot of energy for other people, I'm definitely the last person that I look after. I felt like there was an expectation that I needed to be good for everybody else. My issues, my worries, my pains weren't worthy of being spoken about because they would bring people down." ([Daily Times, November 11, 2025](https://dailytimes.com.pk/1398933/florence-pugh-gets-candid-about-health-struggles-and-hitting-breaking-point/))

An automated transcript of the episode has her going on to call it "that people pleaser thing where you just try and keep things afloat without anybody else knowing that you're paddling for dear life" ([Podscripts](https://podscripts.co/podcasts/the-louis-theroux-podcast/s6-ep6-florence-pugh-discusses-intimacy-coordinators-jumping-off-buildings-and-getting-into-character)). That wording has not been checked against the audio. The same transcript renders "good for everybody else" as "upbeat for everybody else," so this profile does not rest anything on that word.

The reason she gives for hiding pain concerns the room. Her pains "would bring people down." She says nothing about looking weak.

The same move shows up where no relationship is at stake. In March 2020, locked down in Los Angeles, she got a call from her father. "My dad called me yesterday and said, 'Why aren't you dancing and why aren't you cooking?' ... So this morning I woke up and I danced and I felt better and now I'm going to cook." Her advice in what became her "Cooking with Flo" videos: "we have to do things that keep us happy, keep us bubbly and keep moving basically... Get all of the weird horrid anxiety out of your system." ([IBTimes, March 31, 2020](https://www.ibtimes.com/we-have-little-women-star-florence-pughs-father-thank-her-cooking-tutorials-2950336))

She described her teenage years the same way. "So many of the things that I did were quick fixes," she told Vogue in 2023. "Things were so wrong in my head," she said, so she would think, "Oh, well, I can change my nails. Oh, I can buy an eyelash kit." After jumping from the Merdeka 118 skyscraper for _Thunderbolts*_ (2025), she said the scariest discovery was "I could persuade myself to do that," adding, "I can basically pretend to myself not to listen to my instincts." ([Who What Wear, May 1, 2025](https://www.whowhatwear.com/fashion/cover-features/florence-pugh))

Alone, none of these would carry much weight: lockdown cooking was everywhere, teenage quick fixes are ordinary, and the stunt line is one reflection. Together, across her teens, 2019, 2020 and 2025, they suggest a pattern: distress met with movement, pleasure and a lighter register, with the cost noticed afterward. That is the Seven's signature, the habit of reaching for [the next thing](/enneagram-corner/why-the-next-thing-wont-fix-it-type-7) to outrun the hard one, and it is the strongest single strand in her record.

## The case for Type 8

The Eight reading is serious, and it starts with the people who have worked beside her. Cate Shortland, who directed her in _Black Widow_: "She is bloody scary. Steely. Absolutely will not back down. She has a healthy amount of anger in her as a person, at the injustices she sees around her." (Vogue, 2020) [Scarlett Johansson](/personality-analysis/scarlett-johansson) in 2022: "She's unapologetically herself." (Harper's Bazaar) Denis Villeneuve in 2026: "There is something unapologetic about the way she inspires respect." (Vanity Fair) Pugh agrees: "I'm already a bit of a big mouth. I already love telling people what's what." (TheWrap, 2019)

She forces physical commitments past other people's caution. Cutting her own hair on camera in _A Good Person_ (2023) was her idea. Zach Braff, its writer-director and her former partner, recalled that "she just kept saying, 'You'll figure it out, you'll figure it out.'" ([Collider, March 24, 2023](https://collider.com/zach-braff-a-good-person-interview/)) Marvel was reluctant to let her do the Merdeka 118 jump herself because of insurance, and she kept pushing, including with Kevin Feige, until it agreed (Wikipedia). Her stated reason was collective: "I wanted to do that stunt because I knew that it meant that we all got to do it." ([Variety, May 3, 2025](https://variety.com/2025/film/features/florence-pugh-thunderbolts-interview-1236385097/))

She fights in public for people she loves. In April 2020, a birthday post for her then-partner Braff, 21 years older than her, drew so much abuse that she turned off comments for the first time. Her video reply told the abusers to "please unfollow me because the abuse that you throw at him is abuse that you throw at me, and I don't want those followers." ([Nylon, April 8, 2020](https://www.nylon.com/florence-pugh-zach-braff-birthday)) In 2025 she told Theroux, "I'll always defend people that I love, I will always stand up for them." ([Entertainment Weekly via Yahoo, November 12, 2025](https://www.yahoo.com/entertainment/celebrity/articles/florence-pugh-opens-unhealthy-past-130000822.html))

She accepts real costs for causes. At the Together for Palestine benefit in London in September 2025 she said, "Silence in the face of such suffering is not neutrality, it is complicity" ([The Hollywood Reporter, September 25, 2026](https://www.hollywoodreporter.com/news/general-news/florence-pugh-palestine-support-hollywood-sympathy-1236710258/)). She told Vanity Fair in 2026, "I was never scared of it," and estimated she had lost hundreds of thousands of followers. In late September 2026 she posted about a pending sexual-assault case involving Cornell University students, in which some of the accused deny wrongdoing. Speaking on _The View_ about such stories not being picked up, she said, "it made me feel so much rage." ([Variety, September 30, 2026](https://variety.com/2026/tv/news/florence-pugh-cornell-7-instagram-posts-censored-sexual-assault-1236893808/))

She wants control of her own work. She has been an executive producer of _East of Eden_ since 2020, says "I don't like messy sets," and admires producers who "command space." (Vanity Fair, 2026)

On this reading, the line about being the last person she looks after describes one painful relationship, not a type. Her softness on sets, which comes up next, is a strategy forced on women in her industry, and she says as much herself.

## Why the Seven reading still leads

Three observations tip the call, none of them decisive alone.

The first is the reason she gives for hiding pain. An Eight hides vulnerability to avoid being controlled or seen as weak. Pugh says hers "would bring people down," and the same move recurs from _Little Women_ to the lockdown videos.

The second is her most detailed story about a standoff on set. On the Theroux podcast she said it can be "a bit stickier" for women to push back, in case they are called "a nightmare." During a crying scene the director asked for another take after about six. "And when I'm in that state, I'm a bit mushy, so I'm like, 'Okay, we'll do it again.'" Her co-star intervened: "Don't do that, man. Don't put her through that." The director agreed at once, and she reflected that the same words from her "wouldn't have been received well." ([Pearl & Dean, November 11, 2025](https://www.pearlanddean.com/florence-pugh-reveals-male-co-star-spoke-up-when-director-demanded-more-takes-of-difficult-scene/)) That is an unusual story for an Eight to tell about herself. Her own explanation is gendered, and in the same conversation she said, "I've always been able to make sure that I'm heard" ([The Louis Theroux Podcast, official clip](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=QdWZdBSRXZw)). So the story supports the Seven reading without settling it.

The third is what the defiance defends: not being pushed "left and right" or "told how to do things," and "the freedom that people are scared of." In 2025: "I don't want to have things just happen to me any more." ([Harper's Bazaar UK, April 14, 2025](https://www.harpersbazaar.com/uk/culture/culture-news/a64455983/florence-pugh-cover-interview-thunderbolts/)) Her firmest public statements tend to end in a joke and an exit. After the Valentino caption came "Well, don't." When Instagram limited her Cornell post, she reposted with "Tut tut tut. Oh dear. That doesn't look great does it?"

Her handling of _Don't Worry Darling_ fits the same exit pattern. In September 2022 she missed the film's Venice Film Festival press conference (the festival said her flight from the _Dune: Part Two_ shoot landed afterward) but walked the red carpet that night ([Variety, September 5, 2022](https://au.variety.com/2022/film/global/olivia-wilde-florence-pugh-dont-worry-darling-venice-5618)). Reports of tension with the director, Olivia Wilde, rested largely on anonymous sources, and in 2026 Wilde said, "I've never had a screaming match on my set." ([Variety, June 24, 2026](https://variety.com/2026/film/news/olivia-wilde-florence-pugh-screaming-match-dont-worry-darling-1236789286/)) Pugh has never told her side. "I'll probably just let that one sit," she said in 2023 ([Vanity Fair, February 15, 2023](https://www.vanityfair.com/hollywood/2023/02/florence-pugh-hollywood-issue-2023)). Legal or professional prudence could explain that as well as temperament. Either way, for four years she has declined either to fight that conflict in public or to resolve it.

Shortland's "will not back down" and the Cornell rage fit the Eight better. A Seven with a fierce protective streak, or an Eight who learned to soften on sets: the public record can't fully separate them. The tilt comes from the costly private moments she describes, which run toward lightness.

## Could she be a Type 2?

The [Type 2](/enneagram-corner/enneagram-type-2) reading starts with her own words. Being "the last person that I look after" is a Helper's phrase. So is this, from 2024: "I love looking after people. I love caring for people. I love the feeling of someone being there." The most important thing, she said, is "that I'm a good person and people feel good in my presence." (British Vogue) Of her youngest sister: "She was as much my baby as she was my mum's." (Vogue, 2023) The actor Ashley Park told Vanity Fair: "She's a great chef, she's a great host, she's a great designer, she's a great planner." Even her case for rest is about other people: "I am a better person after I've rested. I'm a kinder friend. I'm a kinder lover. I'm a kinder sibling." (Who What Wear)

What keeps the Seven reading ahead is what she describes chasing. "Part of the reason we all do this is because we run away with the circus," she said in 2022. "I think that one of the pulls for me is that I get to see places, see people, befriend people, fall in love with people, and then move on and do it again." (Harper's Bazaar) In 2024 she named the romantic version as a pattern she was trying to break: "Falling is the most amazing feeling, but unfortunately if that's the only thing that you know in a relationship, then that's the thing that you chase. That's not gonna last." (British Vogue) A Two's pattern usually runs toward being needed and hard to replace. Hers, by her own account, ran toward beginnings.

She rejects approval as her engine ("I don't think it's confidence in hoping people like me. I think it's just, like, I don't want to be anyone else"), though the same interview cuts the other way: she keeps friends from age 13 close because "I don't want to be someone that no one else likes outside of this industry." The Two reading is not ruled out. The observation that would separate the two best, how she reacts when her care goes unreturned, is missing from the public record.

## What the Seven reading can't explain

She moves toward pain in her work. "I've never found it a challenge to be acting in pain," she said in 2024. "I sometimes prefer it." She calls it "my duty to play human and ugly." (British Vogue) Of her roles: "the majority of my roles that I've taken on I've been terrified by" ([Q with Tom Power, October 17, 2024](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=wBKwNBGUuwM)). She spent six years bringing _East of Eden_'s Cathy Ames to the screen and defends her fiercely: "It's my responsibility to understand the character, because they can't defend themselves." (Harper's Bazaar UK) Professional craft may explain this. The Seven reading may also be incomplete, and choosing what frightens her could even point to a counterphobic Six.

Her moral anger, and her willingness to lose an audience over it, fit an Eight or a Two more naturally than a Seven.

Since 2024 she has turned toward home and privacy. What she wants, she said that year, is "safety, family, a home and security." (British Vogue) Of her relationship with the actor Finn Cole, she told Vanity Fair in 2026, "I don't want to be a secret, but I also don't want everything to be divulged," and "I feel solid and safe and sturdy." That could be maturity, a good relationship, or a security motive none of the leading readings captures.

And the record has limits. "I walked away from our process not understanding any better than I did beforehand how she works," Kazan told Vanity Fair. The public sees what she shows on press tours, not how she behaves at home.

## Learning to unpack

By her account, _We Live in Time_, shot in 2023 and released in 2024, changed something. "I definitely wanted to wake up to my own decisions and I didn't want to push things anymore," she said afterward (Reign). In April 2025: "I've worked back-to-back since I started, and I've missed so much." (Harper's Bazaar UK) In spring 2025 she took a three-week holiday, her first ever of that length (Who What Wear).

The change reached her love life too. "I think for the first time, I'm not allowing myself to go on a roller-coaster," she said in 2024. (British Vogue) Her rule for self-correction is quick and practical: "You don't like that behavior? Change it now before it becomes comfortable." (Vanity Fair, 2026)

In the 2026 Vanity Fair interview she put it in two lines. "I love packing," she said. "I hate unpacking." (Vanity Fair) It was a throwaway, and the Seven pattern in miniature: the pleasure is in the leaving. The work she describes now is the other half, staying put long enough to unpack.
