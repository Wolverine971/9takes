"Everyone is like, 'You're stepping into Woody's shoes,'" Joseph Zada told [The Hollywood Reporter](https://www.hollywoodreporter.com/tv/tv-features/joseph-zada-east-of-eden-hunger-games-james-dean-1236716673/) (THR) in a profile published October 1, 2026, the day his Netflix series _East of Eden_ premiered. "No, I'm not. These are my characters and my movies — not James', not Woody's. I'm stepping into my own shoes."

James is James Dean, who made Cal Trask famous in the 1955 film of _East of Eden_; Zada plays Cal in Netflix's seven-episode version. Woody is Woody Harrelson, and Zada plays young Haymitch Abernathy in _The Hunger Games: Sunrise on the Reaping_, the prequel about the District 12 tribute who grows into the mentor Harrelson played.

The same profile describes the first time Zada sat beside Harrelson, at a dinner after the prequel was shot. "But everything I knew about him just went out of my mind. I couldn't think of any questions. I forgot everything I wanted to ask him."

THR's writer heard him "not the least bit haunted by the ghosts of movie stars past." About the roles, he isn't. About the people, Zada, an Australian actor born in 2005, has described being unnerved in interviews from July 2025 to October 2026. What he does with that feeling is the case for Enneagram Type 6, the type organized around security and around who can be relied on.

A limit shapes everything below. Almost every substantial source on Zada is an interview published between February 2025 and October 2026, inside the promotional windows of four projects. Nervousness, gratitude and "the cast became a family" are the house style of those formats, and a newcomer sharing a cast list with Glenn Close and Ralph Fiennes would produce them whatever his type. So this reading leans on what he did, especially when it cost him something, and on statements that repeat across years and interviewers.

## What is Joseph Zada's personality type?

Joseph Zada looks most like Enneagram Type 6, at medium confidence. Before a big test he builds footing from trusted people and heavy preparation, and the fear he names most often is how admired actors will see him. Type 9 fits his calm surface and Type 3 his appetite, but neither explains as well what settles him.

Nerves about roles this size would fit [Type 6](/enneagram-corner/enneagram-type-6), [Type 3](/enneagram-corner/enneagram-type-3) or [Type 9](/enneagram-corner/enneagram-type-9). The difference lies in what the alarm is about and what settles it. A Six fears the ground giving way and settles once trusted people and structure are in place. A Three fears failing and settles through results. A Nine fears disruption and settles by tuning the pressure out.

Zada's record favors the Six mainly through what settles him. Other Six markers are thin. He does not visibly test whether people can be trusted, and his mixed record with authority, following a director's repeated instruction while walking out on a principal's ultimatum, fits most young actors as well as it fits a Six. That keeps confidence at medium. For comparison, 9takes reads [Timothée Chalamet](/personality-analysis/timothee-chalamet) and [Jennifer Lawrence](/personality-analysis/jennifer-lawrence) as Sixes and [Austin Butler](/personality-analysis/austin-butler) as a Nine.

## Kicking and screaming into the family trade

Acting was the household business. His father, Jeremy Cumpston, has worked as a director, actor and doctor, and his mother, Jessica Brentnall, as a film producer ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Joseph_Zada)). "We grew up watching movies every single day. We did acting courses on our school holidays," he told Elle Fanning, who plays Effie Trinket in _Sunrise on the Reaping_, for [Interview Magazine](https://www.interviewmagazine.com/film/joseph-zada-elle-fanning-hunger-games) in August 2026. That upbringing is the plainest explanation for his access and his ease on a set.

He did not walk in willingly. According to THR, at 14 he was skipping so much school that his father set a condition for letting it continue: help out on a family friend's stage production of _Romeo and Juliet_. "I went into it kicking and screaming," Zada said. "I was not going to get on stage and embarrass myself." Then: "I would go to rehearsals trying to convince myself that I wasn't enjoying it. But the truth was, I was very much enjoying myself."

"It only properly appealed to me at about 15, when I started helping my older brother with auditions when he'd come back from America," he told Fanning. That brother is Hal, an actor six years older who had won a role in the American series _The Walking Dead: World Beyond_ ([Flaunt](https://www.flaunt.com/post/joseph-zada); [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hal_Cumpston)). "We'd work up to eight hours on auditions," Zada said. "We'd drive each other to the proper limits of a scene and try to do it 100 different ways just for fun." The auditions were Hal's, and the numbers are conversational. What formed was a habit: preparing with someone beside him.

School went worse. On the _Invisible Boys_ set in Perth in early 2024, he told Variety Australia's Julie Hosking: "I had a lot of stuff going on when I was younger and some schools just don't know how to handle that, and I ended up being kicked out." ([Variety Australia, February 13, 2025](https://au.variety.com/2025/tv/features/invisible-boys-shines-light-on-queer-stories-20235)) THR's writer supplies the rest: expelled from a private school, he re-enrolled at a public one, and in his last four weeks the principal gave him an ultimatum in a meeting with his mother. "I had to come to school every single day, or they couldn't legally finish me. But I had a final callback the next week and I just looked at my mum and I was like, 'I'm leaving.' And she just said, 'Yeah.'" The callback was for _Invisible Boys_. His parents "were very happy with me doing whatever I wanted," so the exit cost him little at home. It was still his call, made against an institution's deadline.

## A name for the whole family

"My original name is Cumpston, which my older brother still goes by," he told Fanning. Zada is his grandmother's surname, and he says he was thinking about switching to it "on my last callback" before his big break. "My grandmother's Australian Indigenous and also Afghan. The name comes from my great-grandfather, who was an Afghan cameleer." He says "our family built the first mosque in Australia," and that he felt "a weird spiritual connection" to the name. Wikipedia records his Afghan-Aboriginal heritage and a Barkandji connection, which fits his account, though the sources disagree on which generation the cameleer belonged to.

To THR he described a plan. "I'm convincing my siblings to change too. We all want to stick together as a family and make films together. It'd be awesome for us to have a production company under the same name." Of the name itself: "there's a power to it."

The name claims his heritage publicly, and a separate credit is practical when a brother works as Cumpston. "A power to it," with a company attached, also sounds like brand-building. What stands out is the family he pictures: siblings under one name, making films together. Even the ancestors come as company: "now I feel like I've got my great-grandfather and my grandmother guiding me as my lucky stars."

## The tape with a drone in it

The Haymitch request reached him while he was shooting _East of Eden_, which filmed in New Zealand from October 2024 to March 2025 ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/East_of_Eden_(2026_miniseries))). They asked for one improvised scene. He told them, "I'll have it to you in three weeks," because "I had a plan to shoot the scenes properly and edit them." By THR's account he spent two weeks writing four scenes, then flew home to Sydney to shoot them with his brother and father. "We found different locations, I got costumes, we even got a drone," he said. Asked by Fanning whether Florence Pugh, who plays Cal's mother, had helped, he said no: "I flew back to Australia from New Zealand just so I could work with my older brother. He helped me, and so did my dad and a couple of friends."

The simplest explanation is production: a director father and an actor brother were the best crew for a tape with locations, costumes and a drone. His phrasing still names a person rather than a camera, and the partner from his teens was back at the center of his preparation, now for his own test. Producer Nina Jacobson said in the casting announcement, "Jo prepared like crazy and stole our hearts." ([RUSSH, November 21, 2025](https://www.russh.com/who-is-joseph-zada/))

The call came at 7 a.m. in Sydney in April 2025, after callbacks in Los Angeles. He told Fanning his first reaction: "I just started swearing, like, 'Now I have to do this and it's going to be really hard.'" Fanning laughed and asked, "You were more scared after finding out?" He said "Yes." Then he found his younger sister upstairs: "we jumped up and down and cried together." The swearing was a joke, told more than a year later to a friendly co-star after the film was safely shot.

## Afraid of the people he admires

In July 2025, in Berlin before _Sunrise on the Reaping_ began, he told his friend and _We Were Liars_ co-star Esther McGregor what had scared him about _East of Eden_: "it wasn't so much the legacy of the story that scared me; it was the cast and the filmmakers. These are people I really admire. I was just afraid of how they would perceive me." Then: "But I got over that quickly." ([Behind The Blinds, July 9, 2025](https://www.behindtheblinds.be/love/sharing-the-ride)) On its own, that fear belongs to Threes as much as Sixes, and to most newcomers.

The two famous predecessors got different treatment. Zoe Kazan, _East of Eden_'s showrunner, asked him at his first callback to promise not to watch the 1955 film, and by his account to THR she "begged me not to watch the original film... She said it so many times." He agreed, and waited about eight months, until the series wrapped.

Harrelson he studied first. "For about two straight months, I became obsessed with getting Woody down," he told THR. Then he set the study aside: "The challenge I set for myself was to be the opposite of Woody's character — make him happy, optimistic, caring — and then find a way to get to where he would eventually wind up." While shooting he kept Harrelson out of his head, he told Fanning: "For my nerves and for me to really attack it, I had to not think about that at all." They met only after the film was shot, and "I had his blessing," he told Fanning.

The counterevidence belongs here too. After _East of Eden_, he told Flaunt his confidence had grown: "I just learned that these people are actually normal people and they're doing the same thing that I'm doing. I have it in me to go toe to toe with Florence [Pugh], I think. I feel like I can go toe to toe with anyone now." That is a young actor finding his footing, and a reader could fairly stop there. The mechanism he names is still worth noticing. His confidence arrived once the people he feared had been reclassified as peers.

The reclassification did not hold. Asked by Fanning in June 2026, after both shoots, whether any actors were on his bucket list, he answered: "I love the actors I love, but I'm shit scared of working with them." Fanning laughed. Of Pugh: "She's scary." Then: "It was very daunting, but she's so friendly and makes everyone feel so comfortable." The fear was back eleven months after Behind The Blinds, paired with what settles it: being made comfortable.

That leaves the two lines from the October profile side by side. "My own shoes" is a claim about the work, and two months of study followed by a deliberate departure gives him some right to it. The blank at dinner is about the man. The Six reading explains the second far better than the first, and this profile leaves the first standing.

## What settles him

When Zada explains how nerves resolve, specific people usually become reliable. Of the first weeks of _We Were Liars_, he told McGregor: "I worked myself into a state of so much anxiety and put too much pressure on myself. But by the time I got into the flow, I found my footing." He credited "being more confident in myself, and in the people around me." In Berlin he said he was "phenomenally nervous," then offered his usual fix: "like every other project I've done, I think once I step on set and meet everyone, I'll be fine."

Of Glenn Close, he told People in July 2026: "It means so much to have someone of such stature support you, and Glenn in particular made it clear she was always there for me." ([People via Yahoo, July 7, 2026](https://www.yahoo.com/entertainment/movies/articles/joseph-zada-teases-young-haymitch-110000632.html)) Of his girlfriend, he told THR, "she's helped me relax and not think about work so much."

Company works the same way. Asked by McGregor for her favorite memory of the two of them, he described nightly hangouts in Halifax and gave a reason: "I feel both of us get lonely; we need someone around." On the _Sunrise_ shoot in Spain, Mckenna Grace, who plays Maysilee Donner, made him come to dinner with her mother every night when he was too tired from stunts. He told her he was grateful for "being forced to spend family time so I wasn't chilling in my own head and going crazy by myself." The family was Grace's, assembled on location ([HERO, March 23, 2026](https://hero-magazine.com/article/294797/inside-heroine-24-mckenna-grace-in-conversation-with-joseph-zada)).

His own family appears at the edge of big jobs. From Berlin he said: "I'm here with my older brother, and I just brought over my dad and my little brother. We're going to Portugal tomorrow for the weekend, which will be really fun, just a little boys' trip right before I jump into the deep end with this." A first large pay cheque explains a family holiday as well as any motive does, but with the flight home it makes two cases of family gathered at his own threshold.

Preparation is the other material he builds footing from, and he tends to recruit people into it. Auditioning for _We Were Liars_ in the middle of the _Invisible Boys_ shoot, he was "secretly getting other people in the cast to read it with me, which is probably not very appropriate, but man's got to work," he told [Polyester](https://www.polyesterzine.com/features/joseph-zada-on-the-hunger-games-playing-tennis-and-becoming-johnny-sinclairnbsp). Grace told him "you kept the most intricate notes." For _Invisible Boys_ he read the source novel twice, then, "once I felt confident in that, I focused fully on our version of the story as it was written in the script" ([Star Observer, February 23, 2025](https://www.starobserver.com.au/news/invisible-boys-cast-talk-sex-scenes-sexuality-and-being-seen/235479)). Flaunt's writer summarized his view: if he "just puts all of himself into his work... then he has absolutely nothing to worry about."

The novel and Harrelson follow the same arc: absorb everything, then let go once the camera rolls. Preparation seems to buy a confidence he can trust enough to release. That is also taught craft, and promotional interviews reward gratitude, so most of this is consistent with a Six reading rather than proof of it. Press style does not explain the flight home, or a fear of admired actors that came back after he said it had passed.

## The case for Type 9

Type 9 is organized around keeping inner and outer peace, often by merging with the people nearby and tuning out what would disturb it. Much of Zada's language fits. He told Flaunt he is "really happy, actually," that "You've just got to roll with it," and that he tries to "block it all out and stay in my own lane." The writer credited him with "an uncommonly zen mentality" and said his joking self-description as "extremely chill" was "all said in jest, but it's not untrue." When Fanning asked, "Who are you when you're not a character, Joe?" he answered: "I'm chilling."

The strongest Nine clue is emotional distance. On the _Invisible Boys_ set, where he played the punk outsider Charlie Roth, Hosking found him "remarkably upbeat for someone who has spent much of the past week in tears." He told her, "I don't tend to wear my emotions on my sleeve like Charlie does," and said the role had "taught me to be vulnerable. I have more access to my emotions, and I think that's been a very big gift." THR adds a teenager who went into acting "kicking and screaming," a girlfriend who helps him switch off, and a laugh at having no next job for the first time in three years: "I've got nothing." On this reading the family trips are nesting, the company is comfort, and the nerves are press idiom over an unruffled person.

Three observations resist it. The first is what settles him. Comfort alone would not separate the types, since a Nine also relaxes in a friendly room, and Zada does tune things out, as he did with Harrelson on set. But when he describes nerves resolving, the fix is usually specific people becoming dependable. That is closer to securing footing than to retreat, though it is a lean rather than a clean split.

The second is disclosure. A Nine tends to play disturbance down. Zada broadcasts it. "I'm so ridiculously nervous," he told [Deadline](https://deadline.com/2025/06/joseph-zada-haymitch-abernathy-hunger-games-update-1236429723/) in June 2025, followed by "phenomenally nervous" in July 2025, "shit scared" in June 2026 and the blank beside Harrelson in October 2026. He reports the alarm, then goes to find his people.

The third is that he holds his own line more than a merging Nine usually does. He told his mother "I'm leaving." He set his own three-week deadline for the Haymitch tape. When the _Invisible Boys_ sound department objected to music playing during a scene, "I begged them to let me play it because I needed it to give me that energy," he told Grace. None of these cost him much, and a Nine can act decisively when the wish and the easy path line up. Together they still describe someone who steers.

He even qualifies his own calm. When Fanning followed up on "I'm chilling," he added: "There hasn't been a whole lot of chill until very recently. But yeah, Australia is a safe place." Chilling is also the word he used for what company protects him from: "chilling in my own head and going crazy by myself." This suggests his easygoing surface and his need for company are more connected than the Nine reading allows. Two uses of a casual word cannot prove it.

## "He just gets what he wants"

A Type 3 reading, organized around achievement and being seen to succeed, gained ground in the October 2026 coverage. Its best evidence starts with his _We Were Liars_ co-star Emily Alyn Lind, who reached him by FaceTime during the Los Angeles callbacks. By her account to People, he said, "You can't tell anyone but it's down to me and a couple [other] people for this _Hunger Games_ thing," and "he's naming names that are really big that he's up against." Her verdict: "He just gets what he wants," and "He is going to be one of these guys." ([THR, relaying People, June 26, 2025](https://www.hollywoodreporter.com/movies/movie-news/joseph-zada-against-big-names-hunger-games-prequel-1236300634/)) Kazan told THR: "He could be the biggest movie star in the world if he decided that's what he wants to be."

His own record adds to it: four written scenes and a drone for a one-scene request, a run of back-to-back jobs since he turned 18, and a dated ambition. "I've already started writing. I want to direct a film before I'm 30, that's my goal," he told McGregor in July 2025. "My own shoes" is a self-definition a Three would recognize. The earliest dated fear in the record also concerns failure rather than people. Before _Invisible Boys_ premiered he told Star Observer: "The only thing that scared me, honestly, was hoping I was doing a good job. I was terrified I'd be dreadful! But I think it's come out all right." That is Three-shaped: a fear of failing, settled by the result.

The Three reading struggles with how he talks about his own ability. "I have a lot of ways of preparing but I'm also very new to the game, so I don't want to set anything in stone right now," he told Polyester in June 2025. Even the directing goal came with a disclaimer: "I'm not saying I'll be good at it, but I want to try." Those hedges are exactly what a well-advised teenager says in his first press cycle opposite Hollywood veterans. If he speaks with settled authority about his method once his work is released, the hedging was career position more than temperament.

The first release week split that test. He described his Haymitch approach to THR with authority, then told [Awards Radar](https://awardsradar.com/2026/10/02/interview-joseph-zada-discusses-east-of-eden/) a day later, "With each project, I have many different methods that I'm trying out." The _Sunrise on the Reaping_ press cycle will be the cleaner test.

The sharper difference is what he reaches for under pressure. A Three would be expected to lean on performance and visible results. Zada's recurring move is to bring trusted people in first, and even the over-delivered tape was made at home with his brother and father. Kazan's line is conditional, too: it places the open question in what he decides he wants. A Six can want something intensely, and this reading accounts for the manner of his pursuit: tracking the competition quietly, confiding in a friend, preparing with family. It does not fully explain the appetite Lind saw, and that tension stays open.

## What the record cannot settle yet

He told Flaunt: "I'm kind of in a weird place right now. I don't want anyone to know anything about me. But now I'm in these situations where I do have to talk about it." He gave the reason himself, and most of his later openness came in interviews he was expected to give. What stays open is how much he volunteers beyond the job, from his family name to his nerves to his mother's wishes, and how he weighs that against his stated wish for privacy.
