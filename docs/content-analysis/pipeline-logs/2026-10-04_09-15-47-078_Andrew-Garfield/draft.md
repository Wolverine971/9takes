In November 2021, [Stephen Colbert](/personality-analysis/stephen-colbert) asked Andrew Garfield how art helped him deal with grief. Garfield's mother, Lynn, had died of pancreatic cancer in 2019. He gave two answers in one breath. The first became the clip that followed him: "I hope this grief stays with me because it's the unexpressed love that I never got to tell her." The second rarely traveled with it. He wanted to honor her "through my art and use it as a way to heal, use it as a way to sew up the wounds because that's what we do." ([The Late Show with Stephen Colbert, November 2021](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=_u_TswLQ4ws); [NBC News, November 23, 2021](https://www.nbcnews.com/pop-culture/pop-culture-news/andrew-garfield-shares-tick-tick-boom-helped-cope-loss-rcna6496))

The first half became his reputation: the actor who cries on talk shows and won't let the wound close. That reading drops half of what he said that night and most of what he has said since. Garfield doesn't describe refusing to heal. He describes walking toward the pain on purpose, because that is where he still finds his mother. And the lesson he says grief taught him is not that he feels more deeply than other people. It is the reverse: "I understood that I wasn't special."

That sentence matters more once you know where he started. As a boy in suburban Surrey, he says, he was "enraged at the concept of this being everything." In September 2026 he told a Jesuit podcast that he wants to "free myself into my ordinariness." The distance between those two sentences is the story of his personality.

Garfield was born in Los Angeles on August 20, 1983, and grew up in Epsom, Surrey, after his family moved to England when he was three. He trained at the Royal Central School of Speech and Drama, played the lead in _The Amazing Spider-Man_ (2012) and its sequel, earned Best Actor Oscar nominations for _Hacksaw Ridge_ and _tick, tick... BOOM!_, and won a Tony Award for _Angels in America_ in 2018. ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Andrew_Garfield)) As of October 2026 he stars in Paul Greengrass's _The Uprising_ (US release September 10, 2026) and plays [Sam Altman](/personality-analysis/sam-altman) in Luca Guadagnino's _Artificial_, which premieres at the New York Film Festival on October 5, 2026. ([Wikipedia: The Uprising](<https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Uprising_(2026_film)>); [Wikipedia: Artificial](<https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Artificial_(upcoming_film)>))

Quotations from Marc Maron's _WTF_ (2022) and Anderson Cooper's _All There Is_ (2024) come from automated transcripts; where print coverage carries the same story, this profile cites it.

## What is Andrew Garfield's personality type?

Andrew Garfield most likely fits Enneagram Type 4, the Individualist, at medium confidence. His recurring move is toward feeling, not away from it: a lifelong longing for "more," a wound he calls "not-enough-ness," and grief as his way back to his mother. Type 9 is the strongest alternative. Type 3 is a serious second.

[Type 4](/enneagram-corner/enneagram-type-4) is organized around identity and meaning: the sense that something essential is missing and has to be found or expressed. Being gentle and emotional doesn't settle the question. Many [Type 9s](/enneagram-corner/enneagram-type-9) are both, and some typing sites file Garfield as a Nine, arguing that "Type 9 individuals seek harmony and avoid conflict." ([Know Your Archetypes](https://knowyourarchetypes.com/andrew-garfield-personality-type/))

What separates the two is where he started and what he does with pain. A Nine tends to begin settled into ordinary life and to soften whatever disturbs it. Garfield describes a boy who raged at ordinary life and a man who walks toward pain deliberately. In his story, ordinariness arrives late, as something grief taught him.

[Type 3](/enneagram-corner/enneagram-type-3) is a real contender too: he admits the pull "to be seen, to be appreciated," and has said an Oscar once meant something to him. Both alternatives get their own section below. This is a reading of public interviews, not access to his private life.

## "This is not a childhood"

Garfield was a competitive gymnast as a boy. In a 2024 _Esquire_ cover story, as relayed by _People_, he remembered a coach sitting on his back to force him into the splits and thinking, "this is not a childhood." He quit at about 12 and called it "the first rebellion against my dad and his value system at the time: success and gold medals above any sense of joy, comfort, or pleasure." ([People via Yahoo, October 2024](https://au.lifestyle.yahoo.com/why-andrew-garfield-gave-competitive-045306162.html)) He had told Marc Maron the same story in 2022, down to "my Russian coaches sitting on my back while I'm in a box split." ([WTF with Marc Maron, August 22, 2022](https://podscripts.co/podcasts/wtf-with-marc-maron-podcast/episode-1359-andrew-garfield))

"Joy, comfort, or pleasure" could be a Nine's list as easily as a Four's, so the quitting alone settles nothing. What he says came next is more telling. His brother, now a lung doctor, was the family's "golden boy," he told Maron, and being the other son left him asking, "who am I? What's the point?" He said the position also "liberated" him. He describes a "Truman show feeling like, is this everything? It can't be everything," and says that while he was never a suicidal kid, "I was enraged at the concept of this being everything." In _Esquire_ he put it more quietly: "I feel there is more. And I can't identify what that more is, but I know it exists, and if it doesn't exist, I am in big trouble."

This is the hinge of the type argument. An overlooked son who goes quiet and keeps the peace is a classic Nine story. Garfield tells a different one: the overlooked son who got angry that ordinary life might be all there is.

First he hid in stories. "Films were really my church," he told the Jesuit magazine _America_ in January 2017. "As a young kid it was movies and books; it was nothing remarkable really, just that is where I felt soothed, that is where I felt most myself…safest." In books and movies, "I was transported into myself, into the vast inner landscape of myself." ([America, January 10, 2017](https://www.americamagazine.org/arts-culture/2017/01/10/andrew-garfield-played-jesuit-silence-he-didnt-expect-fall-love-jesus/)) His mother pushed him to try painting, sculpture and music. "And then I did the last resort, which was join the circus and do an outside of school drama class when I was 15," he told Anderson Cooper. "I felt accepted. I felt like I belonged." ([All There Is with Anderson Cooper, October 8, 2024](https://podscripts.co/podcasts/all-there-is-with-anderson-cooper/andrew-garfields-grief)) A new drama teacher later saw him in a play and, as he told Maron, "basically said, I see you."

Being seen became the thing he most wanted and most feared. Before his first public performance after drama school, as Ophelia at the Globe, he felt "Terror at being seen. Terror at revealing and offering my heart," and thought, "I have nothing to give, I have nothing to offer, I'm a fraud."

## A year of not-enough-ness

To prepare for Martin Scorsese's _Silence_ (2016), in which he plays a Jesuit priest, Garfield spent about a year making the Spiritual Exercises of St. Ignatius with the Jesuit writer James Martin, including a silent retreat at St Beuno's in North Wales with his co-star Adam Driver. By his own account he lost 40 pounds and lived celibate for six months, and the Exercises mattered more to him than the film: "If I hadn't made the film that would've been fine."

What he brought to the retreat is the strongest single piece of evidence for the Four reading, because it came years before his mother's death and had nothing to do with a role. "The main thing that I wanted to heal, that I brought to Jesus, that I brought to the Exercises, was this feeling of not-enough-ness," he told _America_. "This feeling of that forever longing for the perfect expression of this thing that is inside each of us." He called creative work "the beautiful agony of never being able to fully express the possibility of love," and added, "My compulsion to work is this longing to express that very thing."

In the same interview he named the temptation the Three reading depends on: "Where I'm tempted constantly to be producing, to be seen, to be appreciated, etc., I was shown the beauty of living a hidden life, of retreating in order to offer myself in a deeper way to my art, to my life, to the world." The pull toward applause is real, and he says so. The answer he reached for was retreat, not a better performance.

Grief was already part of his vocabulary. "I feel like I've been gifted and cursed with a closeness to some grief…the grief of living…" Then he finished the thought: "…the grief of living in a time and a place where a life of joy and love is f–ing impossible." That was grief about the world, not a bereavement, and it came more than two years before his mother died.

He did not come back fixed. "I was brought to my knees by these Exercises, and yet I sit here before you, struggling with the same s—." With the film's release, "I'm back in Shallow-ville." The sublime and the ridiculous stayed close together: after the silent retreat, he told Graham Norton, he and Driver "spent a three-hour car ride in an outpouring of vile language and imagery. There was a lot of giggling and crying too." ([Irish News, February 3, 2017](https://www.irishnews.com/magazine/2017/02/03/news/andrew-garfield-could-not-stop-swearing-after-silent-retreat-to-prepare-for-role-918651/))

Scorsese took the seriousness as real. "People say, 'Well, anybody wants work,' but not necessarily," he told _TIME_. "Andrew actually wanted to think about all these issues." ([TIME, January 12, 2017](https://time.com/4632661/andrew-garfield-martin-scorsese-silence/))

## Running toward it

Lynn Garfield was ill with pancreatic cancer for about a year and a half. Her son describes her as "a person that felt most herself when she was able to heal, care, nourish and contain others in a gentle way. On her hospice bed, she was more concerned with the nurses than she was with her own pain and discomfort." He loved this in her and also fought it. "I was very angry with her for that a lot of the time," he told Maron. "I just wanted her to take care of herself better."

In public, the grief arrived during the _tick, tick... BOOM!_ press tour. "I love talking about it, by the way, so if I cry, it's only like -- it's only a beautiful thing," he told Colbert, calling it "all the unexpressed love." Two months earlier he had told _Variety_, "The good news about me and her is that we left nothing unsaid." Read together, the grief he describes is not regret over things left unsaid. It is love that outlived the person it was for.

Three years later Cooper played him the Colbert clip and asked whether the grief had stayed. "Yeah, it's here now," Garfield said, "and it's the only route to feeling her close again." He described "being that little boy at the bottom of that empty cave in vast darkness and just kind of crying out that's the only moment that she that she comes." CNN's own summary of the episode quotes him: "The wound is the only route to the gift." He described the method as a choice: "I allowed myself to feel broken. I just allowed myself to be in pain. And I didn't run away from it. I ran towards it and I said, help me."

That October he gave Elmo the same idea in a produced Sesame Workshop segment: missing her "makes me feel close to her." ([Sesame Workshop, October 2024](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=EVlXbiP4x2E))

In Enneagram terms, this is the most telling behavior in the record. Under loss, a Nine usually turns the volume down, and a Three usually gets back to work. Garfield turns toward the absence and treats it as the meeting place. By his account, that is how the healing works for him.

He also carries her longing. At a party the night before the 2017 Oscars, after "a glass and a half of wine, which is a rare occurrence for her," his mother was asked by [Jack Black](/personality-analysis/jack-black) whether her son's talent was nature or nurture. "And my mom goes right up to him and grabs him by the lapels. And she says, it was me. It was all me." Telling Cooper the story, Garfield added, "I do owe her her unmet dreams her the sacrifices she's made her longing." On _CBS Sunday Morning_ in 2021 he said, "I think everything I do is in dedication to her." ([CBS News, November 14, 2021](https://www.cbsnews.com/news/the-amazing-andrew-garfield-tick-tick-boom/))

## "I wasn't special"

The Four story so far has a weak point, and Garfield is the one who names it. Enneagram writers describe the Four's central trap as a sense of being different from everyone else, especially in suffering. In 2022 he described having exactly that feeling, and losing it.

Shortly before his mother died, he told Maron, he was staying with a friend on Fire Island, carrying "this fucking pain that I couldn't move." He went into the ocean, "submerged myself and I suddenly just got this download from the water." Then: "And I understood that I wasn't special. I understood that what I was going through felt incredibly acutely unique. And like no one else had been through this agony before. But for whatever, that wave gave me the information that it had been healing people through grief for the last millennia and beyond."

He had told the story in print to _Vanity Fair_ earlier that year: "It was like I got the full download. It reminded me that sons and daughters have been losing their mothers since the dawn of time. And I had this very unique feeling of loss and grief. I'd just jumped into the club of losing the illusion that the person that gives you life is always going to be alive." ([Vanity Fair, via BANG Showbiz, February 18, 2022](https://uk.news.yahoo.com/andrew-garfield-found-jumping-sea-150000500.html)) He told it again to Cooper in 2024.

Three things stuck with him, he told Maron. "I'm not the victim. She was." There was "nothing unusual about the experience I'm experiencing. I'm not alone in it." And the Jewish blessing "may her memory be a blessing."

This is the most distinctive thing in Garfield's record. Many grieving people feel their loss is unique; fewer describe the feeling so exactly and then call it an illusion. A Nine rarely starts from "no one else had been through this agony." Garfield started there and named the correction.

After that, ordinariness keeps appearing as a goal. In 2017 it was the "hidden life." In April 2022, after his second Oscar nomination, he told _Variety_ he wanted to "just be ordinary for a while," then told _The View_ the next day, "I'm just having a holiday... I'm not retiring." ([E! Online, April 27, 2022](https://www.eonline.com/news/1328746/why-andrew-garfield-is-taking-a-break-from-acting)) In September 2026, on _America_'s _The Spiritual Life_ podcast with James Martin, he said, "The idea that we feel that we have to be more than we are, it's heartbreaking." He called "the mask or the persona that we give to the world" a prison "that I long to free myself of," and said he wants to "free myself into my ordinariness, free myself into my ability to just be a very flawed, a very beautiful… very complicated human being." ([America, September 14, 2026](https://www.americamagazine.org/podcasts/2026/09/14/andrew-garfield-father-james-martin-spiritual-life/))

The Enneagram tradition names the Four's virtue equanimity: steadiness that no longer needs strong feeling as proof of being someone. Garfield's version keeps the feeling and drops the exceptionalism.

## The case for Type 9, and for Type 3

The Nine case deserves more than a footnote. Asked by _W Magazine_ whether he is more cat or dog, Garfield said: "Dog, of course. I have no aloofness. I love affection constantly. I'm a pack animal. I love being with the gang. I'm always looking for treats, warmth, coziness, comfort, and play." ([W Magazine, January 4, 2025](https://www.wmagazine.com/culture/andrew-garfield-we-live-in-time-interview)) It was a playful answer, but "coziness, comfort" is Nine vocabulary, and so is his long attraction to a hidden, ordinary life. Scene partners describe someone who gives himself over to them. [Emma Stone](/personality-analysis/emma-stone) told _Teen Vogue_ in 2011, "Andrew is one of the most giving actors I've ever worked with." ([Yahoo, quoting Teen Vogue](https://www.yahoo.com/entertainment/emma-stone-andrew-garfield-relationship-102400178.html)) Florence Pugh, his _We Live in Time_ co-star, said in 2024, "Whatever he did, I would receive, and whatever I did, he would take." ([Collider, October 19, 2024](https://collider.com/florence-pugh-andrew-garfield-we-live-in-time/))

The Four reading still leads on the discriminator: where he started. The Nine's habit is self-forgetting, settling into what is and resisting disturbance. Garfield's account begins with rage at ordinariness and hunger for "more," and his adult method with pain is to go toward it. A Nine reading has to treat the Surrey rage and "no one else had been through this agony" as noise. A Four reading needs them. Collaborators also report more heat than a Nine usually shows. Lin-Manuel Miranda, who directed him in _tick, tick... BOOM!_, said of seeing him in _Angels in America_: "He brought his joy, and he brought his rage, and I didn't know if he could sing, but I just felt like he could do anything."

The Three case is the one Garfield half-makes himself. His father's value system, as he describes it, put "success and gold medals" first, his brother was the "golden boy," and he admits the pull "to be seen, to be appreciated." On _Chicken Shop Date_ in October 2024, [Amelia Dimoldenberg](/personality-analysis/amelia-dimoldenberg) asked when he would win an Oscar. The automatic captions don't label speakers, but the answer comes from the twice-nominated guest: "that whole thing has meant something to me in the past and I think I needed that." When she said an Academy Award might make him more attractive, the reply was "I get it I get it just like my father." ([Chicken Shop Date, October 18, 2024](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=eFS5vxYlfY8)) His 2026 talk of "the mask or the persona" sounds like a Three's growth language too.

What weakens the Three case is what he does when image and inner truth collide. He quit the medal track at 12, against his father. As _The Amazing Spider-Man_ approached release, he told Maron, he was "scared of this massive film coming out and a gajillion people saying, no, we don't we don't like you. We don't like your soul," and his instinct was to go do "something incredibly soulful challenging with members of my kind of theater tribe." He shows the need and the defeat on camera instead of hiding them. And he told Cooper that when his mother died, "like two thirds of my ambition died with her," then corrected himself: "two-thirds of my previous ambition, or the style, the type, or the feeling of that ambition died." He wants to be "more curious about what we're all doing here rather than narrow and driven and certain." He has kept working, through _We Live in Time_, _After the Hunt_, _The Magic Faraway Tree_ and _The Uprising_, so what changed, by his account, is why he works.

A Type 2 reading leans on his warmth and his language of service; defending his _Angels in America_ remarks in 2017, he said, "My only longing is to serve and to keep the world spinning forward for the LGBTQ community." It runs into his anger at his mother's self-erasing care. A Two tends to idealize that kind of giving. Garfield loved her for it and wanted her to stop.

Earlier readings called him a 4w5, the withdrawn, cerebral Four, partly on quotes read out of context. His admitted pull "to be seen, to be appreciated" points at least as plausibly to a 4w3. This profile leaves the wing open.

One explanation needs no Enneagram at all: the sensitive-man persona as a brand. Most of his grief disclosures land in promotional windows, Colbert during _tick, tick... BOOM!_, then Cooper, Elmo and _Chicken Shop Date_ during _We Live in Time_. But the 2017 _America_ interview, with its "not-enough-ness" and "grief of living," predates the bereavement, and the 2022 Maron conversation was not tied to a release. Publicity explains when he talks about grief. It doesn't explain why he describes it in the same terms across nine years.

## What the type doesn't explain

For most of 2021 Garfield denied, over and over, that he was in _Spider-Man: No Way Home_. Afterward he told TheWrap, "There were moments where I was like, 'God, I hate lying.' I don't like to lie and I'm not a good liar." He also called it "rather stressful but also weirdly enjoyable," "like this massive game of Werewolf," and "thrilling, actually." ([BuzzFeed News, quoting TheWrap](https://www.buzzfeednews.com/article/leylamohammed/andrew-garfield-stressful-lying-about-spider-man-no-way-home)) A tidy Four story would have him tormented by the pretense. The record shows a man who hated it and enjoyed it. [Tom Holland](/personality-analysis/tom-holland), speaking of the scene in which Garfield's Spider-Man catches [Zendaya](/personality-analysis/zendaya)'s MJ, said, "The look on his face... is totally genuine, and I'm really proud of him." ([ComicBook.com, quoting The Hollywood Reporter, February 4, 2022](https://comicbook.com/movies/news/spider-man-no-way-home-tom-holland-regrets-not-calling-andrew-garfield-taking-over-role/))

The same month as the Cooper interview, his _Chicken Shop Date_ set the internet buzzing over the flirting ([CNN via WRAL, October 18, 2024](https://www.wral.com/archive/21679343/)), and in it he told Dimoldenberg, "I'm a very private person in my private life." The man who discusses his mother's death on podcasts calls himself private about the rest.

The hardest moment for the sincere-seeker reading came in July 2017, during the London production of _Angels in America_, in which he played Prior Walter, a gay man living with HIV in Tony Kushner's play about the AIDS crisis. At a Q&A tied to the production, reflecting on what right he had as a straight actor to play the part, he said, "I am a gay man right now just without the physical act -- that's all," and cited his weekly _RuPaul's Drag Race_ nights with friends. Many LGBTQ people on Twitter said he was belittling what it is like to be queer; _Advocate_ contributor Nico Lang wrote, "Straight actors, please stop this nonsense." Garfield told BBC Newsbeat, "That's of course not what I meant at all," and said the discussion "was about this play and how deeply grateful I am that I get to work on something so profound." ([The Advocate, July 18, 2017](https://www.advocate.com/theater/2017/7/18/andrew-garfield-confirms-hes-straight-says-gay-remarks-were-twisted)) The coverage reviewed for this profile shows a defense, not an apology. One reading is that total immersion in a role tipped into claiming an experience he had not lived; another, closer to his own, is that remarks about immersion were stripped of context. Either way, it is the clearest public gap between how deeply he feels his way into something and how that lands with the people who live it.

Smaller loose ends remain. He calls most of his old ambition dead while signing on to big commercial projects, and he says "we left nothing unsaid" while speaking of love "that I never got to tell her." None of this breaks the Four reading. None of it is explained by it either.

## Joining the club

Go back to the line that made him famous for grief: "I hope this grief stays with me." Read alone, it sounds like a man clinging to a wound. Read beside everything else he has said, it sounds like a man who has found the place where his mother can still be reached and keeps going back, while using the work to close what can be closed.

The boy in Surrey wanted proof that life held something more than the ordinary. If the Four reading is right, the man found that "more" in the place the boy feared most: an experience every person eventually shares. "I'd just jumped into the club," he told _Vanity Fair_. For someone who once raged that ordinary life might be everything, the deepest thing he has felt turned out to be the most ordinary thing there is. The ocean, he told Cooper, "had a great answer, a really tremendous answer."
