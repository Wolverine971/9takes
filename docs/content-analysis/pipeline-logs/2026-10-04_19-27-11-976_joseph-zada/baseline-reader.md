"Everyone is like, 'You're stepping into Woody's shoes,'" Joseph Zada told [The Hollywood Reporter](https://www.hollywoodreporter.com/tv/tv-features/joseph-zada-east-of-eden-hunger-games-james-dean-1236716673/) (THR) in a profile published October 1, 2026, the day Netflix released _East of Eden_. "No, I'm not. These are my characters and my movies — not James', not Woody's. I'm stepping into my own shoes."

James is James Dean, who made Cal Trask famous in the 1955 film of _East of Eden_. Zada plays Cal as a teenager, in the last two of the Netflix series' seven episodes, with Florence Pugh in the lead. Woody is Woody Harrelson, and Zada plays young Haymitch Abernathy in _The Hunger Games: Sunrise on the Reaping_, the prequel about the District 12 tribute who grows into the mentor Harrelson played.

The same profile describes the first time Zada met Harrelson, seated beside him at a dinner party some time after the prequel was shot. "We chatted and he asked me questions about myself and told me stories," he said. "But everything I knew about him just went out of my mind. I couldn't think of any questions. I forgot everything I wanted to ask him."

THR's writer heard him "not the least bit haunted by the ghosts of movie stars past." About the roles, he isn't. About the people, he is less settled. Zada, an Australian actor born in 2005, told interviewers in July 2025 and again in June 2026 that actors he admired made him nervous. What settles those nerves is the case for Enneagram Type 6, which in this nine-type model of core motivation is the type organized around security and around who can be relied on.

A limit shapes everything below. Almost every substantial source on Zada is an interview published between February 2025 and October 2026, inside the promotional windows of four projects. Nervousness, gratitude and "the cast became a family" are the house style of those formats, and a newcomer sharing a cast list with Glenn Close and Ralph Fiennes would produce them whatever his type. Few of his choices cost him much, so this reading rests mostly on statements that repeat across years and interviewers, which keeps confidence at medium.

The four projects, in the order he shot them: _Invisible Boys_ (Stan, 2025), a series lead filmed in Perth and Geraldton in early 2024; _We Were Liars_ (Prime Video, 2025), a series lead filmed in Nova Scotia later that year; _East of Eden_ (Netflix, 2026), a two-episode part in a series which filmed in New Zealand from October 2024 to March 2025 ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/East_of_Eden_(2026_miniseries))); and _Sunrise on the Reaping_ (Lionsgate film, 2026), with Zada as Haymitch, shot in Spain and Germany from July to November 2025 alongside Glenn Close and Ralph Fiennes.

## What is Joseph Zada's personality type?

Joseph Zada looks most like Enneagram Type 6, at medium confidence. The fears he names, of being judged by admired actors or of doing badly, are ones a Type 3 shares. The Six case rests on what settles them: people he knows and preparation done in advance. Type 9 fits his calm surface, and Type 3 his appetite.

Nerves about roles this size would fit [Type 6](/enneagram-corner/enneagram-type-6), [Type 3](/enneagram-corner/enneagram-type-3) or [Type 9](/enneagram-corner/enneagram-type-9). The difference lies in what the alarm is about and what settles it. A Six fears the ground giving way and settles once trusted people and structure are in place. A Three fears failing and settles through results. A Nine fears disruption and settles by tuning the pressure out.

Zada's record favors the Six mainly through what settles him. Other Six markers are thin. He does not visibly test whether people can be trusted, and his mixed record with authority, following his showrunner's repeated instruction while walking out on a principal's ultimatum, fits most young actors as well as it fits a Six. For comparison, as of October 2026, 9takes reads [Timothée Chalamet](/personality-analysis/timothee-chalamet) and [Jennifer Lawrence](/personality-analysis/jennifer-lawrence) as Sixes and [Austin Butler](/personality-analysis/austin-butler) as a Nine.

## Kicking and screaming onto the stage

Acting was the household business. His father, Jeremy Cumpston, has worked as a director, actor and doctor, and his mother, Jessica Brentnall, as a film producer ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Joseph_Zada)). "It was never strange for us to act, or to be chucked into a short film for someone's friend," he told Elle Fanning, who plays Effie Trinket in _Sunrise on the Reaping_, for [Interview Magazine](https://www.interviewmagazine.com/film/joseph-zada-elle-fanning-hunger-games) in a conversation recorded June 12, 2026 and published that August. Before he turned 14 he had played Toby in _Bilched_, a 2019 teen comedy his older brother Hal wrote and starred in and their father directed ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hal_Cumpston)). That upbringing explains his access and his ease on a set.

What he resisted at 14 was the stage. According to THR, he was skipping so much school that his father set a condition for letting it continue: help out on a family friend's stage production of _Romeo and Juliet_. "I went into it kicking and screaming," Zada said. "I was not going to get on stage and embarrass myself." Then: "I would go to rehearsals trying to convince myself that I wasn't enjoying it. But the truth was, I was very much enjoying myself."

"It only properly appealed to me at about 15, when I started helping my older brother with auditions when he'd come back from America," he told Fanning. "We'd work up to eight hours on auditions," Zada said. "We'd drive each other to the proper limits of a scene and try to do it 100 different ways just for fun." The numbers are a figure of speech, but the habit was real: preparing with someone beside him.

School went the other way. THR reports that he kept "blowing off classes as he pursued auditions and small roles," was expelled from a private school and re-enrolled at a public one. In his last four weeks, at a meeting with his mother, the principal said he had to attend every day or the school couldn't legally finish him. He had a final callback the next week: "I just looked at my mum and I was like, 'I'm leaving.' And she just said, 'Yeah.'" The callback was for _Invisible Boys_. His parents "were very happy with me doing whatever I wanted," so the exit cost him little at home, but it was his call.

## A name for the whole family

"My original name is Cumpston, which my older brother still goes by," he told Fanning. Zada is his grandmother's surname, and he says he was thinking about switching to it "on my last callback" before his big break. "My grandmother's Australian Indigenous and also Afghan. The name comes from my great-grandfather, who was an Afghan cameleer." Wikipedia records his Afghan-Aboriginal heritage and a Barkandji connection, which fits his account, though the sources disagree on which generation the cameleer belonged to.

To THR he described a plan. "I'm convincing my siblings to change too. We all want to stick together as a family and make films together. It'd be awesome for us to have a production company under the same name." Of the name itself: "there's a power to it."

The name claims his heritage publicly, and a separate credit is practical when a brother works as Cumpston. "A power to it," with a company attached, also sounds like brand-building. What stands out is the family he pictures, and it has precedent: _Bilched_ was a Cumpston production, and in July 2025 he said he and Hal were co-writing a film, "and we're both going to star in it" ([Behind The Blinds, July 9, 2025](https://www.behindtheblinds.be/love/sharing-the-ride)). Even the ancestors come as company: "now I feel like I've got my great-grandfather and my grandmother guiding me as my lucky stars."

## The tape with a drone in it

The Haymitch request reached him while he was shooting _East of Eden_. They asked for one improvised scene, "a monologue to the camera," as he put it to THR. He told them, "I'll have it to you in three weeks," because "I had a plan to shoot the scenes properly and edit them." By THR's account he spent two weeks writing four scenes, then flew home to Sydney to shoot them with his brother and father. "We found different locations, I got costumes, we even got a drone," he said. To Fanning he put it differently: "I flew back to Australia from New Zealand just so I could work with my older brother. He helped me, and so did my dad and a couple of friends."

The simplest explanation is production: a director father and an actor brother were the best crew for a tape with locations, costumes and a drone. His phrasing still names a person rather than a camera, and the partner from his teens was back at the center of his preparation, now for his own test. Producer Nina Jacobson said in the casting announcement, "Jo prepared like crazy and stole our hearts." ([RUSSH, November 21, 2025](https://www.russh.com/who-is-joseph-zada/))

The call came at 7 a.m. in Sydney in April 2025, after callbacks in Los Angeles. "I just started swearing, like, 'Now I have to do this and it's going to be really hard,'" he told Fanning. She laughed and asked, "You were more scared after finding out?" He said "Yes." Then he and his younger sister "jumped up and down and cried together." He told it lightly, more than a year later, to a friendly co-star after the film was safely shot.

## Afraid of the people he admires

In July 2025, in Berlin preparing for _Sunrise on the Reaping_, he told his friend and _We Were Liars_ co-star Esther McGregor what had scared him about _East of Eden_: "it wasn't so much the legacy of the story that scared me; it was the cast and the filmmakers. These are people I really admire. I was just afraid of how they would perceive me." Then: "But I got over that quickly." On its own, that fear belongs to Threes as much as Sixes, and to most newcomers.

He handled the two famous predecessors the same way, keeping each out of his head during the work. Zoe Kazan, who created and ran _East of Eden_ and whose grandfather Elia Kazan directed the 1955 film, "begged me not to watch the original film... She said it so many times," he told THR. He waited about eight months, until the series wrapped. "I didn't necessarily feel pressure until I finished the job and watched the film," he told Fanning. "Then I was like, 'Oh, shit.' [Laughs] That is an incredible, incredible performance from James Dean."

Harrelson he studied first. "For about two straight months, I became obsessed with getting Woody down," he told THR. Then he set the study aside: "The challenge I set for myself was to be the opposite of Woody's character — make him happy, optimistic, caring — and then find a way to get to where he would eventually wind up." Suzanne Collins's novel already takes Haymitch from a hopeful 16-year-old to a broken victor ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sunrise_on_the_Reaping)). While shooting he kept Harrelson out of his head, he told Fanning: "For my nerves and for me to really attack it, I had to not think about that at all." They met only after the film was shot, and "I had his blessing, and he was so, so lovely about the whole process."

The counterevidence belongs here too. After _East of Eden_, he told [Flaunt](https://www.flaunt.com/post/joseph-zada) his confidence had grown: "I just learned that these people are actually normal people and they're doing the same thing that I'm doing. I have it in me to go toe to toe with Florence [Pugh], I think. I feel like I can go toe to toe with anyone now." That is a young actor finding his footing, and a reader could fairly stop there. The mechanism he names is still worth noticing. His confidence arrived once the people he feared had been reclassified as peers.

The reclassification covered the people he had worked with. Asked by Fanning in June 2026 whether any actors were on his bucket list, he answered: "I love the actors I love, but I'm shit scared of working with them." Fanning laughed. "I don't know how excited I'd be as opposed to deathly nervous." Eleven months after the Berlin interview, the idols he had not yet met still frightened him.

"My own shoes" is a claim about the work, and he makes it repeatedly: "I could do whatever the hell I wanted with that character," he told Fanning in June 2026. Two months of study followed by a deliberate departure gives him some right to it. The blank at dinner is about the man, and anyone might have had it. What the Six reading can use is the order of events: both giants stayed offstage until his work was done, and only then came "Oh, shit" and the blank. A Nine's tuning-out would produce the same order. His confidence about the work sits outside the Six pattern, and this analysis leaves it standing.

## What settles him

When Zada explains how his nerves resolve, the fix is rarely a result. It is usually familiarity with a set and its people, built before or early in the work. Finding his footing on _We Were Liars_ after working himself "into a state of so much anxiety," he credited "being more confident in myself, and in the people around me, and being more familiar with what it's like being on a big American-made set." In Berlin, having admitted he was "phenomenally nervous," he described his fix: "I've been in prep mode. I got here early to work with the stunt team, the director, check out the costumes... And like every other project I've done, I think once I step on set and meet everyone, I'll be fine."

For the intimate scenes in _Invisible Boys_, he told [Star Observer](https://www.starobserver.com.au/news/invisible-boys-cast-talk-sex-scenes-sexuality-and-being-seen/235479), "we had rehearsals to map out every movement and make sure we both felt comfortable and safe... by the time we got to set, we knew exactly where we needed to be and how everything would unfold." Of Glenn Close, he told People in July 2026 that she "made it clear she was always there for me" ([People via Yahoo, July 7, 2026](https://www.yahoo.com/entertainment/movies/articles/joseph-zada-teases-young-haymitch-110000632.html)).

Only Close's line is about reliability. The Berlin prep and the rehearsal, which is standard practice for such scenes, are about structure. "Meet everyone" is about familiarity and ease, which a Nine would also seek. So the remedy leans Six, toward structure arranged in advance and people he can count on, but it only leans.

Company works on the same nerves. Of nightly hangouts in Halifax with McGregor during _We Were Liars_, he said: "I feel both of us get lonely; we need someone around." On the _Sunrise_ shoot in Spain, Mckenna Grace, who plays Maysilee Donner, made him come to dinner with her mother every night when he was too tired from stunts, and he thanked her for "being forced to spend family time so I wasn't chilling in my own head and going crazy by myself" ([HERO, March 23, 2026](https://hero-magazine.com/article/294797/inside-heroine-24-mckenna-grace-in-conversation-with-joseph-zada)).

Escaping his own head through company could suggest Type 7, which avoids pain by keeping life bright, and he often turns a named fear upbeat: "I know it's going to be one of the best shoots ever." But facing his idols he expects dread, not excitement, and his fix is preparation and familiar people, not fresh options.

Preparation is the other material, and he recruits people into it. Before working with David Morse on _We Were Liars_, he and his father "watched all of David's films together." For _Invisible Boys_ he read the source novel twice, then, "once I felt confident in that, I focused fully on our version of the story as it was written in the script." The novel and Harrelson follow the same arc: absorb everything, then let go once the camera rolls. Preparation seems to buy a confidence he can trust enough to release. That is also taught craft, so it is consistent with a Six reading rather than proof of it.

## The case for Type 9

Type 9 is organized around keeping inner and outer peace, often by merging with the people nearby and tuning out what would disturb it. Much of Zada's language fits. He told Flaunt he is "really happy, actually," that "You've just got to roll with it," and that he tries to "block it all out and stay in my own lane." The writer said his joking self-description as "extremely chill" was "all said in jest, but it's not untrue." When Fanning asked, "Who are you when you're not a character, Joe?" he answered: "I'm chilling."

The strongest Nine clue is emotional distance. Visiting the _Invisible Boys_ set in Perth in early 2024, where he played the punk outsider Charlie Roth, [Variety Australia](https://au.variety.com/2025/tv/features/invisible-boys-shines-light-on-queer-stories-20235)'s Julie Hosking found him "remarkably upbeat for someone who has spent much of the past week in tears." He told her, "I don't tend to wear my emotions on my sleeve like Charlie does." His press habit fits too. In Berlin he said, "I usually just say, 'I'm really excited.' But honestly, now that it's getting closer, I'm phenomenally nervous," then, within a breath, "I'll be fine." The girlfriend he described to THR in October 2026 "helped me relax and not think about work so much," a Nine's remedy.

Two observations resist it, and neither is clean. Zada does tune things out, as he did with Harrelson on set, but his accounts of nerves resolving more often describe structure arranged in advance than pressure muted. And he holds his own line more than a merging Nine usually does. The clearest case is the Harrelson study: two months copying a famous performance, then a self-set challenge to play its opposite, which the novel licensed but he chose. Smaller cases fit, from "I'm leaving" to his mother to his own three-week deadline for the tape. None of this cost him much, and a Nine can act decisively when the wish and the easy path line up. It leans toward someone who steers.

He even qualifies his own calm. When Fanning followed up on "I'm chilling," he added: "There hasn't been a whole lot of chill until very recently. But yeah, Australia is a safe place." Chilling is also the word he used for what company protects him from: "chilling in my own head and going crazy by myself." This suggests his easygoing surface and his need for company are more connected than the Nine reading allows. Two uses of a casual word cannot prove it.

## "He just gets what he wants"

The best case for Type 3, the type organized around achievement and being seen to succeed, starts with his _We Were Liars_ co-star Emily Alyn Lind. She told People that during the Los Angeles callbacks he confided by FaceTime that he was on a short list, and "he's naming names that are really big that he's up against." Her verdict: "He just gets what he wants." ([THR, relaying People, June 26, 2025](https://www.hollywoodreporter.com/movies/movie-news/joseph-zada-against-big-names-hunger-games-prequel-1236300634/)) Kazan told THR: "He could be the biggest movie star in the world if he decided that's what he wants to be."

His own record adds to it: four written scenes and a drone for a one-scene request, a teenager who skipped class to chase auditions, back-to-back jobs from 18 to 20, and a goal "to direct a film before I'm 30," as he told McGregor in July 2025. So do his fears. Before _Invisible Boys_ premiered he told Star Observer, "I was terrified I'd be dreadful! But I think it's come out all right," a fear of failing settled by the result. The others run the same way, from "embarrass myself" at 14 to reminding himself in 2025 "not to be afraid of whether it's good or not" to "deathly nervous" about his idols in 2026.

In June 2025 he told [Polyester](https://www.polyesterzine.com/features/joseph-zada-on-the-hunger-games-playing-tennis-and-becoming-johnny-sinclairnbsp): "I'm also very new to the game, so I don't want to set anything in stone right now. I'm young, I want to be able to experiment with a bunch of different ways and a bunch of different processes." Those hedges sit badly with a Three's projected competence, but he gave his own reason for them, and in October 2026 he still told [Awards Radar](https://awardsradar.com/2026/10/02/interview-joseph-zada-discusses-east-of-eden/), "With each project, I have many different methods that I'm trying out."

The remedy is where the Three reading weakens: his accounts of nerves resolving rarely run through results. The over-delivered tape does not decide it: it is the record's clearest act of performing to win, made with the best crew he had. Kazan's line is conditional, too, placing the open question in what he decides he wants. This reading accounts for the manner of his pursuit, tracking the competition quietly and preparing with other people. It does not fully explain the appetite Lind saw, and that tension stays open.

## What the record cannot settle yet

As of October 4, 2026, his released series leads were _Invisible Boys_, which premiered on Stan on February 13, 2025, and _We Were Liars_, released on Prime Video on June 18, 2025, which has filmed a second season. _East of Eden_ premiered on Netflix on October 1, 2026, and _Sunrise on the Reaping_ is scheduled for US theaters on November 20, 2026 ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Hunger_Games:_Sunrise_on_the_Reaping)).

In October 2026, THR reported that he planned to move to Los Angeles early in 2027. "My reps are eager for me to move," he said, but L.A. is also his own wish: "It's got literally everything I could possibly want." In June 2026 he had called Australia "a safe place" while telling Fanning he wanted to live in Los Angeles, New York or London anyway, "so I can watch football every day," as his older brother does, though his mother would rather he stayed home.

As of October 2026, the move and the November release were the next tests of this reading. If acclaim visibly settles him and he credits results rather than people and preparation, the Three reading gains. If he lets the next role and the next city choose him, the Nine reading gains. Leaving the place he calls safe already complicates the simplest Six picture. The Six reading predicts that fame will not be what steadies him, and that before the next big test he will again build footing from people he trusts and preparation done in advance.
