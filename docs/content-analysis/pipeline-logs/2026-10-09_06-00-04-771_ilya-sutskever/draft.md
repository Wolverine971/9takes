In a deposition on October 1, 2025, for [Elon Musk](/personality-analysis/elon-musk)'s lawsuit against OpenAI, a lawyer for OpenAI walked Ilya Sutskever through the memo he had written for OpenAI's board two years earlier. One line said that [Greg Brockman](/personality-analysis/greg-brockman) "was essentially fired from Stripe." What was the basis for that? "Mira told me." Did you seek to verify it with Greg? "No." Why not? "It didn't occur to me." ([Sutskever deposition, Musk v. Altman, October 1, 2025](https://storage.courtlistener.com/recap/gov.uscourts.cand.433688/gov.uscourts.cand.433688.340.1.pdf))

Geoffrey Hinton tells a story about the first time he met him. Hinton was programming in his University of Toronto office when there was "an urgent knock" on the door. A young student said he was cooking fries over the summer but would rather be working in Hinton's lab. Make an appointment, Hinton said. "How about now." Hinton handed him the Nature paper on backpropagation. A week later the student came back and said he hadn't understood it, and Hinton was disappointed: "it's only the chain rule." "Oh no, no, I understood that," the student said. "I just don't understand why you don't give the gradient to a sensible function optimizer," a question Hinton says "took us quite a few years to think about." ([Geoffrey Hinton, Sana AI Summit, 2024](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=4W5askrV-wY))

The teenager who would not take backpropagation on authority grew into the man who built the most consequential case of his life from someone else's screenshots and did not think to check them. That gap is the subject of this profile.

Sutskever was born in 1986 in what was then Gorky, in the Soviet Union, grew up in Jerusalem from about age five, and emigrated to Canada at 16. The University of Toronto admitted him out of Grade 11; he stayed for a mathematics degree in 2005 and a master's and PhD under Hinton ([U of T Magazine, September 28, 2022](https://magazine.utoronto.ca/people/alumni-donors/heard-it-through-the-ai-openai-ilya-sutskever/); [U of T News, June 6, 2025](https://www.utoronto.ca/news/ilya-sutskever-leader-ai-and-its-responsible-development-receives-u-t-honorary-degree)). He co-founded OpenAI in 2015 and became its chief scientist, voted to fire [Sam Altman](/personality-analysis/sam-altman) on November 17, 2023, reversed within days, left in May 2024 and founded Safe Superintelligence Inc., which he has run as CEO since July 2025. People are searching for him now for two reasons. On May 11, 2026 he testified in the Musk trial and disclosed an OpenAI stake worth about $7 billion ([Reuters, May 11, 2026](https://wtvbam.com/2026/05/11/former-openai-executive-sutskever-discloses-nearly-7-billion-stake-in-ai-firm/)). And Luca Guadagnino's film _Artificial_, reviewed out of the New York Film Festival on October 5, 2026, has Yura Borisov playing him as "an eccentric, eager, prodigy incapable of lying" ([TheWrap, October 5, 2026](https://thewrap.com/creative-content/movies/artificial-review-new-york-film-festival)). The film is a screenwriter's construction. The record is more specific, and stranger.

## What is Ilya Sutskever's personality type?

Ilya Sutskever most likely fits Enneagram Type 5, the Investigator, at medium confidence. His costliest choices protect a private, self-directed pursuit of one question, how learning works, and the conditions to think it through without intrusion. Type 6, the Loyal Skeptic, is the strongest alternative, and Type 1, the Reformer, is a credible third.

[Type 5](/enneagram-corner/enneagram-type-5) would rather have the answer than the room. It spends its attention on one problem, keeps other people at a distance until that problem is solved, and counts intrusion as the main cost of living. Sutskever's version is unusually pure about the machine and unusually thin about people. A [Type 6](/enneagram-corner/enneagram-type-6) reading explains much of the same record through vigilance about who holds power and a need for trusted allies. A [Type 1](/enneagram-corner/enneagram-type-1) reading fits his moral vocabulary and his taste for a single uncompromised goal. The question that separates the Five from the Six is practical: when the threat stopped being abstract, did he move toward people or away from them?

This is a reading of the public record. It claims no access to his mind and makes no clinical judgment. The most damaging material here consists of allegations he made against Altman, which were never adjudicated, and anonymous accounts gathered during a factional fight. Both are labeled as such.

## "How about now"

Hinton's other memories run the same way. "His raw intuitions about things were always very good," he says, and "he always thought for himself." Years before the field agreed, "Ilya was always preaching that you just make it bigger and it'll work better, and I always thought that was a bit of a copout." ([Hinton, Sana AI Summit](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=4W5askrV-wY)) [Dario Amodei](/personality-analysis/dario-amodei) dates his own conversion to the scaling hypothesis to a sentence Sutskever said to him around 2016 or 2017: "The thing you need to understand about these models is they just want to learn." ([Lex Fridman Podcast, November 11, 2024](https://lexfridman.com/dario-amodei-transcript/)) Sutskever's version, in October 2023: "I had one very explicit belief, which is: one doesn't bet against deep learning," and "a big fraction of my work can be explained by that." ([MIT Technology Review, October 26, 2023](https://www.technologyreview.com/2023/10/26/1082398/exclusive-ilya-sutskever-openais-chief-scientist-on-his-hopes-and-fears-for-the-future-of-ai/))

The question underneath has not changed in twenty years. To his alumni magazine in 2022 he recalled thinking as a teenager "a lot about the nature of existence and consciousness," and feeling "very strongly that learning was this mysterious thing: humans clearly learn, computers clearly don't." ([U of T Magazine](https://magazine.utoronto.ca/people/alumni-donors/heard-it-through-the-ai-openai-ilya-sutskever/)) To [Jensen Huang](/personality-analysis/jensen-huang) in March 2023 his starting point was "curiosity about what is consciousness." ([NVIDIA GTC fireside chat, March 2023](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=I6qQinoY9WM)) On camera for the documentary _iHuman_, asked what inspires him: "I like thinking about the very fundamentals, the basics." Then: "What is learning? What is experience? What is thinking?" ([The Guardian, iHuman footage, November 2, 2023](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=9iqn1HhFJ6c)) Three audiences, one question. Origin stories get polished. What these establish is what he says drew him, held steady across a career.

## "Even more real, perhaps, than you are"

Two writers who sat with him a year apart saw the same person. The alumni magazine in 2022 called him "a fairly private, work-oriented man" and noticed that when he spoke about theory, "his eyes go over your head, as he moves into a private space where the ideas are real to him," adding: "even more real, perhaps, than you are." ([U of T Magazine](https://magazine.utoronto.ca/people/alumni-donors/heard-it-through-the-ai-openai-ilya-sutskever/)) MIT Technology Review in 2023 found him "deliberate and methodical when he talks," with "long pauses when he thinks about what he wants to say and how to say it, turning questions over like puzzles he needs to solve." "He does not seem interested in talking about himself." His own account: "I lead a very simple life," he said. "I go to work; then I go home. I don't do much else. There are a lot of social activities one could engage in, lots of events one could go to. Which I don't." ([MIT Technology Review](https://www.technologyreview.com/2023/10/26/1082398/exclusive-ilya-sutskever-openais-chief-scientist-on-his-hopes-and-fears-for-the-future-of-ai/))

Both profiles were written when he was the busiest research scientist in the world, and plenty of researchers describe their lives this way whatever their temperament. What makes it count is that the pattern held in the moments where a social instinct would have mattered most.

## Three weeks after "Open"

He joined OpenAI after a 2015 dinner in Palo Alto with Altman, Musk and Brockman. "We just spoke and we vibed and we continued the conversation. Two days after the dinner I emailed Sam to say, I'd like to be involved if you're interested." ([U of T Magazine](https://magazine.utoronto.ca/people/alumni-donors/heard-it-through-the-ai-openai-ilya-sutskever/)) He turned down a $6 million a year offer from Google to do it, and Brockman later said the two of them were "joined at the hip." ([Wired, May 12, 2026](https://web.archive.org/web/2026id_/https://dnyuz.com/2026/05/12/ilya-sutskever-stands-by-his-role-in-sam-altmans-openai-ouster-i-didnt-want-it-to-be-destroyed/))

On January 2, 2016, three weeks after the founding, he wrote to Musk, Altman and Brockman: "As we get closer to building AI, it will make sense to start being less open. The Open in openAI means that everyone should benefit from the fruits of AI after its built, but it's totally OK to not share the science (even though sharing everything is definitely the right strategy in the short and possibly medium term for recruitment purposes)." Musk replied: "Yup." ([OpenAI email exhibits compiled on LessWrong](https://www.lesswrong.com/posts/5jjk4CDnj9tA7ugxr/openai-email-archives-from-musk-v-altman-and-openai-blog)) OpenAI published the exchange in 2024 to defend itself against Musk. It still records an instinct, nine years before his sealed lab: once knowledge becomes powerful, hold it.

The second email is harder for the Type 5 reading, and it belongs here. On September 20, 2017, during the fight over whether Musk would control a for-profit OpenAI, an email signed "Greg & Ilya" went from Sutskever's account to Musk and Altman. It opened by admitting that the two had withheld their concerns: "we were afraid of harming the relationship, having you think less of us, or losing you as partners." It told Musk "it is a bad idea to create a structure where you could become a dictator if you chose to." It told Altman: "we haven't been able to fully trust your judgements throughout this process, because we don't understand your cost function," and "We don't understand why the CEO title is so important to you. Your stated reasons have changed." Nine minutes later Musk wrote back: "Guys, I've had enough. This is the final straw." ([email exhibits](https://www.lesswrong.com/posts/5jjk4CDnj9tA7ugxr/openai-email-archives-from-musk-v-altman-and-openai-blog)) Two authors share these sentences, so none can be assigned to Sutskever alone. But the fear of losing partners and the distrust of a leader's shifting reasons are the language of a [Type 6](/enneagram-corner/enneagram-type-6), and six years later the same two concerns, control and candor, drove the firing.

His picture of the danger was already fixed on power rather than malice. On camera between 2016 and 2019: "I think AI has the potential to create infinitely stable dictatorships." A superintelligence would not "actively hate humans," but "it is going to be too powerful." Then, flatly: "The future is going to be good for the AI regardless. It would be nice if it were good for humans as well." ([The Guardian, iHuman footage](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=9iqn1HhFJ6c)) The frame survived the decade. In November 2025: "The whole problem is the power. The whole problem is the power." ([Dwarkesh Podcast, November 25, 2025](https://www.dwarkesh.com/p/ilya-sutskever-2))

## Feel the AGI

Here is the behavior the type does not neatly explain. Two days after the firing, The Atlantic reported that Sutskever "began to behave like a spiritual leader, three employees who worked with him told us." His refrain was "feel the AGI." At OpenAI's 2022 holiday party at the California Academy of Sciences he "led employees in a chant: 'Feel the AGI! Feel the AGI!'" At a 2023 leadership offsite, according to two people, he commissioned a wooden effigy representing an "unaligned" AI and "set it on fire to symbolize OpenAI's commitment to its founding principles." Hinton, named, told the same reporters that rapid progress had made him "more confident that AGI would arrive soon and thus more focused on preventing its possible dangers." Sutskever did not respond to a request for comment. ([The Atlantic, November 19, 2023](https://web.archive.org/web/2023id_/https://www.theatlantic.com/technology/archive/2023/11/sam-altman-open-ai-chatgpt-chaos/676050/)) In 2025 Fortune, relaying The Atlantic's excerpt of Karen Hao's book _Empire of AI_ and its anonymous sources, reported that he told colleagues in 2023, "We're definitely going to build a bunker before we release AGI," adding, "Of course, it's going to be optional whether you want to get into the bunker." Neither OpenAI nor Sutskever responded. ([Fortune, May 20, 2025](https://fortune.com/2025/05/20/chatgpt-openai-ilya-sutskever-chief-scientist-planneddoomsday-bunker-agi)) That account is third-hand.

Fives rarely lead chants. The employee sources were anonymous and speaking during a factional fight, and ritual in a lab whose product is belief may be management craft rather than temperament. But the chant also had a private meaning he later supplied himself. He told Dwarkesh Patel in 2025 that "in practice, it's very hard to feel the AGI. It's very hard to feel the AGI. We can talk about it, but imagine having a conversation about how it is like to be old when you're old and frail." ([Dwarkesh Podcast](https://www.dwarkesh.com/p/ilya-sutskever-2)) To graduates in June 2025 he said the coming change is "very very difficult to internalize and to really believe on an emotional level. Even I struggle with it." ([U of T convocation address, June 6, 2025](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=zuZ2zaotrJs)) Read that way, the man leading the chant was trying to drag an abstraction into feeling, starting with himself.

He has never been cold about the machine. Two weeks before the board vote, asked for the most surprising thing in his career: "the fact that when I speak to it, I feel understood." ([No Priors, November 2, 2023](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Ft0gTO2K85A)) It was the people in the room he did not model.

## A year of considering

His sworn account of November 2023 is the most reliable part of the record, and the most revealing. He had been considering Altman's removal, he testified, for "At least a year." Planning? "'Planning' is the wrong word because it didn't seem feasible." At trial, by Reuters' account, he said he had "spent about a year gathering evidence" for the board. ([deposition](https://storage.courtlistener.com/recap/gov.uscourts.cand.433688/gov.uscourts.cand.433688.340.1.pdf); [Reuters](https://wtvbam.com/2026/05/11/former-openai-executive-sutskever-discloses-nearly-7-billion-stake-in-ai-firm/))

When the independent directors, "most likely Adam D'Angelo," asked him for material, he wrote a memo whose first page read: "Sam exhibits a consistent pattern of lying, undermining his execs, and pitting his execs against one another." That was clearly his view at the time? "Correct." What action was appropriate? "Termination." He sent it to the three independent directors through a disappearing link and not to Altman, "Because I felt that, had he become aware of these discussions, he would just find a way to make them disappear." The disappearing link was "Because I was worried that those memos will somehow leak," a "generalized concern." He wrote a second memo about Brockman. The screenshots, "most or all," came from [Mira Murati](/personality-analysis/mira-murati): "I fully believed the information that Mira was giving me." On one allegation: "In hindsight, I realize that I didn't know it. But back then, I thought I knew it. But I knew it through secondhand knowledge." Asked whether he had since learned facts that changed his view of it, he answered: "No." What had changed, he said, was method: "I've learned the critical importance of firsthand knowledge for matters like this." ([deposition](https://storage.courtlistener.com/recap/gov.uscourts.cand.433688/gov.uscourts.cand.433688.340.1.pdf))

These are allegations. Altman was reinstated within days and has led OpenAI since, the memo's claims were never tested in court, and Musk's case was dismissed on May 18, 2026 as time-barred, with Musk saying he would appeal ([Al Jazeera, May 19, 2026](https://www.aljazeera.com/news/2026/5/19/musk-vs-altman-what-to-know-about-the-openai-verdict)). Sutskever has not retreated from them. At trial he said an "environment where executives don't have the correct information" is not "conducive to reach any grand goal." ([Wired](https://web.archive.org/web/2026id_/https://dnyuz.com/2026/05/12/ilya-sutskever-stands-by-his-role-in-sam-altmans-openai-ouster-i-didnt-want-it-to-be-destroyed/))

OpenAI's lawyer read him an account that he "had expected the employees of OpenAI to cheer." "I had not expected them to cheer, but I have not expected them to feel strongly either way." ([deposition](https://storage.courtlistener.com/recap/gov.uscourts.cand.433688/gov.uscourts.cand.433688.340.1.pdf)) Reuters reported his testimony that he "avoided the internet for most of the weekend after the board vote" and was not initially aware of Microsoft's plan to absorb Altman, Brockman and staff. Within days, by the same account, he was worried the company could collapse ([Reuters](https://wtvbam.com/2026/05/11/former-openai-executive-sutskever-discloses-nearly-7-billion-stake-in-ai-firm/)). When Anthropic's leaders joined a call about merging with OpenAI, he was "very unhappy about it." Why? "Because I really did not want OpenAI to merge with Anthropic." Why not? "I just didn't want to." ([deposition](https://storage.courtlistener.com/recap/gov.uscourts.cand.433688/gov.uscourts.cand.433688.340.1.pdf))

On November 20 he posted: "I deeply regret my participation in the board's actions. I never intended to harm OpenAI. I love everything we've built together and I will do everything I can to reunite the company." ([post on X, November 20, 2023](https://techstartups.com/2023/11/20/i-deeply-regret-my-participation-in-the-boards-actions-chatgpt-4-creator-ilya-sutskever-says/)) Two days later: "There exists no sentence in any language that conveys how happy I am." ([Fortune, November 25, 2023](https://fortune.com/2023/11/25/elon-musk-what-scared-ilya-sutskever-openai-sam-altman-return/)) In 2026 he explained the reversal not as a change of mind about Altman but as protection. "I felt a great deal of ownership of OpenAI," he said. "I felt like I put my life into it, and I simply cared for it, and I didn't want it to be destroyed." ([Wired](https://web.archive.org/web/2026id_/https://dnyuz.com/2026/05/12/ilya-sutskever-stands-by-his-role-in-sam-altmans-openai-ouster-i-didnt-want-it-to-be-destroyed/)) Hinton, on the day his Nobel Prize was announced, said: "I'm particularly proud of the fact that one of my students fired Sam Altman. And I think I better leave it there." ([Fortune, October 9, 2024](https://fortune.com/2024/10/09/openai-sam-altman-geoffrey-hinton-nobel-prize-physics-ilya-sutskever-toronto)) That is Hinton's reading, a safety act. Sutskever's own account centers on candor, and his retreat on attachment.

## If he is a Six

The rival case deserves its strongest form. A [Type 6](/enneagram-corner/enneagram-type-6) is organized around security: scanning for threat, testing authority, binding to trusted people. Read that way, the 2017 email is the key document, with its fear of "losing you as partners" and its inability to trust a leader whose "stated reasons have changed." The worry that runs from "AGI dictatorship" in 2017 through "infinitely stable dictatorships" to "The whole problem is the power" is a Six's lifelong question: who holds it? A year of quietly gathering evidence against a leader he distrusted, reliance on one trusted ally's reports, a strike followed within three days by retreat and regret, rituals that make colleagues share a belief and a fear, and his 2025 prediction about AI companies once the AI "will start to feel powerful" all fit: "They'll become much more paranoid." ([Dwarkesh Podcast](https://www.dwarkesh.com/p/ilya-sutskever-2)) So does his warmth toward mentors: having Hinton at his university, he told graduates, was "one of my life's great strokes of luck." ([U of T convocation address](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=zuZ2zaotrJs))

Type 1 has a case too. Musk, by then Altman's adversary and a competitor, told the New York Times DealBook summit in late 2023: "I think Ilya actually has a strong moral compass." He added: "He really sweats it over questions of what is right." ([Fortune, December 9, 2023](https://fortune.com/2023/12/09/elon-musk-says-openai-ilya-sutskever-should-join-xai-or-tesla/)) The memo is a moral indictment, his lab is built around "one goal and one product," and his research taste is a doctrine of correctness: "Ugliness, there's no room for ugliness. It's beauty, simplicity, elegance, correct inspiration from the brain." ([Dwarkesh Podcast](https://www.dwarkesh.com/p/ilya-sutskever-2))

## What he did when it was real

The test is what a person does when the threat stops being abstract. A Six under real danger moves toward people and structures: lines up allies, checks the facts with several sources, anticipates how the group will react, looks for a protector. A Five withdraws into the private mind and treats the social world as a variable not yet computed.

His sworn account sits on the second side. A year of "considering" without planning. A case built from one colleague's screenshots, unverified because "It didn't occur to me," and sent by disappearing link rather than confrontation. Colleagues he expected not to "feel strongly either way," and a weekend off the internet while they decided otherwise. Then quiet: his May 14, 2024 departure post was "his first tweet in over five months," and he had been absent from the company's major launches. ([Gizmodo, May 14, 2024](https://gizmodo.com/ilya-sutskever-leaves-openai-1851477658)) He did not seek a new protector or a new alliance. He built a sealed lab and turned down a buyer.

The reversal is the Six reading's best moment, and his own explanation undercuts it. He did not say he had been wrong about Altman; at trial he still defended the firing. He said "I simply cared for it" and, about the merger, "I just didn't want to." That is attachment to the thing he built, once the cost became concrete and visible, which a Five accommodates. His stated source of persistence points the same way. "One thing that guides me personally is an aesthetic of how AI should be, by thinking about how people are, but thinking correctly." And: "The top-down belief is the thing that sustains you when the experiments contradict you." ([Dwarkesh Podcast](https://www.dwarkesh.com/p/ilya-sutskever-2)) Not allies, not preparation, not approval: an inner conviction. Hinton's first memory of him is a student who would not accept backpropagation on authority.

The One reading strains on the same facts. A reformer reverses on principle; he reversed on care. He is at ease saying he does not know: "being humble in the face of the uncertainty seems to be also a part of this whole happiness thing," in 2020 ([Lex Fridman Podcast, May 8, 2020](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=13CZPWmke6A)); "I don't feel confident answering questions like this," at NeurIPS in December 2024 ([NeurIPS 2024 Test of Time talk](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=1yvBqasHLZs)); and about misjudging ChatGPT, "what the hell, it is true." ([MIT Technology Review](https://www.technologyreview.com/2023/10/26/1082398/exclusive-ilya-sutskever-openais-chief-scientist-on-his-hopes-and-fears-for-the-future-of-ai/)) Nothing in the record shows the resentment or daily rigidity a One usually leaves behind.

The limits are real. A phobic Six can also freeze and go quiet. Accepting a stack of secondhand screenshots whole from one trusted colleague looks like loyalty as much as detachment. The deposition answers were given under objection and may be legally minimal. The record favors Five. It does not eliminate Six, which is why this profile holds the type at medium confidence.

## Scale in peace

"After almost a decade, I have made the decision to leave OpenAI," he posted on May 14, 2024, promising "a project that is very personally meaningful to me." Altman called him "easily one of the greatest minds of our generation, a guiding light of our field, and a dear friend," and added that "his warmth and compassion are less well known but no less" important. ([Gizmodo](https://gizmodo.com/ilya-sutskever-leaves-openai-1851477658)) Altman is the only named colleague on record describing his warmth, and he is the man Sutskever voted to fire.

The founding statement of Safe Superintelligence Inc., dated June 19, 2024, promised "the world's first straight-shot SSI lab, with one goal and one product: a safe superintelligence," with "no distraction by management overhead or product cycles." Its reasoning ends: "This way, we can scale in peace." ([SSI founding statement](https://ssi.inc/)) To Reuters that September: "Our first product will be the safe superintelligence." ([Reuters, September 5, 2024](https://srnnews.com/ilya-sutskever-on-how-ai-will-change-and-his-new-startup-safe-superintelligence/)) It is the 2016 email built into a company: a sealed room for one idea, with no product, no papers and no customers until it is done.

The room held under pressure. In July 2025 his co-founder and CEO Daniel Gross left and Sutskever took the title: "You might have heard rumors of companies looking to acquire us. We are flattered by their attention but are focused on seeing our work through." ([TechCrunch, July 3, 2025](https://techcrunch.com/2025/07/03/ilya-sutskever-will-lead-safe-superintelligence-following-his-ceos-exit)) In November he named the suitor: "we were fundraising at a $32 billion valuation, and then Meta came in and offered to acquire us, and I said no. But my former cofounder in some sense said yes." Asked about one of his research ideas, he declined: "unfortunately, we live in a world where not all machine learning ideas are discussed freely, and this is one of them." SSI, he said, is "an attempt to be a voice and a participant." ([Dwarkesh Podcast](https://www.dwarkesh.com/p/ilya-sutskever-2)) In July 2026 NVIDIA announced a "substantial investment" and access to its Vera Rubin systems that would let SSI "increase its compute by an order of magnitude." His line: "We have research that is worthy of scaling up." ([NVIDIA, July 27, 2026](https://nvidianews.nvidia.com/news/ilya-sutskevers-safe-superintelligence-inc-and-nvidia-announce-long-term-strategic-partnership)) TIME, naming him to its 2026 list, noted that he rarely discusses SSI in public and reported the investment at $5 billion, a figure NVIDIA has not confirmed ([TIME100 AI, August 27, 2026](https://time.com/collection/time100-ai/2026/ilya-sutskever/)). Secrecy is standard at frontier labs and doubles as a fundraising story. A refused exit at a $32 billion valuation, at the cost of a co-founder, is not standard.

## Constant struggle

At his alma mater on June 6, 2025, he offered graduates one piece of advice: "to accept reality as it is and to try not to regret the past and try to improve the situation." Then he stopped. "But it's hard. It's hard. It's a constant struggle with one's emotion. And that's why I mention it to you." A moment later: "And also a reminder for myself. Constant struggle." ([U of T convocation address](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=zuZ2zaotrJs)) Feeling, in his telling, is managed by perspective, and the managing is work.

On May 11, 2026 he took the stand in Oakland in a dress shirt and slacks, "the first male witness to testify without a suit jacket," and testified for about an hour "barely making eye contact with anyone." Wired's reporter read him as "dejected about no longer being involved with OpenAI"; that is one journalist's reading of a face. An OpenAI lawyer told the court that he "has been estranged from Brockman and Altman ever since." ([Wired](https://web.archive.org/web/2026id_/https://dnyuz.com/2026/05/12/ilya-sutskever-stands-by-his-role-in-sam-altmans-openai-ouster-i-didnt-want-it-to-be-destroyed/)) He undercut the man suing. Asked whether he had promised Musk that OpenAI would stay a nonprofit, he said, "I made no such promise." Then: "The mission of OpenAI is larger than its nonprofit or for-profit structure." ([reporters' courtroom posts, May 11, 2026](https://chatgptiseatingtheworld.com/2026/05/12/musk-v-altman-trial-week-3-ilya-sutskever-provides-captivating-testimony-favorable-to-openai/))

The film gives audiences a prodigy "incapable of lying," "so pure in his pursuit of research." The record gives something more exact: a man who checked the machine for twenty years and did not think to check the room, who turned down $6 million a year and then an acquisition at a $32 billion valuation to keep one question his own, and who says the hardest work he does is emotional. The first words of his longest public conversation since founding SSI were not about any of it. "You know what's crazy? That all of this is real." ([Dwarkesh Podcast](https://www.dwarkesh.com/p/ilya-sutskever-2))
