In May 2021, seven months before Steven Spielberg's _West Side Story_ reached theaters, Mike Faist sat down in Park Slope with [The New York Times](https://www.nytimes.com/2021/05/27/arts/television/mike-faist-panic-west-side-story.html) and tried to describe what making it had felt like. He called it "transcendental: either I didn't feel like I was myself, or I was the most authentic version of myself. I can't really tell which one."

The paper predicted the film would put him "on Hollywood's speed dial." He was turning down offers anyway, and selling his Brooklyn apartment to head back to Ohio.

"I don't want to just be a working actor anymore," he said. "I just don't want to follow the trajectory of what the industry wants me to do." Asked what that trajectory was, he said, "Put on a cape and wear a mask."

Faist, born in 1992 and raised in Gahanna, Ohio, has made that move more than once. He originated Connor Murphy in _Dear Evan Hansen_ and left the show in 2018. After playing Art Donaldson in _Challengers_ in 2024, opposite [Zendaya](/personality-analysis/zendaya) and Josh O'Connor, and then shooting _East of Eden_, he worked on nothing new for about two years. Netflix premiered the series on October 1, 2026.

Each time, the reasons he gave pointed inward. And for most of a decade he has said that being himself in public is harder than being someone else.

> "I actually feel a lot more comfortable being a character than I do being myself in the public eye. I feel a lot braver, quite frankly, to be someone else."

He said that to his friend, the designer Jonathan Anderson, in a conversation [Interview](https://www.interviewmagazine.com/film/a-reluctant-interview-between-mike-faist-and-jonathan-anderson) published on October 6, 2026. This analysis is about what he protects when he steps back, and what it costs him.

## What is Mike Faist's personality type?

Mike Faist's personality type is Enneagram Type 4, the Individualist. Fours sit in the heart triad, whose core emotion is shame. The fears he names most often are not being good enough and being seen as himself. He has repeatedly stepped back from work, and from momentum, when it stopped feeling like his own.

A [Type 4](/enneagram-corner/enneagram-type-4) wants to be himself and be seen that way, and carries a sense that something essential in him is missing or will be found wanting. Faist shows both halves: embarrassment about being himself in front of people, and a rule for choosing work, repeated since 2021, that it has to come from "a place of passion and love."

This is a read of the public record at medium confidence. [Type 6](/enneagram-corner/enneagram-type-6) is a serious rival, because the feeling Faist reports most often is anxiety. [Type 9](/enneagram-corner/enneagram-type-9) is also in play. Both get their full case below.

## "I keep fooling everyone"

As a small child in Gahanna, Faist watched old MGM musicals on repeat, above all _Singin' in the Rain_, which the family played for an elderly relative with dementia. (He told [PBS's American Masters](https://www.pbs.org/wnet/americanmasters/podcast/mike-faist-interview/) it was his great-grandmother; his mother, Julie, told [Another Man](https://www.anothermag.com/another-man/15568/mike-faist-challengers-the-bikeriders-interview-profile) it was his grandmother.) The man on the screen was Gene Kelly. "I'm going to be Gene Kelly one day," he told his mother.

She remembers a serious child. "He was born an adult," she said. "At six, he told me he wanted to start saving for college. At six!"

High school went worse. "I was really unhappy in high school," he said on [Broadway.com](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=lFGPADR4r5k) in December 2016, and "if I wasn't performing, I wasn't happy." A friend from those years, now his roommate, told Another Man that teenage Faist was "a loose cannon." "Eccentric," Faist corrected. "Floaty and an idiot."

He graduated a year early, in 2009, and his father drove him to New York at 17. "I was terrified," he told Another Man. "So was my dad. But I knew I wanted to do this more than anything in the world."

He dropped out of the American Musical and Dramatic Academy after two semesters, sold tickets for off-Broadway shows in Times Square and at times lived on food stamps. In those years, the magazine reports, he ran into the actor Ben Platt while delivering for Postmates and felt "so ashamed that he pretended he was doing something else."

Then fear froze him. After quitting school, he told a [BUILD Series](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=1SyXL1F43Qg) audience in March 2017, he "went maybe six, eight months without auditioning for anything 'cause I felt too incapable. I didn't really want to put myself out there. I was too scared, too whatever."

What ended it was another person: "a friend of mine at the time dragged me kicking and screaming to an audition for _White Christmas_ in Springboro, Ohio at this dinner theater," he told [Interview](https://www.interviewmagazine.com/culture/discovery-mike-faist). He booked it, at $150 a week, and "that gave me enough confidence to finally go and say, 'I can actually do this.'"

The doubt has a decade of paper trail. Asked at the BUILD interview how he kept landing musicals when he did not consider himself a strong singer, he said, "You know, I keep fooling everyone." Of the _West Side Story_ dance call, he told [Screen International](https://www.screendaily.com/features/do-i-have-to-dance-mike-faist-on-moving-from-stage-to-screen-for-west-side-story/5168275.article) in 2022, "I'm not good enough. I had no business being in that room, quite frankly." Remembering the 17-year-old who left Ohio hoping to get good, he told PBS, "I still feel that way. Maybe I'll be good one day."

## "Who I actually am"

Faist made his Broadway debut at 20, in the original cast of _Newsies_ in 2012. In 2015 he joined _Dear Evan Hansen_ at Washington's Arena Stage as Connor Murphy, the troubled teenager whose suicide sets the plot moving, and stayed with it to Broadway, where it opened in December 2016. The role earned him a 2017 Tony nomination.

He says the part taught him how to prepare. Floundering early on, he found LiveThroughThis.org, which tells the stories of people who survived suicide attempts, and spoke with its founder. "It really grounded me with what I wanted to do with the role," he told Another Man. "Since then, research has been my way to feel more secure, to feel like I am prepared."

The show also cost him. In 2015, while the creators reworked it, he feared his character "was going to be radically altered," [The Columbus Dispatch](https://web.archive.org/web/2021/https://www.dispatch.com/entertainmentlife/20171207/gahanna-native-faist-identified-emotionally-with-lead-characters-dilemma) reported. "I was in this dark, depressed place for a little while," he told the paper. He took a small film about a bullied foster teenager because "it's almost therapeutic, to liberate myself from those feelings, by lashing out in the film."

What he guarded hardest was the gap between Connor and himself. He rarely did the stage-door autograph line, he told [MTV News](https://web.archive.org/web/2017/http://www.mtv.com/news/3002027/mike-faist-dear-evan-hansen-interview/) in April 2017, signing only "maybe twice a week" for repeat customers. Fans "feel like they know us, in a way," he said, and "I don't want to take that away from them, by having them meet who I actually am."

> "I just would hate for that illusion to be broken by having interaction with me, that, maybe, [fans] were expecting something else."

That spring he described Evan and Connor to Interview, and the sentence drifted from the characters into the first person: "they don't want people to see them because if we allow ourselves to be seen, actually seen, and allow [ourselves] to be vulnerable, then we lose the game."

He left in the summer of 2018, [The Washington Post](https://www.stripes.com/living/entertainment/movies/2021-12-13/faist-actor-west-side-story-profile-entertainment-3955668.html) reported. Asked by the Times in 2021 why he was not in the film adaptation, he said, "I feel like I couldn't do it." Eight shows a week, he explained, "very much turns into relying on your technique and the job of it," and "I didn't really have it in me anymore." He did not say whether a film role was offered.

## "Like being used as a vessel"

Spielberg came backstage at _Dear Evan Hansen_ one night in 2016 and asked him to audition for _West Side Story_, Another Man reports. In a [studio featurette](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=asZ5_hxcpDA), Spielberg said he "was actually considering delaying the movie" to get Faist, and called him "a deep feeler as a human being and as an actor."

Faist went in expecting to disappear. He feared Spielberg would be "a dictator," he told [Gold Derby](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=R1377wu21GE) in December 2021, and that the shoot would be "Hollywood overproduced," with the actor reduced to a prop. Instead, he told [Esquire](https://www.esquire.com/uk/culture/film/a61081999/mike-faist-the-bikeriders-interview/) in 2024, "Steven never made me feel I needed to ask permission to do anything."

Two things he did on that set show him fully engaged. When the Jets first assembled, he said, "I have to step in here kind of and do something," because "I wanted to actually create a family, a brotherhood, a tribe." He had each Jet pick an activity the whole gang had to do. One of them was a LARPing session, he told BAFTA in 2022.

The second was a gesture. In one scene a bartender points a gun at Riff, and Riff presses his temple to the barrel. It was not in the script. Faist says he went to Spielberg and the screenwriter, Tony Kushner, on the day: "I think I have this idea. Just go with me on it. If you hate it, tell me." "What Mike came up with in that moment," Kushner told the Post, "is the whole character in this one little gesture."

Kushner's favorite memory of the shoot was quieter. After the Jets splashed paint over a mural of the Puerto Rican flag, crew members set about scrubbing it off. "One of the people washing it was Mike," he said. "And it really wasn't in any way done for display. It was something that he needed to do because he felt terribly about what his character had just done, and he needed to do something to make himself feel okay about it." The motive is Kushner's reading. The scrubbing is something he watched.

Faist's own description of that shoot is the most revealing thing in his record. "It felt like: I'm not me right now, I'm being used to tell the story in the best way possible," he told Another Man in 2024.

> "It was like being used as a vessel, and that was the best feeling ever."

In 2021 he could not decide whether that state was losing himself or finding himself. That question is where the Four and Nine readings part, and he has never settled it.

## Walking away at the top

After the film came out, Kushner told [GQ](https://www.gq.com/story/mike-faist-gq-hype) in 2026, friends making movies called him to ask whether Faist would take this part or that part. "And I think in both cases he turned them down. And he was very nice about it, but the stuff that they were asking just didn't feel right to him at the moment, and it had nothing to do with size of the role or anything."

Circumstance pressed on him too. He had left New York at the start of the pandemic and spent much of the next two years driving the country with his rescue dog, Austin, and writing a screenplay, the Times and Screen International reported. His father, Kurt, had fallen ill with pulmonary fibrosis. In 2021 Faist moved home and bought a house in Columbus's German Village, which he renovated largely himself, with some help from his father.

He kept working, by his own rules. He made _Challengers_ with Luca Guadagnino and played the photographer Danny Lyon in _The Bikeriders_ alongside [Austin Butler](/personality-analysis/austin-butler) and [Tom Hardy](/personality-analysis/tom-hardy). He told PBS he chose that last job "because all I had to do was just be the observer." He had told its director, Jeff Nichols, "that I only really want to do pieces that come from a place of love," he recalled to Another Man.

> "Like, it's coming from me. Like not necessarily for love, but from love. It may not be good [laughs] but it's coming from that place of 'I'm free, I'm liberated, I'm the most authentic I can be.'"

His father died on December 12, 2023, of a blood clot after three years of pulmonary fibrosis, and his grandfather died weeks later. "All the men have passed," he told GQ. "It's my sister, my mom and my grandmother, and me." At a pizza place his father loved, the Another Man writer watched his voice crack as he talked about his parents' work. "That's something that physically hurts me," he said. "They just worked so hard to make sure I was able to do what I do."

_Challengers_ opened in April 2024. He was dreading the press tour, the magazine reported, but he did it. What stopped was new work.

_Sgt. Rock_, a DC film he was reportedly attached to with Guadagnino, fell apart "more or less in prep," its screenwriter Justin Kuritzkes told GQ. In June 2025 Faist was announced opposite Michelle Williams in an off-Broadway [_Anna Christie_](https://www.russh.com/anna-christie-mike-faist-michelle-williams). By August 26 he had [withdrawn](https://www.newyorktheatreguide.com/theatre-news/news/tom-sturridge-brian-darcy-james-join-michelle-williams-in-anna-christie-off-broadway). No reason has been made public. Kushner told GQ he was "intrigued by what he's decided to do and what he has decided not to do and what he's fought hard to get out of."

Faist's own explanation pointed inward again. "I think a lot of people will do one thing and then they double down and they get onto the next thing. They want this momentum," he told GQ. "I don't think that I have that kind of stamina." He told Cultured:

> "I think that for myself, and for everyone, it's very easy to get swept up in noise. It makes it difficult for me to really listen, just being able to hear myself think, and feel what is right for me."

What brought him back was a test he set before taking the job. Zoe Kazan, who adapted _East of Eden_, told GQ they met twice before he agreed. "He was very clear with me, 'I don't need you to convince me. I just need to know that this is right for me, because when I jump in, I jump in 100%. If you have me, you're going to have me 100%.'"

After his first dailies came in, Netflix's response, as Kazan tells it, was "We'll pay for another episode." In August 2026, GQ reported, he was about to begin rehearsals for his first production since _East of Eden_, and he would not say what it was.

Put those episodes side by side and they have a direction. In 2017 he described acting to the Dispatch as "a strange industry, where you're constantly having to prove yourself," a life lived "all while trying to persuade people that I'm worth it."

Each time since, the security he gave up was the kind that industry pays out: momentum, a New York apartment, the next big part. The reasons he gave sat inside him: "coming from me," "right for me," "feel what is right for me." That is the Four's trade. He protects the authenticity of what he shows the world and pays for it in security.

Circumstance explains part of every episode: the pandemic, his father's illness, a studio film collapsing. And he has never said why he left _Anna Christie_.

## Why not Type 6?

The best argument against the Four reading is the feeling Faist reports more than any other. He told BAFTA in March 2022:

> "I tend to get anxious or have a bit of anxiety before every project, no matter what it is."

"Why did they hire me?" he went on. "I always like have this awful, awful thing in my whole body before like starting any sort of project." Opening _Brokeback Mountain_ in London the next year, he told the [Associated Press](https://www.clickondetroit.com/entertainment/2023/05/22/brokeback-mountain-brings-two-rising-acting-stars-to-the-london-stage/), "We're always nervous."

Anxiety is the Six's signature emotion, and Faist manages it the way a Six does. He prepares for security: research is "my way to feel more secure." He leans on a clear leader. A showrunner with a specific vision "helps any sort of anxieties because it's like, well, someone's steering the ship," he told PBS. "The real hope is that you can surround yourself with people, quite frankly, who are a lot smarter than you," he told [BBC Radio 1](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=gMOhYHCmN9w) in October 2026.

He builds groups and stays loyal to them: the Jets, the family he moved home to help, and the [Ohio Artists Gathering](https://www.columbusunderground.com/gahanna-native-and-tony-nominee-mike-faist-brings-inaugural-ohio-artists-gathering-to-short-north-stage-rs1/), a one-week festival he organized in Columbus in August 2018 because "I wanted to bring this work to a city I love and call home."

His first paid job came from a friend's push. He tests a commitment before giving it, as Kazan saw. His ambivalence about the job itself, "I can't tell if I hate acting or if I love it too much," fits a Six's approach and retreat. And in 2026 he described fear as the thing he chases. "That is kind of the only feeling that I'm really searching for within any of this," he told [Forbes](https://www.forbes.com/sites/oliviasingh/2026/10/01/how-fear-led-mike-faist-to-east-of-eden/). A counterphobic Six moves toward what frightens him, though he gave versions of that answer all through one press cycle.

So why does the Four reading still lead? Start with what the fear is about. "Why did they hire me?" is a question about adequacy. The prop he dreaded becoming on _West Side Story_ was an actor with no voice. "I'm embarrassed to do this interview," he told Anderson. Those are fears of being found wanting and of being seen, the heart triad's shame. A Six's anxiety more often fixes on safety, backing, or whether people can be trusted. Apart from the Spielberg worry, which dissolved on contact, Faist almost never voices suspicion of particular people.

Then look at what he gives up. In none of the decisions on record does he describe weighing security. He gives it away. Under overload he moves off, usually alone or with the dog, more than toward allies or structure. After the pandemic shutdown it was the open road; after _Challengers_, two years without a new production.

On the _East of Eden_ set he warned Florence Pugh and Christopher Abbott in advance, "I'm just going to be quiet. I just want to be over here. I'm just going to be off in a corner." And the test he put to Kazan's offer was about himself. He needed "to know that this is right for me."

The Six case still explains some things better: the anxiety before every job, the reliance on trusted leaders, the instinct to build a community. The record also lacks the scene that would separate them most clearly, Faist in open conflict or let down by a collaborator. A Six would grow wary and look for firmer ground. A Four would withdraw and insist on what feels true. Until that account exists, the Four reading holds at medium confidence.

## The quiet, and the case for Type 9

A Nine reading starts from the vessel. His best experience at work is a kind of self-forgetting. He talks about "noise" and about lacking "stamina," and he told Cultured, "Maybe there was an aspect of me just trying to say, I'm going to pull back from all of this, and allow things to be quiet." People describe easy company. His _Panic_ co-star Jessica Sula told the Times he is "so fabulously ridiculous and wonderful." Anderson told him, "You're just a modest person, Mike."

What argues against a Nine is how much he feels and lets show. "I'm far too emotional," he told Esquire in 2024, explaining a bad high-school tennis season. He teared up over his parents' hard work in front of a reporter he had met the day before. A Nine tends to turn strong feeling down. Faist moves toward it, and he refuses firmly instead of drifting, as Kushner's calls and _Anna Christie_ show.

A [Type 5](/enneagram-corner/enneagram-type-5) reading leans on _The Bikeriders_, where, he said on [set](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Zs5lOIJy-I4), "it's not about me at all." But that role was a photographer documenting a motorcycle club. And Five detachment fits poorly with a man who got a pilot's license on what he called a "really crazy and really impulsive" whim and turned the Jets into a tribe.

## What the type doesn't explain

Some of what is on record sits awkwardly with any Four reading, and it belongs in the portrait.

He wants to be useful. "I would like to ultimately be of service and of use; that's when I feel at my best," he told the Times in 2021. "So acting is just constantly giving," he told PBS. "You have to be really giving to whatever that person needs."

Florence Pugh saw that from across a scene. For a later _East of Eden_ moment with only a few lines, which an assistant director would usually read, "he insisted on coming back early to be in the room and reading with me," she told [TV Insider](https://www.tvinsider.com/1294095/east-of-eden-cathy-charles-florence-pugh-mike-faist-interview/). "And what that created was a whole relationship." Kushner's mural story belongs here too.

He is funny, often at his own expense. At the _Challengers_ press conference he announced that he and O'Connor "hate each other," a bit he had committed to so hard in one interview, O'Connor said, "that it sounded like I bullied you." Asked by GQ whether he would play Mary Todd in _Oh, Mary!_, he said, "I would rock Mary Todd."

And his account of the job won't hold still. In 2021 he told the Times, "I don't care enough." In 2026 he told Cultured, "I think, unfortunately, I'm doomed to do this," and told PBS, "I don't know why I keep going, 'cause I'm an addict."

## "Just watch my movies"

The Four reading predicts one thing about Faist that his record keeps confirming. He shows himself most fully as someone else.

He spent his first Broadway years mostly avoiding the stage door. He found the best feeling of his working life as a vessel. In 2026, after two years out, he told GQ that going on a walk with a reporter was "a big step."

Kazan, who told GQ she is "very careful not to push on" his privacy, reached for an animal: "There's something about Mike that reads to me like a hermit crab or something. There's this shell on the outside and you can't tell what's inside."

"I'm always saying," Faist told GQ, "if you want to get to know me, just watch my movies."

Asked by PBS what keeps him going, he said he loves the work and keeps a loose grip on it.

> "I love this thing very, very much, but I don't want to hold it so tightly because I think if you hold it too tightly, maybe it'll die."
