In the eighth episode of her podcast, _That's Enough with Grace Reiter_, released in May 2025, the comedian reached the segment the show was built on: her overshare. Her usual confessions were bodily and gross, and this time, she said, she would "steer away from gross ones cuz I've been doing gross all the time." Then: "I think to me what really is an overshare is this."

> "I feel so guilty with the success I have. I'm thinking about becoming a Christian."

Her guest, the comedian Veronika Slowikowska, laughed. Reiter explained that "people compliment me like a lot," and when they tell her "you're going to be something, kid," her reaction is "I can't handle this." Then: "But if I believed in God, I could give all of it to God." If you think she's awesome, she reasoned, "you think God's awesome."

The delivery was comic, with a riff on celebrity converts. The worry underneath was stated plainly: "the ego can get so so large. And that's what I'm terrified of." ([_That's Enough_, Episode 8](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=i_GWPEL741s), May 2025)

Reiter, born in 2001 near Madison, Wisconsin, and raised in Chippewa Falls, has posted sketch comedy online since 2019. [The New York Times](https://www.boston.com/culture/entertainment/2026/09/18/snl-new-cast-2026/) credits her rise to "physical comedy, theatrical overacting and 'take the bit too far' energy." In 2022 she was an original cast member of _Stapleview_, a live TikTok sketch show, and from 2023 she worked in Syracuse as one of five core cast members of American High's sketch shorts. In 2025 she performed Off-Broadway in _Ginger Twinsies_ and played Tara, a recurring role, on HBO's _The Chair Company_.

That November she posted _The Hunger Games (but better)_, a nearly 45-minute parody she directed and starred in as Katniss. By September 2026 it had nearly 14 million views. Netflix ordered _Minimum Wage_, an American High series starring Reiter, in April 2026, and in September 2026 NBC named her a featured player on _Saturday Night Live_. At 25, the Times noted, she was the show's first cast member born in the 21st century.

Her father told the [Leader-Telegram](https://www.leadertelegram.com/news/daily-updates/chi-hi-graduate-joins-cast-of-saturday-night-live/article_d5fcf0b8-d0a9-4001-99b1-7f8b1884028b.html) that SNL had "been her goal forever." This analysis follows what she does when the goal starts arriving, using her interviews and podcasts from 2022 to 2026, mostly from 2025 and 2026, and testimony from people who lived and worked with her.

## What is Grace Reiter's personality type?

Grace Reiter's personality type is most likely Enneagram Type 6, the Loyalist, at medium confidence. Sixes sit in the head triad, whose core emotion is fear. From 2024 to 2026 she waved off praise, kept asking whether she was allowed to share, and steadied a long-running stage fright with preparation. Type 3 is the closest rival.

A [Type 6](/enneagram-corner/enneagram-type-6) is built to anticipate trouble and to look for support that will hold. Most Sixes aim that vigilance at outside threats. Reiter aims much of it at her own rise: the praise, the views, the ego she is "terrified of."

Her ambition points to [Type 3](/enneagram-corner/enneagram-type-3), the Achiever, and her guilt keeps Type 1 in view. Both are weighed below. The Six leads less because of how she takes a compliment, which a modest Three would share, than because of how often she asks permission and what she leans on when she is scared.

## "You hate compliments more than anyone I know"

One podcast bit could be a pose. The person who lives with her describes the same thing.

After the parody came out, her roommate, the comedian and director Max Zavidow, joined her on the show, in [an episode](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=v5rENwP8ICA) released in April 2026. He began to praise her, and she cut him off: "we can't do this on my podcast." He answered:

> "Well, what's funny is that you hate compliments more than anyone I know."

He tied it, unprompted, to the overshare from months before: "It's the same thing of like why you wanted to find God." Then he added, "like you got to get used to it." She protested, "No, I feel like I'm good at it now." "Okay, you're getting better," he allowed. By then, she said, strangers on the street shouted [Jennifer Lawrence](/personality-analysis/jennifer-lawrence)'s name at her and called her Katniss, "which I guess was kind of always my dream."

Later he asked whether she still felt called to Christianity. She said no. "I think I'm just like, okay, well, this is my life and this is the things I want to do. So, like this stuff is going to happen." Her conclusion: "either enjoy it or just ignore it."

Outside her household the pattern is looser. In February 2026 the comedian Gianmarco Soresi, who worked with her on _Ginger Twinsies_ and has interviewed her twice on his podcast, [_The Downside_](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=ZcLMexvqHJI), told her that when she described the parody in rehearsals, "you were really underplaying it." Then he praised her directing. She credited the source film ("it also like again already did exist"), then said:

> "But thank you for the compliment. Thank you for the compliment. I'm sorry."

Moments later, after he contrasted her parody with Gus Van Sant's shot-for-shot remake of _Psycho_, she gave in: "Okay. Okay. Okay. I'll take I'll take that. I'll take it."

When the actor Busy Philipps, a co-producer of _Ginger Twinsies_, told her on [_That's Enough_](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=kR3EE_G-EY4) in November 2025 that she was "big in our house," where Philipps's daughters are fans, Reiter objected: "I don't think you're allowed to perceive yourself like that, though. You can't." Asked why, she said, "that's not for me," and then, "Like I'm for you guys."

Of _The Chair Company_, created by and starring [Tim Robinson](/personality-analysis/tim-robinson), she told the actor Kyle MacLachlan [in April 2026](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=wMtFoCeII34), "I don't think I was acting at all."

Some of this is method and some is manners. She has called self-mockery "comedy 101," and she grew up in the Upper Midwest, where waving off a compliment is ordinary politeness. Nor does she dislike attention itself. Of her birthdays she said, in [an episode](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Vjz_NmJF8R8) released in March 2026, "I want the attention but I also don't want to ask for it."

## "The fear is actually being successful"

In [an episode](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=uAqkjPXE-lY) released in February 2026, less than three months after the parody went up, Reiter told a story she felt had gone too far. Then:

> "I knew this was going to happen too because The Hunger Games was so successful. I was like something's going to happen and it is my own hand that brings down myself."

It was half a joke, about an embarrassing story rather than a career. It closed the episode on a laugh. But it echoes things she has said without a punchline.

In April 2025 the creator Coy Piso asked her on his show, [_Couch Surfing_](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=0bP_zcZydbs), how she kept the possibility of success from turning into a fear of failure. "I don't know if it's necessarily a fear of failure," she said, then allowed, "I guess maybe it kind of is," before landing on "maybe like a fear of actually trying." Then she got personal:

> "I feel like my whole life I've kind of half halfass everything."

Now that she was "starting to see the success that I wanted," she said, "now I actually care about this stuff," and "the fear is actually being successful like the possibility of like oh this could be even bigger than I thought it could be."

A month later, asked by Slowikowska whether she had lows, she said she worried "oh I'll never be funny again or oh I'll like never make another good video again." The fear runs both ways.

Her words don't settle whether she fears failing or succeeding, and she wasn't sure herself. What they show is that success raised the stakes instead of settling them. Circumstance explains some of it too. In about a year her career moved from TikTok to HBO, Netflix and SNL, which would give anyone in her mid-20s reasons to feel like an impostor.

## "Is that okay?"

The podcast's premise was oversharing. Its host kept checking whether she was allowed to. "Why did I make this whole podcast over sharing when everything I over share I'm like, 'Is that okay?'" she asked in February 2026.

Her old American High castmates Ryan and Aidan Micho see the same reflex. When one of them asked whether she was coming on their podcast, [_Volley for Serve_](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=a0jf8KWHRP0), he recalled in May 2026, she answered, "Do you want me to only if you want me to?" Later one of them told her, "If we don't answer within like the first 0.1 seconds, you immediately get shy and walk it down." Her reply: "Oh, okay. This is good. I'm learning a lot about myself."

On the same show she said she avoids opinion shows such as _Subway Takes_ "because I don't have an opinion and I don't want to be judged for my opinion." Her sharper opinions mostly stay private. Zavidow calls her "one of the all-time great haters." Her explanation: "I think it's because I'm afraid of like offending people." She holds back, she said, because "maybe it could come back to me." In September 2025 she told Soresi, on [her own show](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=PmHpEfGp0yY), "I think I'm a people pleaser."

When she does confront someone, it comes out sideways. At American High, she told the host of [_The Yvette Show_](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=irWMQuIOvmI) in December 2025, a roommate kept leaving paper towels around. She said nothing, then raised it "at like the worst time ever," at breakfast in front of everyone, with a guest star in the house. He asked why she hadn't just told him before. Her verdict: "sometimes it's just that easy, but you build it up in your head."

This is the Six's checking loop: scan for hidden disapproval, then test it against what people actually say. In her own accounts the test usually comes back fine. Fear of judgment is also close to universal for a young performer with millions of followers and a comment section, and she tells most of these stories as jokes on herself.

## "Not even nervous at all"

Reiter dates her stage fright to seventh grade. In January 2025 she told Soresi on [_The Downside_](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=iE7kKL0kUAk) that during a speech about the Hubble telescope her hands shook so badly she could barely flip her note cards. She told her father, "and then he called me the shakes." From then on, she said, she believed she had stage fright. "I don't think he understood how personally or how much that was going to affect me," she said, though she framed the belief as her own doing: "at one point I instilled in me I have stage fright and then I could never shake it."

She does not tell it as a wound. When Philipps asked whether some childhood hardship had made her funny, she said, "I can't pinpoint trauma really anywhere," and "I think my family was just funny."

The next year she won a choir solo in "You Raise Me Up," but in rehearsal she could not sing it in front of one new person, the pianist. She hid behind the last row and cried, and the solo went to someone else. "I was so mad at myself," she said. She has told the choir story at least three times, in January 2025, [July 2025](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=YatrI4ziPjA) and April 2026.

She never did plays or musicals in school, the Leader-Telegram reported. Her live record runs through _Stapleview_'s live TikTok broadcasts in 2022 and the Off-Broadway run of _Ginger Twinsies_, a parody of _The Parent Trap_, from July to October 2025. "I don't know if I enjoyed the repetition," she said of that run in August 2026 on [_I Never Liked You_](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=gYbGompZjS8), but "performing live every night was awesome." The girl who could not sing in front of a pianist was, by the fall of 2026, performing live on network television. What steadies her is structure.

In December 2024 she told [_Passion Pod_](https://podcasts.apple.com/us/podcast/episode-167-grace-reiter-actress-content-creator/id1484505634?i=1000680559361) about her first SNL showcase that year:

> "I like knew that that was coming. So I like worked for it and I practiced and then I was like, not even nervous at all."

She credited the preparation over the talent: "it's not because I'm like good at this, which I think I am good at it, but I am good at it."

That showcase did not lead to a job. Her friend Jane Wickline, a _Stapleview_ castmate, had just joined SNL, which Reiter called "encouraging." She also said "obviously SNL is like the dream," but "I don't know if I could handle that work," and she preferred "a one and done thing." That could be a Six's self-doubt or simply someone softening a disappointment. In the summer of 2026 she went through the showcases again, and on September 16 NBC [announced](https://deadline.com/2026/09/snl-season-52-casts-grace-reiter-saidah-belo-osagie-1237105604/) her hire.

Dread before and fine after is her routine with live comedy too. "I stress myself out," she told Zavidow, and then "I get up there and I have fun and I'm like oh it's like fine."

She also looks for guidance outside herself. In her journal, she told Soresi in February 2026, she wrote, "I want a teacher. I want a mentor. I want a father." She added at once that she wrote the last one mostly because it sounded poetic.

## Why not Type 3?

The best case against the Six reading is her ambition, which is old, explicit and measured in numbers.

"I grew up in the YouTube era," she told [Volume One](https://volumeone.org/sites/chippewafalls/articles/2022/03/29/293816-from-chippewa-falls-to-la-grace-reiter-joins), a Chippewa Falls magazine, in March 2022. "YouTubers were my celebrities. I think my dream had always been to be an Internet persona." Her father says she has "always been a ham for the camera, and loved attention."

She keeps score:

> "I feel like the goalpost is always moving cuz once I hit 1 million, I was like, 'Yeah, but like all the really Tik Tok famous people have 3 million.'"

That was to [Page Six](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=LB8IV4nRnX0) in December 2025. Of the parody she said, "If I don't get a million, I'm actually going to be really really embarrassed." She made it, she said in the [making-of video](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=l52Dh0lQbIc), because "I want to be the lead in a movie for my birthday." In August 2026 she said, "I don't take time to really process or enjoy anything. I'm just like work mode all the time."

In a conversation with the podcaster Drew Afualo released in September 2026, she said of starring on a set, "I am a princess. Like I want to be treated like a princess." Then: "I feel really bad, but like this is nice."

The strongest line for a Three came as an overshare. In [July 2025](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=TBv2EmLpGqs) she told her podcast guest, "I feel like I don't exist unless I mean funny. And when I'm not funny, I feel like I don't exist." She revised it on the spot: "So maybe it's more I'm present." Still, a self that feels most real while performing sits close to the Three's core.

Enneagram teachers describe a modest form of Three, often called the self-preservation Three, that wants admiration without being seen to want it. That Three would wave off compliments, apologize for them and fear a swollen ego. Asked by Piso how she copes with the pressure, Reiter said, "I just make it and I'm just like okay moving on. Moving on. I got to do the next thing." She keeps several projects going so she won't get "too caught up in the numbers and the comments." Turning one success into the next task is a Three's move, even if her stated aim was to keep the numbers at bay.

So the praise-deflection that opens this article does not settle the case. Both readings predict it, and so do Midwestern manners. The ego fear and her hedged answer about failure fit both too.

The Six still leads, narrowly, on two observations. First, she asks permission more than she polishes an image. A Three, even a modest one, tends to claim the role and manage how it looks. Reiter asks whether she is allowed: "Is that okay?" on her own overshare show, "Do you want me to only if you want me to?" to old castmates, "I don't think you're allowed to perceive yourself like that" to Philipps. In 2022 she told the Wisconsin podcast [_Passion Pod_](https://podcasts.apple.com/us/podcast/episode-89-grace-reiter-tiktok-comedian/id1484505634?i=1000566209332) that her recurring mistake was "not taking myself more seriously like allowing myself to say I'm a comedian."

Second, what steadies her is preparation and people rather than the result. She practiced her way to "not even nervous at all," and her journal asks for a mentor. Driving to the airport in 2024 after a film shoot in Baton Rouge, she said, "I'm not really religious at all," but she prayed anyway: "I'm so grateful if this is all it ever is is just like making like little indie films with my friends."

The case has limits. The follower ladder fits both types, because a Six can keep climbing when no number ever feels safe ("It never feels real," she told Piso). Much of the record is comedy, where self-deprecation is the joke. And she does use laughter as a gauge. On the _Minimum Wage_ set, where the crew had to stay quiet for sound, she said, "I'm always trying to like make people break cuz I'm like I want I need to know."

The clearest test is still ahead. Her father said in September 2026 that SNL was not letting her give interviews, and none had appeared by mid-October 2026. When she first describes a hard stretch in network television at length, on a loose, long-form podcast rather than a press stop, listen for what she says was at stake. A Three would more likely describe a threat to her standing, fixed by a result: a sketch that landed. A Six would more likely describe the fear of being found out, and credit what she leaned on: preparation, a trusted person, the structure of the week.

## Why not a One, a Nine or a Two?

[Type 1](/enneagram-corner/enneagram-type-1) is the second live alternative, and the record gives it real material. The overshare that opens this article is about guilt. She fears a swollen ego and, she told Soresi, brought her therapist "a list of like all like the mistakes I made today." On the parody set, she told Yvette, she was "the one friend that's like no but like we have to take this serious you guys." Her harsh self-labels, "lazy" among them, are what an inner critic produces.

What separates the readings is the reason she gives for holding back. A One restrains herself because something would be wrong. Reiter's reasons are about consequences and permission: she is "afraid of like offending people," worries an opinion "could come back to me," and asks, "Is that okay?" A One usually knows where the line is. Reiter usually asks someone else. That keeps the Six ahead, but One stays in the running.

[Type 9](/enneagram-corner/enneagram-type-9) fits the coasting. In 2022 she called herself "a procrastinator and a lazy person at heart," and she avoids confrontation. In 2024 she said she struggles with "motivation stuff" and moves when "somebody else is like, hey, you have to do this." That fits a Nine at least as well as a Six. But a Nine settles when idle, and that same year she said "the most time when I get stressed is when I'm not doing stuff." Her output, a weekly podcast for a year and a nearly 45-minute parody, also argues against the lazy label.

[Type 2](/enneagram-corner/enneagram-type-2) fits the people-pleasing, and her wish not to seem "needy" could belong to a Two. The behavior points elsewhere. Leaving American High, she told friends, "please text me cuz like I won't text you," and the record shows no habit of making herself indispensable.

## What the type doesn't explain

She craves novelty. Looking back on the _Ginger Twinsies_ run, she said, "I just need like novelty."

She also defies the crowd. When a parody of Harry Styles fan fiction brought her about a million followers around 2020, fans wanted more. "I'm not going to be like the Harry Styles girl," she decided. Five years later she put it more bluntly:

> "Because the people wanted me to do it. And I said, 'Fuck the people.'"

She said in 2025 that she was "not a risk taker" and was "protecting myself" on a four-wheeler, yet the comedian Eric Sedeño told her, "you have no shame to just like make the joke as hard as you can." A Six can push straight at what scares her, but the simpler reading may be that her risks happen inside a bit, where a character takes the hit.

She can be bossy, and she knows it. Telling the comedian Sabrina Wu on [_That's Enough_](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=2T-yIeGPqXs) in January 2026 how she handles riffs she doesn't like, she said, "I would shut that down immediately," and then judged herself: "And that's not fair." As she put it, "I'm very like older sister in that way." After shooting _Twilight (but better)_ with friends in June 2026, she told Afualo, "I like cried afterwards cuz I was like, I'm so sorry I made you guys do that."

And she is trusting in a way the suspicious-Six stereotype doesn't predict. Her producer, Lizzy Stewart, told her in the March 2026 anniversary episode, "if I tell you something you're going to assume that I'm telling you the truth," and "You're so earnestly here." That trust sits oddly beside how often she checks whether people really want her there.

Her oversharing is selective, too. She told Soresi on her show in September 2025 that "the real overshare stuff would be like sad and like not funny." Stewart says Reiter has only ever asked her to cut one story from the show: "You cut it for the other person."

## "You will believe it"

Near the end of her [conversation](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=crG4upus05Y) with Afualo on _The Comment Section_, recorded before the casting news and released on September 23, 2026, the host called Reiter "unbelievably hilarious." Reiter thanked her, then joined in a bit of mock self-praise, and Afualo noticed the wince:

> "You're you're you're wincing when you say it, but you will believe it."

Keep saying it, Afualo added, and "you'll stop grimacing when you say it." Reiter leaned in: "actually, you know what? Yes. No, we're doing it." She would, she said, "start faking it until I make it."

The two readings make different predictions from here, though not about the wince. Getting used to applause would not settle the type, since people of every type do, and by April 2026 Zavidow already thought Reiter was "getting better." The readings part over what she does with the applause once it stops stinging. A Three would fold it into the next goal and the next number. A Six would keep pointing it outward, at the preparation, the people and the structure that let her risk the stage.
