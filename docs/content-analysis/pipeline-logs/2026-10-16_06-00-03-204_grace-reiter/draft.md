In the eighth episode of her podcast, _That's Enough with Grace Reiter_, released in May 2025, the comedian reached the segment the show was built on: her overshare. Her usual confessions were bodily and gross, and this time, she said, she would "steer away from gross ones cuz I've been doing gross all the time." Then she offered this:

> "I feel so guilty with the success I have. I'm thinking about becoming a Christian."

Her guest, the comedian Veronika Slowikowska, laughed. Reiter explained that "people compliment me like a lot," and when they tell her "you're going to be something, kid," her reaction is "I can't handle this." Then: "But if I believed in God, I could give all of it to God." If you think she's awesome, she reasoned, "you think God's awesome."

The delivery was comic, with a riff on celebrity converts. The worry underneath was stated plainly: "the ego can get so so large. And that's what I'm terrified of." ([_That's Enough_, Episode 8](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=i_GWPEL741s), May 2025)

Reiter, born in 2001 near Madison, Wisconsin, and raised in Chippewa Falls, has posted sketch comedy online since 2019. In 2022 she was an original cast member of _Stapleview_, a live TikTok sketch show, and from 2023 she worked in Syracuse as one of five core cast members of American High's sketch shorts. In October 2025 she appeared on HBO's _The Chair Company_. A month later she posted _The Hunger Games (but better)_, a nearly 45-minute parody she directed and starred in. In September 2026 NBC named her a featured player on _Saturday Night Live_. At 25, [The New York Times](https://www.boston.com/culture/entertainment/2026/09/18/snl-new-cast-2026/) noted, she was the show's first cast member born in the 21st century.

Her father told the [Leader-Telegram](https://www.leadertelegram.com/news/daily-updates/chi-hi-graduate-joins-cast-of-saturday-night-live/article_d5fcf0b8-d0a9-4001-99b1-7f8b1884028b.html) that SNL had "been her goal forever." This analysis follows what she does when the goal starts arriving, across about four years of her interviews and podcast episodes and the testimony of people who live and work with her.

## What is Grace Reiter's personality type?

Grace Reiter's personality type is Enneagram Type 6, the Loyalist. Sixes sit in the head triad, whose core emotion is fear. Reiter's fear shows up when things go well: she pushes praise away, wishes for someone or something to hold her success, and expects it to be taken from her.

A [Type 6](/enneagram-corner/enneagram-type-6) is built to anticipate trouble and to look for support that will hold. Most Sixes aim that vigilance at outside threats. Reiter aims much of it at her own rise: the praise, the views, the ego she is "terrified of."

The read is medium-confidence. Her ambition is real and long-running, which points to [Type 3](/enneagram-corner/enneagram-type-3), the Achiever, and that case gets its own section below. The deciding question is what she does when approval actually arrives.

## "You hate compliments more than anyone I know"

One podcast bit could be a pose. The people around her describe the same thing.

After the parody came out, her roommate, Max Zavidow, joined her on the show, in [an episode](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=v5rENwP8ICA) released in April 2026. He began to praise her, and she cut him off: "we can't do this on my podcast." He answered:

> "Well, what's funny is that you hate compliments more than anyone I know."

He tied it, unprompted, to the overshare from months before: "It's the same thing of like why you wanted to find God." She protested, "No, I feel like I'm good at it now." "Okay, you're getting better," he allowed. Strangers on the street now shout [Jennifer Lawrence](/personality-analysis/jennifer-lawrence)'s name at her and call her Katniss, she said, "which I guess was kind of always my dream."

Later in the episode he asked whether she still felt called to Christianity. She said no. "I think I just like don't think about it," she said. "I think I'm just like, okay, well, this is my life and this is the things I want to do."

The pattern holds with people outside her household. In February 2026 the comedian Gianmarco Soresi told her on his podcast, [_The Downside_](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=ZcLMexvqHJI), that before the premiere "you were really underplaying it," and praised her directing. She credited the source film ("it also like again already did exist"), then said:

> "But thank you for the compliment. Thank you for the compliment. I'm sorry."

When the actor Busy Philipps told her on [_That's Enough_](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=kR3EE_G-EY4) in November 2025 that she was "big in our house," where Philipps's daughters are fans, Reiter objected: "I don't think you're allowed to perceive yourself like that, though. You can't." Asked why, she said, "that's not for me," and then, "Like I'm for you guys."

She had just watched a clip of Madonna describing the artist as "the manager of your talent." The idea "makes me feel good," she said, because she isn't "the owner of it." Otherwise "you start tying your selfworth to all of it and then it gets you know like the views and the likes."

Praise for her acting gets the same treatment. Of _The Chair Company_, created by and starring [Tim Robinson](/personality-analysis/tim-robinson), she told the actor Kyle MacLachlan [in April 2026](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=wMtFoCeII34) that "people have told me I did good in the show." "I don't think I was acting at all," she said, and then, "oh my god, I tricked them all." On set, she told [_The Yvette Show_](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=irWMQuIOvmI) in December 2025, she barely talked to Robinson "cuz I was scared. I was intimidated."

Some of this is method and some is manners. She has called self-mockery "comedy 101," and she grew up in the Upper Midwest, where waving off a compliment is ordinary politeness. Nor does she dislike attention itself. Of her birthdays she said, in [an episode](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Vjz_NmJF8R8) released in March 2026, "I want the attention but I also don't want to ask for it."

## "My own hand that brings down myself"

In [an episode](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=uAqkjPXE-lY) released in February 2026, less than three months after the parody went up, Reiter told a story she felt had gone too far. "This actually this has been the only time in the podcast where I've actually felt like I have overshared," she said. Then:

> "I knew this was going to happen too because The Hunger Games was so successful. I was like something's going to happen and it is my own hand that brings down myself."

It was half a joke, about an embarrassing story rather than a career. But it describes a habit: at the moment of success, she looks for the fall.

The habit has a history. In April 2025 the creator Coy Piso asked her on his show, [_Couch Surfing_](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=0bP_zcZydbs), how she kept the possibility of success from turning into a fear of failure. "I don't know if it's necessarily a fear of failure," she said. It was "maybe like a fear of actually trying." Then she got personal:

> "I feel like my whole life I've kind of half halfass everything."

Now that she was "starting to see the success that I wanted," she said, "the fear is actually being successful like the possibility of like oh this could be even bigger than I thought it could be."

In 2022, living back in Wisconsin after a few months in Los Angeles with _Stapleview_, she told the local [_Passion Pod_](https://downloads.pod.co/dd47e031-3896-47f1-8ac5-ce83f089122f/b5816dc0-61ed-41ac-8eca-e06d4e86e438.mp3) that her recurring mistake was "not taking myself more seriously like allowing myself to say I'm a comedian or allowing myself to say I'm an actress." The trip had taught her "maybe I'm not an awful horrible person."

For a Six, success can raise the stakes: more to lose, more people watching, more ways to be found out. Her words fit that better than they fit a fear of failing. Circumstance explains some of it too. In about a year her career moved from TikTok to HBO, Netflix and SNL, which would give anyone in her mid-20s reasons to feel like an impostor.

## "Is that okay?"

The podcast's premise was oversharing. Its host kept checking whether she was allowed to. "Why did I make this whole podcast over sharing when everything I over share I'm like, 'Is that okay?'" she asked in February 2026.

The check runs through her conversations:

- After a night she was embarrassed about, she asked her friends, "Do you guys think of me differently now?" They told her they didn't care. (_The Yvette Show_, December 2025)
- On friendship: "I just like hope my friends are actually my friends." (_That's Enough_, with Zavidow)
- On the parody's comment section: "I'm always like someone they're going to be mean to me and they never are, which is very sweet." ([_I Never Liked You_](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=gYbGompZjS8), August 2026)
- On opinion shows such as _Subway Takes_, which she has avoided "because I don't have an opinion and I don't want to be judged for my opinion." ([_Volley for Serve_](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=a0jf8KWHRP0), May 2026)

Her sharper opinions mostly stay private. Zavidow calls her "one of the all-time great haters." Her explanation: "I think it's because I'm afraid of like offending people." She holds back, she said, because "maybe it could come back to me." In September 2025 she told Soresi, on [her own show](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=PmHpEfGp0yY), "I think I'm a people pleaser."

When she does confront someone, it comes out sideways. At American High, she told Yvette, a roommate kept leaving paper towels around. She said nothing, then raised it "at like the worst time ever," at breakfast in front of everyone, with a guest star in the house. She said "it like felt like it spilled out of me because I was so nervous to tell him." He asked why she hadn't just told him before. Her verdict: "sometimes it's just that easy, but you build it up in your head."

Her longtime collaborators, the brothers Ryan and Aidan Micho, see the same reflex. When they asked whether she was coming on their podcast, one of them recalled in May 2026, she answered, "Do you want me to only if you want me to?" Later in that episode one of them told her, "If we don't answer within like the first 0.1 seconds, you immediately get shy and walk it down."

This is the Six's checking loop: scan for hidden disapproval, then test it against what people actually say. In her own accounts the test usually comes back fine. Fear of judgment is also close to universal for a young performer with millions of followers and a comment section, and she tells most of these stories as jokes on herself.

## "The shakes"

Reiter dates her stage fright to middle school. In January 2025 she told Soresi on [_The Downside_](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=iE7kKL0kUAk) that running for class president in sixth grade hadn't scared her. Then, in seventh grade, during a speech about the Hubble telescope, her hands shook so badly she could barely flip her note cards. She told her father, "and then he called me the shakes." From then on, she said, she believed she had stage fright.

> "I don't think he understood how personally or how much that was going to affect me."

She said she hoped he had been joking. The next year she won a choir solo in "You Raise Me Up," but in rehearsal she could not sing it in front of one new person, the pianist. She hid behind the last row and cried, and the solo went to someone else. "I was so mad at myself," she said. She has told the choir story at least three times, in January 2025, [July 2025](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=YatrI4ziPjA) and April 2026.

The girl who could not sing in front of a pianist now performs live on network television. What steadies her is structure. In December 2024, home in Wisconsin before a move to New York, she told [_Passion Pod_](https://podcasts.apple.com/us/podcast/episode-167-grace-reiter-actress-content-creator/id1484505634?i=1000680559361) about her first SNL showcase that year:

> "I like knew that that was coming. So I like worked for it and I practiced and then I was like, not even nervous at all."

She credited the preparation over the talent: "it's not because I'm like good at this, which I think I am good at it, but I am good at it." And she has learned that she moves when "somebody else is like, hey, you have to do this."

That showcase did not lead to a job. That December she said, "obviously SNL is like the dream," but "I don't know if I could handle that work," and she preferred "a one and done thing." In the summer of 2026 she went through the showcases again, and on September 16 NBC [announced](https://deadline.com/2026/09/snl-season-52-casts-grace-reiter-saidah-belo-osagie-1237105604/) her hire.

Dread before and fine after is her routine with live comedy too. "I stress myself out," she told Zavidow, and then "I get up there and I have fun and I'm like oh it's like fine."

She also looks for guidance outside herself. In her journal, she told Soresi in February 2026, she wrote, "I want a teacher. I want a mentor. I want a father." She added at once that she wrote the last one mostly because it sounded poetic, and she speaks of her father warmly. When Philipps wondered aloud whether she should be doing her own kids' homework, Reiter said of her parents, "If anything I wish they punished me more."

In therapy, she told Soresi, she brought "a list of like all like the mistakes I made today." Hearing her describe her inner monologue, he said "you're a completely non-functional human being," then pointed out that she produces anyway: "you don't miss things."

For a Six, safety comes from preparation, from obligations someone else sets and from trusted guides. That is where Reiter finds her footing.

## Why not Type 3?

The best case against the Six reading is her ambition, which is old, explicit and measured in numbers.

"I grew up in the YouTube era," she told [Volume One](https://volumeone.org/sites/chippewafalls/articles/2022/03/29/293816-from-chippewa-falls-to-la-grace-reiter-joins), a Chippewa Falls magazine, in March 2022. "YouTubers were my celebrities. I think my dream had always been to be an Internet persona." She described the pressure to stay that persona, "Because that's what made people like you in the first place." Her father says she has "always been a ham for the camera, and loved attention."

She keeps score:

> "I feel like the goalpost is always moving cuz once I hit 1 million, I was like, 'Yeah, but like all the really Tik Tok famous people have 3 million.'"

That was to [Page Six](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=LB8IV4nRnX0) in December 2025. Early in her fame she went to the Mall of America "cuz there's going to be a lot of people there and I feel like I have to get recognized by someone." Of the parody she said, "If I don't get a million, I'm actually going to be really really embarrassed." She made it, she said in the [making-of video](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=l52Dh0lQbIc), because "I want to be the lead in a movie for my birthday." She takes credit where she thinks it's due ("I casted everybody perfectly"), and in August 2026 she said, "I don't take time to really process or enjoy anything. I'm just like work mode all the time."

On a Three reading, the deflection is image management, a way to avoid looking vain while the climb continues. Some lines support that. Told by the comedian Eric Sedeño [in January 2026](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=yjs2d3Yrwe4) that she makes every joke as hard as she can, she said, "I feel like I'm always saying I could do more, be bigger, go harder. So like it's good to know it's landing."

So why does the Six lead? It comes down to what happens when the approval comes. A Three tends to take admiration in as proof and turn it into the next goal. Reiter pushes it away. She apologizes for a compliment, wants God to hold the praise, borrows Madonna's idea that she only manages her talent, and after her biggest hit predicts her own downfall. Her roommate and Soresi, who see her up close, describe the aversion independently, and in September 2026 a third host noticed it too.

The fear she names is the second clue. A Three's dread is usually failing in public. Asked directly, Reiter said, "I don't know if it's necessarily a fear of failure." What she describes instead is the danger of trying fully, and of success itself.

And when she pictures enough, it is small and shared. Driving to the airport in 2024 after finishing a film shoot in Baton Rouge, she said, she prayed: "I'm so grateful if this is all it ever is is just like making like little indie films with my friends." Asked about credit for an idea in February 2026, she said, "it wasn't really my idea it was all of our idea cuz we were improvising."

The case has limits. The follower ladder fits both types, because a Six can keep climbing when no number ever feels safe ("It never feels real," she told Piso). Much of the record is comedy, where self-deprecation is the joke. And she does use the audience as a gauge. Of a live show she said, "I'm always trying to like make people break cuz I'm like I want I need to know."

The clearest test is still ahead. Her father said in September 2026 that SNL was not letting her give interviews, and none had appeared by mid-October. When she can describe a hard week there at length, will she credit the response (Three), or the castmates, preparation and someone in charge who got her through it (Six)?

## Why not a One, a Nine or a Two?

[Type 1](/enneagram-corner/enneagram-type-1) has real support. On the parody set, she told Yvette, she was "the one friend that's like no but like we have to take this serious you guys," and the mistakes list is a One's habit. But her self-review is about whether she hurt someone ("I said this like weird thing to my friend. What what how do I fix that now?"), she checks it against other people rather than an inner standard, and a One rarely describes a lifetime of half-effort.

[Type 9](/enneagram-corner/enneagram-type-9) fits the coasting. In 2022 she called herself "a procrastinator and a lazy person at heart," and she avoids confrontation. But a Nine settles when idle, and in 2024 she said "the most time when I get stressed is when I'm not doing stuff." Her output, a weekly podcast for a year and a nearly 45-minute parody she produced with friends, also argues against the label.

[Type 2](/enneagram-corner/enneagram-type-2) fits the people-pleasing, but not the distance she keeps. Leaving American High, she told friends, "please text me cuz like I won't text you," because "I don't want to feel like needy."

## What the type doesn't explain

She craves novelty. After _Ginger Twinsies_, an Off-Broadway parody of _The Parent Trap_ that ran from July to October 2025, she said, "I don't know if I enjoyed the repetition," but "performing live every night was awesome." Then: "I just need like novelty." She keeps "a couple projects going on at a time" so there is always "something else to immediately move on to."

She also defies the crowd. When a parody of Harry Styles fan fiction brought her about a million followers around 2020, fans wanted more. "I'm not going to be like the Harry Styles girl," she decided. Five years later she put it more bluntly:

> "Because the people wanted me to do it. And I said, 'Fuck the people.'"

She said in 2025 that she was "not a risk taker" and was "protecting myself" on a four-wheeler, yet Sedeño told her, "you have no shame to just like make the joke as hard as you can." A Six can push straight at what scares her, but the simpler reading may be that her risks happen inside a bit, where a character takes the hit.

She can be bossy, and she knows it. Telling the comedian Sabrina Wu on [_That's Enough_](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=2T-yIeGPqXs) in January 2026 how she handles riffs she doesn't like, she said, "I would shut that down immediately," and then judged herself: "And that's not fair." Her younger sister, Molly, says that for a while "it was really hard to go on vacations with you."

And she is trusting in a way the suspicious-Six stereotype doesn't predict. Her producer, Lizzy Stewart, told her in the March 2026 anniversary episode, "if I tell you something you're going to assume that I'm telling you the truth," and "You're so earnestly here." That trust sits right beside her worry that her friends may not really be her friends.

Her oversharing is selective, too. She told Soresi in 2025 that "the real overshare stuff would be like sad and like not funny." In October 2025, on [_Literally Life Changing_](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=2zVjVhc2TBY), she said "nobody knows me and nobody knows actually really what I'm feeling." Stewart says Reiter has only ever asked her to cut one story from the show: "You cut it for the other person."

## "You will believe it"

Near the end of [a conversation](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=crG4upus05Y) recorded before the casting news and released on September 23, 2026, a week after it, Reiter repeated a line of praise about herself. The host, Drew Afualo, was watching her face:

> "You're you're you're wincing when you say it, but you will believe it."

Keep saying it, Afualo added, and "you'll stop grimacing when you say it."

The two readings make different predictions from here. If Reiter is a Three, a season of applause will teach her to believe it. If she is a Six, the applause will keep feeling like something to hand to someone else.
