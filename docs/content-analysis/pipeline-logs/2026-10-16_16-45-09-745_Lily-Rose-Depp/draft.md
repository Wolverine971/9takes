Lily-Rose Depp auditioned for Robert Eggers's _Nosferatu_ over Zoom. She was at her family's place in the French countryside, and one of her audition scenes was "a big freak-out scene." So she gathered things to throw.

> "I had a roll of paper towels, and some books, and I just threw shit all over the room. I started moving around like crazy. I wanted to show that I wasn't scared to go there."

The audition left Eggers in tears, [Vanity Fair](https://www.vanityfair.com/hollywood/story/lily-rose-depp-nosferatu-awards-insider) reported in November 2024. Depp remembers the nerves. "I was really nervous," she told the magazine, and she had expected to lose the part: "I thought for sure it wasn't going to go my way."

She got it. Ahead of the film's December release, David Rooney wrote in [The Hollywood Reporter](https://www.hollywoodreporter.com/movies/movie-reviews/nosferatu-review-bill-skarsgard-lily-rose-depp-nicholas-hoult-robert-eggers-1236074321/) that "the movie belongs to Depp, whose performance is a revelation."

That pairing runs through her adult career. She expects to lose the role, and then she goes further in it than almost anyone would ask.

Depp was born on May 27, 1999, in Neuilly-sur-Seine, outside Paris, the daughter of Johnny Depp and the French singer and actor Vanessa Paradis. She grew up between Los Angeles and France, became a Chanel ambassador at 16, in 2015, and left school in 2016 to act. She made her screen debut in Kevin Smith's _Tusk_ (2014). Years of French and independent film followed, with two César nominations for Most Promising Actress, before HBO's _The Idol_ (2023) and _Nosferatu_ (2024) brought her to a far bigger audience.

What makes her interesting is where she goes for steadiness when the doubt arrives. Her answer has barely changed since 2021.

## What is Lily-Rose Depp's personality type?

Lily-Rose Depp's personality type is Enneagram Type 6, the Loyalist. Sixes belong to the head triad, and the emotion at its center is fear. Depp names her fear often, expects to lose the parts she wants, and steadies herself by trusting the people who chose her. Then she walks straight into the roles that scare her.

A [Type 6](/enneagram-corner/enneagram-type-6) lives with a running question: is this safe, and can I count on it? Sixes scan ahead for what could go wrong, look for someone or something solid to lean on, and stay loyal to what holds. Some Sixes, called counterphobic, handle fear by walking into it, as if to prove it has no hold on them.

This is a read of her public record, held at medium confidence. Nearly all of that record is interviews given while she was promoting something, and circumstance explains part of every pattern below. The strongest rival is [Type 4](/enneagram-corner/enneagram-type-4), the Individualist, because of her pull toward darkness and the misunderstood. It gets its full case further down.

## "Are they sure that I'm the one?"

In 2021 her _Silent Night_ co-star [Keira Knightley](/personality-analysis/keira-knightley) interviewed her for [Interview](https://www.interviewmagazine.com/film/whats-it-like-being-lily-rose-depp-keira-knightley-finds-out) and asked whether she ever suffered from anxiety on set or stage fright.

"Definitely," Depp said. "The peak of that is right before I start something."

> "It's possible for me to get in my head and be like, 'Am I going to be able to do this? What have I signed up for? Are they sure that I'm the one?'"

She told herself the nerves meant she cared. Then she named what steadies her: "And trust is so huge. There's so much to be said about feeling comfortable and connected with your partners, with your director, and with your crew members."

The same sequence returns with every big job. Of _The Idol_, she told [Elle](https://www.elle.com/culture/celebrities/a41894075/0125-0141-an-idol-rising-december-2022/) in November 2022, "I did not think at all that this was going to go my way." She called her casting "the best surprise ever" and said, "I can't believe they wanted me to do it." In February 2023 she told [i-D](https://i-d.co/article/lily-rose-depp-interview-the-idol/) how she handles the feeling that she doesn't deserve the chance:

> "You just have to jump in and have some kind of faith that, if they've chosen me, then hopefully I've been chosen for a reason."

By _Nosferatu_ the question had the same shape. She told Vanity Fair that when someone asked her about the hardest part of the role, she answered, "Getting over the imposter syndrome of, like, 'Why am I here and why do these people think that I can do this?'"

Look at where the reassurance comes from. Each time, she settles the doubt by leaning on the judgment of the people who did the choosing. They must have had a reason. She rarely answers it by naming what is special about her. That outward move is the center of the Six reading.

Imposter feelings are close to universal among actors, especially ones whose place is argued over in public. What points toward a type is how she resolves the feeling, and that comes from her own account in interviews tied to a release.

The rejection behind the forecasts was real. Defending her in 2023, her mother told Madame Figaro, as quoted by [Paris Match Belgique](https://www.parismatch.be/actualites/people/2023/04/07/les-gens-se-trompent-vanessa-paradis-sort-du-silence-et-defend-sa-fille-lily-rose-depp-accusee-de-favoritisme-SVOWB757NBHQZGWDRGBIVIXB2I/), that her daughter had gone to many castings where she wasn't chosen, and had been unhappy about it, like everyone else. Paradis is the most interested witness there is.

Interviewers notice the nerves in person. Elle's writer found a "self-effacing woman" who apologized for being a few minutes late because of a sick cat. i-D's writer found her "endearingly jittery." When the writer admitted to getting nervous before interviews, she said she gets just as nervous being interviewed. "We can be nervous together!"

## "Ready to see me fail"

The doubt has an audience. In the same 2022 Elle interview she was asked about the "nepo baby" debate. "I'm familiar," she said.

"I can definitely say that nothing is going to get you the part except for being right for the part," she went on. "The internet cares a lot more about who your family is than the people who are casting you in things. Maybe you get your foot in the door, but you still just have your foot in the door." She also said, "I just hear it a lot more about women, and I don't think that it's a coincidence."

The remarks drew public criticism, [PAPER](https://www.papermag.com/lily-rose-depp-nepotism-comments) reported, and pulled other famous families into the argument.

Three months later her tone had changed. "I'm so careful about these conversations now," she told i-D. Her parents, she said, had done their best to give her a normal childhood. "And obviously, that still was not a normal childhood."

That adjustment fits a Six's sensitivity to how the group is reading her. Any publicist would also have advised it.

By 2024 she described the scrutiny as fuel.

> "I feel like people have been ready to see me fail, in a way, since I was a kid. That has made me only want to work harder and prove people wrong. Not in a vindictive way at all, but just in a sense of, like, fuel to my fire."

Proving people wrong through effort could point to Type 3, the Achiever, and she has been a face of Chanel since 2015. What strains that reading is how readily she volunteers doubt and expected failure instead of projecting success. The goal she names is to be seen as reliable: "I do want to prove that I'm a hard worker and I'm not here for anything else but to work hard."

## "I like the fear a little bit"

In the i-D answer about being chosen for a reason, she kept going. "I think that there's nothing more exciting than being like, 'Wow, I'm so nervous. I feel so challenged by this. How on earth am I going to pull this off?' You could ask for no better fuel to make you want to work really, really hard." She finished the thought a few lines later.

> "If you're not scared, then what's the point? I like the fear a little bit."

Her choices back that up. In 2021 she told Knightley, "Weird and wonderful is definitely the direction I'm trying to go." Of _The Idol_, she told Elle, "I'm not interested in making anything puritanical. I'm not interested in making anything that doesn't challenge me, or challenge other people, honestly." For _Nosferatu_ she trained in butoh, rehearsed the possession scenes in a corset, and held still for long stretches covered in stage blood. During one of them, she told [Natalie Portman](/personality-analysis/natalie-portman) in an [Interview](https://www.interviewmagazine.com/film/im-a-sucker-for-a-crazy-love-story-lily-rose-depp-by-natalie-portman) conversation published in December 2024, "I was just thinking to myself, 'Everything is mental. I'm good. I'm chilling.'"

Off set, the fear doesn't turn into fuel. When Portman asked about the most daredevil thing she had done, Depp said, "I used to be way more daredevil-ish when I was a kid, and now I'm scared of everything." Asked what happened, she said, "I don't know! I think the world started to scare me a lot."

Put those together and you get the counterphobic Six. Fear is the standing condition. The answer is to walk toward the specific thing that scares her, so the fear can't win. An Eight or a Seven would talk about appetite or control. She talks about being scared and about proving she isn't.

Plenty of actors call fear fuel. The weight here comes from how often she frames her riskiest choices that way, across three years and three outlets.

## Inside a frame she trusts

The risk comes with a condition, and she states it whenever she describes intimate or extreme work. The people around her have to feel safe.

In 2022 she told Elle that Sam Levinson and Abel Tesfaye (The Weeknd), two of the co-creators of _The Idol_, were "two people who make me feel incredibly safe and protected."

> "Talk about trust and comfort. I had to go to a lot of really vulnerable places in the show. Abel was the best partner that I could have ever asked for. He has my back and I have his."

That was to i-D in 2023. At the Cannes press conference that May, [Eye For Film](https://www.eyeforfilm.co.uk/feature/2023-05-23-lily-rose-uncovers-the-dark-side-of-celebrity-as-her-new-tv-series-shows-in-cannes-feature-story-by-richard-mowe) reported, she summed it up: "It's all about surrounding yourself with good people."

Structure does the same job for her. On _Nosferatu_ she expected to improvise the possession scenes and found them choreographed with the movement coach Marie-Gabrielle Rotie. "I was grateful not to have to shape it on my own," she told Portman. "I didn't realize how choreographed it was going to be, but that helped infuse every moment with an intention," she told Vanity Fair. Many scenes were shot in a single take, and she described what that did to the set: "Nobody wants to be the one that messes it up for everybody, which brings this real team feeling. I felt a lot of support from everyone."

Her worry is letting the team down. Nicholas Hoult, her co-star, saw the other side of that. "There was never a moment of 'I've done my bit, now it's on you'," he told [Harper's Bazaar UK](https://www.harpersbazaar.com/uk/culture/a63212831/lily-rose-depp-interview-nosferatu/). "I'd work with her again in a heartbeat."

Some of this is the job. On a sexually explicit series, trust-and-safety language is standard, and Eggers's storyboarded method imposes form on every actor in the scene. Still, she didn't chafe at the fixed form. She was relieved by it, which counts more against the Four than it proves the Six.

## Standing by _The Idol_

The costliest test of that trust came in public. On March 1, 2023, three months before _The Idol_ aired, Rolling Stone published a report on its production. As the magazine later [summarized it](https://www.rollingstone.com/tv-movies/tv-movie-news/the-idol-team-respond-rolling-stone-report-cannes-premiere-1234740292/), interviews with 13 members of the cast and crew described often chaotic working conditions and a show that had taken a dark turn. HBO denied the claims to [Entertainment Tonight](https://www.etonline.com/node/200095) and said the creative team had been "committed to creating a safe, collaborative, and mutually respectful working environment."

Depp answered with a statement about Levinson himself, which ET published the next day.

> "Sam is, for so many reasons, the best director I have ever worked with. Never have I felt more supported or respected in a creative space, my input and opinions more valued."

At the Cannes press conference in May, as Rolling Stone reported, she said it again: "it's always sad and disheartening to hear mean, false things about someone you care about, and it wasn't my experience shooting the show."

The series premiered at Cannes to harsh reviews, aired on HBO that June, and was cancelled after one season.

By November 2024 there was no show left to sell. "I love it. Honestly, I love it," she told Vanity Fair. Some people, she said, were always going to find it too much. "But I stand by it. We made a choice and we went for it." Of Levinson and his wife, the producer Ashley Levinson, she said, "They're like family to me." Asked whether she was disappointed there was no second season, she said, "I know that we'll do something again together one day, and I'm excited for that day. Everything happens for a reason."

Chosen for a reason in 2023. Everything happens for a reason in 2024. Loyalty to the people who backed you, held hardest when they are under attack, is the Six's signature.

She had professional reasons to defend her own show in 2023, and HBO had more. Those reasons explain less of 2024, when she was promoting a different film for a different studio, though no actor gains by criticizing a former director in the press.

## "Hold tight to the people you love"

Loyalty is a value she names outright. "I love people who love their people," she told Harper's Bazaar UK in a November 2024 interview published in January 2025.

> "The fashion industry, the film industry... these are crazy businesses. When you find people who feel real and you connect with them, you have to hold onto that."

The people she means are mostly long-held. "I have had my LA best friends since forever," she told the magazine, and she told Portman, whom she met at 16, that "you have to have people around you that you've known forever that are going to anchor you." The Harper's piece ends with her heading off for fish and chips with her long-term security guard, who says she is "like a daughter" to him.

Talking about the November 2024 US election in that interview, she said, "It's a horrific time, and I'm scared, and I'm sad." Then she gave her answer to it: "When things feel out of control and you feel powerless, you have to hold tight to the people you love and look after those people."

## Why not Type 4?

The Four reading deserves a real hearing, because much of it comes straight from her. "I'm interested in the darker underbelly of things," she told Harper's Bazaar UK. At three she watched _Edward Scissorhands_, in which her father plays the gentle outsider, and was upset by it, "Not because I thought he was scary, but because everyone was being so mean to him and I got really upset." Then she connected it to _Nosferatu_:

> "Edward's the good guy and Nosferatu's kind of the bad guy, but there's a part of me that feels a little bit of empathy for Nosferatu. I mean, am I sick for feeling that way?"

She describes herself in Four language. "I feel like I've always been full of dualities, and my double culture adds to that," she told Portman. "There are so many sides to me," she told Harper's. "I really feel that paradox in myself." She calls acting "an emotional outlet for me—I feel like it helps me, I don't know, heal things with myself, perhaps."

She measures herself against her mother. When Paradis sends her old photos and says, "We're twins," Depp told i-D, her answer is, "You're so much prettier than me." And she admires stars who kept their distance. Of Lauren Bacall and Gene Tierney, whom she studied for _The Idol_, she told i-D, "They almost had this confidence in the discomfort that they could provoke in people."

Her father, [Johnny Depp](/personality-analysis/johnny-depp), whom 9takes reads as a Type 4, spent years making gothic films with Tim Burton. Asked by Harper's whether the taste runs in the family, she said, "We're very different actors, but of course you're the product of your environment." A shared taste is not a shared type, but it is a reason to take the Four seriously.

So why does the Six still lead? Three reasons.

First, where she goes when doubt hits. A Four steadies herself through her own feeling, through what is true and distinct in her. Depp points outward, to the people who chose her and the reason they must have had.

Second, how she holds the dark feeling. Her childhood memory is about a crowd being cruel to an innocent, and her sympathy for the monster arrives with a check on whether it is acceptable: "am I sick for feeling that way?" She also places the darkness in the job: "I'm a happy person, so I like to explore the underbelly of that at work." She doesn't go method, she told Vanity Fair, partly because "I'm too attached to my life, I feel like." She needs "to be able to call my friends at the end of the day and be myself." And she told Portman:

> "I love going to the butcher and the grocery store and having dinner at my friends' houses. Or, having a lazy morning with my boyfriend. Those things are always going to be the center of my life."

Third, the frame. A Four is more likely to chafe at imposed form. She was "grateful not to have to shape it on my own."

Even the dualities have a literal source. She ties them to two families and two countries: "when I'm with my dad's side of the family, I'm like, yeah, we're all Kentuckians."

A reader who puts her aesthetic and emotional life first could fairly call her a Four. Nothing in the public record settles it, which is why the read is held at medium confidence.

A [Type 9](/enneagram-corner/enneagram-type-9) case leans on how she shrinks her worries ("Girl, you don't matter that much," she told i-D) and prizes ordinary routines. But a Nine tends to numb fear, and Depp names hers again and again.

## What the type doesn't explain

Some of her record sits awkwardly with an anxious Six.

At 16 she made an early, irreversible decision. She left school, she told Knightley, because "I have really strong gut instincts, and I felt like, 'You know what? This is my moment to transition into the next phase of my life.'" Counterphobic commitment explains part of that. It does not explain the confidence.

She keeps a deliberate distance, and she gives a craft reason for it. Audiences, she told Australia's Daily Telegraph, as quoted by [Music Times](https://www.musictimes.com/articles/107196/20241230/lily-rose-depp-talks-privacy-fame-hollywoods-allure-actor-needs-anonymity.htm) in December 2024, are "not supposed to get to know 'you' that well, because you want them to believe you as other characters." That reason needs no type at all.

Then there is her father. During the 2022 defamation trial between Johnny Depp and Amber Heard she made no public comment, which frustrated some of his fans, [Jezebel](https://jezebel.com/lily-rose-depp-doesnt-want-to-be-defined-by-her-dad-1849790873) noted. Explaining that choice to Elle that November, she said:

> "I feel really entitled to my secret garden of thoughts. I also think that I'm not here to answer for anybody, and I feel like for a lot of my career, people have really wanted to define me by the men in my life, whether that's my family members or my boyfriends, whatever. And I'm really ready to be defined for the things that I put out there."

She vouched loudly for a director in 2023 and kept silent about her own father in 2022. Her stated reason is privacy and a wish to be judged on her work. Legal caution, or simply not wanting to relive it, would explain the silence as well. The record shows only what she said.

The record is also thin where it matters most. There is no long-form testimony from the people closest to her, only her mother's defense and a director's or co-star's praise in a release week. Nobody has described Depp let down by someone she trusted. That is the scene that would test the loyalty reading hardest.

## The farmer's wife

Eggers cast her again. In _Werwulf_, set in 14th-century England, she plays the wife of a farmer, played by Aaron Taylor-Johnson, in a town haunted by a beast. It is due in US theaters on December 25, 2026, and in the UK on January 1, 2027. In September 2026 she premiered _Alpha Gang_, a Zellner brothers science-fiction comedy, at Telluride on September 5 and then at TIFF, playing one of a gang of aliens who find themselves with human emotions.

Eggers described the _Werwulf_ role to [Empire](https://www.empireonline.com/movies/features/werwulf-trailer-explained-by-robert-eggers-interview-exclusive/) in June 2026. The character "is so far from Lily, physically and in her general way of holding herself," he said. "So to be this mediaeval farmer's wife, this mother who is super grounded, was a challenge which she embraced and was super into."

When she talks about that role, listen for where she says the steadiness came from: the people and the frame around her, or a feeling she found in herself. The first keeps the Six reading. The second would strengthen the Four.

She gave Portman the clearest picture of how the system works. The inner life stays protected until the work begins.

> "You have all your super-private feelings in your backpack, and you go to work and the scene starts and you let it out."
