Lily-Rose Depp auditioned for Robert Eggers's _Nosferatu_ over Zoom. She was at her family's place in the French countryside, and one of her audition scenes was "a big freak-out scene." So she gathered things to throw.

> "I had a roll of paper towels, and some books, and I just threw shit all over the room. I started moving around like crazy. I wanted to show that I wasn't scared to go there."

The audition left Eggers in tears, [Vanity Fair](https://www.vanityfair.com/hollywood/story/lily-rose-depp-nosferatu-awards-insider) reported in November 2024. Depp remembers the nerves. "I was really nervous," she told the magazine, and she had expected to lose the part: "I thought for sure it wasn't going to go my way."

She got the part of Ellen Hutter, a young woman traumatized by the vampire Count Orlok. Ahead of the film's December 2024 release, David Rooney wrote in [The Hollywood Reporter](https://www.hollywoodreporter.com/movies/movie-reviews/nosferatu-review-bill-skarsgard-lily-rose-depp-nicholas-hoult-robert-eggers-1236074321/) that "the movie belongs to Depp, whose performance is a revelation."

Depp was born on May 27, 1999, in Neuilly-sur-Seine, outside Paris, the daughter of Johnny Depp and the French singer and actor Vanessa Paradis, and grew up between Los Angeles and France. At 14 she was "pretty casually told to jump into a scene" on the set of Kevin Smith's _Tusk_ (2014), a film her father was in, [i-D](https://i-d.co/article/lily-rose-depp-interview-the-idol/) reported in 2023. Smith is the father of her childhood best friend. In 2015 she became a Chanel ambassador, a house her mother had worked with since the 1990s, and at 16 she left school to act.

French and independent films followed, with two César nominations for Most Promising Actress. Then came HBO's _The Idol_ (2023), a sexually explicit series in which she played Jocelyn, a pop star whose world, as Elle described the trailer, featured "paparazzi, nightclubs, cocaine, and S&M."

It went the same way both times. She expected to lose the part. Then she gave it everything it asked for.

The interesting part is what happens in between, once the doubt arrives.

## What is Lily-Rose Depp's personality type?

Lily-Rose Depp's personality type is Enneagram Type 6, the Loyalist. Sixes belong to the head triad, and the emotion at its center is fear. Depp names her fear often, answers it mostly through people she trusts and hard work, and then takes the roles that scare her.

A [Type 6](/enneagram-corner/enneagram-type-6) lives with a running question: is this safe, and can I count on it? Sixes scan ahead for what could go wrong, look for someone or something solid to lean on, and stay loyal to what holds. Some Sixes, called counterphobic, handle fear by walking into it.

This is a read of her public record, held at medium confidence. Nearly all of it comes from interviews given while she was promoting something, and circumstance explains part of every pattern below. The strongest rival is [Type 4](/enneagram-corner/enneagram-type-4), the Individualist, and much of its case comes from her own words.

## "Are they sure that I'm the one?"

In 2021 her _Silent Night_ co-star [Keira Knightley](/personality-analysis/keira-knightley) interviewed her for [Interview](https://www.interviewmagazine.com/film/whats-it-like-being-lily-rose-depp-keira-knightley-finds-out) and asked whether she ever suffered from anxiety on set or stage fright.

"Definitely," Depp said. "The peak of that is right before I start something."

> "It's possible for me to get in my head and be like, 'Am I going to be able to do this? What have I signed up for? Are they sure that I'm the one?'"

Her first answer was her own: the nerves were "a sign that I'm invested." Then she named what steadies her: "And trust is so huge. There's so much to be said about feeling comfortable and connected with your partners, with your director, and with your crew members."

When Knightley asked whether she worried about being judged because of her parents, the answer pointed inward: "I'm not the same kind of actor as my dad or my mom, and I'm very much my own person."

Of _The Idol_, she told [Elle](https://www.elle.com/culture/celebrities/a41894075/0125-0141-an-idol-rising-december-2022/) in November 2022, "I did not think at all that this was going to go my way," because "everybody and their mother goes in for these things." In February 2023 she told i-D how she handles the doubt:

> "You just have to jump in and have some kind of faith that, if they've chosen me, then hopefully I've been chosen for a reason."

By _Nosferatu_ the doubt had the same shape. The hardest part of the role, she told Vanity Fair, was "Getting over the imposter syndrome of, like, 'Why am I here and why do these people think that I can do this?'" Her next sentence turned inward.

> "Getting to a place where I felt confident enough within myself to be like, 'I can do this and I am here for a reason,' I definitely have struggled with that."

Chosen for a reason in 2023. Here for a reason in 2024. The phrase stayed, and the reason moved closer to home. When the confidence isn't there, her fallback is plainer: "I may feel this way, but I have to go to work tomorrow."

Look at where the reassurance comes from, and there are two answers. Before a role starts, she reaches outward, to the trust of her collaborators and the judgment of the people who chose her. When the question is her parents or her right to be there, she reaches inward, to being "my own person" and "here for a reason."

The Six reading rests on the first being her reflex and the second being the thing she works toward. She says she has "struggled with" the inward answer, and when it fails she falls back on showing up. A Four reading would reverse that order.

Imposter feelings are close to universal among actors, especially ones whose place is argued over in public. What points toward a type is how she resolves the feeling.

The rejection behind the forecasts was real. Her mother told Madame Figaro in 2023, as quoted by [Paris Match Belgique](https://www.parismatch.be/actualites/people/2023/04/07/les-gens-se-trompent-vanessa-paradis-sort-du-silence-et-defend-sa-fille-lily-rose-depp-accusee-de-favoritisme-SVOWB757NBHQZGWDRGBIVIXB2I/), that her daughter had gone to many castings where she wasn't chosen. Paradis is the most interested witness there is.

## "Ready to see me fail"

The doubt has an audience. In late 2022 the internet was arguing about "nepo babies," children of famous parents assumed to have gotten ahead through them, and Depp was one of the examples it named. Asked about it by Elle, she said, "I'm familiar."

"I can definitely say that nothing is going to get you the part except for being right for the part," she went on. "The internet cares a lot more about who your family is than the people who are casting you in things. Maybe you get your foot in the door, but you still just have your foot in the door." She said she hears the label "a lot more about women," and acknowledged, "I know my childhood didn't look like everybody's childhood."

The remarks drew public criticism anyway, [PAPER](https://www.papermag.com/lily-rose-depp-nepotism-comments) reported. In February 2023 she told i-D, "I'm so careful about these conversations now." Any publicist would advise that caution, and it fits a Six or a Three equally well.

By November 2024, she told Vanity Fair, the scrutiny had become fuel.

> "I feel like people have been ready to see me fail, in a way, since I was a kid. That has made me only want to work harder and prove people wrong. Not in a vindictive way at all, but just in a sense of, like, fuel to my fire."

Proving people wrong could point to [Type 3](/enneagram-corner/enneagram-type-3), the Achiever. Her volunteered doubt doesn't rule that out. In the same Vanity Fair answer she called humility "incredibly important, especially in this business," and after 2022 modesty was also the safer image. The goal she names is effort: "I do want to prove that I'm a hard worker and I'm not here for anything else but to work hard."

What strains the Three is that in 2024 she said she loved _The Idol_, a panned, cancelled show an image-minded Three might have let fade. Pride in her own work could explain that for any type, so Type 3 stays in the running.

## "I like the fear a little bit"

The i-D answer about being chosen for a reason kept going: "I think that there's nothing more exciting than being like, 'Wow, I'm so nervous. I feel so challenged by this. How on earth am I going to pull this off?'" A few lines later she finished the thought.

> "If you're not scared, then what's the point? I like the fear a little bit."

Her choices back that up. "Weird and wonderful is definitely the direction I'm trying to go," she told Knightley in 2021. For _Nosferatu_ she trained in butoh, rehearsed the possession scenes in a corset, and held still for long stretches covered in stage blood. During one of them, she told [Natalie Portman](/personality-analysis/natalie-portman) in an [Interview](https://www.interviewmagazine.com/film/im-a-sucker-for-a-crazy-love-story-lily-rose-depp-by-natalie-portman) conversation published in December 2024, "I was just thinking to myself, 'Everything is mental. I'm good. I'm chilling.'"

She talks about appetite too. "There's nothing more fun to me than sinking my teeth into something," she told Elle, and she told Vanity Fair she gravitates toward big emotional roles "because I like the challenge of it. I like to get in there."

Off set, the fear doesn't turn into fuel. When Portman asked about the most daredevil thing she had done, Depp said, "I used to be way more daredevil-ish when I was a kid, and now I'm scared of everything."

The exchange was a light one, about going to space and jumping out of planes. Asked what happened, she said, "I think the world started to scare me a lot," and wondered, "I wonder if I'm more aware of the dangers of the world or something?"

My reading is the counterphobic Six: fear comes up often in her own account, and at work she walks toward the specific thing that scares her. That label could absorb boldness on set and caution off it alike, so it needs a line. Hers falls at the frame. She walks into fear inside a structure she trusts, a director, a choreographer, a crew, and avoids it where there is none. If she took big risks with no trusted structure around them, or described her riskiest work only as appetite, the reading would weaken.

Plenty of actors call fear fuel, and only two of her statements frame a risky choice explicitly through fear: the 2023 i-D answer and the 2024 audition story. That is a pattern in her language, not proof of a motive.

## Inside a frame she trusts

That frame is a condition she states whenever she describes intimate or extreme work. In 2022 she told Elle that Sam Levinson and Abel Tesfaye (The Weeknd), two of the co-creators of _The Idol_, were "two people who make me feel incredibly safe and protected." In 2023 she told i-D about Tesfaye:

> "Talk about trust and comfort. I had to go to a lot of really vulnerable places in the show. Abel was the best partner that I could have ever asked for. He has my back and I have his."

Structure does the same job for her. On _Nosferatu_ she expected to improvise the possession scenes and found them choreographed with the movement coach Marie-Gabrielle Rotie. "I was grateful not to have to shape it on my own," she told Portman.

Many scenes were single takes, which made each one depend on the whole crew, "the dolly grip, and the focus puller, and the cameraman." Her description of that set: "Nobody wants to be the one that messes it up for everybody, which brings this real team feeling. I felt a lot of support from everyone."

Nicholas Hoult, her co-star, saw her side of it. "There was never a moment of 'I've done my bit, now it's on you'," he told [Harper's Bazaar UK](https://www.harpersbazaar.com/uk/culture/a63212831/lily-rose-depp-interview-nosferatu/). "I'd work with her again in a heartbeat."

Some of this is the job. On a sexually explicit series, trust-and-safety language is standard, and Eggers's storyboarded method imposes form on every actor in the scene. Still, she didn't chafe at the fixed form. She was relieved by it, which counts more against the Four than it proves the Six.

## Standing by _The Idol_

The most public test of that trust came on March 1, 2023, three months before _The Idol_ aired, when Rolling Stone published a report on its production. As the magazine later [summarized it](https://www.rollingstone.com/tv-movies/tv-movie-news/the-idol-team-respond-rolling-stone-report-cannes-premiere-1234740292/), interviews with 13 members of the cast and crew described often chaotic working conditions and a show that had taken a dark turn. Crew members said it changed after the original director, Amy Seimetz, left and Levinson took over, and three sources said his scripts contained disturbing sexual and physically violent scenes between her character and Tesfaye's. HBO denied the claims to [Entertainment Tonight](https://www.etonline.com/node/200095), saying the creative team had been "committed to creating a safe, collaborative, and mutually respectful working environment."

The report concerned her own role. Rolling Stone said it had included a statement from her defending the series, and on March 2 ET published her defense of Levinson.

> "Sam is, for so many reasons, the best director I have ever worked with. Never have I felt more supported or respected in a creative space, my input and opinions more valued."

She went on about her own experience as the performer: Levinson cared "how we feel performing it" and "has always created an environment in which I felt seen, heard, and appreciated." At Cannes that May, Rolling Stone reported, she said, "it's always sad and disheartening to hear mean, false things about someone you care about, and it wasn't my experience shooting the show."

The series premiered at Cannes to harsh reviews, aired on HBO that June, and was cancelled after one season.

By November 2024 there was no show left to sell. "I love it. Honestly, I love it," she told Vanity Fair. "I stand by it. We made a choice and we went for it." She called the shoot "a huge turning point for me" and said of Levinson and his wife, the producer Ashley Levinson, "They're like family to me. I'm excited to get to work with them again one day."

The plainest explanation needs no type. She was the lead, the claims concerned her character's scenes, and she described a set she remembered differently and work she was proud of. She had professional reasons to defend her own show in 2023, and HBO had more. Those reasons explain less of 2024, when she was promoting a different film for a different studio, though no actor gains by criticizing a former director in the press.

What the loyalty reading adds is the shape of the defense: she vouched for the person, not only the work, and still called the Levinsons family. "I love people who love their people," she told Harper's Bazaar UK that November. Talking about that month's US election, she said, "It's a horrific time, and I'm scared, and I'm sad." Then she gave her answer to it: "When things feel out of control and you feel powerless, you have to hold tight to the people you love and look after those people."

Plenty of types defend their people. What points to the Six is the link she draws herself, from fear straight to holding on.

## Why not Type 4?

A Four is driven by identity: the search for what is true and singular in herself, and a sharp sense of what feels missing. The case for Depp as a Four is strong, and most of it comes from her.

"I'm interested in the darker underbelly of things," she told Harper's Bazaar UK. At three she watched _Edward Scissorhands_, in which her father plays the gentle outsider, and was upset by it, "Not because I thought he was scary, but because everyone was being so mean to him and I got really upset." Then she connected it to _Nosferatu_:

> "Edward's the good guy and Nosferatu's kind of the bad guy, but there's a part of me that feels a little bit of empathy for Nosferatu. I mean, am I sick for feeling that way?"

Her work is where the inner life goes. "The most exciting thing is getting to explore parts of myself," she told the magazine. "You get to hide behind all these beautiful artifices, the costumes, the story, but the place I'm drawing from is deeply personal." She calls acting "an emotional outlet for me—I feel like it helps me, I don't know, heal things with myself, perhaps," and says, "I'm better at crying than laughing."

The darkness doesn't stay at work, either. She doesn't go method, she told Vanity Fair, partly because "I'm too attached to my life," but on _Nosferatu_, "I do think I brought the darkness home, for sure... I was carrying her with me when I was at home."

She describes herself in Four language. "I feel like I've always been full of dualities, and my double culture adds to that," she told Portman, tying it partly to her family's two sides: "when I'm with them, I feel like I'm 100 percent French, and when I'm with my dad's side of the family, I'm like, yeah, we're all Kentuckians." To Vanity Fair she described respecting two artist parents while "trying to find my own identity in this world."

Her father, [Johnny Depp](/personality-analysis/johnny-depp), whom 9takes reads as a Type 4, spent years making gothic films with Tim Burton. Asked by Harper's whether the taste runs in the family, she said, "We're very different actors, but of course you're the product of your environment." A shared taste is not a shared type, but it is a reason to take the Four seriously.

So why does the Six still lead? Two reasons, and a weaker third.

First, what her doubts are about. A Four's doubt tends to circle identity: am I deficient, ordinary, unseen? Hers are mostly about the job and the people counting on her: "Am I going to be able to do this?" in 2021, "I really don't want to fuck this up" in 2023. Her answers run mostly through trust, preparation and showing up. The identity question is real, but it attaches to a literal circumstance, being two famous artists' child during a public argument about nepotism, which would press it on anyone.

Second, the link between fear and people. When things felt out of control in 2024, her answer was to "hold tight to the people you love." A Four is more likely to steady herself through what she feels than through whom she holds.

Third, and weakest, the frame. She was "grateful not to have to shape it on my own."

A reader who puts her emotional life and her search for identity first could fairly call her a Four, and the 2024 interviews push that way. Nothing in the public record settles it, which is why the read is held at medium confidence.

A [Type 9](/enneagram-corner/enneagram-type-9) case leans on how she shrinks her worries ("Girl, you don't matter that much," she told i-D) and prizes ordinary routines like "going to the butcher and the grocery store," as she told Portman. But a Nine tends to numb fear, and Depp names hers again and again.

## What the type doesn't explain

Some of her record sits awkwardly with an anxious Six.

At 16 she made an early, irreversible decision. She left school, she told Knightley, because "I have really strong gut instincts, and I felt like, 'You know what? This is my moment to transition into the next phase of my life.'" Counterphobic commitment explains part of that. It does not explain the confidence.

She keeps a deliberate distance and gives two reasons. One is craft: audiences, she told Australia's Daily Telegraph, as quoted by [Music Times](https://www.musictimes.com/articles/107196/20241230/lily-rose-depp-talks-privacy-fame-hollywoods-allure-actor-needs-anonymity.htm) in December 2024, are "not supposed to get to know 'you' that well, because you want them to believe you as other characters." The other is upbringing: "I've just been raised in a manner that has taught me that privacy is something that's important to protect," she told Elle in 2022. Neither needs a type.

She has named one exception. Harper's Bazaar UK reported in November 2024 that she had been with the musician Danielle Balbuena, who performs as 070 Shake, for two years. "I've always been really private about my personal life," Depp said, "but it's hard not to talk about her because I'm so happy."

Then there is her father. In May 2016, at 17, days after Amber Heard made abuse allegations against him in court, she posted on Instagram that he was "the sweetest most loving person I know," [CBS/AP](https://www.cbsnews.com/news/lily-rose-depp-comes-to-dad-johnny-depps-defense-after-domestic-violence-allegations/) reported. During the [2022 defamation trial](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Depp_v._Heard) between Johnny Depp and Heard she made no public comment, which frustrated some of his fans, [Jezebel](https://jezebel.com/lily-rose-depp-doesnt-want-to-be-defined-by-her-dad-1849790873) noted. She explained the choice to Elle that November:

> "When it's something that's so private and so personal that all of a sudden becomes not so personal…I feel really entitled to my secret garden of thoughts. I also think that I'm not here to answer for anybody."

People, she added, had long wanted "to define me by the men in my life."

She vouched loudly for her father in 2016, kept silent about him in 2022, and vouched loudly for a director in 2023. Her stated reasons for the silence are privacy, her upbringing and a wish to be judged on her work. Ordinary caution about a family's legal fight would explain it as well. The record shows only what she said.

The record is also thin where it matters most. As of October 2026 there is no long-form testimony from the people closest to her, only her mother's defense and a director's or co-star's praise in a release week. Nor has anyone yet described Depp let down by someone she trusted. That is the scene that would test the loyalty reading hardest. The record also never shows her questioning a director or institution she had backed, the skeptical side of a Six, so this Loyalist reading rests on one side of the type.

## The next test

Eggers cast her again. In _Werwulf_, a 14th-century werewolf film scheduled, as of October 2026, to open in US theaters on December 25, 2026, she plays a farmer's wife opposite Aaron Taylor-Johnson. Eggers told [Empire](https://www.empireonline.com/movies/features/werwulf-trailer-explained-by-robert-eggers-interview-exclusive/) in June 2026 that the character "is so far from Lily, physically and in her general way of holding herself," and called playing "this mother who is super grounded" a challenge she embraced.

Her _Werwulf_ press, from late 2026, is the next place to check where she says the steadiness came from: the people and the frame around her, or a feeling she found in herself. The first keeps the Six reading. The second, which she had already begun to describe by 2024, would strengthen the Four.

Whatever it shows, she has described the arrangement she wants. On _Nosferatu_, by her own account, some of it came home with her anyway.
