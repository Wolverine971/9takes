Lily-Rose Depp auditioned for Robert Eggers's _Nosferatu_ over Zoom, from her family's place in the French countryside. One of the scenes was "a big freak-out scene," she told [Vanity Fair](https://www.vanityfair.com/hollywood/story/lily-rose-depp-nosferatu-awards-insider) in November 2024, so she came prepared.

> "I had a roll of paper towels, and some books, and I just threw shit all over the room. I started moving around like crazy. I wanted to show that I wasn't scared to go there."

It worked. Eggers later told Deadline, as quoted by [Hello!](https://hellomagazine.com/film/733824/johnny-depps-daughter-lily-roses-performance-in-new-horror), "Myself, the casting director, and even the videographer were in tears, because it was so powerful."

The same Vanity Fair interview has the other half of the story. "I was really nervous," she said of that audition, and she had assumed she would lose: "I thought for sure it wasn't going to go my way."

She was scared, she said so, and she threw the paper towels anyway.

That pairing is the puzzle in her public record. By my count of her interviews from 2021 to 2025, fear is the hard feeling she names about herself most often. She also keeps choosing roles that demand a lot of exposure, emotional and physical. What connects the two, as far as her own words show, is trust. She goes all the way in where she feels safe.

She was born on May 27, 1999, in Neuilly-sur-Seine, outside Paris, to the actor Johnny Depp and the French singer and actor Vanessa Paradis, and grew up between Los Angeles and France with her younger brother, Jack ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lily-Rose_Depp)). Her first role came at 14, on the set of Kevin Smith's _Tusk_, a film her father was in, where she was "pretty casually told to jump into a scene," [i-D](https://i-d.co/article/lily-rose-depp-interview-the-idol/) reported. Smith's daughter was her childhood best friend. In 2015 Chanel, a house her mother had worked with since the 1990s, made her an ambassador at 15 or 16. She left school at 16 to act. French and independent films followed, including _The Dancer_ (2016), which earned her a César nomination, and _The King_ (2019). Then came Jocelyn, the pop star at the center of HBO's sexually explicit _The Idol_ (2023), and Ellen Hutter, the young woman in the grip of the vampire Count Orlok in _Nosferatu_ (2024).

## What is Lily-Rose Depp's personality type?

Lily-Rose Depp's personality type is [Enneagram Type 6](/enneagram-corner/enneagram-type-6), known as the Loyalist. Sixes are in the head triad, where the core emotion is fear. She talks about her fear often, steadies it with people and structures she trusts, and inside that safety she takes big creative risks.

The nine types sort into three triads by the hard feeling at their center. For the heart types (Two, Three, Four) it is shame. For the gut types (Eight, Nine, One) it is anger. For the head types (Five, Six, Seven) it is fear, and the three handle it differently. Fives pull back into knowledge. Sevens outrun it with fun.

Here is how that works for a Six, in plain steps. She scans ahead for what could go wrong. She looks for support she can count on: a person, a plan, a group, an authority. Once she has it, she can act, sometimes boldly. Without it, the worry keeps running.

Depp describes every one of those steps herself.

This is a read of her public interviews, held at medium confidence, and [Type 4](/enneagram-corner/enneagram-type-4) is a close rival with its own section below.

## "If you're not scared, then what's the point?"

In February 2023, before _The Idol_ aired, she told i-D how she handles the feeling that she might not belong in a role:

> "You just have to jump in and have some kind of faith that, if they've chosen me, then hopefully I've been chosen for a reason. That's all that you can do."

The answer goes on, and she quotes her own self-talk: "Okay, I really don't want to fuck this up, so let me work as hard as I possibly can to make sure that I have left no rock unturned." Then, in the next breath: "If you're not scared, then what's the point? I like the fear a little bit."

Faith in the people who chose her, preparation down to the last rock, and fear kept on as fuel. That is a Six's toolkit, laid out in one answer.

In 2021, [Keira Knightley](/personality-analysis/keira-knightley) asked her for [Interview](https://www.interviewmagazine.com/film/whats-it-like-being-lily-rose-depp-keira-knightley-finds-out) whether she gets anxious on set. "Definitely," she said. Before a job starts she can get in her head: "Am I going to be able to do this? What have I signed up for? Are they sure that I'm the one?"

Her first move in that answer is her own self-talk. The nerves are "a sign that I'm invested and excited and wanting to do the best job possible." Her second move is other people: "And trust is so huge. There's so much to be said about feeling comfortable and connected with your partners, with your director, and with your crew members."

The word keeps coming back. Sam Levinson, who co-created and directed _The Idol_, and her co-star Abel Tesfaye (the Weeknd), she told [Elle](https://www.elle.com/culture/celebrities/a41894075/0125-0141-an-idol-rising-december-2022/) in 2022, are "two people who make me feel incredibly safe and protected." Of Eggers, on the [Happy Sad Confused](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Ai7ieVFwSbw) podcast in January 2025: "I had that trust with him because I knew that no matter where he was leading me it was going to be a great place to be."

She likes being given a structure, too. Before _Nosferatu_ she expected to improvise the possession scenes, and they turned out to be tightly choreographed. "I was grateful not to have to shape it on my own," she told [Natalie Portman](/personality-analysis/natalie-portman) for [Interview](https://www.interviewmagazine.com/film/im-a-sucker-for-a-crazy-love-story-lily-rose-depp-by-natalie-portman) in December 2024. Eggers, she said on the podcast, is "not going to let you leave until that scene is perfect," and she found "comfort in that."

Some of this is the job. On a sexually explicit series, trust-and-safety language is standard, and a lot of it was said while promoting a show. She also talks plainly about appetite: "I really like emotional scenes because they're challenging. I feel stimulated by that," she told Vanity Fair. The trust talk on its own proves little. It matters because the same pattern shows up in her life off set.

## "Now I'm scared of everything"

When Portman asked whether she would go to space, Depp laughed: "No, I am so scared." Portman asked about the most daredevil thing she had ever done.

> "I used to be way more daredevil-ish when I was a kid, and now I'm scared of everything."

It was a light exchange about skydiving, bungee jumping and zip lining, so it shouldn't carry much weight, but her explanation is telling. "I think the world started to scare me a lot," she said, and a moment later: "I wonder if I'm more aware of the dangers of the world or something?"

Fame supplies plenty of dangers. She told i-D she overthinks being photographed: "If I leave my house and I look like shit and someone takes a photo, is it going to be, 'She looks like hell, she must be depressed?'" When strangers approach her, she told Elle, she worries "that they're going to think I'm really weird or rude because I just get really shy and kind of anxious." That second worry is about being judged, which is shame territory. Her line on fame itself is blunter: "I'm not here to give myself to the world to be eaten alive."

Her answer to danger is people she has had for a long time. "This industry can be so crazy and you can lose your footing really easily," she told Portman. "I feel like it's corny because people always say this, but you have to have people around you that you've known forever that are going to anchor you."

After the November 2024 US election she told [Harper's Bazaar UK](https://www.harpersbazaar.com/uk/culture/a63212831/lily-rose-depp-interview-nosferatu/), "It's a horrific time, and I'm scared, and I'm sad." Then she said what she does about it:

> "When things feel out of control and you feel powerless, you have to hold tight to the people you love and look after those people."

That is the clearest link in her record between fear and allegiance, and it was about politics, not a film she was selling.

Put the work and the life side by side and a pattern shows. Inside a frame she trusts (a director with a plan, a trusted co-star, friends she has known forever), she walks toward what scares her. Outside one, she steps back. She won't jump out of a plane. She told Vanity Fair, "I'm not on Twitter. I'm not reading stuff." She told Elle she likes to "retreat back into my real life and just be a normal person."

That claim can be tested. If she started taking big risks with no trusted people or structure around her, the Six reading would weaken.

## Standing by _The Idol_

On March 1, 2023, Rolling Stone published a report on _The Idol_'s production. Summarizing it in May, [Rolling Stone](https://www.rollingstone.com/tv-movies/tv-movie-news/the-idol-team-respond-rolling-stone-report-cannes-premiere-1234740292/) said 13 cast and crew members had described an "often chaotic" set and a show that changed after its original director, Amy Seimetz, left and Levinson stepped in. Three sources told the magazine that Levinson's scripts contained what Rolling Stone called "disturbing sexual and physically violent scenes" between Depp's and Tesfaye's characters.

The claims came from anonymous sources and were disputed. HBO said the early episodes "did not meet HBO standards" and that the team was committed to "a safe, collaborative, and mutually respectful working environment." Levinson said the specifics "felt completely foreign to me." This page doesn't try to settle any of it.

What matters here is that the reporting was about her own role. Rolling Stone noted that its report had included statements from Depp defending the series. On March 2, she gave a statement to [Entertainment Tonight](https://www.etonline.com/node/200095):

> "Sam is, for so many reasons, the best director I have ever worked with. Never have I felt more supported or respected in a creative space, my input and opinions more valued."

At Cannes that May she said, "it's always sad and disheartening to hear mean, false things about someone you care about, and it wasn't my experience shooting the show."

Each time, her stated reason is her own experience as the actor in those scenes. He "has always created an environment in which I felt seen, heard, and appreciated," her statement said. That is the plainest explanation, and it needs no type at all. The lead actor described a set she remembered differently. She was also promoting the show.

Promotion explains less of what came after. In November 2024, with the series panned and cancelled, she told Vanity Fair, "But I stand by it. We made a choice and we went for it." She added, "I'm very close with Sam and Ashley [Levinson]. They're like family to me," and said she hoped to work with them again. On the podcast in January 2025, the only part of that period that upset her, she said, was "people talking negatively about him."

Plenty of people defend their friends, so loyalty alone doesn't make her a Six. What ties it to the Six is the reason she gives elsewhere for holding on: when things feel out of control, you "hold tight to the people you love." No source shows the loyalty cost her anything, so it is a pattern, not a test.

## The secret garden

She has vouched for her father, too. On May 29, 2016, when she was 17, days after Amber Heard alleged in a Los Angeles court that Johnny Depp had been abusive, she posted on Instagram that he was "the sweetest most loving person I know," [CBS News/AP](https://www.cbsnews.com/news/lily-rose-depp-comes-to-dad-johnny-depps-defense-after-domestic-violence-allegations/) reported. The post did not mention the allegations. She was a minor, so it is context here, not type evidence.

As an adult she went the other way. During her father's 2022 [defamation trial](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Depp_v._Heard) against Heard, she said nothing in public, which frustrated some of his fans, [Jezebel](https://jezebel.com/lily-rose-depp-doesnt-want-to-be-defined-by-her-dad-1849790873) wrote. That November she told Elle why.

> "When it's something that's so private and so personal that all of a sudden becomes not so personal…I feel really entitled to my secret garden of thoughts."

She went on that people have "really wanted to define me by the men in my life," and that she was "really ready to be defined for the things that I put out there."

She also credits her upbringing. "I've just been raised in a manner that has taught me that privacy is something that's important to protect," she told Elle.

So she vouched publicly for her father in 2016, stayed silent about him in 2022, and vouched publicly for her director in 2023. Her stated reasons are privacy and a wish to be judged on her own work. A Four reading would stress the self-definition. A Six reading would stress caution in a public minefield. The record shows only what she said.

She has named one exception to her privacy. Talking to Harper's Bazaar UK about her partner of about two years, the musician Danielle Balbuena (070 Shake), she teared up: "I've always been really private about my personal life, but it's hard not to talk about her because I'm so happy."

## "People have been ready to see me fail"

In the same November 2022 Elle interview she was asked about the "nepo baby" debate, the online argument over children of famous parents who are assumed to have gotten ahead through them. She said "nothing is going to get you the part except for being right for the part." Later in the answer: "Maybe you get your foot in the door, but you still just have your foot in the door." She also said, "I know my childhood didn't look like everybody's childhood."

The remarks drew a backlash ([PAPER](https://www.papermag.com/lily-rose-depp-nepotism-comments)). Critics had material: her first role came on a family friend's set, and her Chanel deal came through a house her mother had worked with for decades. Three months later she told i-D, "I'm so careful about these conversations now."

By 2024 she described the scrutiny differently. Vanity Fair asked whether she had struggled with insecurity. "Absolutely," she said, and called the hardest part of _Nosferatu_ "getting over the imposter syndrome of, like, 'Why am I here and why do these people think that I can do this?'" Then she described the answer she had been working toward:

> "Getting to a place where I felt confident enough within myself to be like, 'I can do this and I am here for a reason,' I definitely have struggled with that. Humility is incredibly important, especially in this business."

In 2023 the doubt was settled by the people who chose her. Here she wants it settled "within myself," and she says getting there has been a struggle. That is a real shift, and it counts toward the Four reading below.

The same interview shows what she does with the pressure. "I feel like people have been ready to see me fail, in a way, since I was a kid. That has made me only want to work harder and prove people wrong." She called it "fuel to my fire," and said, "I do want to prove that I'm a hard worker and I'm not here for anything else but to work hard."

Her mother, speaking to Madame Figaro in 2023 as quoted by [Paris Match Belgique](https://www.parismatch.be/actualites/people/2023/04/07/les-gens-se-trompent-vanessa-paradis-sort-du-silence-et-defend-sa-fille-lily-rose-depp-accusee-de-favoritisme-SVOWB757NBHQZGWDRGBIVIXB2I/), called her "une bosseuse comme on en voit peu" (a hard worker like few others). Paradis is the most interested witness there is.

Proving people wrong through hard work could point to [Type 3](/enneagram-corner/enneagram-type-3), the Achiever, who is driven by being seen as valuable. Her Chanel deal fits that, and so does treating humility as a professional value ("especially in this business"). Volunteered doubt alone can't separate a Six from a Three managing her image. What strains the Three is how little she guards the image: right after _The Idol_ she spent _Nosferatu_ "looking like a corpse every day," which she called "a workout for my self-esteem." In March 2026 she told ELLE France, as reported by [Purepeople](https://www.purepeople.com/article/lily-rose-depp-evoque-une-periode-mouvementee-avec-sa-mere-vanessa-paradis_a548691/1), that pleasing everyone "ce n'est pas mon ambition" (isn't my ambition).

## Why not Type 4?

Type 4, the Individualist, sits in the heart triad, where the core emotion is shame. A Four's search is for identity: to be distinct, to be seen for who she really is, and to find what feels missing. Fours often pour intense feeling into art.

Depp's case for the Four is strong, and it is in her own words.

She is drawn to the dark and the mistreated. At three, Edward Scissorhands upset her "because everyone was being so mean to him," she told Harper's Bazaar UK. Of _Nosferatu_: "there's a part of me that feels a little bit of empathy for Nosferatu. I mean, am I sick for feeling that way?" She says, "I'm interested in the darker underbelly of things."

She describes acting as personal. "It's an emotional outlet for me—I feel like it helps me, I don't know, heal things with myself, perhaps," she told Vanity Fair. To Harper's Bazaar UK:

> "The most exciting thing is getting to explore parts of myself. You get to hide behind all these beautiful artifices, the costumes, the story, but the place I'm drawing from is deeply personal."

The work doesn't always stay at work. She avoids method acting because "I'm too attached to my life," but after _Nosferatu_, "I do think I brought the darkness home, for sure." She says, "I'm better at crying than laughing, which—I don't know what that says about me as a person," and a moment later, "I'm a happy person, so I like to explore the underbelly of that at work." She calls herself "full of dualities."

Then there are the inward answers. Asked by Knightley whether she worried about being judged because of her parents, she said, "I'm not the same kind of actor as my dad or my mom, and I'm very much my own person." On the podcast: "I just need to know in my heart why I'm doing something and I like the weird stuff, you know?" In 2026: "il faut tracer sa route" (you have to forge your own path).

It would be easy to say the Four runs in the family, since 9takes reads her father, [Johnny Depp](/personality-analysis/johnny-depp), as a Type 4. A parent's type proves nothing about a child, and she has said they are "very different actors."

Three things keep the Six ahead.

**First, the hard feeling she names about herself is fear, not shame.** Fear comes up about her own life in six of her interviews from 2021 to 2025: work, fame, strangers, physical risk and politics. Shame words about herself are rare and come as jokes. Asked on the podcast about her parents' shadow, she said, "I'm not ashamed." That count has limits. Two of those interviews were promoting a horror film, Knightley asked about anxiety directly, and the worry about strangers has a shame flavor.

**Second, she guards her privacy against danger, not against being misunderstood.** A Four protects a distinct inner self from being misread. Her words are threat words: "eaten alive," "lose your footing," and, when ordinary life stops mattering, "That's when things get dangerous." Her fix is ordinary life itself: "I love going to the butcher and the grocery store and having dinner at my friends' houses."

**Third, the Four material sits mostly in how she talks about art, roles and taste.** That is her profession, where drawing on real feeling is standard craft. Of acting as an outlet, she added, "I feel like a lot of us do this for that reason." Her strongest identity statement, "very much my own person," came when Knightley asked about her famous parents, a question any child of two stars would answer by insisting on her own work.

The inward answers are real, though, and they get more frequent from 2024 to 2026. She frames them as learned. "If people still want to talk shit or see me in a certain way, then that's not my problem," she told Vanity Fair. "I've had to grow into that feeling as well." The ELLE France line starts with "Désormais" (now, as opposed to before). That fits a Six building her own inner authority. It also fits a Four who has outgrown a famous family. That overlap is why this read is medium confidence and not high.

There is a [Type 9](/enneagram-corner/enneagram-type-9) case, too. She is usually relaxed about auditions, she said on the podcast, because her mother taught her that "roles go to the people who are meant to play them." Of people wanting her to fail: "it just is what it is." But Nines tend to numb distress, and she names hers constantly. Nines also avoid friction, and she told Elle she wants work that will "challenge me, or challenge other people."

## What the type doesn't explain

Some of her record doesn't fit a careful Six.

She left school at 16 on her own call. "I have really strong gut instincts," she told Knightley. She was already working and had family behind her, but nobody handed her that frame.

She likes provocation. Of _The Idol_ she told Vanity Fair, "I like to do things that maybe not everybody's down to do."

Many Sixes test authority before they trust it. Her public record shows trust in directors and collaborators, and no testing of them. That leaves the Loyalist reading one-sided.

And the record is thin where it matters most. As of October 2026 there is no long-form testimony from the people closest to her, only her mother's defense, her longtime security guard's remark that she is "like a daughter" to him, and praise from collaborators in release weeks. Nobody has described Depp let down by someone she trusted. That is the scene that would test the loyalty reading hardest.

## The next test
