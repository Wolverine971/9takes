Lily-Rose Depp auditioned for Robert Eggers's _Nosferatu_ over Zoom, from her family's place in the French countryside. One of the scenes was "a big freak-out scene," she told [Vanity Fair](https://www.vanityfair.com/hollywood/story/lily-rose-depp-nosferatu-awards-insider) in November 2024, so she came prepared.

> "I had a roll of paper towels, and some books, and I just threw shit all over the room. I started moving around like crazy. I wanted to show that I wasn't scared to go there."

The same Vanity Fair interview has the other half of the story. "I was really nervous," she said of that audition, and she had assumed she would lose: "I thought for sure it wasn't going to go my way."

She was scared, she said so, and she threw the paper towels anyway.

Her reason for going so big was practical: "I felt like my job was to show him that I could go there. If I get the job, we'll fine tune the intricacies of it after." She had never worked with Eggers, but she didn't go in cold. At their first meeting, she told Vanity Fair, he had already said, "I'm sorry, I know Zoom auditions suck." And she had done a session with "our dialect coach before the audition," she said on the [Happy Sad Confused](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Ai7ieVFwSbw) podcast in January 2025, because "I hope the accent isn't, like, ridiculous." Accent prep is routine, so this proves little alone. Still, the leap was less solo than it looks: a little support first, then a big swing of her own.

She admits to nerves or fear in six of the nine long interviews this profile draws on from 2021 to 2025, and when she describes getting through something frightening, she usually names someone else: a director, a co-star, an old friend. Where she goes for steadiness is the question here.

She was born on May 27, 1999, in Neuilly-sur-Seine, outside Paris, to the actor Johnny Depp and the French singer and actor Vanessa Paradis, and grew up between Los Angeles and France with her younger brother, Jack ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lily-Rose_Depp)). Her parents separated in 2012 after 14 years together; she is bilingual.

Her first role came at 14, on the set of Kevin Smith's _Tusk_, a film her father was in, where she was "pretty casually told to jump into a scene," [i-D](https://i-d.co/article/lily-rose-depp-interview-the-idol/) reported. In 2015, the year she turned 16, Chanel, a house her mother had worked with since the 1990s, made her an ambassador. Independent films followed, including _The Dancer_ (2016), which earned her a César nomination, and _Planetarium_ (2016), in which [Natalie Portman](/personality-analysis/natalie-portman), now an old friend, picked her to play her younger sister. Then came Jocelyn, the pop star at the center of HBO's sexually explicit _The Idol_ (2023), and Ellen Hutter, the young woman in the grip of the vampire Count Orlok in _Nosferatu_ (2024).

## What is Lily-Rose Depp's personality type?

On her public record, Lily-Rose Depp is most likely [Enneagram Type 6](/enneagram-corner/enneagram-type-6), the Loyalist, at medium confidence. Sixes sit in the head triad, where the core emotion is fear. When she doubts herself, she usually steadies through people and structures she trusts, then takes big creative risks inside that support. [Type 4](/enneagram-corner/enneagram-type-4) is close behind; Type 3 isn't ruled out.

Being scared doesn't make someone a Six; nearly any actor auditioning for Eggers would admit to nerves. What marks a Six is the strategy: find something solid to stand on, such as a person, a plan or an authority, and act boldly from there. Sixes also doubt those supports, and many test an authority before trusting it. Depp shows the first half often. The second barely appears in her public record.

## "If they've chosen me"

In February 2023, before _The Idol_ aired, she told i-D how she handles the feeling that she might not belong in a role:

> "You just have to jump in and have some kind of faith that, if they've chosen me, then hopefully I've been chosen for a reason. That's all that you can do."

Then her self-talk: "Okay, I really don't want to fuck this up, so let me work as hard as I possibly can to make sure that I have left no rock unturned." And in the next breath: "If you're not scared, then what's the point? I like the fear a little bit."

Faith in the people who chose her, preparation down to the last rock, and fear kept on as fuel. Hard work and fear as fuel fit plenty of types. The first move is the one to watch: she settles her doubt by borrowing confidence from the people who picked her.

In 2021, [Keira Knightley](/personality-analysis/keira-knightley), her co-star in _Silent Night_, asked her for [Interview](https://www.interviewmagazine.com/film/whats-it-like-being-lily-rose-depp-keira-knightley-finds-out) whether she gets anxious on set. "Definitely," she said. Before a job she can wonder, "Am I going to be able to do this? What have I signed up for? Are they sure that I'm the one?" She calls the nerves "a sign that I'm invested," then turns to other people: "And trust is so huge," she said, naming her partners, her director and her crew.

The word keeps coming back. Sam Levinson, who co-created and directed _The Idol_, and her co-star Abel Tesfaye (the Weeknd), she told [Elle](https://www.elle.com/culture/celebrities/a41894075/0125-0141-an-idol-rising-december-2022/) in 2022, are "two people who make me feel incredibly safe and protected." Eggers was new to her, but she said on the same podcast, "I had that trust with him because I knew that no matter where he was leading me it was going to be a great place to be." Of the choreographed possession scenes, she told Portman for [Interview](https://www.interviewmagazine.com/film/im-a-sucker-for-a-crazy-love-story-lily-rose-depp-by-natalie-portman), "I was grateful not to have to shape it on my own."

Some of this is the job. On a sexually explicit series, trust-and-safety language is standard, and a lot of it was said while promoting a show. She also talks plainly about appetite: "I really like emotional scenes because they're challenging. I feel stimulated by that," she told Vanity Fair. The trust talk on its own proves little. It matters because the same pattern shows up in her life off set.

## "People around you that you've known forever"

Off set, most of the fears she names are about being judged. With strangers, she told Elle, she worries "that they're going to think I'm really weird or rude because I just get really shy and kind of anxious," not, the magazine noted, for her safety. Being an artist, she said, "also comes with this thing where people feel like they know you, even though they don't." Then: "I'm not here to give myself to the world to be eaten alive." That worry sits closer to the heart types than to a Six's fear of danger. Sixes fear the group's judgment too, so it doesn't sink the Six reading, but her fear vocabulary can't decide the case.

What she does with the pressure is more telling. "This industry can be so crazy and you can lose your footing really easily," she told Portman. "I feel like it's corny because people always say this, but you have to have people around you that you've known forever that are going to anchor you." Then, to Portman herself: "Even you, I'm so grateful that I met you when I was 16, and to have one of my very favorite actresses to be able to call when I'm struggling with a scene." To Elle in 2022: "At the end of the day, what really matters to me is what my family and friends and loved ones think of me."

In November 2024, after the US election, she told [Harper's Bazaar UK](https://www.harpersbazaar.com/uk/culture/a63212831/lily-rose-depp-interview-nosferatu/), "It's a horrific time, and I'm scared, and I'm sad." In the same interview, in a line the magazine presented as her "solution," she said:

> "When things feel out of control and you feel powerless, you have to hold tight to the people you love and look after those people."

That is the clearest link in her record between fear and allegiance. It came in a _Nosferatu_ promotional interview, but it was about the election, not the film.

Put the work and the life side by side and a pattern shows. Inside a frame she trusts (a director with a plan, a trusted co-star, friends she has known forever), she walks toward what scares her. The other half, stepping back where there is no frame, rests on thin evidence: she isn't on Twitter, doesn't read her coverage and likes to "retreat back into my real life," as plenty of public figures do.

That claim can be tested. If she started taking big risks with no trusted people or structure around her, the Six reading would weaken. One past case already strains it: her decision to leave school at 16, discussed below. The audition strains it only in part: the accent session came first, but the big swing on camera was her own.

## Standing by _The Idol_

On March 1, 2023, Rolling Stone published a report on _The Idol_. As the magazine [summarized it](https://www.rollingstone.com/tv-movies/tv-movie-news/the-idol-team-respond-rolling-stone-report-cannes-premiere-1234740292/) that May, its interviews with 13 cast and crew members produced claims of an "often chaotic" set, and crew members said the show changed after its original director, Amy Seimetz, left and Levinson stepped in. Three sources said his scripts contained what Rolling Stone called "disturbing sexual and physically violent scenes" between Depp's and Tesfaye's characters.

The show's team disputed it. HBO told [Entertainment Tonight](https://www.etonline.com/node/200095) that the early episodes "did not meet HBO standards so we chose to make a change," its explanation for the switch, and that the team had been committed to "a safe, collaborative, and mutually respectful working environment." This page doesn't try to settle any of it.

The reporting was about her own role, and she answered it. On March 2 she gave ET a statement:

> "Sam is, for so many reasons, the best director I have ever worked with. Never have I felt more supported or respected in a creative space, my input and opinions more valued."

At Cannes that May she said, "it's always sad and disheartening to hear mean, false things about someone you care about, and it wasn't my experience shooting the show."

Each time, her stated reason is her own experience as the actor in those scenes. He "has always created an environment in which I felt seen, heard, and appreciated," her statement said. That is the plainest explanation, and it needs no type at all. She was also promoting the show.

Promotion explains less of what came after. In November 2024, with the series panned and cancelled, she told Vanity Fair, "But I stand by it. We made a choice and we went for it." She said it during her _Nosferatu_ awards campaign, so some incentive remained, but the reasons she gave were personal. In the same answer she said, "I'm very close with Sam and Ashley [Levinson]. They're like family to me," and called making the show "a huge turning point for me," where she learned "how to dance, and sing."

Plenty of people defend their friends, so loyalty alone doesn't make her a Six. Tying it to the Six is this profile's inference, not a reason she gave; her "hold tight" line was about the election. On their own, her defense of Levinson and her silence about her father (below) are type-neutral. No source shows the loyalty cost her anything, so it is a pattern, not a test.

## The secret garden

During her father's 2022 [defamation trial](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Depp_v._Heard) against his ex-wife, Amber Heard, she said nothing in public, which frustrated some of his fans, [Jezebel](https://jezebel.com/lily-rose-depp-doesnt-want-to-be-defined-by-her-dad-1849790873) wrote. That November she told Elle why.

> "When it's something that's so private and so personal that all of a sudden becomes not so personal…I feel really entitled to my secret garden of thoughts."

She went on that she is "not here to answer for anybody," that people have "really wanted to define me by the men in my life, whether that's my family members or my boyfriends," and that she was "really ready to be defined for the things that I put out there." She also credits her upbringing: "I've just been raised in a manner that has taught me that privacy is something that's important to protect."

So she spoke up for her director in 2023 after staying quiet about her father in 2022. Her stated reasons are privacy and being judged on her own work, and the second is a reason a Four would give. The trial's scale or legal caution could also explain it, though she has never said so. For a reading built on holding tight to her people, it is the hardest fact in her file.

## "People have been ready to see me fail"

In the same Elle interview she was asked about the "nepo baby" debate over children of famous parents. She said "nothing is going to get you the part except for being right for the part," and "Maybe you get your foot in the door, but you still just have your foot in the door. There's a lot of work that comes after that." She offered the example of a doctor's child who "went to medical school and trained," and said of the label, "I just hear it a lot more about women, and I don't think that it's a coincidence."

The remarks drew a backlash ([PAPER](https://www.papermag.com/lily-rose-depp-nepotism-comments)). Three months later she told i-D, "I'm so careful about these conversations now," and of the childhood her parents tried to keep normal, "obviously, that still was not a normal childhood."

In November 2024, Vanity Fair asked whether she had struggled with insecurity. "Absolutely," she said, and called the hardest part of _Nosferatu_ "getting over the imposter syndrome of, like, 'Why am I here and why do these people think that I can do this?'" Then:

> "Getting to a place where I felt confident enough within myself to be like, 'I can do this and I am here for a reason,' I definitely have struggled with that. Humility is incredibly important, especially in this business."

In 2023 the doubt was settled by the people who chose her. Here she wants it settled "within myself," and she says getting there has been a struggle. That is a real shift, and it counts toward the Four reading below.

"I feel like people have been ready to see me fail, in a way, since I was a kid," she told Vanity Fair. "That has made me only want to work harder and prove people wrong." As two artists' daughter, "trying to find my own identity in this world, has been interesting when everybody's thinking that you're here for the wrong reasons." Then she named her choice:

> "You either can sit there and cry about it and be like, 'This isn't fair!' or you can be like, 'Okay, I'm just going to work really, really hard and do the best that I can.'"

## Why not Type 4?

Type 4, the Individualist, sits in the heart triad, where the core emotion is shame, and searches for identity: to be distinct and seen for who she really is. Depp's case for it is strong, in her own words.

She is drawn to the dark and the mistreated. At three, _Edward Scissorhands_ upset her "because everyone was being so mean to him," she told Harper's Bazaar UK, and she admits "a little bit of empathy for Nosferatu." Acting "helps me, I don't know, heal things with myself, perhaps," she told Vanity Fair.

It would be easy to say the Four runs in the family, since 9takes reads her father, [Johnny Depp](/personality-analysis/johnny-depp), as a Type 4. A parent's type proves nothing about a child. Asked whether a taste for the gothic runs in the family, she said, "Sure, I guess you could say so," and "We're very different actors, but of course you're the product of your environment."

Her inward answers about identity go back years. Asked by Knightley in 2021 about being judged for her parents, she said, "I'm not the same kind of actor as my dad or my mom, and I'm very much my own person," then added, "obviously you're an extension of your family." On the podcast: "I just need to know in my heart why I'm doing something and I like the weird stuff, you know?"

In the interviews this profile draws on, her answers about confidence turn inward from 2024. "If people still want to talk shit or see me in a certain way, then that's not my problem," she told Vanity Fair. "I've had to grow into that feeling as well." In March 2026 she told ELLE France, as reported by [Purepeople](https://www.purepeople.com/article/lily-rose-depp-evoque-une-periode-mouvementee-avec-sa-mere-vanessa-paradis_a548691/1), "Désormais, il m'est plus facile de ne pas prendre les choses trop à cœur" (now it's easier for me not to take things too much to heart).

The obvious tests don't settle it. Her fear words, as shown above, mostly concern being judged. And all six interviews where she admits fear came while promoting something, three for _Nosferatu_, two in conversations with actors she had worked with. No costly choice separates the Six from the Four: her reasons for the 2022 silence, which frustrated some of her father's fans, lean Four, and her care after the nepo-baby backlash fits a Six and a Three alike. Strip the craft talk from both sides and what remains is close: being misknown and the secret garden for the Four, old friends who "anchor you" and "hold tight" for the Six.

Two observations tip it, narrowly, toward the Six.

**First, where she goes when she doubts herself.** Her outward answers cover the most ground: a hard scene, a "crazy" industry, a world out of control. Her inward answers cluster around two questions: whether she deserves her place as two stars' daughter, and whether people will like her strange taste. Both would push anyone to insist on her own path.

**Second, what she does with being misjudged.** A Four tends to dwell on being misunderstood and on what sets her apart. Depp names the self-pitying response, "This isn't fair!", and turns it down for work. In 2026 she said of her mother, "En vrai, on est très semblables" (honestly, we're very alike). For a type whose search is to be distinct, that is an unusual note. Neither rules out the Four, and both fit a Three.

The inward drift since 2024 fits a Six building her own authority, or a Four who has outgrown a famous family. That overlap is why this read is medium confidence and not high.

## What about Type 3 and Type 9?

Proving people wrong through hard work could point to [Type 3](/enneagram-corner/enneagram-type-3), the Achiever, who is driven by being seen as valuable. A decade as a Chanel ambassador fits, and so do calling humility important "especially in this business" and growing "so careful" after the backlash. Volunteered doubt alone can't separate a Six from a Three managing her image.

What leans against the Three is what she says she measures herself by. Of whether people like her work, she said on the podcast, "at the end of the day, that's not what's important to me." She tends to hand credit to others: on _Nosferatu_, to the choreography, the storyboards and a crew with "this real team feeling." All of it is self-report from press tours, where modesty is expected. The Three is weaker than the Four here, but it isn't ruled out.

There is a [Type 9](/enneagram-corner/enneagram-type-9) case, too. She is usually relaxed about auditions, she said on the podcast, because her mother taught her that "roles go to the people who are meant to play them." But the calm has limits she names. Of _Nosferatu_: "I really, really want this one," and that audition made her "really nervous." Her calm covers the ordinary; her nerves rise with what she wants most. Nines also tend to avoid open conflict, and she took a public side in the fight over _The Idol_ repeatedly, from March 2023 to January 2025. The Nine is the weakest of the three alternatives.

## What the type doesn't explain

Some of her record doesn't fit a careful Six.

She left school at 16 on her own call. "I have really strong gut instincts," she told Knightley. She "had already started working" and wanted to pursue acting "wholeheartedly." Her account names no trusted person or plan behind the decision, so it is the clearest past case that strains this profile's test.

She likes provocation. Of _The Idol_ she told Vanity Fair, "I like to do things that maybe not everybody's down to do."

As of October 2026, the Six's skeptical side is missing. Many Sixes test authority before they trust it. Her public record shows trust in directors and collaborators, and no testing of them. That leaves the Loyalist reading one-sided.

And the record is thin where it matters most. As of October 2026 this profile found no long-form testimony from the people closest to her, only her mother's [2023 praise](https://www.parismatch.be/actualites/people/2023/04/07/les-gens-se-trompent-vanessa-paradis-sort-du-silence-et-defend-sa-fille-lily-rose-depp-accusee-de-favoritisme-SVOWB757NBHQZGWDRGBIVIXB2I/) of her work ethic, her longtime security guard's remark to [Harper's Bazaar UK](https://www.harpersbazaar.com/uk/culture/a63212831/lily-rose-depp-interview-nosferatu/) that she is "like a daughter" to him, and praise from collaborators in release weeks. Nobody has described Depp let down by someone she trusted. That is the scene that would test the loyalty reading hardest.

## How to test this read

The first test scheduled after this profile was written is Eggers's _Werwulf_, set as of October 2026 for release in the US on December 25, 2026, and in the UK on January 1, 2027 ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Werwulf)). She plays a medieval farmer's wife, a character Eggers told [Empire](https://www.empireonline.com/movies/features/werwulf-trailer-explained-by-robert-eggers-interview-exclusive/) in June 2026 "is so far from Lily, physically and in her general way of holding herself."

When she describes what got her past her doubts about that role, as opposed to how she found its emotions, does she credit the people and the frame around her, or a confidence she found in herself? The first keeps the Six. The second strengthens the Four. The same question applies to every hard role after it.

Portman once asked her how she switches between the thin skin acting needs and the thick skin fame needs. She started with how she was raised: "I've grown up being taught that your private life and your inner life is so precious, and that it really needs to be protected." A few sentences later she gave the image:

> "You have all your super-private feelings in your backpack, and you go to work and the scene starts and you let it out."
