Ella Beatty beat the reporter to the Carlyle.

Vogue's Elise Taylor arrived at 2:58 p.m. for a 3 p.m. coffee in February 2024, expecting to wait. Beatty was already there, her coat neatly folded over her arm, following one of her father's cardinal rules: "Always be on time." In her case, early.

Then, before Taylor could ask, Beatty raised the subject every story about her would raise. "I totally acknowledge that there is an inherent privilege and exposure that comes from having well-known actors as parents," she told [Vogue](https://www.vogue.com/article/meet-ella-beatty-the-breakout-star-of-feud-whos-now-heading-to-broadway). "And I really hope that I can offer something meaningful." Taylor's words for her were "Exceptionally poised."

Sarah Paulson, now one of her closest friends, told [Town & Country](https://www.townandcountrymag.com/leisure/arts-and-culture/a73571951/ella-beatty-lizzie-borden-interview-2026/) in September 2026, "There is something unnervingly, dazzlingly self-assured about Ella."

Beatty describes something else. Of winning the title role in _Monster: The Lizzie Borden Story_, she told [Variety](https://variety.com/2026/tv/features/monster-lizzie-borden-ella-beatty-1236866041/) that month, "I was over the moon and also really scared."

She is the youngest of four children of Annette Bening and Warren Beatty, raised in Los Angeles. She trained at Juilliard from 2018 to 2022 and, as she told Paulson, "didn't work professionally at all until I graduated from acting school." Since January 2024 she has gone from FX's _Feud: Capote vs. The Swans_ to Broadway, replacing Elle Fanning in _Appropriate_, then to the stage opposite [Hugh Jackman](/personality-analysis/hugh-jackman) and the lead in the film _Act One_.

_Monster_, Ryan Murphy and Ian Brennan's Netflix anthology, premiered on September 17, 2026. In its second week it drew 14.5 million views and topped Netflix's global English-language TV list, [TheWrap](https://www.thewrap.com/creative-content/tv-shows/monster-the-lizzie-borden-story-ratings-viewership-week-2-netflix/) reported. She also appears in Sarah Polley's film of _The Bell Jar_, opposite [Billie Eilish](/personality-analysis/billie-eilish), which had no release date as of October 18, 2026.

So people are looking her up, and they find a public record under three years old, almost all of it promotional. It holds one steady pattern: she names fear easily, then answers it with preparation and people she trusts. What that pattern is for is the open question. This is an open case: a leading read held loosely, the alternatives beside it, and what would decide between them.

## What is Ella Beatty's personality type?

Leading read: Enneagram Type 6, held loosely at low confidence. Type 9 is the main alternative, and Types 2 and 3 are still in play. She names fear readily and answers it with preparation and trusted people. But her public record is under three years old and mostly promotional, so nothing yet separates these reads.

Those four types span all three of the Enneagram's triads. A [Type 6](/enneagram-corner/enneagram-type-6) is in the head triad, whose core emotion is fear, and looks for ground to trust in preparation, allies and the people in charge. A [Type 9](/enneagram-corner/enneagram-type-9) is in the body triad, whose anger stays mostly out of sight, and keeps the peace by going along. [Type 2](/enneagram-corner/enneagram-type-2) and [Type 3](/enneagram-corner/enneagram-type-3) share the heart triad, where the emotion is shame. Twos secure a place by being needed, Threes by succeeding.

Fear is the emotion Beatty names most. That is why Six leads, and why it can't lead by much: fear is also the ordinary weather of an actor in her first lead.

## The name, and the tapes

She has never pretended the name is irrelevant. In a June 2025 conversation for [Interview](https://www.interviewmagazine.com/theater/meet-ella-beatty-daughter-of-hollywood-royalty-and-sarah-paulsons-kindred-spirit), Paulson asked whether she had ever thought she shouldn't use her last name. Beatty said no.

> "It really never occurred to me until I started working. I do feel a kind of responsibility towards an artistic legacy that I don't feel ashamed of. I would hope that my work would be powerful enough to offer something specific. I'd hope that my work would be enough to make me separate, but I also understand why people do that."

The answer hasn't changed shape since Vogue: name the advantage, don't argue with it, promise work in return. It is also what a publicist would tell any famous child debuting in the 2024 "nepo baby" moment, so it shows media sense more than motive.

How she got the work is easier to check. For _Appropriate_, Paulson and her co-star Michael Esper read with several actresses. "When you walked out, Michael and I turned to each other and we're like, 'It's got to be Ella Beatty,'" Paulson recalled. She also told her, "I had no idea who your parents were."

_Monster_ came from a self-tape. Max Winkler, who directed six of the eight episodes and is an executive producer, told [ScreenRant](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=RooR7nJOmiM) that after watching a tape from casting director Tiffany Little Canfield he called Murphy and said, "We got her." Murphy watched all seven tapes without being told which one, then called back and asked, "Is it Ella?" Both men are selling the show, and she had already worked for Murphy on _Feud_, so no one can measure what the name did before the tape. The accounts agree that a tape was required.

She frames the inheritance as belonging and duty. On [_The Drew Barrymore Show_](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=e8IWMwnMAEE) in September 2026, she agreed with Barrymore's wish to "honor this family" and said the family profession "helped me feel a sense of belonging really young. But the older I get, the more I feel that responsibility to kind of carry that torch on." It is the most Six-flavored vocabulary in the record, though Barrymore supplied the prompt.

## "Really scared," then off book

_Monster_ shows the full pattern. "I felt a huge responsibility to the part, to the anthology, to the real person," she told Variety. At Netflix's September 9 press conference, [reported by Showbiz Junkies](https://www.showbizjunkies.com/tv/netflix-monster-lizzie-borden-cast-press-conference/), she said she also felt, "How am I gonna do this and what does it mean?" So she started before pre-production, because that work "can help you feel like you're starting."

She told [Collider](https://collider.com/monster-lizzie-borden-story-netflix-ella-beatty/):

> "Right away, when I found out I had the part, I listened to every single podcast and video that's ever been published in the world. I couldn't be stopped if I had a WiFi connection."

At the same press conference, asked how they had prepared, Rebecca Hall said, "I did absolutely nothing." Charlie Hunnam had spent a couple of days reading. Beatty described a book, a movement coach and work before pre-production. The difference was hers, not the job's.

_Appropriate_ was the same move under more pressure. She told the acting teacher Anthony Meindl on his [_In The Moment_ podcast](https://soundcloud.com/anthonymeindl/anthonyinterviewsellabeatty) in February 2025 that she had about two days of table work before taking over, learned the blocking from a video and the understudies, and "came in off book, so that would never be an issue." The director, Lila Neugebauer, told her from the start to find her own version. "Because I felt that assurance from her going into it, I didn't feel a pressure to replicate anybody else's," Beatty said.

First she removed the risk she could control. Then she took permission from the person in charge. Then she brought herself.

On nights off from _Sexual Misconduct of the Middle Classes_ in 2025, she missed the play. "Sometimes we have four or five days between performances and I get really nervous," she told Paulson, so she took home the director's "diary book" from rehearsals. Her approach as a newcomer, she told Town & Country, was to "sit very quietly, watch, and pray, and then work extremely hard and show up off-book."

The record pushes back too. Of _Appropriate_, she also told Paulson, "I didn't have a lot of time to be scared because I got the job and then we started." Nerves and over-preparation are ordinary in young actors, and a famous name raises the cost of being caught unprepared. The people who work with her see composure. Jackman told Town & Country she is "always prepared, kind, funny, and fearless." Paulson's word is "self-possessed."

## The relief of being someone else

The most specific thing she has said about herself cuts both ways.

Talking to Meindl during _Ghosts_ rehearsals, she found an upside in the rush of the _Appropriate_ takeover. "Part of what was so great about it is that I didn't have time to worry about it," she said. "I didn't really have time to get self-conscious or, um, you know, self-involved about it." Then she turned Meindl's point about wasted worry on herself:

> "Being present and kind of being where your feet are, it's something I try to try really hard and fail at often frequently to live by. It's just like, you know, so much of what we worry about in life, so many of the things that we are so, so emotionally fraught about, you know, you can't handle until you're there. And I think trusting yourself to have, you know, the tools and the ability to handle it when you're faced with it is, um, something I'm still really trying to do. And God knows that was something I did in that play."

Read as a Six, that is the type's growth task in her own words: learning to trust that she can handle what she fears before it arrives. Read as a Nine, the telling detail comes first. The rush felt good because it left no room for her.

She keeps describing that relief. "It's a relief to come to work and remember you're being someone else," she told Paulson. In 2026 she told Town & Country she has "a certain amount of discomfort about being a public person" and wants audiences "thinking about the character and not me," while allowing, "There is also joy in sharing your life with people."

Credit runs the same way. "I feel like the luckiest lady in the world," she told [Entertainment Tonight](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=DdDLa9vctOA) before the premiere. Of every project since, she told Meindl, "there's a part of me that feels like, Oh my God, I can't believe they picked me." Asked by Town & Country how she had changed, she flagged the reflex first. "I'll answer authentically even though I want to deflect," she said.

A Three tends to own the result. She hands it to luck and collaborators, and admits the urge to deflect. That fits a Nine's self-discounting and a Six's expectation of the worse outcome. It is also the expected register for a famous child in nepotism-conscious press.

## The people she calls

When pressure rises, she reaches for a small circle. "We call every 15 minutes," she said of Paulson on [TODAY](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Q6n83ZQZX2w) in September 2026, a joke with a point. Paulson, she said, is "there for advice at any minute." On _Feud_, Tom Hollander came to her dressing room privately to offer to run lines, and with her mother it's "once a day, debrief," she told [Drew Barrymore](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=GYkLldtGQ3s).

She has said what the circle is for, especially around auditions, where "you don't really know, you know, if you're even kind of accepted in that room yet," as she told Meindl. Her remedy is "a friend to call to be like, Oh my God, that audition was a nightmare."

Leaning on mentors is ordinary at 24 in a new city, and several of hers approached her first.

Winkler sees a version of this from the outside. "There's a wide-eyedness to Ella that can sometimes conceal how devastatingly perceptive she is," he told Town & Country. "She is completely tuned into what everybody else in the room is doing." Each live type can claim that line: a Six scanning the room for safety, a Two for what people need, a Nine merging with it, a Three reading the audience. It is also the craft rule she says her parents taught her. "What the other person is doing is always more interesting than what you can create," she said on TODAY.

## Six, Nine, Two or Three: weighing the case

### Leading read: Type 6

The Six case is what she volunteers across three years and very different rooms: fear, then a plan that runs through preparation and people she trusts. In February 2025 she named the Six's central work, trusting herself to handle what she worries about, as "something I'm still really trying to do."

Against it, nearly all of this is her own account in promotional settings, and the two longest conversations were with friends. Her colleagues describe calm. She also shows none of the testing of authority that often comes with Six. She attaches fast ("we just kind of fell in love immediately," she said of Paulson on TODAY) and defers easily to "what was on the page."

### Closest rival: Type 9

The Nine case is how she presents: credit to luck and others, relief at being someone else, a wish to disappear into roles. Her word for work going well is "available." Lizzie's sensibility "felt really available right away," she told Entertainment Tonight. Her advice to her younger self, she told Meindl: "Take it a day at a time."

What strains it is that Nines tend to turn fear down, and she turns it up and goes to work on it. "I was kind of voracious after I got the part," she told [No Intervals](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=ZPPP5dTamkM). Her ambition is old and plain. Asked by Paulson whether she had always wanted to act, she said, "Always, always."

### Also live: Type 2

The Two case is the warmth: fast attachments, unstinting praise for collaborators, and a job on _Monster_ she described to [Gayety](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Kwtu_M-LkBQ) as "to love and advocate for the person." If she weren't acting, she told Meindl, she would be "teaching theater to really little ones." Against it, in this record she is mostly the one being looked after, and no account shows her giving care or resenting that it goes unnoticed.

### Also live: Type 3

The Three case is the polish: the fast start, "Exceptionally poised," the nepotism question handled before anyone asks, the hope that her work will "make me separate." Against it are the deflection she admits, her discomfort with being public, and a goal that is moral rather than professional: "I hope I'm becoming someone who, over time, becomes more conscientious and thoughtful."

Types 1 and 4 were tested and set aside. One fits her earnestness ("I am not a good liar," she told Paulson), but nothing shows an inner critic aimed at standards. Four fits her sensitivity, but she says she feels "just like a very normal lady."

## What would settle it

These reads part ways under pressure, and the record hasn't caught her there yet. Five things would.

1. **Her first real criticism.** A badly reviewed project, or a piece arguing her career was handed to her. Does she doubt herself and prepare harder (Six), shrug and keep the peace (Nine), worry about her collaborators (Two), or defend the work with results (Three)?
2. **A creative disagreement.** As of October 18, 2026, no account of one from her had been found. Does she test whether she can trust the director (Six), go along until it feels "available" (Nine), protect the relationship (Two), or do whatever makes the performance land (Three)?
3. **Awards season and _The Bell Jar_.** Golden Globe nominations are due in early December 2026, ahead of a January 10, 2027 ceremony, according to [CBS](https://www.paramountpressexpress.com/cbs-entertainment/shows/84th-annual-golden-globes/about/), and _The Bell Jar_ has no release date yet. Does she start owning the work (Three), or are nerves and self-trust still her subject once she is no longer new (Six)?
4. **Her next few projects.** Does she return to trusted people, such as Murphy's company or Paulson (Six); take whatever comes (Nine); choose for the people involved (Two); or climb toward bigger leads (Three)?
5. **Someone who knew her before 2024.** A Juilliard classmate or teacher describing her under pressure. Do they remember worry and preparation, easy accommodation, caretaking, or drive?

Whichever way it settles, some of this will hold. She was early to the Carlyle and off book for the Belasco. And when Town & Country pointed out that in one year she played a daughter dragged away from acting by her parents and a daughter who kills hers, she didn't offer a theory. "What is the universe saying? I don't know," she said with a chuckle. "I'm going to be examining that."
