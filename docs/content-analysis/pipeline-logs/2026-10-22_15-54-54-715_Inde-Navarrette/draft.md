Before _Obsession_ started shooting, Inde Navarrette told its writer-director, Curry Barker, two things.

The first was a confession. "I said, 'I don't know how to scream, and I'm feeling very shy,'" she told [Collider's Perri Nemiroff](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=zEB4C4SIAbY) in May 2026.

The second was a refusal. "I'm really sorry, but I'm not going to show you any of the freakouts in rehearsal," she remembers telling him, on The Hollywood Reporter's [_Awards Chatter_ podcast](https://www.hollywoodreporter.com/movies/movie-news/obsession-star-inde-navarrette-marvel-rogue-awards-chatter-1236727817/) in October 2026. "And I think I saw him die."

_Obsession_, in which a wish on a supernatural toy leaves her character, Nikki, obsessed with her co-worker Bear ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Obsession_%282025_film%29)), made her famous. It premiered at the Toronto International Film Festival on September 5, 2025, and opened in U.S. theaters on May 15, 2026. It cost between $750,000 and $1 million to make, depending on the report ([NBC News](https://www.nbcnews.com/pop-culture/pop-culture-news/obsession-curry-barker-box-office-hit-focus-features-rcna346824)), and had grossed more than $520 million worldwide by October 22, 2026, according to [The Numbers](https://www.the-numbers.com/movie/Obsession-%282026%29). She hosts _Saturday Night Live_ on October 31, 2026 ([Variety](https://variety.com/2026/film/news/inde-navarrette-hosting-snl-x-men-stunt-training-1236916105/)).

The two things she told Barker are the puzzle of her public record. The confession names a fear and hands it to someone she trusts. The refusal protects something, and she has explained it two ways: to keep the performance true, and to avoid being judged too early. Is she guarding her safety, or the impression that she can do the job? Her record, nearly all of it from a press and awards campaign that began in May 2026, can't yet answer that. So this is an open case.

## What is Inde Navarrette's personality type?

Leading read: Enneagram Type 6, held loosely at low confidence. Type 3 is the closest rival, and Types 7 and 4 are still in play. She names her fears and secures against them before she acts, but her wish to be "taken seriously" fits Three just as well. Her record is young, so nothing yet separates them.

[Type 6](/enneagram-corner/enneagram-type-6) and [Type 7](/enneagram-corner/enneagram-type-7) sit in the head triad, whose core emotion is fear. A Six meets fear by finding solid ground: trusted people, rules set in advance, preparation. A Seven moves toward the next exciting thing. [Type 3](/enneagram-corner/enneagram-type-3) and [Type 4](/enneagram-corner/enneagram-type-4) sit in the heart triad, whose core emotion is shame. A Three answers it by succeeding and being seen to succeed, a Four by being true to a self that feels unlike anyone else's.

Fear is the emotion she names most often. "When I received the phone call that I had gotten the part, my heart sank to my butt. I realized I had to work on my anxiety," she told [W](https://www.wmagazine.com/culture/inde-navarrette-cover-obsession-interview-photos) in October 2026. That is why Six leads. Credibility is the motive she names most plainly, which is why Three is close. Almost all of this is her own account, and it shows what she says and does, not what she feels. As of October 2026, she has not publicly named an Enneagram type.

## A childhood told four ways

She was born Danielle Fabiola Navarrette in 2001; profiles place her birth in Tucson, Arizona ([HOLA! USA](https://www.hola.com/us/celebrities/20260611906877/inde-navarrette-real-name-things-you-didnt-know/)). "I would always say, 'Mommy, I do.' That's how I got my nickname, Inde, because I was very independent," she told the Substack [_mixed feelings_](https://www.mixedfeelings.earth/p/inde-navarrette-interview-obsession).

"My dad's a Marine, so I grew up moving around a lot," she told [Variety](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=pnvlSfoB0Fw) in July 2026. She has described those years at least four ways:

- **Fun**, to Variety. Other military kids she knew hated leaving their friends. "Me, I was like new story, new place."
- **Lonely**, to [Complex News](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=IUAsUy_aKR8) in June 2026. Asked whether she kept friends, she said no: "making friends was always really difficult for me."
- **Adaptive**, to [Schön!](https://schonmagazine.com/interview-inde-navarrette/) in May 2026: "I went to 11 different schools and got really good at making myself comfortable anywhere."
- **Mixed**, to Kyle Buchanan in October 2026: "I'm a little jealous sometimes of people that had... one city, one state that they lived in, but simultaneously not, because I feel like I kind of fit in anywhere."

The childhood can't settle the type. A Seven reading could claim the happy moves, a Six reading the lonely ones. Read together, they are a story about adapting.

## "No room for doubt"

The pre-fame record is thin: short interviews from her years on _13 Reasons Why_ (2020) and The CW's _Superman & Lois_ (from 2021). It matters because nobody was campaigning for an Oscar yet. In March 2021 she told [CBR](https://www.cbr.com/superman-lois-inde-navarrette-sarah-cushing-interview/) how she handled auditions once she drove herself, without her mother along to turn each one into a day out:

> "I needed to kind of make a pact with myself of, 'You know what? I'm going to give it 110%.' That way, whenever I leave, there's no room for doubt, because doubt can really just weed its way in."

That month she told readers of [Sweety High](https://www.sweetyhigh.com/read/inde-navarrette-superman-lois-interview-032821) to "work through the hole of doubt" with "a distraction—such as drawing for me—or talking to someone." Of fans' comments on Twitter: "I'm careful with what I read." In 2022 she told [Looper](https://www.looper.com/783590/inde-navarrette-on-the-superman-lois-cast-honoring-culture-and-sarahs-mental-health-exclusive-interview/) that Emmanuelle Chriqui, who played her character's mother, was "able to look down and guide me in different ways."

Doubt is the named enemy. The defenses are set up in advance or found in someone older. That is the earliest Six material on record. The earliest Seven material is in the same Sweety High interview: "I love skydiving but unfortunately can't go anymore because of COVID."

In June 2023, budget cuts ended her run as a series regular, along with several castmates ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Inde_Navarrette)). About a month later, in TikTok replies quoted by [Cinemablend](https://www.cinemablend.com/superheroes/superman/superman-and-lois-cut-lead-inde-navarrette-sarah-cortez-season-4), her reaction was plain: "But, at least you guys get a Season 4." By 2026, after a hit, the cut had become a liberation. "As soon as they were kind of like, 'Dog, you're free.' I was like, 'Dude,'" she told Collider. That turn toward freedom is the kind of reframe Sevens are known for, but it came in hindsight.

## "You look beautiful, you're fine"

The Three case got stronger in October 2026. On [_The Kyle Buchanan Show_](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=NNAgv-7qYIo), she said she once asked a director on an earlier show to let her see playback, to put herself back inside an intimate scene on a crowded set. "And he goes, you look beautiful, you're fine." She did not name him or the show. She says the exchange left her "more careful with my words." She "really, really wanted to hunker down and play a role" in which she would be seen to know what she was doing and to understand "the human emotion behind it. And I could be taken seriously in a different route."

Before _Obsession_, she says, she told her team, "I really want to prove myself." When the result was received well, she thought, "this is validation." On _Awards Chatter_ she gave the same reason for wanting the part: "sometimes people can't see what you're capable of until you quite literally show them."

She had used almost the same words about her streams. After _Obsession_ wrapped, she went about a year and a half without another acting job, and she told [Nylon](https://www.nylon.com/entertainment/inde-navarrette-encounter-obsession-interview) she walked dogs and streamed video games to make money. At some point, moving into what she called "a new chapter of my life," she deleted many of the Twitch streams. "I deleted all of them just cuz... I got scared," she told Variety. She feared people would see her old streaming self "and now they don't take me seriously," and she called it "not based on fact." After fans reposted the clips anyway, she said, "my fear was for nothing."

She hedges the same way about verdicts that haven't arrived. Asked about Oscar buzz in July 2026, she told Variety that "there's a part of my brain that is... just kind of having a safety net of if it doesn't happen, it doesn't mean that I didn't do a good job." A month earlier she told Complex News, "I'm very sensitive. So, I kind of want to protect my peace."

Each episode reads two ways. As a Six, she secures against a bad outcome before it exists: she deletes the streams, builds the net, keeps her hopes down. As a Three, what she protects is the verdict that she is good. No episode on record yet forces those motives apart.

## The freakouts she kept from Barker

Barker answered her confession with a plan, by her account: "I'm going to schedule multiple days where it's just you and I, and then whenever you get comfortable with me, then we're going to do it in front of other people." He screamed alongside her, and a game of charades "created a really safe space," she told Collider. The plan was his, and warming up a nervous lead is ordinary directing.

She kept part of herself back from him anyway. The first day's shooting included one of Nikki's freakouts, and on _Awards Chatter_ she gave a craft reason for keeping them unrehearsed: "I really wanted to keep that safe before it needed to come out in order to play it true." She knew the cost to him. "I know Curry's nervous," she said, and refusing him felt like "really being a dick."

A week later, Buchanan asked whether she had wanted to preserve the surprise, or whether something about it felt exposed. She took the word. Exposure, she said, "was something that I really wanted to avoid," because "I was a very sensitive person at the time." Being judged before she was ready "would have killed me and that would have killed Nikki."

What she gave Barker instead, she says, was questions: "as many questions as humanly possible and not caring if you're annoying." For smaller moments she used him as a mirror. "I'm quite sensitive and so I didn't want to take anything home. So, I kind of used Curry as a mirror where I put full trust in him," she told [GoldDerby](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=T1hlZg2YyjU).

Her trust came in stages: questions first, privacy for the big moments, the director's eye for the small ones. And overriding a nervous director on day one is self-trust, which a picture of her leaning on others doesn't predict.

As an actor, she told Buchanan, "it's really uncomfortable when you don't know what somebody else is thinking." On _Obsession_, "that was my biggest fear of, oh shit, am I doing a good job?"

That one sentence holds both readings. Discomfort at not knowing what others think is a Six's. Fear of the verdict on her work is a Three's.

## Six, Three, Seven or Four: weighing the case

### Leading read: Type 6

The Six case rests on order. She names the fear, then secures against it before anything goes wrong, and she did it before fame as well as after. As _Obsession_ broke out, she told People, as relayed by [Just Jared](https://www.justjared.com/2026/05/24/inde-navarrette-avoiding-social-media-obsession/), that she and her mother were limiting what she saw online because "I really want to make sure that I'm protecting myself first." When the rapper Big Sean messaged her, her first thought, she told Complex News, was "Oh, no," and she handed the phone to her boyfriend to read it first. (It was kind.) She finds guides: Chriqui on _Superman & Lois_, Barker on _Obsession_. Asked what she wants people to know about her, she ranked trust above liking:

> "I think I'd want people to know that they don't have to like me, I just want them to trust me."

Against it, nearly all of this is self-report from a campaign that rewards candor about fear, and filtering social media is ordinary advice for anyone newly famous.

### Closest rival: Type 3

The Three case is the motive she states outright: the playback story, "prove myself," the streams. It runs back to leaving high school early, she told Variety, "to work at a coffee shop because headshots were really expensive and my mom couldn't afford them." On _Awards Chatter_ she said she can "make it last as long as humanly possible or throw it all away."

Against it, she deflects attention. She calls herself "100 percent a cat" and told W, "I don't like to be seen unless I want to be seen." When Gemma Chan told her she was a fan, she says she shot back, "Don't talk about my accomplishments—I want to talk about yours!" Her first move with Barker was to admit what she couldn't do. All of this comes from press, where a Three would also tilt humble, so Three stays close.

### Also live: Type 7

The Seven case is her appetite. "On my 18th birthday, I went skydiving," she told [_Jimmy Kimmel Live_](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=IFUny5cZAdk) in June 2026, and she got pierced the same day. Going into an _Obsession_ audition, she told [THR](https://www.hollywoodreporter.com/movies/movie-features/obsession-ending-inde-navarrette-explained-twist-interview-1236597250/), she thought, "I'm going to have so much fun with this, because who cares?"

What strains it is where her high starts. "Whenever you're really scared of something, but you really want to do it justice and do it correctly and then you get that high of... accomplishing it and feeling proud of it," she told GoldDerby. The feared thing is a responsibility she might get wrong, not a sensation she wants, though the payoff, pride, is one a Three would claim too. Deleting streams she loved, out of fear, is a hard fit for a Seven. The skydiving stays unexplained.

### Also live: Type 4

The Four case is her inner world. "I've always lived in my own little world since I was young," she told Buchanan, adding that the films she watched made her feel like "quite a dramatic child." Against it, she talks about belonging more than being different. The horror community, she told Variety, "has been the most welcoming out of anyone that I've ever been a part of and I don't ever want to leave that."

Nine and Eight were tested and set aside. She names her anxiety rather than numbing it, and she describes standing up for herself as something playing Nikki taught her: "I felt like that was something I needed to learn."

## What would settle it

On the record so far, safety and image point the same way. What would separate Six from Three is a moment when they don't.

1. **The Oscar nominations,** due January 21, 2027, on the [Academy's schedule](https://press.oscars.org/news/academy-and-abc-announce-show-dates-99th-and-100th-oscarsr). Passed over, does she fall back on her net and her people (Six), move on to the next fun thing (Seven), or set out to overturn the verdict with a bigger move (Three)? Nominated, does she talk about new pressure (Six) or about proof (Three)?
2. **Her first public misstep,** such as a sketch that lands badly when she hosts _Saturday Night Live_, or a run of real criticism. Does she pull back and lean on trusted people (Six), laugh it off (Seven), or repair her image (Three)?
3. **A choice where safety and prestige pull apart:** a trusted director and team over a bigger, riskier project (Six), the prestige role despite the risk (Three), or the strangest new option for its own sake (Seven).
4. **Testimony from before fame.** So far, the only colleagues found speaking about her on record worked on _Obsession_. Family or _Superman & Lois_ castmates could say whether, off camera, she seeks reassurance, stimulation or a good impression.
5. **A fuller retelling** of the Twitch deletion or the playback story, with a date, an adviser or a motive: did she fear losing her footing, or looking less than excellent?

She has already named the test she wants. "I really want to prove that _Obsession_ wasn't just lightning in a bottle," she told Nylon in July 2026. A few sentences later: "I also want to fall on my face publicly and have something that people don't like [but] maybe I do." She laughed. "So, I'm looking forward to that."
