In January 2026, Barron's put Dan Ives on its cover under a question: "He's Wall Street's Biggest Showman. Should You Trust Him?" He didn't answer the reporters' emails, and he blocked both of them on LinkedIn and X.

That July, his publicist reached out to the same two reporters to set up an interview.

Ives had just left Wedbush Securities and launched a new firm. He took the video call from a car, in one of his pastel shirts. "Research is in my DNA," he told them. "It's something that I love. I eat and breathe it."

They asked why he had stepped down as chairman of a crypto company, among other questions. "We didn't hear many specifics," they wrote. What they got was this:

> "I needed to simplify and make sure anything I do, I'm 100% in."

As the call wound down, his spokeswoman said he had to get to his next meeting. Ives had one more line. "I'm unblocking you," he told them. ([Barron's, July 17, 2026](https://www.itiger.com/hans/news/2652281643))

Ives had used that word before, about somebody else. In May 2019, cutting his Tesla price target to $230, he wrote that the company "should be laser focused on shoring up core demand for Model 3 and simplifying its business model and expense structure" instead of expanding into "insurance, robotaxis, and other sci-fi projects/endeavors." ([Fox Business, May 20, 2019](https://www.foxbusiness.com/markets/why-tesla-stock-fell-monday))

In July 2025 he asked Tesla's board to set "guardrails" for how much time [Elon Musk](/personality-analysis/elon-musk) spent at the company. Musk replied on X: "Shut up, Dan." ([Business Insider, July 8, 2025](https://www.aol.com/news/elon-musk-tells-tesla-bull-203948526.html))

Two months later, Ives became chairman of a public crypto company while keeping his day job as Wedbush's head of tech research. Barron's noticed the echo in its cover story: "Ives himself has recognized that too many jobs can be a hindrance for executives." ([Barron's, January 31, 2026](https://www.itiger.com/news/2607047708))

Ives is a technology stock analyst, known on financial TV for neon jackets and for telling investors the tech boom is still early. He spent about 16 years at FBR Capital Markets and eight at Wedbush, where he was global head of technology research until July 1, 2026. Since July 15, 2026 he has been a partner and senior managing director at Yorkville Ives & Co., a "modern merchant bank" he [formed with Yorkville Securities](https://www.prnewswire.com/news-releases/dan-ives-and-yorkville-securities-form-yorkville-ives-a-reimagined-modern-merchant-bank-302825821.html).

The usual question about him is whether the bullishness is personality or job. The record says both. The more revealing question is what he does when a new opportunity starts to cost him something. That answer points to Enneagram Type 7.

## What is Dan Ives's personality type?

Dan Ives's personality type is Enneagram Type 7, the Enthusiast. Sevens belong to the head triad, the three types organized around fear, and they handle it by keeping their options open and their attention on what could go right. Ives does that with markets and with his own career, taking on new roles faster than he drops old ones.

Fear is an odd word for a man known for buy-the-dip notes and highlighter-colored suits. In a [Type 7](/enneagram-corner/enneagram-type-7), though, fear rarely looks like worry. It looks like momentum.

The pattern runs in a few plain steps. A Seven feels a limit closing in: a dull routine, a bad outcome, a single path. They reframe it as an opening. They add another option so they are never down to one. Then they keep moving, because sitting still with the bad feeling is the thing they avoid. The usual cost is focus.

Ives's own words keep landing on that idea. Dressing conventionally, he has said, would be "constricting." Twenty-five years of analyst work became "the treadmill." Asked on the [Smeal Academy Podcast](https://www.iheart.com/podcast/269-the-smeal-academy-podcast-161181326/episode/s2e1-interview-with-daniel-ives-wedbush-securities-269031864) in February 2025 how companies should handle AI ethics and regulation, he answered that "the biggest risk for companies, colleges, is actually just being behind." He has said he is "not afraid to stumble." The fear he does name is falling behind.

This read comes from interviews, filings and reporting, and it is held at medium confidence. His job pays for optimism and visibility, which muddies any read of him. The closest rival is [Type 3](/enneagram-corner/enneagram-type-3), the Achiever, and that case gets its own section below.

## The color came before the cameras

He told [Fortune](https://fortune.com/2026/07/18/dan-ives-modern-merchant-bank-yorkville-why-left-wall-street/) in July 2026 that he has been a "pretty funky dresser" since growing up on Long Island in the 1980s. He toned it down when he reached Wall Street in the 1990s. Then, "over time I kind of ripped a Band-Aid off and just went with whatever works in that morning."

When he explains the clothes, he talks about how they make him feel and think. "My style makes me feel good in a stressful job, talking stocks and dealing with clients and billions of dollars," he told the [New York Post](https://nypost.com/2024/05/28/lifestyle/meet-the-best-dressed-man-on-wall-street/) in May 2024. Later in the same interview he said that when he dresses funky, "it makes me feel more relaxed, and I do think it helps me do my job better." Then he went further.

> "If I dress conventional, it would be constricting in terms of how I think."

The clearest look at him off camera comes from Alice Tecotzky of Business Insider, who went shopping with him in SoHo for a feature published in September 2025. He "didn't look twice at anything in a neutral tone," she wrote. He bought a $58 powder blue hat that said "SOHO SURF," even though he was already wearing a hat and holding another one from lunch. He said "Oh, that's so cool" about piece after piece.

At one point she asked which tech stocks excited him most, just as a store employee handed him mint green and lilac hats. "I'm not sure he heard me," she wrote. What he said was, "Dude, that thing is sick!" ([Business Insider, September 20, 2025](https://www.aol.com/articles/learned-shopping-trip-dan-ives-091102640.html))

It was also a business stop. Ives led her to Snow Milk, the Brooklyn brand behind his clothing line, where "he was careful to point out his shirt hanging on the wall." One afternoon arranged with a reporter proves little on its own.

People who have worked with him describe the enthusiasm as old news. "He's genuine and genuinely enthusiastic about what he does. He has been for a long time," Gil Luria, head of tech research at D.A. Davidson and formerly Wedbush's director of research, told [Barron's](https://www.itiger.com/news/2607047708) in January 2026.

None of this settles a type. It does show that the color came before the TV bookings, and that the enthusiasm shows up off camera.

## The bull who can turn

The job pulls one way. Wall Street analysts rate far more stocks Buy than Sell, and even there Ives stood out. Of the 45 stocks he covered in January 2026, FactSet showed 41 rated Outperform, four Neutral and none Underperform. ([Barron's, January 31, 2026](https://www.itiger.com/news/2607047708))

When he left, Wedbush's chief executive [described the firm](https://www.wedbush.com/wedbush-announces-departure-of-dan-ives-to-new-venture-remains-committed-to-its-global-technology-banking-and-research-platform/) as a place where "distinctive talents like Dan can develop their voice and build their individual brand." Even his friend Dan Nathan, a skeptic of the AI trade, told him on a podcast that being overwhelmingly bullish is "basically how the sell-side works."

So his optimism on air proves nothing about temperament by itself. The turns are the test.

In April 2019 he downgraded Tesla to Neutral and cut his target to $275 from $365. According to a Spanish-language report on the note, he called the quarter one of the biggest debacles in 20 years of covering tech stocks and said his team could no longer look investors in the eye and recommend the shares at current levels until Tesla began to take its medicine. ([El CEO, April 25, 2019](https://elceo.com/tecnologia/no-puedo-mirar-a-los-ojos-a-inversionistas-y-recomendarles-tesla-analista/))

A month later came "a code red situation at Tesla." He did not return to Outperform until April 2021, when he raised his target to $1,000 and called first-quarter deliveries a "paradigm changer." ([The Motley Fool, April 6, 2021](https://www.fool.com/investing/2021/04/06/tesla-stock-could-surge-51-to-1000-according-to-th/))

The mid-2020s showed the same split. In March 2025 he called Tesla's brand damage "a brand tornado crisis moment for Musk and Tesla" and told [Fortune](https://fortune.com/2025/03/20/tesla-mega-bull-dan-ives-elon-musk-doge/), "The clock struck midnight." He kept his Outperform rating.

After the "Shut up, Dan" post that July, he answered by text: "Elon has his opinion and I get it, but we stand by what the right course of action is for the Board." Two months later he told Business Insider that Musk had "pivoted very nicely."

He has also been wrong in his own direction. In December 2021 he forecast the Nasdaq at 19,000 by the end of 2022, according to a TechNews summary of his predictions note. ([TechNews, December 27, 2021](https://technews.tw/2021/12/27/technology-forecast-2022/)) The index closed 2022 at 10,466.88, down 33.1% for the year. ([CNBC, December 30, 2022](https://www.cnbc.com/2022/12/29/stock-market-futures-open-to-close-news.html)) In May 2022, partway down, he wrote that he remained "steadfastly bullish." ([MacDailyNews, May 3, 2022](https://macdailynews.com/2022/05/03/apple-microsoft-remain-top-stock-picks-as-tech-turns-into-have-and-have-nots-analyst/amp/))

So the optimism is a default he can override. Look at what trips the override. In 2019 it was a weak quarter and Tesla's "sci-fi" side projects. In 2025 it was Musk's politics. In the record opened for this page, his alarm goes off at a company's distraction or a broken quarter. It has not gone off at the idea that the AI boom itself might be overdone.

"This is not a bubble," he said in an interview Barron's quoted in January 2026. "This is the start of the fourth industrial revolution."

## The side roles piled up

On June 4, 2025, Wedbush launched the Dan IVES Wedbush AI Revolution ETF, built on his research. He receives a flat, annual fee for the use of his name and likeness, and the fund crossed $1 billion in assets six months after launch. ([Barron's, January 31, 2026](https://www.itiger.com/news/2607047708))

In September 2025, Eightco Holdings, a company worth $4.4 million the Friday before, named him chairman and said it would sell new stock to buy Worldcoin. That is the token of World, the iris-scanning identity project run by OpenAI CEO [Sam Altman](/personality-analysis/sam-altman). By Monday's close, Eightco's implied market value was $8.5 billion. ([Barron's, September 9, 2025](https://www.itiger.com/hant/news/2566587306))

> "I am so excited to be named Chairman of Eightco Holdings Inc., marking the next step in the AI revolution around authentication and Proof of Human."

He told Barron's the chairmanship was "in addition to my role as Head of Tech Research at Wedbush, which I expect to be for years to come."

He met Sen. Ted Cruz and other senators in that capacity and posted, "Great to be driving the AI, ORBS, and Crypto vision on Capitol Hill." Before the end of 2025 he had also joined the advisory board of Zeta Global, a marketing-tech company that competes with Salesforce, one of the stocks he covered. ([Barron's, January 31, 2026](https://www.itiger.com/news/2607047708))

When he took the Eightco seat, it had been two months since he asked Tesla's board to put guardrails on Musk's time. By the end of 2025 his own week ran across Wedbush research, a crypto company's board, Capitol Hill and a marketing company's advisory board. Each new seat put his most valuable asset, his standing as an objective analyst, at a little more risk.

The bill came on January 31, 2026. Barron's reported that the Eightco, Zeta and ETF roles "aren't regularly disclosed in research reports" and often overlapped with his coverage. Mark Bradshaw, who chairs Boston College's accounting department, said he had never seen a scenario like Ives's simultaneous roles. Attorney Adam Gana called the chairmanship "a serious issue."

Wedbush said the reporting "may unintentionally conflate topics that are, in reality, separate" and cited his "unwavering integrity." This page found no regulatory action on those matters as of October 24, 2026.

His public answer came on CNBC after the story ran: "I get the scrutiny, but everything's vetted. If I chew gum, Wedbush has to know about it." ([Barron's, July 17, 2026](https://www.itiger.com/hans/news/2652281643))

About a week and a half later, on the podcast with Nathan, he said the roles were all public. "I also get that it breaks the mold," he said, and "no one's ever seen anything like it." Later in the conversation: "I'm like an open book." He also said he would "continue to evaluate new ones" as "more opportunities come," and keep looking at the existing ones. ([OK Computer podcast, February 11, 2026](https://riskreversal.substack.com/p/dan-ives-the-1-trillion-buying-opportunity-dce))

Six weeks after the cover story, he stepped down from Eightco. In July 2026 he left Wedbush. The research job he had expected to keep "for years to come" lasted ten months after he said it.

## "I needed to simplify"

Wedbush announced his departure on July 1, 2026. Barron's reported that it came one day after he had initiated coverage of SpaceX. He told [CNBC](https://www.cnbc.com/2026/07/01/tech-analyst-dan-ives-is-exiting-wedbush-for-a-new-venture.html) the new firm would be about "seeing the opportunities that are around the corner."

He gave Fortune the longer version. "As an analyst I always talk about companies, whether good or bad, what they've done. But I've never built anything," he said.

> "I wanted to get off the treadmill and actually build something myself."

In the launch-week interviews, he spoke in possibilities. Asked by [Business Insider](https://www.aol.com/articles/dan-ives-wall-street-analyst-095701000.html) what kinds of deals the firm would pursue, he said it would be a "Swiss Army knife." The partnership, he said, was "really about trying to figure out a one plus one equals four." The reporter found him "light on details."

Some of that is caution. The firm's research arm was still awaiting FINRA approval, and a new partnership has reasons to say little.

When Fortune's business editor, Nick Lichtenberg, reached him on Zoom, Ives wore a loud tropical shirt with "Margaritaville" across the chest.

Lichtenberg laid out two readings of the move: the famous optimist who drank his own Kool-Aid, or an analyst who finally has skin in the game. Ives said, "If you told me five years ago that we'd be having this conversation, that was not on the bingo card." The goal, he said, was "building something special for the next decade."

He also gave Fortune a more personal reason for his appetite for risk. From 2015 through 2025, he said, he dealt with his father's Alzheimer's and his mother's Parkinson's. His mother died in 2020 at 70 and his father in 2025 at 79.

> "I had great parents that passed away young and I'm not afraid to stumble."

"I am 51 today but I view life through a different lens than I did when I was 40 years old after going through this nightmare," he said. On looking back at choices where "you should have gone left instead of right," he said, "I'm not gonna like live life like that." He added that he doesn't mean "jumping off a cliff to get some Instagram picture." ([Fortune, July 18, 2026](https://fortune.com/2026/07/18/dan-ives-modern-merchant-bank-yorkville-why-left-wall-street/))

Grief can push anyone toward what he called "only one life to live," so this does not type him on its own. What it adds is his own account of where the risk tolerance comes from. He ties it to time running out, and the account says nothing about being seen.

The simplified version kept growing. On September 30, 2026, Ives Ultra AI Opportunities, a closed-end fund that trades on the NYSE as IVAI, priced a $200 million IPO. Ives chairs the board of managers of the fund's adviser but is not its portfolio manager. ([PR Newswire, September 30, 2026](https://finviz.com/news/396853/ives-ultra-ai-opportunities-inc-nyse-ivai-announces-pricing-of-200-million-initial-public-offering))

On October 8 he [published his Ives AI 30 list](https://www.financialcontent.com/article/gnwcq-2026-10-8-dan-ives-publishes-the-latest-ives-ai-30-research-identifying-the-next-wave-of-ai-opportunities) under the Yorkville Ives name. "We believe we are still only in the third inning of the AI revolution," he said.

As of October 24, 2026, his FINRA record listed six outside business activities, including the Snow Milk line, the Zeta board, a licensing fee for his image and likeness on the Dan Ives Wedbush AI Power and Infrastructure ETF, and the chairmanship of an Ives Ultra fund. Three of the six began in July or September 2026. ([FINRA BrokerCheck](https://files.brokercheck.finra.org/individual/individual_4205842.pdf))

Some of that is simply the new firm's business, and leaving the Eightco seat was a real cut. Still, in practice "simplify" meant trading a crypto chairmanship and a day job for a bigger venture with his name on the door.

## Why not Type 3?

Threes sit in the heart triad, where the core emotion is shame, and they answer it by becoming what success looks like to the people watching. The [Type 3](/enneagram-corner/enneagram-type-3) case for Ives is strong, and much of it is in his own words.

He speaks brand fluently. "It's definitely played a role in the global success, because of the way that I dress," he told Business Insider in 2025. To a Penn State student outlet he said "a kind of brand was created, but it was never that intention." ([Onward State, March 28, 2025](https://onwardstate.com/2025/03/28/the-seeds-of-all-that-was-penn-state-wall-street-personality-dan-ives-journey-from-penn-state-to-tv/))

On an alumni podcast he called an AI day he sponsors at Penn State "a way to really also brand Smeal, brand Penn State as an AI leader," adding, "that's something that's very important to us from a branding perspective."

His name and likeness are on two ETFs, a research list, a fund and a firm. He tells a hard-work story about credibility: "a lot of hard work, a lot of weekends to build myself from a technology outsider to gaining credibility within tech." ([UMD Smith School, December 2025](https://www.rhsmith.umd.edu/smith-profiles/bright-spot-wall-street))

He toned his clothes down when he arrived on Wall Street, an adjustment to an audience. He protects the image, too: a publicist handles hard questions, he blocked the Barron's reporters, and Wedbush asked Barron's to change a June 2025 headline that called him a fund pitchman.

On a Three reading, Eightco and Capitol Hill were steps up in reach. "Simplify" was a reputational retreat once the roles became a story, and his own bank is the next rung.

Here is where Seven pulls ahead. A Three guards the asset that makes them admired, and for a sell-side analyst that asset is credibility as an objective judge of stocks. Ives kept adding roles that put it at risk, in public, after a national magazine and outside experts said so. Days later he said he would keep evaluating new ones.

When the damage came, he did not run a repair campaign built on specifics. He avoided the critics, defended the roles as new and open in friendly rooms, then called his exit simplifying and moved into a bigger venture described in possibilities. His explanation of the clothes points the same way. It is about mood and freedom, and he has called the look the lesser part: "The fashion is what they see, but the content is ultimately what's important."

Limits apply. Legal caution and PR handling could explain the vague answers, and the Eightco seat also bought visibility a Three would value. That is why the read stays at medium confidence. An Eight reading fails faster: an Eight would have taken the fight to Barron's, and Ives blocked its reporters.

## What the type leaves out

The biggest gap is a chapter he rarely mentions. In February 2016 he left sell-side research for an operating job at Synchronoss Technologies, a mobile cloud company, where he led investor relations. ([NJBIZ, February 16, 2016](https://njbiz.com/synchronoss-names-svp-of-finance-corporate-development/)) The SEC says he was terminated in mid-2017.

In a settled 2022 order, the SEC found that he helped negotiate one year-end 2016 deal, promising the other company it would not suffer a loss, through which Synchronoss improperly recognized about $3.6 million in revenue.

Without admitting or denying the findings, he paid a $15,000 penalty and accepted three years of limits on acting as a salesperson in certain deals.
