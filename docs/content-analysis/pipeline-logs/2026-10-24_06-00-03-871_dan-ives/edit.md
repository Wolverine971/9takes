While two Barron's reporters were working on a cover story about Dan Ives, the Wall Street tech analyst known for bullish TV calls and neon jackets, he didn't return their requests for comment and blocked both of them on LinkedIn and X. The story ran on January 31, 2026, under a question: "He's Wall Street's Biggest Showman. Should You Trust Him?" ([Barron's, January 31, 2026](https://www.itiger.com/news/2607047708))

That July, his publicist reached out to the same two reporters to set up an interview.

Ives had just left Wedbush Securities and launched a new firm. He took the video call from a car, in one of his pastel shirts. "Research is in my DNA," he told them. "It's something that I love. I eat and breathe it."

They asked when his talks with the new firm began, why he had left a crypto company's chairmanship, and why he had started adding disclosures to his posts on X after their story. "We didn't hear many specifics," wrote the reporters he had blocked. Part of what they got was this:

> "I needed to simplify and make sure anything I do, I'm 100% in."

As the call wound down, his spokeswoman said he had to get to his next meeting. Ives had one more line. "I'm unblocking you," he told them. ([Barron's, July 17, 2026](https://www.itiger.com/hans/news/2652281643))

He had used "simplify" before, about somebody else. In May 2019, cutting his Tesla price target to $230, he wrote that the company "should be laser focused on shoring up core demand for Model 3 and simplifying its business model and expense structure" instead of expanding into "insurance, robotaxis, and other sci-fi projects/endeavors." ([Fox Business, May 20, 2019](https://www.foxbusiness.com/markets/why-tesla-stock-fell-monday))

In July 2025 he asked Tesla's board to set "guardrails" for how much time [Elon Musk](/personality-analysis/elon-musk) spent at the company. Musk replied on X: "Shut up, Dan." ([Business Insider, July 8, 2025](https://www.aol.com/news/elon-musk-tells-tesla-bull-203948526.html))

Two months later, Ives became chairman of a public crypto company while keeping his day job as Wedbush's head of tech research. Barron's noticed the echo in its cover story: "Ives himself has recognized that too many jobs can be a hindrance for executives." ([Barron's, January 31, 2026](https://www.itiger.com/news/2607047708))

Ives built his name as a technology stock analyst, known on financial TV for neon jackets and for telling investors the tech boom is still early. After about 16 years at FBR Capital Markets and two in corporate and market-research jobs, he spent eight at Wedbush as global head of technology research. Since July 15, 2026 he has been a partner and senior managing director at Yorkville Ives & Co., a "modern merchant bank" he [formed with Yorkville Securities](https://www.prnewswire.com/news-releases/dan-ives-and-yorkville-securities-form-yorkville-ives-a-reimagined-modern-merchant-bank-302825821.html) to combine "Research, banking, trading, and capital."

The usual question about him is whether the bullishness is personality or job. The record says both. The more revealing question is what he says he is protecting when a choice starts to cost him something.

## What is Dan Ives's personality type?

Dan Ives reads as Enneagram Type 7, the Enthusiast, at medium confidence, with Type 3, the Achiever, a close rival. Sevens are head-triad types, driven underneath by fear, who steer away from limits and toward what could go right. Ives explains his own choices that way: conventional dress would be "constricting," and his analyst career became "the treadmill."

Fear is an odd word for a man known for buy-the-dip notes and highlighter-colored suits. In a [Type 7](/enneagram-corner/enneagram-type-7), though, fear seldom shows up as worry. It shows up as momentum, and Ives has never named it. He says he is "not afraid to stumble." The fear here is this page's inference from what he does and from the words he reaches for when something narrows.

His job pays for optimism and visibility, which muddies any read of him, and [Type 3](/enneagram-corner/enneagram-type-3) explains much of the same record. The call is close.

## The color came before the cameras

He told [Fortune](https://fortune.com/2026/07/18/dan-ives-modern-merchant-bank-yorkville-why-left-wall-street/) in July 2026 that he has been a "pretty funky dresser" since growing up on Long Island in the 1980s. He toned it down when he reached Wall Street in the 1990s. Then, "over time I kind of ripped a Band-Aid off and just went with whatever works in that morning."

He explains the clothes first by how they make him feel and think. "My style makes me feel good in a stressful job, talking stocks and dealing with clients and billions of dollars," he told the [New York Post](https://nypost.com/2024/05/28/lifestyle/meet-the-best-dressed-man-on-wall-street/) in May 2024. When he dresses funky, "it makes me feel more relaxed, and I do think it helps me do my job better." The audience comes up too: "I wore a traditional sports jacket with just a little color and people took that very bearishly," he joked.

> "If I dress conventional, it would be constricting in terms of how I think."

Business Insider's Alice Tecotzky went shopping with him in SoHo for a September 2025 feature. He "didn't look twice at anything in a neutral tone," she wrote. At the first store he bought a $58 powder blue hat that said "SOHO SURF," even though he was already wearing a hat and holding another one from lunch.

Then he led her to Snow Milk, the Brooklyn brand behind his clothing line, where "he was careful to point out his shirt hanging on the wall," a stop she called possibly "a good business move." Among his collaboration's clothes, he said "Oh, that's so cool" about piece after piece.

At one point she asked which tech stocks excited him most, just as a store employee handed him mint green and lilac hats. "I'm not sure he heard me," she wrote. What he said was, "Dude, that thing is sick!" ([Business Insider, September 20, 2025](https://www.aol.com/articles/learned-shopping-trip-dan-ives-091102640.html))

In his own collaboration's store, delight and promotion can't be pulled apart. One afternoon arranged with a reporter proves little on its own.

"He's genuine and genuinely enthusiastic about what he does. He has been for a long time," Gil Luria, head of tech research at D.A. Davidson and formerly Wedbush's director of research, told [Barron's](https://www.itiger.com/news/2607047708) in January 2026.

None of this settles a type. It does show that the color came before the TV bookings, and that the enthusiasm shows up off camera.

## The bull who can turn

The job pulls one way. Analysts rate far more stocks Buy than Sell, and even there Ives stood out: of the 45 stocks he covered in January 2026, FactSet showed 41 rated Outperform, four Neutral and none Underperform. ([Barron's, January 31, 2026](https://www.itiger.com/news/2607047708))

Wedbush's chief executive [described the firm](https://www.wedbush.com/wedbush-announces-departure-of-dan-ives-to-new-venture-remains-committed-to-its-global-technology-banking-and-research-platform/) as a place where "distinctive talents like Dan can develop their voice and build their individual brand." His friend Dan Nathan, a skeptic of the AI trade, told him on a podcast that being overwhelmingly bullish is "basically how the sell-side works," the sell side being brokerage analysts who publish research for investor clients.

So his optimism on air proves nothing about temperament by itself. The turns are the test.

In April 2019 he downgraded Tesla to Neutral and cut his target to $275 from $365. A month later came "a code red situation at Tesla." He did not return to Outperform until April 2021. ([The Motley Fool, April 6, 2021](https://www.fool.com/investing/2021/04/06/tesla-stock-could-surge-51-to-1000-according-to-th/))

By 2025 Business Insider was calling him "Wall Street's biggest Tesla bull," and his turns came inside the bull case. That March, with Musk running DOGE, the Trump administration's cost-cutting effort, he called Tesla's brand damage "a brand tornado crisis moment for Musk and Tesla," urged Musk to step back from DOGE and kept his Outperform rating. ([Fortune, March 20, 2025](https://fortune.com/2025/03/20/tesla-mega-bull-dan-ives-elon-musk-doge/)) By June the robotaxis he had once filed under "sci-fi" were a centerpiece: "We view this autonomous chapter as one of the most important for Musk and Tesla in its history as a company." ([Fortune, June 20, 2025](https://fortune.com/2025/06/20/tesla-robotaxi-austin-texas-elon-musk-dan-ives/))

After the "Shut up, Dan" post that July, he answered by text: "Elon has his opinion and I get it, but we stand by what the right course of action is for the Board." Two months later he told Business Insider that Musk had "pivoted very nicely."

He has also been wrong in his own direction. In December 2021 he forecast the Nasdaq at 19,000 by the end of 2022, according to a [TechNews summary](https://technews.tw/2021/12/27/technology-forecast-2022/) of his predictions note. The index closed 2022 at 10,466.88, down 33.1% for the year. ([CNBC, December 30, 2022](https://www.cnbc.com/2022/12/29/stock-market-futures-open-to-close-news.html)) In May 2022, partway down, he wrote that he remained "steadfastly bullish." ([MacDailyNews, May 3, 2022](https://macdailynews.com/2022/05/03/apple-microsoft-remain-top-stock-picks-as-tech-turns-into-have-and-have-nots-analyst/amp/))

So the optimism is a default he can override. In 2019 the trigger was a weak quarter and Tesla's "sci-fi" side projects. In 2025 it was Musk's politics, then tariffs, which he wrote would "set the U.S. tech world back a decade." ([The Motley Fool, April 7, 2025](https://www.fool.com/investing/2025/04/07/why-shares-of-apple-are-getting-hammered-today/))

In the record opened for this page, as of October 2026, his alarm goes off at a company's distraction, a broken quarter or a policy threat to the tech trade. It has not gone off at the idea that the AI boom itself might be overdone.

"This is not a bubble," he said in an interview Barron's quoted in January 2026.

## The side roles piled up

On June 4, 2025, Wedbush launched the Dan IVES Wedbush AI Revolution ETF, built on his research. Barron's reported in January 2026 that he received a flat, annual fee for the use of his name and likeness. Wedbush said he was barred from holding the ETF or its stocks, with an "information barrier" between him and the unit running it. ([Barron's, January 31, 2026](https://www.itiger.com/news/2607047708))

In September 2025, Eightco Holdings, a company worth $4.4 million the Friday before, named him chairman and said it would sell new stock to buy Worldcoin. That is the token of World, the iris-scanning identity project run by OpenAI CEO [Sam Altman](/personality-analysis/sam-altman). By Monday's close, Eightco's implied market value was $8.5 billion. ([Barron's, September 9, 2025](https://www.itiger.com/hant/news/2566587306))

He said he was "so excited" and told Barron's the chairmanship was "in addition to my role as Head of Tech Research at Wedbush, which I expect to be for years to come." He met Sen. Ted Cruz and other senators in that capacity and posted, "Great to be driving the AI, ORBS, and Crypto vision on Capitol Hill." ORBS was Eightco's new ticker.

The surge did not last. After peaking, Barron's reported in January 2026, the stock "quickly fell and has since shed 97% of its value." In a year-end chairman message, Ives said, "When we look at ORBS, we think it's an undervalued asset." This page found no other comment from him on the fall.

Before the end of 2025 he had also joined the advisory board of Zeta Global, a marketing-tech company that competes with Salesforce, one of the stocks he covered.

Barron's spelled out the stakes. Investors rely on analysts' research, and FINRA, the industry body that regulates brokerage analysts, bars conduct that compromises their objectivity and requires them to disclose conflicts. Each new seat put his standing as an objective analyst at a little more risk.

## After the cover story

The bill came on January 31, 2026. Barron's reported that the Eightco, Zeta and ETF roles "aren't regularly disclosed in research reports" and often overlapped with his coverage. As Eightco's chairman he lobbied for AI and crypto, "the same industries he's tasked with covering." The ETF held stocks he rated. In December 2025 he kept his Outperform rating on Salesforce without disclosing that he advised a rival.

Barron's also noted that his X bio listed some of the roles and that the Zeta seat appeared in a footnote of his FINRA record. Mark Bradshaw, who chairs Boston College's accounting department, said he had never seen a scenario like Ives's simultaneous roles and worried about research "tainted because of an affiliation with the ETF that holds the stocks for which he's doing research." Attorney Adam Gana called the chairmanship "a serious issue."

Zeta said its advisers serve "in a nonfiduciary, non-governance capacity." Wedbush said the reporting "may unintentionally conflate topics that are, in reality, separate" and cited his "unwavering integrity." This page found no regulatory action on those matters as of October 24, 2026.

What he did next ran in two directions. The Monday after the story, he took the questions on CNBC's Fast Money, a show Nathan appears on, as Nathan later recalled. "I get the scrutiny, but everything's vetted. If I chew gum, Wedbush has to know about it," he said on CNBC. ([Barron's, July 17, 2026](https://www.itiger.com/hans/news/2652281643))

About a week and a half after the story, on Nathan's podcast, he said the roles were all on his FINRA registration and that he had nothing to do with managing the ETF. "I also get that it breaks the mold," he said, and "no one's ever seen anything like it." Later: "I'm like an open book." He said he would "continue to evaluate new ones" as "more opportunities come," and look again at existing ones. ([OK Computer podcast, February 11, 2026](https://riskreversal.substack.com/p/dan-ives-the-1-trillion-buying-opportunity-dce))

At some point after the article, Barron's noted, he began adding disclosures to his posts on X. FINRA also lists a new likeness fee for him on a second Wedbush fund, the Dan Ives Wedbush AI Power and Infrastructure ETF, starting April 1, 2026.

Six weeks after the cover story, he stepped down from Eightco. In July 2026 he left Wedbush. The research job he had expected to keep "for years to come" lasted ten months after he said it.

## "I needed to simplify"

He told [CNBC](https://www.cnbc.com/2026/07/01/tech-analyst-dan-ives-is-exiting-wedbush-for-a-new-venture.html) the new firm would be about "seeing the opportunities that are around the corner," and [Business Insider](https://www.aol.com/articles/dan-ives-wall-street-analyst-095701000.html) that leaving was a "tough decision" after months of talks with Yorkville. He gave Fortune the longer version. "As an analyst I always talk about companies, whether good or bad, what they've done. But I've never built anything," he said.

> "I wanted to get off the treadmill and actually build something myself."

He also explained "simplify." Asked by Barron's whether his new team had discussed the January reporting, he said, "A number of the roles I no longer have." Building the bank was "a gargantuan task," and "This is something where I really needed to be 110% focused." Focus, his old prescription for Tesla, was now his own stated aim.

In launch week he spoke in possibilities. Asked by Business Insider what kinds of deals the firm would pursue, he said it would be a "Swiss Army knife." The partnership, he said, was "really about trying to figure out a one plus one equals four." The reporter found him "light on details."

Some of that is caution. At launch, Barron's reported, the firm's research arm was still awaiting FINRA approval, and a new partnership has reasons to say little.

When Fortune's business editor, Nick Lichtenberg, reached him on Zoom, Ives wore a loud tropical shirt with "Margaritaville" across the chest. The goal, he said, was "building something special for the next decade."

He also gave Fortune a personal source for what the magazine called his "tolerance for calculated risk." From 2015 through 2025, he said, he dealt with his father's Alzheimer's and his mother's Parkinson's. His mother died in 2020 at 70 and his father in 2025 at 79.

> "I had great parents that passed away young and I'm not afraid to stumble."

On looking back at choices where "you should have gone left instead of right," he said, "I'm not gonna like live life like that." He added that he doesn't mean "jumping off a cliff to get some Instagram picture." ([Fortune, July 18, 2026](https://fortune.com/2026/07/18/dan-ives-modern-merchant-bank-yorkville-why-left-wall-street/))

Grief can push anyone toward what he called "only one life to live," so this does not type him on its own.

Most of what followed belongs to the new firm. On September 30, 2026, Ives Ultra AI Opportunities, a closed-end fund that trades on the NYSE as IVAI, priced a $200 million IPO. ([PR Newswire, September 30, 2026](https://finviz.com/news/396853/ives-ultra-ai-opportunities-inc-nyse-ivai-announces-pricing-of-200-million-initial-public-offering)) Yorkville Ives owns 49.9% of its adviser, according to [IFR](https://www.ifre.com/equities/2493743/ives-ultra-ai-brings-spac-like-ipo), and Ives chairs the adviser's board but is not the fund's portfolio manager. On October 8 he [published his Ives AI 30 list](https://www.financialcontent.com/article/gnwcq-2026-10-8-dan-ives-publishes-the-latest-ives-ai-30-research-identifying-the-next-wave-of-ai-opportunities) under the Yorkville Ives name: "We believe we are still only in the third inning of the AI revolution."

Leaving Eightco was a real cut, and his stated aim was focus. Still, the build carries his name, and as of October 24, 2026 his FINRA record still listed outside roles beyond the firm: the Snow Milk clothing line, the Zeta advisory board and the likeness fee on the Wedbush power-and-infrastructure ETF. ([FINRA BrokerCheck](https://files.brokercheck.finra.org/individual/individual_4205842.pdf))

## Why not Type 3?

Threes sit in the heart triad, where the core emotion is shame, and they answer it by becoming what success looks like to the people watching. The [Type 3](/enneagram-corner/enneagram-type-3) case for Ives is strong, and much of it is in his own words.

He speaks brand fluently. "It's definitely played a role in the global success, because of the way that I dress," he told Business Insider in 2025. "Even people who don't know my stock calls, they know me as the person who wears the funky shirts on CNBC." To a Penn State student outlet he said "a kind of brand was created, but it was never that intention." ([Onward State, March 28, 2025](https://onwardstate.com/2025/03/28/the-seeds-of-all-that-was-penn-state-wall-street-personality-dan-ives-journey-from-penn-state-to-tv/))

As of October 2026 his name is on two ETFs, a research list, a fund and a firm, and at the launch he said he brought his brand and research expertise to the partnership. He tells a hard-work story: "a lot of hard work, a lot of weekends to build myself from a technology outsider to gaining credibility within tech." ## What the type leaves out

The biggest gap is a chapter that rarely appears in the interviews opened for this page. In February 2016 he left sell-side research for an operating job at Synchronoss Technologies, a mobile cloud company, where he led investor relations. ([NJBIZ, February 16, 2016](https://njbiz.com/synchronoss-names-svp-of-finance-corporate-development/)) The SEC says he was terminated in mid-2017. Its order does not say why.

In June 2022 the SEC charged Synchronoss and seven senior employees over accounting the company had restated, about $190 million in revenue, and the company paid a $12.5 million penalty. Ives was one of four current or former employees who settled over side-letter agreements, with penalties from $15,000 to $90,000. ([SEC release, June 7, 2022](https://mondovisione.com/media-and-resources/news/sec-charges-new-jersey-software-company-and-senior-employees-with-accounting-rel-202267/))

In a settled 2022 order, the SEC found that he helped negotiate one year-end 2016 deal, promising the other company it would not suffer a loss, through which Synchronoss improperly recognized about $3.6 million in revenue. It found the fee was contingent on resale, "as Ives knew or recklessly disregarded," and that the revenue "materially inflated" 2016 results until a restatement reversed it. The violations were of internal-controls and books-and-records rules.

Without admitting or denying the findings, he paid a $15,000 penalty and accepted three years of limits on acting as a salesperson in certain deals. ([SEC, June 7, 2022](https://www.sec.gov/files/litigation/admin/2022/34-95052.pdf)) His FINRA record answers "No" to whether the order rests on rules against fraudulent, manipulative or deceptive conduct.

After a year at GBH Insights, a market research firm, he joined Wedbush in 2018, so the 2026 exit was his second departure from sell-side research. Apart from a 2020 interview bio, the Synchronoss years do not appear in the career accounts he gave in the interviews opened for this page, though his FINRA filing lists the job and the order. The type cannot say what that chapter meant to him, and this page does not guess.

## The Strip, without the mobsters

"We're going to look back on this period," he told Fortune in July 2026, "and realize this is building the Vegas Strip in 1955." Five months earlier, when Nathan pushed on AI spending, he had reached for a Las Vegas casino under construction to make the same point. "You have to have some faith," he said, and "be a believer."

Fortune's editor asked the obvious follow-up: without the mobsters?

Ives paused. "Without the mobsters."

It was a joke. It is also the read in one exchange. He sees a boom, he hears the question about its cost, and after a beat he picks the version where it works out. He has given himself the "next decade" to find out.
