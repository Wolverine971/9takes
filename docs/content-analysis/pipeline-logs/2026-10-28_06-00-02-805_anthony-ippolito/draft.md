Before anyone had cast a movie about the making of _Rocky_, Anthony Ippolito hung a heavy bag in his grandmother's basement and started turning himself into Sylvester Stallone.

"I didn't have a script, so I was just working with my imagination, trying to capture the physicality of the character," he told [TheWrap](https://www.thewrap.com/creative-content/movies/anthony-ippolito-i-play-rocky-how-he-was-cast-interview/) at the Toronto International Film Festival in September 2026. He had read a news article saying Peter Farrelly was making the film. A friend and his cousin helped him shoot the tape, and his agent sent it to the producers.

Farrelly tells the ending. The studio loved the script, he recalled at [The Hollywood Reporter's TIFF studio](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=rWiOZ9ZNwTs), but wanted to know how he would ever find a Stallone when casting hadn't even started. "I said, he found us."

That tape became _I Play Rocky_. The film premiered at TIFF on September 12, 2026, and opens in limited U.S. release on November 6, expanding nationwide on November 20 ([Variety](https://variety.com/2026/film/awards/i-play-rocky-tiff-premiere-peter-farrelly-stallone-oscars-1236859905/)). Variety's Clayton Davis called his performance "a star-making turn."

Ippolito is a Long Island native in his late twenties who has acted since childhood. Before this he had an ensemble role alongside [Odessa A'Zion](/personality-analysis/odessa-azion) on Netflix's _Grand Army_ (2020) and played a young Al Pacino in Paramount+'s _The Offer_ (2022), which starred [Miles Teller](/personality-analysis/miles-teller). Some outlets call _Grand Army_ his breakout. _I Play Rocky_ is his first lead.

Ask him about playing either icon and fear comes up fast. Playing Pacino was "pretty terrifying, it makes you want to throw up," he told [Variety](https://variety.com/2022/scene/news/offer-premiere-godfather-50-years-later-1235238228/) in 2022. Playing Stallone brought "a lot of intimidation at the beginning," he said at the Rome Film Fest in October 2026 ([Screen Daily](https://www.screendaily.com/news/i-play-rocky-star-anthony-ippolito-on-the-weight-of-responsibility-playing-a-young-sylvester-stallone/5221614.article)).

Yet the move that made his career is one nobody invited.

So which is he: a man who quiets fear by preparing until he feels ready, or a man making sure the right people see him? Almost everything he has said in public comes from red carpets, festival junkets and short written Q&As. That record can't yet tell those motives apart, which makes this an open case.

## What is Anthony Ippolito's personality type?

Leading read: Enneagram Type 6, and it is a low-confidence call. Type 3 is the closest rival, and Types 9 and 1 are still in play. In both icon roles he named the fear and answered it with research. But his uninvited bid to be "seen" fits Three too, and the record is too young to choose.

[Type 6](/enneagram-corner/enneagram-type-6) sits in the head triad, where the core emotion is fear. A Six handles it by building solid ground before stepping out: research, a plan, people worth trusting.

[Type 3](/enneagram-corner/enneagram-type-3) sits in the heart triad, where the core emotion is shame, and a Three answers it by achieving where the right people can see. [Type 9](/enneagram-corner/enneagram-type-9) and [Type 1](/enneagram-corner/enneagram-type-1) sit in the body triad, built around anger: a Nine smooths it into calm, a One turns it into getting things right.

Six leads because the fear shows up in his own words in two roles four years apart, followed each time by the same response. Three stays close because his boldest act was a bid to be seen. All of it shows what he says and does in public, not what he feels.

## The tape nobody asked for

His own account to TheWrap has two stages. The first tape was improvised: "It kind of felt like a bit of a long shot, but it was a creative exercise, and it was actually really fun." The producers then sent the script and asked for a proper audition, and he shot several more scenes on his own, TheWrap reports.

Farrelly remembers it differently. At THR's studio he said Ippolito "had somehow found the script" and taped three scenes. In a video for [IMDb](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=TP7M9Ce7eUM) that month, he placed the taping "in his own apartment." Both men agree the tape arrived before casting began. "He was the first person we looked at, the only person," Farrelly said in Rome.

Why tape himself at all? He knew getting in front of the filmmakers "wasn't necessarily going to be easy," he told [Collider](https://www.youtube.com/shorts/Hhf0cENNyXE) at TIFF. Then he named what he was after:

> "I just really felt that at least if I was seen and I could present my interpretation, then I'd be able to live with it."

Read one way, the key word is "seen," the move of someone who needs the right people to notice. Read another, the key phrase is "live with it": he was guarding against regret. One junket answer can't separate those.

At THR's studio, the interviewer asked whether he ever hesitated to take a role that means so much to people. "I was fighting for it," he said. Farrelly cut in: "By the way, it wasn't much of a fight."

It wasn't his first self-tape under pressure. In 2020, playing baseball for Baruch College, he broke his hand in a game, and his _Grand Army_ audition fell the day after a procedure on it. "I emailed my agent like, 'Listen, I'm gonna have to take a pass on this,'" he told the [New York Post](https://nypost.com/2020/10/14/meet-the-cast-of-netflixs-gritty-new-nyc-high-school-drama-grand-army/).

The agent suggested a tape. "But I kept my hand out of the frame 'cause I didn't want them to see I had a broken hand."

A broken hand explains the first instinct. Still, the pattern shifted. In 2020 someone else proposed the tape. For _I Play Rocky_, he made one before anyone asked.

## "It makes you want to throw up"

The fear language starts four years before Stallone.

At _The Offer_'s premiere in April 2022, the line about wanting to throw up came with two more. Playing Pacino was also "a freaking honor," he told Variety, and it "made me feel a huge weight of responsibility, but I had so much fun." To [The Hollywood Reporter](https://www.hollywoodreporter.com/tv/tv-news/the-offer-the-godfather-premiere-1235133610/) that night, he said, "I'm kind of terrified of [him] seeing it because he's someone I've looked up to, and I respect his work."

In Rome four years later, the vocabulary came back almost word for word, now paired with what he does about it:

> "I was a fan of the movies and a fan of the character, and I understand how many people are, so I did feel the weight of the responsibility. But like any other situation, you just start by researching as much as you can."

His co-star AnnaSophia Robb reached for the same word at CinemaCon in April 2026: "It's so easy to root for Anthony because he really took on this character and the weight of carrying this movie," she told [Fandango](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=exlWOS3GGuQ).

The order holds each time. Name the difficulty, then go to work. Asked by IMDb where he started with Stallone's voice and posture, he said, "You start by saying, I don't know how to do this, and it's daunting, but you have to kind of be humbled by the task and figure it out from there piece by piece."

He spoke to Stallone briefly by Zoom about a month before shooting. He told THR it was "a lot to take in," with "a lot going on in my head," and "I was really just trying to listen as much as possible and hear his perspective."

With both icons he studied the actor before the legend: Pacino before _The Godfather_, and Stallone's bit parts from 1972 to 1976. "When you watch someone long enough you kind of pick up certain things, idiosyncrasies if you're open to receiving them," he told [Haute Living](https://hauteliving.com/2022/05/anthony-ippolito-plays-al-pacino-in-paramounts-the-offer/712929/) in 2022.

Farrelly saw the same habits from the director's chair. "He was so prepared, you know, and humble," he said in the IMDb video. Ippolito kept ending sentences with a phrase Stallone used in his 1970s interviews. And Farrelly learned only in pre-production, by asking, that Ippolito had played Pacino, which Farrelly called "the best part of The Offer." By then Ippolito already had the job.

One detail complicates the pattern. In 2020, asked whether his first series-regular role brought pressure, he told [Pop-Culturalist](https://pop-culturalist.com/?p=27426): "A little bit, but I felt so strongly about the material that it was more of an excited energy than a nervous one."

The fear clusters around the two icon roles, playing famous men who might watch. Almost any actor would feel that, and press tours reward a newcomer's humility.

## Two years in Gleason's Gym

The other thing he did before anyone asked was change his body. At the TIFF premiere he put the effort at "a year and a half to two years" ([USA Today](https://www.youtube.com/shorts/u0yVV1eVpgM)). [Variety](https://au.variety.com/2026/film/news/i-play-rocky-anthony-ippolito-sylvester-stallone-dog-41877/) reports that he bulked up from about 150 pounds to roughly 180 at Gleason's Gym in Brooklyn.

Asked on Collider whether he had gained "Like 40?" pounds, he said, "Something like that, yeah." Farrelly compared it to [Robert De Niro](/personality-analysis/robert-de-niro) in _Raging Bull_.

He talks about it as endurance. It was "a consistency thing" and "an entire lifestyle shift," he told Variety, and it "tested me in more ways than one," he told IMDb.

He has since taken most of the weight off. "It's a lot to maintain," he told Collider, and "it's not my natural."

A Three builds the body a leading man needs. A Six prepares until fear has nowhere left to stand. His own words are about consistency and being tested, not about how he looked, and the studio needed the physique either way.

## Imitation or essence

Playing real people invites one judgment: copy or essence?

Casting director John Papsidera cast him as Pacino after the first actor chosen withdrew. His verdict: "Again, not an exact duplicate, but his eyes did a lot of the heavy lifting and more people commented about him than almost any other role" ([Daily Actor](https://dailyactor.com/actor-tips/the-offer-casting-director-john-papsidera), relaying Below the Line, November 2022).

Stallone's verdict on _I Play Rocky_ depends on who's reporting it. Farrelly said at TIFF in September 2026 that Stallone watched the film alone and loved it.

At a 92NY event reported on September 29, 2026, Stallone himself said: "Kind of mixed because I'm not dead yet." Shown a clip of Ippolito in character, though, he said, "I actually thought it was me the first moment, there were a couple of angles" ([AAP](https://www.newcastleherald.com.au/story/9359765/im-not-dead-yet-stallones-mixed-feelings-on-biopic/)).

TIFF critics split on the same question. [The Film Stage](https://thefilmstage.com/tiff-review-i-play-rocky-is-a-crowdpleasing-winner/) wrote that he "manages to avoid imitation and instead bring to the character something soulful." TheWrap's Chase Hutchinson wrote that "Ippolito credibly impersonates the voice of Stallone, but the delivery lacks the iconic actor's soul" ([Rotten Tomatoes](https://editorial.rottentomatoes.com/article/i-play-rocky-first-reviews/)).

How he answers that criticism is one of the clearest tests still ahead.

## Six, Three, Nine or One: weighing the case

### Leading read: Type 6

The Six case rests on a sequence that repeats across two roles and four years. He names the fear ("terrifying," "intimidation," "daunting," "I don't know how to do this"). He answers it with study and deference to the source. Then he takes the bold step anyway, which is how a Six who moves toward what scares him tends to look.

What he notices in Stallone points the same way. "He did feel very much like a lone wolf, kind of like an orphan in life in a lot of ways," he said in Rome. Stallone had "not too many people in his corner at the time, other than a few," he told Fandango.

That is his reading of Stallone, not a self-portrait, but it shows where his eye goes: to support, and to its absence.

Against it: the fear clusters where nearly anyone would feel it. Little else in the public record looks like a Six. There is no worst-case planning on display, no testing of loyalty, no wariness of authority.

### Closest rival: Type 3

The Three case is the shape of his biggest moves. He put himself in front of the _I Play Rocky_ filmmakers before anyone asked, and in 2020 he taped an audition with a broken hand kept out of frame. He framed the Stallone tape around being seen.

At Baruch, he told [HollywoodLife](https://web.archive.org/web/2021id_/https://hollywoodlife.com/2020/10/26/grand-army-joey-sexual-assault-george-anthony-ippolito-interview/) in 2020, "I was trying to do everything": college baseball, coursework, acting classes, a coach and auditions. He started acting, he told [Vulkan](https://vulkanmagazine.com/grand-armys-anthony-ippolito/), "originally just wanting to get commercials or something to pay for college." [The Hollywood Reporter](https://www.hollywoodreporter.com/movies/movie-news/rocky-anthony-ippolito-to-play-sylvester-stallone-1236354857/)'s casting story read him the same way: "Ippolito is said to have pursued the part of Stallone with the same relentless drive."

Against it, he doesn't sell himself where it would be easy. He didn't tell his director about his best-known credit until asked. At the premiere he cast his break as other people's bet, thanking those "willing to take a chance on me."

Asked in 2020 what his life would be without acting, he said he'd "probably be away at college majoring in psych, philosophy or cultural anthropology. I think everything else about my life would pretty much be the same." His recurring words are weight and fear, not winning.

### Also live: Type 9

The Nine case is his receptivity. He describes preparation as taking people in, "really trying to soak it all in," he told Fandango of Stallone's old interviews. His biggest lesson from _Grand Army_ was "the true value in being an ensemble member." What strains it is the self-started, two-year pursuit of a lead nobody had offered him.

### Also live: Type 1

The One case is his moral vocabulary around _Grand Army_, a show about sexual assault and consent. He praised its creator, Katie Cappiello, as "committed to authenticity and truth" in "an industry where so much of the contrary exists."

He told HollywoodLife, "As an actor, it's your duty to tell the story as honestly and as truthfully as possible." His advice to his high-school self: "Stop being so hard on yourself, man."

Against it, serious answers suited that material, and a One's irritation at what is wrong barely surfaces in his record. Fear does.

None of the four explains everything. Asked by Haute Living what he does when he isn't acting, he said, "Either playing music or painting." Swiss reporters at the Zurich Film Festival described him as "likable, cheerful" ([blue News](https://www.bluewin.ch/en/people/stallone-s-co-star-i-ve-been-beaten-up-a-bit-li.3620629)), which squares oddly with all the talk of intimidation.

## What would settle it

So far, fear and ambition point the same way: toward the bold move, made after heavy preparation. Separating them takes a moment when they pull apart. This case is due for review on December 15, 2026, after the wide release.

1. **His own retelling of the tape.** In the first long-form interviews after the November release, does he explain it as getting seen and taking his shot (Three), preparing until he felt ready and not letting down the people who trusted him (Six), loving the story and wanting in (Nine), or owing Stallone an honest portrayal (One)?
2. **The imitation reviews.** When critics say he copied the voice without the soul, does he point to results and momentum (Three), second-guess himself and work it through with people he trusts (Six), shrug it off (Nine), or defend the craft choice on principle (One)?
3. **The next role.** Does he take the biggest lead on offer (Three), return to trusted collaborators such as Farrelly for security's sake (Six), pick a small character piece that, in his 2020 words to Vulkan, "speaks to me" (Nine), or choose a project because it tells a truth (One)?
4. **People who knew him before.** The only people on record who have worked with him or met him are Farrelly, Papsidera, Robb and Stallone, all in promotional or casting settings. _Grand Army_'s cast and creator, or Lynette Sheldon, the acting coach he called "a huge mentor," could say whether off camera he over-prepares (Six), drives (Three), goes along (Nine) or holds exacting standards (One).

There's one more clue, and it may be nothing more than a teenager's taste. His high-school senior quote, which he credits to Marlon Brando, was "Only the one who walks his own way can't be overtaken."

A motto about refusing the race sits awkwardly with a Three, and a motto about going it alone sits awkwardly with a Six. Whether it was a passing pick or a compass is one more thing the next few years should show.
