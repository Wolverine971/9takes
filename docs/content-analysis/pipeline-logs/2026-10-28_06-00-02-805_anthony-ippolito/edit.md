Before anyone had cast a movie about the making of _Rocky_, Anthony Ippolito hung a heavy bag in his grandmother's basement and started turning himself into Sylvester Stallone.

"I didn't have a script, so I was just working with my imagination, trying to capture the physicality of the character," he told [TheWrap](https://www.thewrap.com/creative-content/movies/anthony-ippolito-i-play-rocky-how-he-was-cast-interview/) at the Toronto International Film Festival in September 2026. He had read a news article saying Peter Farrelly, the director of _Green Book_, was making the film.

Farrelly tells the ending. The studio loved the script, he recalled at [The Hollywood Reporter's TIFF studio](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=rWiOZ9ZNwTs), but wanted to know how he would ever find a Stallone when casting hadn't even started. "I said, he found us."

That tape won him the lead in _I Play Rocky_, the story of how a then-unknown Stallone wrote _Rocky_ and refused to sell it unless he could play the lead ([Screen Daily](https://www.screendaily.com/news/i-play-rocky-star-anthony-ippolito-on-the-weight-of-responsibility-playing-a-young-sylvester-stallone/5221614.article)). His casting was reported on August 27, 2025 ([The Hollywood Reporter](https://www.hollywoodreporter.com/movies/movie-news/rocky-anthony-ippolito-to-play-sylvester-stallone-1236354857/)). The film premiered at TIFF on September 12, 2026, and was scheduled to open in limited U.S. release on November 6, 2026, expanding nationwide on November 20 ([Variety](https://variety.com/2026/film/awards/i-play-rocky-tiff-premiere-peter-farrelly-stallone-oscars-1236859905/)). Variety's Clayton Davis called his performance "a star-making turn."

Ippolito is a Long Island native in his mid-to-late twenties as of 2026. He made his TV debut as a baseball-throwing toddler, the [New York Post](https://nypost.com/2020/10/14/meet-the-cast-of-netflixs-gritty-new-nyc-high-school-drama-grand-army/) reported, and childhood news spots for baseball got him an agent, he told [Pop-Culturalist](https://pop-culturalist.com/?p=27426). He played the younger version of Adam Sandler's character in _Pixels_ (2015). Before _I Play Rocky_ he had an ensemble role alongside [Odessa A'Zion](/personality-analysis/odessa-azion) on Netflix's _Grand Army_ (2020) and played a young Al Pacino in Paramount+'s _The Offer_ (2022), which starred [Miles Teller](/personality-analysis/miles-teller). Some outlets call _Grand Army_ his breakout. _I Play Rocky_ is his first lead.

Ask him about playing either icon and fear comes up fast. Playing Pacino was "pretty terrifying, it makes you want to throw up," he told [Variety](https://variety.com/2022/scene/news/offer-premiere-godfather-50-years-later-1235238228/) in 2022. Playing Stallone brought "a lot of intimidation at the beginning," he said at the Rome Film Fest in October 2026 ([Screen Daily](https://www.screendaily.com/news/i-play-rocky-star-anthony-ippolito-on-the-weight-of-responsibility-playing-a-young-sylvester-stallone/5221614.article)).

Yet the move that made his career is one nobody invited, and he remembers making it as fun.

So which is he: a man who quiets fear by preparing until he feels ready, or a man making sure the right people see him? As of October 2026, almost everything he has said in public comes from red carpets, festival junkets and short written Q&As. That record can't yet tell those motives apart, which makes this an open case.

## What is Anthony Ippolito's personality type?

Leading read: Enneagram Type 6, and it is a low-confidence call. As of October 2026, Type 3 is nearly level, and Types 9 and 1 are still in play. In both icon roles he named the difficulty and answered it with research. The Stallone self-tape fits Three just as well, and the record is too young to choose.

[Type 6](/enneagram-corner/enneagram-type-6) sits in the head triad, where the core emotion is fear; a Six builds solid ground before stepping out. [Type 3](/enneagram-corner/enneagram-type-3) sits in the heart triad, built around shame; a Three achieves where the right people can see. [Type 9](/enneagram-corner/enneagram-type-9) and [Type 1](/enneagram-corner/enneagram-type-1) sit in the body triad, built around anger: a Nine smooths it into calm, a One turns it into getting things right.

The sections below lay out the evidence for each. All of it shows what he says and does in public, not what he feels.

## The tape nobody asked for

His own account to TheWrap has two stages. The first tape was improvised: "It kind of felt like a bit of a long shot, but it was a creative exercise, and it was actually really fun." The producers then sent the script and asked for a proper audition, and he shot several more scenes on his own, TheWrap reports.

Farrelly remembers it differently. At THR's studio he said Ippolito "had somehow found the script" and taped three scenes. In a September 2026 video for [IMDb](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=TP7M9Ce7eUM), he placed the taping "in his own apartment." Both men agree the tape arrived before casting began. "He was the first person we looked at, the only person," Farrelly said in Rome.

Why tape himself at all? His plainest answer is enthusiasm for a story that had inspired him. "I had known a little bit of Stallone's story, so I was like, 'This is going to be a great movie,' and I really wanted to be a part of it," he told TheWrap. "It didn't seem very plausible to some people at the time, but I was really, really enthusiastic about it."

He did the work first. As with Pacino, he told [Collider](https://www.youtube.com/shorts/Hhf0cENNyXE) at TIFF, "so much of the homework had to be done prior to even submitting the tape." Then he named what he was after:

> "I just really felt that at least if I was seen and I could present my interpretation, then I'd be able to live with it even if it didn't go my way. I just really had to try to get in the door."

In casting, "being seen" is shop talk for getting in front of the people who decide, so the word is weak evidence on its own. Read one way, the key word is "seen," the move of someone who needs the right people to notice. Read another, the key phrase is "live with it": he was guarding against regret. One junket answer can't separate those. The clause "even if it didn't go my way" tilts only slightly toward regret: he could accept losing, as long as he had tried.

At THR's studio, the interviewer asked whether he ever hesitated to take a role that means so much to people. "I was fighting for it," he said. Farrelly added: "By the way, it wasn't much of a fight. We never considered anyone else after him."

It wasn't his first self-tape under pressure. In 2019, playing baseball for Baruch College, he broke his hand in a game, and his _Grand Army_ audition fell the day after a procedure on it. "I emailed my agent like, 'Listen, I'm gonna have to take a pass on this,'" he told the [New York Post](https://nypost.com/2020/10/14/meet-the-cast-of-netflixs-gritty-new-nyc-high-school-drama-grand-army/) in 2020.

The agent suggested a tape. "But I kept my hand out of the frame 'cause I didn't want them to see I had a broken hand."

The broken hand explains why he first offered to pass. Still, the contrast is real: in 2019 the tape was his agent's idea, and for _I Play Rocky_ it was his own.

## "It makes you want to throw up"

The fear language starts four years before Stallone. At _The Offer_'s premiere in April 2022, the line about wanting to throw up came with two more. Playing Pacino was also "a freaking honor," he told Variety, and it "made me feel a huge weight of responsibility, but I had so much fun."

To [The Hollywood Reporter](https://www.hollywoodreporter.com/tv/tv-news/the-offer-the-godfather-premiere-1235133610/) that night, he said "trying to fill those shoes is kind of impossible," so "I looked into as much old footage as I could find with him from that time period." Then: "I'm kind of terrified of [him] seeing it because he's someone I've looked up to, and I respect his work." THR's headline gave the whole cast an "Absolutely Terrifying" task, so the register wasn't his alone.

In Rome four years later, the vocabulary came back almost word for word, with the same answer attached:

> "I was a fan of the movies and a fan of the character, and I understand how many people are, so I did feel the weight of the responsibility. But like any other situation, you just start by researching as much as you can."

Asked by IMDb where he started with Stallone's voice and posture, he said, "You start by saying, I don't know how to do this, and it's daunting, but you have to kind of be humbled by the task and figure it out from there piece by piece."

With both icons he studied the actor before the legend: Pacino before _The Godfather_, and Stallone's bit parts from 1972 to 1976.

Farrelly saw the same habits from the director's chair. In the IMDb video he said: "Very Stallone-like in his approach to this. He just made it happen. He was so prepared, you know, and humble." And Farrelly learned only in pre-production, by asking, that Ippolito had played Pacino, which Farrelly called "the best part of The Offer." By then Ippolito already had the job.

One detail complicates this. In 2020, asked whether his first series-regular role brought pressure, he told Pop-Culturalist: "A little bit, but I felt so strongly about the material that it was more of an excited energy than a nervous one."

The fear clusters around the two icon roles, playing famous men who might watch. Almost any actor would feel that, and press tours reward a newcomer's humility.

## Training for a part he already had

The body came after the tape. "After we got it, the one thing he wasn't was he wasn't buff like Stallone," Farrelly said on Collider in September 2026. "So we had him working out for a year and learning how to box." At the TIFF premiere he put the whole effort at "a year and a half to two years" ([USA Today](https://www.youtube.com/shorts/u0yVV1eVpgM)).

[Variety](https://au.variety.com/2026/film/news/i-play-rocky-anthony-ippolito-sylvester-stallone-dog-41877/) reports that he bulked up from about 150 pounds to roughly 180 at Gleason's Gym in Brooklyn. Farrelly compared it to [Robert De Niro](/personality-analysis/robert-de-niro) in _Raging Bull_.

He talks about it as endurance. It was "a consistency thing" and "an entire lifestyle shift," he told Variety, and it "tested me in more ways than one," he told IMDb.

Because the production set the goal, the transformation says more about his stamina than his motive. His own words are about consistency and being tested, not about how he looked, and the studio needed the physique either way.

## Imitation or essence

Casting director John Papsidera, who cast him as Pacino after the first actor chosen withdrew, said: "Again, not an exact duplicate, but his eyes did a lot of the heavy lifting and more people commented about him than almost any other role" ([Daily Actor](https://dailyactor.com/actor-tips/the-offer-casting-director-john-papsidera), relaying Below the Line, November 2022).

Stallone's verdict on _I Play Rocky_ depends on who's reporting it. Farrelly said at TIFF in September 2026 that Stallone watched the film alone and loved it.

At a 92NY event reported on September 29, 2026, Stallone himself said: "Kind of mixed because I'm not dead yet." Shown a clip of Ippolito in character, though, he said, "I actually thought it was me the first moment, there were a couple of angles" ([AAP](https://www.newcastleherald.com.au/story/9359765/im-not-dead-yet-stallones-mixed-feelings-on-biopic/)).

Most of the first TIFF reviews gathered by [Rotten Tomatoes](https://editorial.rottentomatoes.com/article/i-play-rocky-first-reviews/) praised the performance, and a minority called it imitation. [The Film Stage](https://thefilmstage.com/tiff-review-i-play-rocky-is-a-crowdpleasing-winner/) wrote that he "manages to avoid imitation and instead bring to the character something soulful." TheWrap's Chase Hutchinson wrote that "Ippolito credibly impersonates the voice of Stallone, but the delivery lacks the iconic actor's soul."

## Six, Three, Nine or One: weighing the case

### Leading read: Type 6

The Six case is what he does with a daunting job, in 2022 and again in 2026: he names the difficulty, then studies until he has footing. Order matters. Before the tape came homework and enthusiasm; the fear words ("intimidation," "daunting") came once the role was his. Nothing on record shows fear before the tape, and asked at THR whether he ever hesitated, he said he was "fighting for it," so it can't be read as a Six forcing himself toward what scares him.

The fear itself doesn't help. In 2022 he dreaded Pacino "seeing it"; in 2026 he felt the weight of the fans. Both are fears of falling short in front of people whose regard matters, which belongs to Three as much as Six.

So why list Six first? On a narrow tiebreaker. He meets a hard task by starting from not knowing ("I don't know how to do this") and building "piece by piece," and he did his homework before the tape, as for Pacino. Starting from not knowing sits more naturally with a Six than with a Three guarding an image of competence. It is a thin edge, also said on a press tour.

Against it: the fear clusters where nearly anyone would feel it. Little else in the public record looks like a Six. There is no worst-case planning on display, no testing of loyalty, no wariness of authority. The fear words rarely travel alone, either. When his casting was announced in August 2025, he called the role "an absolute dream come true" (Entertainment Weekly, via the [South China Morning Post](https://www.scmp.com/magazines/style/people/celebrities/article/3367710/meet-anthony-ippolito-who-stars-sylvester-stallone-upcoming-biopic-i-play-rocky)).

### Closest rival: Type 3

His biggest moves fit a Three: the Stallone tape, and in 2019 an audition taped with a broken hand kept out of frame. Farrelly's "He just made it happen" points the same way, as did THR's casting story, which tied him to Stallone's bet on himself: "In a fitting echo of that spirit, Ippolito is said to have pursued the part of Stallone with the same relentless drive." An unknown betting on himself to star in a film about an unknown betting on himself is Three's best story. It is also the studio's marketing line, so it can't prove motive on its own.

At Baruch, he told [HollywoodLife](https://web.archive.org/web/2021id_/https://hollywoodlife.com/2020/10/26/grand-army-joey-sexual-assault-george-anthony-ippolito-interview/) in 2020, "I was trying to do everything": college baseball, coursework, acting classes, a coach and auditions. He started acting, he told [Vulkan](https://vulkanmagazine.com/grand-armys-anthony-ippolito/), "originally just wanting to get commercials or something to pay for college."

The evidence against Three deserves the same press-tour discount as the fear. At the premiere he cast his break as other people's bet, thanking those "willing to take a chance on me," which premieres call for. Farrelly's story that Ippolito never mentioned playing Pacino came from pre-production, after he was hired, when there was nothing left to sell. What survives is small. Asked in 2020 what his life would be without acting, he said he'd "probably be away at college majoring in psych, philosophy or cultural anthropology. I think everything else about my life would pretty much be the same." His fear words usually come paired with honor, fun and gratitude, and talk of winning is hard to find.

### Also live: Type 9

The Nine case is his receptivity. He describes preparation as taking people in, "really trying to soak it all in," he told Fandango of Stallone's old interviews. His biggest lesson from _Grand Army_ was "the true value in being an ensemble member." His plainest motive for the tape, joining a story he loved, fits Nine as well as anything. What strains it is how hard he went after a lead.

### Also live: Type 1

The One case is his moral vocabulary around _Grand Army_, where his character, George Wright, is one of two friends who sexually assault Joey, A'Zion's character, in a fictional storyline about consent. He praised its creator, Katie Cappiello, as "committed to authenticity and truth" in "an industry where so much of the contrary exists." Asked by HollywoodLife how the cast made that scene safe to film, he said, "As an actor, it's your duty to tell the story as honestly and as truthfully as possible." His advice to his high-school self: "Stop being so hard on yourself, man."

Against it, serious answers suited that material, and a One's irritation at what is wrong barely surfaces in his record. Fear does.

None of the four explains everything. Asked by [Haute Living](https://hauteliving.com/2022/05/anthony-ippolito-plays-al-pacino-in-paramounts-the-offer/712929/) in 2022 what he does when he isn't acting, he said, "Either playing music or painting." Swiss reporters at the 2026 Zurich Film Festival described him as "likable, cheerful" ([blue News](https://www.bluewin.ch/en/people/stallone-s-co-star-i-ve-been-beaten-up-a-bit-li.3620629)), which squares oddly with all the talk of intimidation.

### Set aside for now: Type 4

Music, painting, a 2020 wish to work on "something that speaks to me" (Vulkan) and his reading of Stallone are where a Four would show. "He did feel very much like a lone wolf, kind of like an orphan in life in a lot of ways," he said in Rome, then went straight on: "He had so much going against him and he persevered through so much. His endurance, I think, radiates through Rocky." That is his reading of Stallone, not a self-portrait, and it echoes the film's own underdog pitch. Nothing in his record speaks of feeling different or misunderstood, and he valued the _Grand Army_ ensemble, so Four stays off the live list.

## What would settle it

So far, fear and ambition point the same way: toward the bold move, made after heavy preparation. Separating them takes a moment when they pull apart. This case is due for review on December 15, 2026, after the wide release.

1. **His own retelling of the tape.** His answer so far, enthusiasm for a story that inspired him, fits every reading. In long-form interviews after the November 2026 release, does he say it was about getting noticed (Three), preparing until he felt ready and not letting down people who trusted him (Six), joining a story he loved, win or lose (Nine), or owing Stallone an honest portrayal (One)?
2. **The imitation reviews.** Gracious answers are standard on an awards campaign, so only his stated reasons count. Asked at length, does he credit results and momentum (Three), working it through with people he trusts (Six), not taking it in (Nine), or a principled defense of the craft choice (One)?
3. **The next role.** Offers and agents drive the choice itself, so the test is the reason he gives: the size of the opportunity (Three), trust in the people making it, such as Farrelly (Six), the chance to disappear into an ensemble (Nine), or what the project gets right (One).
4. **People who knew him before.** As of October 2026, nobody who has known him beyond a single production has spoken about him on record; Farrelly, Papsidera, co-star AnnaSophia Robb and Stallone all spoke in promotional or casting settings. _Grand Army_'s cast and creator, or Lynette Sheldon, the acting coach he called "a huge mentor," could say whether off camera he over-prepares (Six), drives (Three), goes along (Nine) or holds exacting standards (One).

There's one more clue, and it may be nothing more than a teenager's taste. His high-school senior quote, which he credits to Marlon Brando, was "Only the one who walks his own way can't be overtaken."

Both leading reads can claim half of it. "Can't be overtaken" keeps the race and promises not to lose it, which suits a Three; "walks his own way" refuses anyone else's course, which doesn't. A Six who builds his own footing could own "his own way"; one who leans on guidance couldn't. Whether it was a passing pick or a compass is one more thing the next few years should show.
