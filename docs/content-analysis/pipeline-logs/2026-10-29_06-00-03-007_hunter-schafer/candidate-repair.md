In May 2016, a public radio reporter in North Carolina sat down with a 17-year-old named Hunter Schafer and her parents. Weeks earlier, Schafer had become the youngest plaintiff in the ACLU's lawsuit against House Bill 2, the state law that made it illegal for her to use the women's bathroom in a public building. Asked whether it felt validating when strangers assumed she was a cisgender girl, she said it used to. Now that she was "exploring the nonbinary part of myself," it was different.

"I just wish that some people could see more to me than that right away," she told [WUNC](https://www.wunc.org/education/2016-05-11/q-a-a-nc-teen-and-her-parents-on-the-transition-from-male-to-female). She liked people to know she was not a cis girl. "I'm proud to be a trans person."

Eight years later, she told GQ she had been offered "tons of trans roles" and did not want them, and that keeping transness out of her billing had taken work. "If I let it happen, it would still be giving 'Transsexual Actress' before every article ever."

> "I worked so hard to get to where I am, past these really hard points in my transition, and now I just want to be a girl and finally move on."

([GQ, April 2, 2024](https://www.gq.com/story/hunter-schafer-creativity-awards-profile))

Read fast, that is a reversal. Read slowly, it is the same complaint. At 17 the label that flattened her was "cis girl." At 25 it was "trans actress." The content changed. The objection to being reduced to a category before anyone looked did not.

That is one way to hear it; the simpler way is that her self-concept changed, which it did. But a person who only wanted the right label would have been satisfied twice, and she was not. Schafer is an actress, model and visual artist, best known for playing Jules, a transgender teenager, on HBO's Euphoria, which premiered in June 2019. That objection runs through ten years of interviews, and it is where the case for her personality type starts.

## What is Hunter Schafer's personality type?

Hunter Schafer's personality type is Enneagram Type 4, the Individualist. Fours are driven by a need for an identity that is truly their own and to be seen for it. Schafer has spent her life making an inner self visible through art, and she resists any label that would flatten that self, even when carrying it would pay.

[Type 4](/enneagram-corner/enneagram-type-4) sits in the heart triad, the three types organized around shame, which in a Four is less embarrassment than a running comparison between the person inside and the person the world reflects back. A Four builds an inner world where the real self can live, finds a medium to carry it outside, and then checks whether the world saw the right thing; the usual cost is a chronic sense of being misread.

Her own summary, to Allure in 2020:

> "That's been the project of my life. Just trying to feel seen and learn how to see others correctly."

([Allure, August 11, 2020](https://www.allure.com/story/hunter-schafer-september-cover-interview))

The read is held at medium confidence. Nearly everything here is her own account, given in a genre that rewards artists for sounding authentic, and a trans adolescence in North Carolina would make identity the central question for anyone. The closest rival is [Type 3](/enneagram-corner/enneagram-type-3), the Achiever, and it gets a full hearing below.

## A world built because the real one would not hold her

Her mother dates the pull to "maybe from 18 months on": Catwoman and Hawkgirl over the male superheroes, and at three a pink dress to preschool every day. (WUNC, 2016)

Her parents, a Presbyterian pastor and a youth minister, kept her away from the television and parked her at a table with paper and crayons. "Something parallel to an addiction was formed with drawing," she told Lorde on [the A24 Podcast](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=7wKrY6E0ymI) in 2022.

> "I'm pretty sure I needed it as a way to externalize what I didn't feel I could externalize with myself," she told GQ. "I was a trans kid who didn't transition until I was in high school. I had this whole world and person inside of me that couldn't come out in the way it was supposed to. I think I really needed it as a tool."

Middle school in Raleigh was the hard stretch. On [Call Her Daddy](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=ujo2BHqx0PY) in 2024 she named one of her "afflictions with life" in a sentence that works as a key to everything after.

> "I have never been able to really help but be who I am."

So she "always stuck out like a sore thumb," she said. She came out as gay in seventh grade and as transgender in ninth, the 2013-14 school year. Her first two attempts did not get through; the third landed, Katy Schafer told WUNC, because "we had kind of lost our kid." Hunter finished that freshman year in a skirt. ([Rolling Stone, August 9, 2024](https://www.rollingstone.com/tv-movies/tv-movie-features/hunter-schafer-cuckoo-euphoria-interview-1235076587/)) Puberty had brought a depression that, she told AnOther in 2023, "never really goes away." In the same breath: "You just figure out how to live with it, live a life with it. And I'm on meds now, which are great." ([Attitude](https://attitude.co.uk/culture/film-tv/hunter-schafer-says-depression-was-a-catalyst-for-her-transition-446595))

Her theory of those years, to Harper's Bazaar in 2021:

> "When your exterior world and your body and your self are not in line with who you are, you turn inward. And my theory is that I built a really rich inner world until I started feeling like myself in my body."

([Harper's Bazaar, December 2021](https://www.harpersbazaar.com/culture/features/a38248731/hunter-schafer-interview-december-2021-january-2022/))

By senior year, at an arts high school in Winston-Salem, the art was answering HB2: red underwear with giant hands over the crotch reading "Peel away every perception." The earliest statement of this profile's theme dates from April 2017, before fame could reward it. "The piece is dealing with how people perceive me versus how I feel," she told [HuffPost](https://www.huffpost.com/entry/hunter-schafer_n_58f4c937e4b0b9e9848d0f2f). "They're almost like journal entries." Seven years later she said she had felt "Oh, I'm trans. I should be making art about this," when "that's not really what I wanted to be making art about."

A page of her high-school journal, shown to GQ, reads "Fuck skool, fuck skool, fuck skool. I just want to go to New York and never leave!!! I want to start living." A modeling check got her there at 18. "I couldn't believe my fucking life," she said of the first job; GQ's word for it is validation.

## Euphoria, and what she let in

The casting call circulated on Instagram among trans models in New York in 2018, and her agency asked her to go in. The show was Euphoria, created and written by Sam Levinson for HBO; the part was Jules, a trans teenager. Her first reaction, she told [Dazed](https://www.dazeddigital.com/film-tv/article/43123/1/hunter-schafer-euphoria-hbo-series-rowan-blanchard), was suspicion of "a white, cis, straight man writing what seems like a really intense show" about identities not his own. She went in anyway, through at least five callbacks, and gave up her place at Central Saint Martins to take the part.

Two choices from her first year say more than the performance did.

The first was a refusal. The casting director put her in a Method acting class in New York, and she dropped out after two weeks "because it is so hardcore," she told Lorde. Her broader objection, to Harper's Bazaar in 2021, was about identity.

> "I've already spent a lot of my life trying to be someone I'm not," she explained. "And even if it is my job title, I'm not interested in doing that again for long periods of time."

The second was the opposite of a refusal. Jules was written close to her: for the character's therapy scenes, she told [Awards Focus](https://awardsfocus.com/interviews/hunter-schafer-interview-euphoria-jules/), she chose "to draw upon my own experiences as far as my own personal ways of thinking about trans-ness." She let Jules in. "In season one of Euphoria, I was basically Jules. Jules was me, I was her," she said in an [ELLE video](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=nsFdoeVcffo) in October 2026.

On this reading, the line she guards is not between herself and a role but between the self she built and anything that could overwrite it. Jules was made from the same material, so Jules was allowed in.

Spring 2020 tested the structure. She was 21 and alone in Los Angeles when season two was halted. "I had probably what's close to a mental breakdown and then bought a truck and drove across the country" was how she first described it to GQ. She considered checking into a mental-health facility, Harper's Bazaar reported in 2021. Instead she drove five days east to her sister in Boone, North Carolina, which GQ reports she sees as the first adult choice she made for her own good. "I lived where I had to live for modeling and went where people told me to go," she said. The drive left her with a rule: "I have to be in control and decide how to survive this."

In Boone, she and Levinson wrote the Jules special, "Fuck Anyone Who's Not a Sea Blob," with a first draft in about a week. It remains the only writing credit Levinson has shared on the show; every other episode through the May 31, 2026 finale is [credited to him alone](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Euphoria_season_3).

> "It was the most cathartic, artistic experience I've ever had."

"It might have catalyzed something reminiscent of an addiction," she told Awards Focus in 2021, "because I am going to be fiending for that sort of catharsis for the rest of my life."

Release and craving, not achievement. That is the vocabulary of someone who makes things to metabolize feeling, and it is the first place the Type 3 alternative starts to lose ground.

## The drag version of who I am

Fame came fast: a million Instagram followers within a month of the Euphoria premiere, she told [ELLE UK](https://www.elle.com/uk/life-and-culture/culture/a43094213/hunter-schafer-interview-euphoria-career/) in 2023. What she built in response is the most useful fact about her.

> "I always feel like the image that I put out into the world, like on Instagram, whatever, is like very much like the drag version of who I am. And it's like an image that I've put together to like give to the world."

Later she split herself in two: the public Hunter Schafer and "Hunti," her friends' name for her. "Those are two different people," she said. The famous one is "my drag," and "having those be separate is so important for my fucking sanity."

Fame itself, in her telling, is a lever. "I don't personally gain anything from it," she said; it has made public life harder "as a socially anxious person," but "it's an amazing tool to, like, get what I want and to make what I want happen." Life would be perfect, she added, "if I could walk away from all of this with the money."

Here the Type 3 reading gets its best material, and she supplies it. Asked by [ELLE](https://www.elle.com/culture/movies-tv/a74133846/hunter-schafer-blade-runner-2099-interview-2026/) in 2026 who is cool, she named Fran Lebowitz, Björk and Patti Smith.

> "People who are not thinking about perception at all. And I think about perception all the time. It's a big part of my job. It's not cool at all."

Notice the shape of that answer, though: she idealizes three people for a quality she feels she lacks, then marks herself down by comparison, which is a Four's reflex more than a Three's.

The label she has tried on and shed is activist. At 17 she accepted it, writing in [Teen Vogue](https://www.teenvogue.com/story/transgender-teenager-fight-against-bathroom-laws-hunter-schafer) that she had joined the suit "to represent other transgender youth in North Carolina"; by 2018 she told [the Times](https://www.nytimes.com/2018/05/30/style/hunter-schafer-transgender-activist-model.html), "I don't know if I would call myself an activist, as much as someone who's just vocal about being trans." In 2019 she explained the arc: "because people have been telling me I'm an activist, I took on that label. But in retrospect, I don't think that's what I am." By 2024, to Rolling Stone: "People love to call me an activist," she said. "It's like, 'No. I'm just a tranny who's famous,' you know?"

A Three manages an image to be admired for it. What she describes is managing one so the admiration lands on the decoy while she is somewhere else.

Her one public rupture with the community that made her a symbol came in August 2022, when she liked, and commented "!!!!!" on, an Instagram post that [Out](https://www.out.com/celebs/2022/8/24/heres-why-some-people-are-calling-hunter-schafer-transmedicalist) described as being "about the difference between binary trans people who medically transition, and nonbinary trans people who don't," and as blaming the latter for recent attacks from the right on trans healthcare. "Many people accused Schafer of being transmedicalist," Out reported. Two weeks later she wrote that "i hold absolutely no hatred towards non-binary folks," that she had agreed with "another t-girl's post" about an imbalance in visibility, and that "I am not a trans medicalist." ([Seventeen, September 6, 2022](https://www.seventeen.com/celebrity/movies-tv/a41092048/hunter-schafer-non-binary-rumors/)) She did not apologize and she did not double down. She narrowed the claim to what she meant.

Two years later she described where that left her. She had "kind of lost interest in achieving some sort of utopia," she told GQ.

> "I am totally cool with people hating me for being trans or calling me a man. I am not interested in trying to convince them anymore."

"And I'm going to be over here with people I love." GQ's writer read it as a little cynicism adopted "as a protective measure."

Withdrawal from causes it was not. Five weeks before that profile ran, on Monday, February 26, 2024, she was arrested with dozens of others at a Jewish Voice for Peace cease-fire protest inside 30 Rockefeller Plaza, where President Biden was taping Late Night with Seth Meyers, in a T-shirt reading "CEASE-FIRE NOW." ([NBC News, February 28, 2024](https://www.nbcnews.com/nbc-out/out-news/euphoria-star-hunter-schafer-arrested-nyc-protest-support-palestinians-rcna140939); [Vulture](https://www.vulture.com/article/hunter-schafer-arrested-pro-palestine-protest-biden.html)) She picked the cause; the label she refuses is the one other people hand her.

## What she measures the work against

Asked by a Cal State Fullerton student in April 2023 how she handles criticism, she located the judgment inside.

> "And I've known that I'm proud and I feel secure in the artistic truth of it before it ever comes out and is open to critique."

She conceded that harsh critiques from high-profile strangers can be a bit unsettling, as the [Orange County Register](https://www.ocregister.com/2023/05/08/actress-hunter-schafer-discusses-her-art-and-lgbtq-advocacy/) paraphrased it on May 8, 2023. "If you're not receiving criticism, you're probably not doing something right, and that is also what art is for, creating conversation," she added.

"Most of the time when I watch myself, I have to watch through my hands," she told [Variety](https://variety.com/2024/film/global/hunter-schafer-cuckoo-berlin-film-festival-euphoria-1235909251/) in February 2024. Cuckoo, the first film she shot and her first lead, was the exception: "I didn't have to do that with this movie."

Producers approached her in spring 2024 for the lead in Blade Runner 2099 and she turned it down; she did not want more television. They came back in June, after more than a month of trying to cast the part, and showed her what they wanted to build. "I was like, 'I am willing to devote half a year of my life to this because I really believe in it,'" she told Rolling Stone. The series was scheduled to premiere on Prime Video in November 2026.

And still the doubt. "I always kind of get into the spiral of, Am I meant to be doing this?" she told GQ in 2024. In 2020 it was "just riding the wave that life is handing me and trying to be okay and keep my shit together." Rolling Stone's 2024 profile ends on her answer to all of that. "At the end of the day, it's about what my heart feels is right," she said, and finished: "Because all the answers are right there."

## Little pockets of special, intense relationships

By 2021 she had not lived more than three or four years in one city since Raleigh, and she described the result to Bazaar as "these little pockets of special, intense relationships." By March 2026 she said of Los Angeles, "I technically live there, but I'm never there." No relationship of hers has lasted past two years, she told ELLE in 2026. In April 2024 she confirmed, with Rosalía's permission, that they had dated for about five months in late 2019; Rosalía is "family no matter what."

Her relationship with Dominic Fike, her Euphoria co-star, was public from early 2022 and over by mid-2023. In February 2023 she called it "a safety net for one another." That July, Fike said he was "done being in relationships right now," citing co-dependency. ([ELLE, September 19, 2024](https://www.elle.com/culture/celebrities/a61895883/hunter-schafer-dating-history/)) In August 2024, on Call Her Daddy, Schafer talked about her "last relationship" without naming him; the identification rests on her description and on the reporting that followed. She credited him first: "I've said all these amazing things with him. He was not perfect and it's no secret." Then: "It's part of why the relationship ended, is I got cheated on for the first time," adding that he had written about it in his songs. No response from Fike turned up in the sources checked for this page as of October 2026.

What she did with it is the telling part. Her first thought went to what it said about her as a trans woman: "Oh my God, like, is this it?" The fault landed on herself first. Then she relocated it. Cheating, she decided, "has nothing to do with you at all," and "it has everything to do with that person and whatever kind of pain they're in."

## Why not Type 3?

The Type 3 case starts in her own words.

She thinks about perception "all the time." At 18 she was already curating Instagram, "more aware of an aesthetic that I was interested in and wanted to uphold," and her first paid day on camera was, in GQ's word, validation. "I knew fame could get me the things that I wanted to do in life," she said on Call Her Daddy. She chose roles so that "everything feels a step away from the other and a little bit different." And she admits the pull of reception herself. "I have to keep checking in with myself and making sure I'm doing it for the right reasons and for the love of making stuff," she told Vogue in 2024; the year before, to ELLE UK, "you can kind of get swept up in it."

The Three her words fit is not the textbook one. It is the disidentified Three who keeps a work self and a home self, calls the persona a costume, and privately wonders whether the jig is up. Every sentence above is compatible with that Three, including her recurring "Am I meant to be doing this?"; impostor doubt is common in Threes. Where she says her validation lives settles little either, since that is self-report in a genre that rewards it.

What separates them is how she explains the making itself. In 2018, to Dazed:

> "No matter what I've worked on or what art practices I've delved into, they've always been an attempt to world-build. Whatever was not in my direct vicinity that I needed in order to feel fulfilled, I tried to create."

In 2021, the Jules special was catharsis she would be "fiending" for. In 2024, to GQ, making things was "a reason to live," and on Call Her Daddy, "what I was put on this earth to do." A Three's account of the work is organized around what will land. Hers is organized around what was missing and what it releases, and she described it that way before fame and after.

Two audience-facing revisions point the same way. The first is the hook. A Three keeps the label that pays; she objected to "cis girl" in 2016 and to "trans actress" in 2024, each the flattering one at the time.

The second is less clean. In 2026 she explained to ELLE why she had held on to her earlier self-description: she "thought there was something really cool and badass about it," an idea of herself kept for its meaning. She frames the change as queerness as philosophy "versus how it applies and works in real life, in the context of the world that we're living in," which is also how a Three revises; what tips it is the reason she gives for the earlier stance. Her parents' resistance in ninth grade and the 2022 clarification do not separate the types.

**Type 9** fits "riding the wave," "went where people told me to go," and the way she dissolved into Jules, and the record keeps feeding it: "down for the ride and not trying to push back against it" in 2026, and "I'm going to be over here with people I love," which reads as Nine withdrawal as easily as Four retreat. What separates them: Nines forget themselves to stay merged, and her record is of a self that would not stay hidden when hiding was safer, from three coming-out attempts and a femme boy who "stuck out like a sore thumb" in Raleigh to a Method class quit to avoid being "someone I'm not" and a craving for catharsis rather than the numbing a Nine reaches for.

**Type 6** has a sourced line the first draft missed: "I feel like I need to keep infiltrating to the point where I feel safe enough in this world to do whatever I want," she told Vogue in 2024, beside "a safety net" and "the training wheels." That sentence ends "to do whatever I want": self-expression, not belonging or guidance. And at the decisive moments she has chosen exposure over cover, alone, and announced the result afterward: a lawsuit at 17 while afraid of public speaking, an eight-minute video about a passport marked M, a surgery disclosure weighed in a hotel tea room and then made. A Six under threat gathers allies and checks the plan. She checks with herself.

## What the Four reading leaves out

She is light. The New York Times called her "the sunny Schafer" in 2019; Variety found her "light and airy"; GQ called her "silly and curious." Her stylist, Dara Allen, says "She's down to clown." Dan Stevens talks about "a real kind of impish sense of humor." She agrees. She has always had, she says, "pretty easy access to like joy," and she calls herself "a pretty happy kid" despite the pain. A Four who does not perform melancholy is still a Four, but the affect does not match the depression and the craving for catharsis she reports elsewhere.

She holds two opposite postures without resolving them. "I can't just wing this shit. I really have to be intentional about the way I'm moving in the world," she told GQ in 2024. By 2026 she was "down for the ride and not trying to push back against it, or self-sabotage." Both halves are hers.

She is unusually unbothered by misperception for a type supposed to feel it acutely; she says she is "totally cool" with being called a man. Whose misperception matters, though. What she shrugs off is hostile strangers' misgendering, which she assigns to them, the way she assigned the cheating to the person who did it. What she objects to, in 2016 and in 2024, is being reduced by people who are actually looking. GQ's writer declined to call it hardening: "It's not so much that Schafer has hardened herself, but more that she's learned to embrace a little cynicism as a protective measure."

Nearly all of this is self-report, the collaborator testimony is promotional, and nothing on the record comes from Levinson, Rosalía or her siblings.

## The flatter answer

The longest-running evidence for the type is not her gender. It is the way she revises a publicly stated description of herself when it stops matching what she feels, and takes the judgment that follows. Her gender is not evidence of any personality type, and neither is a medical decision; as ELLE put it, "every trans person is different."

In 2016 she said she was "exploring the nonbinary part of myself" and "swinging back to somewhere in between." In October 2026 she told ELLE that where she had landed was "flatter": "I'm a woman. I'm a girl."

Then, in a hotel tea room, after weighing whether to say it, she told the writer: "I just had bottom surgery in December, which was amazing, and I love it so much." The surgery is not evidence of anything about her type. Her reasoning is.

> "For a long time I've embraced the gender-expansive part of myself, and I really wanted to try to make that previous body work, because I thought there was something really cool and badass about it."

ELLE describes her hitting a wall "where this theoretical queer utopia didn't match up with the world she encountered every day," and notes that she may be judged for the decision by queer and cis people alike.

That is the adult version of a pattern that began with a ninth-grader finishing the year in a skirt. When a replacement passport came back marked M in February 2025, she posted an eight-minute video: "I don't give a fuck that they put an M on my passport. It doesn't change really anything about me or my transness." ([Rolling Stone, February 21, 2025](https://www.rollingstone.com/tv-movies/tv-movie-news/hunter-schafer-gender-marker-changed-passport-trump-order-1235275355/))

ELLE's interview ends with a childhood memory of a friend's birthday party, where she was asked to join a choreographed dance and was, in the writer's words, "so cripplingly aware of how she was being viewed, it wasn't fun." "I was so shy and so scared to dance in front of other kids, I was crying. I was so nervous and scared," she said. "I still did it, trying to smile, but I kept being like, 'No.' I think that's what I was at my core before this job broke me open."

A shy kid, miserable at being looked at, who did the dance anyway. She does not say why. Years later, on a podcast, in the middle of a joke about her afflictions, she described the same core in one sentence.

"I'm so thankful that I have not really been given a choice to be anyone but who I am."
