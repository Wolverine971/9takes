In 2014, on the press tour for _Guardians of the Galaxy_, Chris Pratt looked back on the stretch when he slept in a van or a tent on a beach in Maui. "It's a pretty awesome place to be homeless," he [told The Independent](https://nzherald.co.nz/entertainment/chris-pratt-gets-in-shape-and-takes-over-the-galaxy/OCBW2TTH64HG4ZNUJLXWWGSHMY). Then he described the routine:

> "We just drank and smoked weed and worked minimal hours, 15 to 20 hours per week, just enough to cover gas, food and fishing supplies. You know, it was a charming time."

He had arrived on the island broke. A door-to-door sales venture had collapsed and left him in debt, and he had let his hometown believe it was going great. Of the day his friends picked him up in Maui, he told [Vanity Fair](https://www.vanityfair.com/hollywood/2017/01/chris-pratt-cover-story) in 2017: "They had a cooler of beer. But I was not in a great place."

Both accounts are his. Side by side, they show a move that runs through his life: he meets a bad stretch by finding the fun in it, first while living it and again when he tells the story.

Christopher Michael Pratt was born on June 21, 1979, in Virginia, Minnesota, and grew up mostly in Lake Stevens, Washington. He landed his first regular TV role on _Everwood_ in 2002, broke through as Andy Dwyer on _Parks and Recreation_ (2009 to 2015), and became a leading man as Star-Lord in _Guardians of the Galaxy_ (2014) and Owen Grady in _Jurassic World_ (2015).

## What is Chris Pratt's personality type?

Chris Pratt's personality type is Enneagram Type 7, known as the Enthusiast. Enthusiasts are in the head triad, and their core emotion is fear. In Pratt it shows as a habit of turning hardship into a good time. After a failed sales job, he lived in a van in Maui and later called it "a charming time."

Head types like [Type 7](/enneagram-corner/enneagram-type-7) meet life by anticipating what comes next, as opposed to leading with gut instinct or with feeling. A Seven's fear is specific: being trapped in pain, boredom or limitation. The defense runs in plain steps. A Seven feels the bad feeling coming. He turns it into a good story or the next adventure. Then he reaches for pleasure so he doesn't have to sit in it.

Pratt's record shows that loop from his late teens to 2026. Nearly all of it comes from his own interviews, which is the evidence a public typing has to work with. The strongest rival is [Type 3](/enneagram-corner/enneagram-type-3), the Achiever, and it gets a full hearing below.

## "What you're selling is yourself"

After high school Pratt dropped out of community college, answered a newspaper ad and sold coupons door to door. In a high-school sales contest, he recalled on the podcast [_Not Dead Yet_](https://www.spreaker.com/episode/chris-pratt--71008192) in March 2026, classmates sold 17 or 22 cards, and he sold 500. "So I loved it," he said.

The job taught him, he said, that "regardless of the product you're selling, what you're selling is yourself and you're selling your attitude." The company started each morning with a chant, "PMA equals OPM, which was positive mental attitude equals other people's money."

He chased the company's prize, an office of his own, for 15 months, and got one outside Denver. "I wasn't even 21," he told Vanity Fair. Then it came apart. "I was too much of a people pleaser to be a good manager," he said in 2026. He sank into debt and told his boss, "I'm so depressed. I can't do it." His mother sent him a ticket home. Looking back, he calls the venture "definitely a failure."

Back in Lake Stevens, he covered it up, as he told Vanity Fair:

> "I had everyone convinced I'd been off doing this great sales job and making money, and I wasn't. I had no prospects, no job, was still sort of riding the glory of high school."

In 2026 he filled in the rest. He had been "kind of like the star of my hometown," and now he was the guy "that like used to be cool and was going to parties. And I just felt like super depressed, you know?" A friend who lived in Hawaii saw him and said, "dude, you don't look good. You seem off."

"I was off and I was partying a lot," Pratt said. He was "slipping down the wrong path."

Here the Three and Seven readings split. People of every type hide a failure, so the hiding settles little. A Three, ashamed of how a failure looks, tends to throw himself at the next win to repair the picture. Pratt went to parties until a friend bought him a one-way ticket to Maui.

## The shifts he gave away

In Maui he lived in a van and a tent on the beach and waited tables at Bubba Gump Shrimp. He was good at that job too: "I was Gumper of the year," he told Vanity Fair.

He also told the magazine this:

> "I was always giving away my shifts because I didn't have much overhead. I lived in a van."

That line does more work than any other in the Seven reading. At about 19, freshly failed and, by his account, depressed, he had a job he was good at and gave the hours away. He drank, smoked weed, fished and worked "just enough." No camera was on him. If the hidden failure had been about his worth, this was the moment to start repairing it, and he spent it on a beach.

He has told that stretch three ways, from "a pretty awesome place to be homeless" in 2014 to a rescue from "the wrong path" in 2026. Press tours tune a story to the room. What stays the same in every version is what he did there.

One night outside a grocery store, while a friend was inside buying them alcohol, a stranger named Henry asked Pratt what he was doing that night. Pratt said he was going to party. "I was charmed by this guy, don't know why," he told Vanity Fair, though it "should've made me nervous but didn't." Henry said "Jesus told me to talk to you" and took him to church.

Then the actor and director Rae Dawn Chong sat at one of his tables, on a day he "wasn't even supposed to take a shift." She asked if he acted. In Vanity Fair's reconstruction of his telling, he answered, "Fuck, yeah, I act. Put me in a movie." In 2026 he said he "kicked in my door to door sales skills." She flew him to Los Angeles for her film _Cursed Part 3_, which Vanity Fair reported "was never released." Of the day she walked in, he said, "I always wanted to go to Hollywood."

## Where Andy came from

His father, Dan, was "from the old school. He was a boxer and a bouncer," Pratt told [Men's Health](https://www.menshealth.com/entertainment/a40289867/chris-pratt-interview-new-movies-series/) in 2022, and he could be "mean." The son was built differently:

> "I am a sensitive person. My dad knew that when I was a youngster, and it kind of made him dislike me. Or not dislike me but act like he disliked me—'cause he probably grew up in a world where a guy like that could get eaten alive. And so he wanted to put calluses on me. Early on, I developed humor as a self-defense mechanism—I developed Andy, really."

Andy, he went on, was "my clown that I had honed my entire life."

He told [GQ](https://www.gq.com/story/chris-pratt-cover-story) much the same in 2015. As a kid he was "obnoxious, really hyperactive, disruptive," and also "really sensitive. I cried a lot." In high school he walked into the coach's office wearing only a sock, and not on his feet. "I liked making people feel uncomfortable."

A sensitive boy, a father who treated sensitivity as a danger, and a clown built to keep the hurt out: that is the Seven's oldest defense, told in his own words. The account holds steady across seven years of interviews, but it is his alone, and his father died in 2014.

The family ran on hope more than on security. They moved to chase a gold mine, he said in 2026: "We were total dreamers and there was no safety net."

## "Pure euphoria"

In Los Angeles, Pratt was "typecast as the bad boyfriend," he told GQ, until Andy Dwyer on _Parks and Recreation_. Early in the second season, Pratt saw himself on screen and thought, "Wow, I'm getting fat." The next thought was a joke:

> "And then immediately I was like, 'This is the funniest...' I was making myself laugh. I was: 'That's where it's at. There's no one doing that. No one being like a super-confident dumb fat guy.' So I started."

He told the showrunner he wanted to get bigger. "The laughs kept coming," he said, and he thought, "Oh great, I found my niche—this is paying better than the asshole-boyfriend parts." By his account to GQ, the highlights of his days were the eating and drinking.

In 2025, on [_Club Random_](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=56M4_TvQkBA), Bill Maher asked whether anything was better about those years. Pratt's answer was eating:

> "I ate and in the moments I was eating it was pure euphoria. And then the minute I stopped eating, I did not feel good until I ate again."

Set that beside the best-known passage about those years. In 2015, Pratt told GQ's Chris Heath that Anna Faris, his wife at the time, had probably preferred him heavier. Heath asked why:

> "I think I was outwardly having more fun. I was more fun to be around, probably. That image that I was casting, to convince people that I was okay, was a really fun person to hang around. Now I have less fun. I focus more."

Elsewhere in the interview, he said he was "the first guy in line to buy that line of bullshit." He compared himself with Chris Farley, who in Pratt's view "buried himself in an addiction to hide the fact that he didn't feel what he was projecting on the outside." Then he widened it: "I think that's often the case with comedians."

Read together, those lines describe a Seven's mask. The cheerful front was there to show people he "was okay," which is a claim about pain. He believed it before anyone else did. It ran on food and drink. And it covered a man who, as GQ reported his words, "didn't feel too great about who I was."

The heavy years ended for work. Auditioning for _Moneyball_ (2011), he heard, "We're not gonna cast you—you're too fat," so he "decided to drop the weight, like in wrestling." He kept playing Andy until 2015 while his weight rose and fell with his roles. Vanity Fair wrote that Pratt "had always wanted to play an action hero." The diet traded a smaller pleasure for a bigger adventure.

## A mind that keeps leaving the room

The loop shows up in small moments too. Asked by GQ in 2015 about a traffic run-in, Pratt first repeated his story, that a driver had cut him off and he was "mad as hell." Then, in the same answer, he turned it around:

> "No, I'm sure she didn't. It was probably my fault for not paying attention. I've got a terrible sense of direction. My mind is constantly wandering, and I get lost all the time."

The anger lasted one clause before he talked himself out of it and blamed his wandering mind.

James Gunn, his _Guardians_ director, told GQ that Pratt has "a new idea every day about everything." Retiring to a ranch to hunt birds, Pratt said, is "kind of what I secretly want to do anyways." Pressed on it, he predicted that "three months later I would be tired of bird hunting and I would be like '...Guardians Ten?'" In 2022 he told Men's Health he has no "ten-year plan." On [_SmartLess_](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=BRa2Vx-3A8Q) that year he said he is "still ambitious" but doesn't want to spend his time trying "to build like an empire": "I want to work to live."

On _Not Dead Yet_ he gave his clearest account of how hope works for him. Talking about why audiences turn on celebrities, he described "a certain brand of hopelessness that exists in people who have it all, but still lack joy." When you have nothing, he said, at least there's hope in the next thing: "just get that ticket, baby." For him, "having a nice house and a cool car and the ability to go on whatever fishing trip I want" has not been enough. As a boy he would "fill those catalog cards out and just sit them by my bedside." Now he could order everything in the magazine, and it doesn't "fill the hole inside of my heart that is in a very specific shape," a shape he says only God can fill.

That is the Seven's trap, described from the inside. Hope lives in the next ticket, and once the tickets are easy to get, the hole is still there. The site's piece on [why the next thing won't fix it](/enneagram-corner/why-the-next-thing-wont-fix-it-type-7) traces the same pattern in other Sevens. Pratt's answer to it is faith, which is where the type explains him least.

## Why not Type 3?

Type 3 is the strongest rival, and Pratt supplies its best line himself. Describing the years before his 2018 MTV speech, he said on _Not Dead Yet_:

> "You're always kind of selling yourself. I always sort of understood how I was seen in the marketplace."

He had no haters back then, he said, because "I don't stand for anything," and he called himself "completely deferential to whatever the world wants. I'll give you literally anything. Just keep me employed as an actor."

The rest of the Three case is real. He hid the sales failure behind a success story. He knew the happy fat guy had a price: "I also understood that there was value to it," he told GQ, and the persona was "paying better." He described his _Guardians_ audition as a sale: "You make them think you already are that guy." He says he has been "money motivated my whole life because we never had anything." Gunn told GQ that Pratt "didn't trust himself to just be Chris Pratt." And in October 2026 he said the comedy years were a time when "people really responded well to it."

Three things tip the case to Seven.

First, selling was the trade of both his jobs. Door-to-door sales and auditions reward reading a room in every type, and charm comes as naturally to a Seven as to a Three. He framed it that way himself, as a peek behind the veil of Hollywood, where people understand how they're seen in the marketplace.

Second, what he did with the failure. Hiding it looks like a Three. What came next does not: parties, then a beach where he gave his shifts away. The heavy years repeat the shape. The front was there "to convince people that I was okay," he bought it before anyone, and food and drink kept it going. "Value" came in the same breath, as a comic noticing why the act worked.

Third, what he wants from a role. A Three wants top billing. Asked about Andy in 2026, Pratt said what he loved was "being a jazz soloist, but not the lead singer."

The limits are real. Most of the Seven evidence is his own retelling in interviews, the cleanest test came when he was about 19, and the "pure euphoria" line comes from auto-captions of a podcast. A reader can still argue Three. The argument here is that Three explains the selling, and Seven explains the choices.

## Why not Type 8, 6 or 9?

The Eight energy is easy to see. He hunts, fishes and wrestled in high school. Men's Health wrote that what he and his friends care about, "in his telling of it," is "the f words: faith, fishing, fighting." He keeps score. Of actors who hit on his wife in front of him, he told GQ, "I can think of exactly who they are, too, and I hope they fucking audition for Guardians of the Galaxy." About his faith: "I'm not going to apologize for that."

Core [Eights](/enneagram-corner/enneagram-type-8) lead with anger, though, and his own words point elsewhere: "I am a sensitive person," and "too much of a people pleaser to be a good manager." On [_Happy Sad Confused_](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=SSjd73MXaqg) in January 2026, he said Bennett Miller provoked him on _Moneyball_ ("He made me so mad") to get the character to do "something that I don't do authentically very well, which is anger." His road rage lasted one clause. The Eight shows in his hobbies and a few hard stands, which reads as a wing.

A [Six](/enneagram-corner/enneagram-type-6) reading has material too. He leans on trusted people. His older brother talked him out of joining the army ("I think he saw something in me") and, he said in 2026, into posting an underwear photo that Pratt thought "seems weird." His manager has had him turn down "huge checks."

But a Six scans for danger and doubts first. Pratt hands that job to others: his manager is "calculated and critical in a way that I'm not." When a stranger approached him outside a store, it "should've made me nervous but didn't." And of answering the ad for the coupon job, he said on SmartLess that "zero critical thinking skills were ever taught to anyone in my household." Security is not what he chases.

A [Nine](/enneagram-corner/enneagram-type-9) reading notices the deference, the "work to live," and the way he handles critics by reciting Proverbs 26:20, "For lack of wood, the fire will go out" ([The Christian Post](https://www.christianpost.com/news/chris-pratt-reveals-moment-he-surrendered-his-platform-to-god.html), 2025). But Nines go quiet and withdraw. Pratt's escapes are loud: euphoric eating, parties, hunting trips, a new idea every day. And when something exciting is on offer, he moves first: "Put me in a movie."

That leaves the wing, which is open. Pratt has the trusted circle of a 7w6 and the appetite for a fight of a 7w8. On motive, the sensitivity and the people-pleasing lean slightly toward the 6 wing. The 8 is louder in what he does for fun.

## "I stood for something"

His faith began in the Seven pattern. He told The Christian Post in 2025, during a Lent partnership with the Catholic prayer app Hallow, that he knelt at church altars as a child, at 19 and in his 30s. "I'd make promises, but I didn't keep them," he said. Months or years later, "I'm off doing the same stuff that got me down the wrong path in the first place."

In August 2012 his son Jack was born premature and spent about a month in neonatal intensive care. Pratt "made a deal with God again." After Jack recovered, he said, "That was the moment that I was like, 'Moving forward, I'm going to give my platform to God.'"

That vow held. At the 2018 MTV Movie & TV Awards he mixed jokes ("Don't be a turd") with "God is real. God loves you. God wants the best for you." Pratt believes the speech helped start the online hostility toward him, including the "Worst Chris" meme that ranked him last among [Chris Hemsworth](/personality-analysis/chris-hemsworth), Chris Evans, Chris Pine and himself. By 2026 he set the speech against his deferential years. He said "I stood for something" and "I didn't backtrack."

His account has shifted with the setting. In 2022 he told Men's Health, "really I'm not a religious person," separating man-made religion from belief in "a very real God." [The Daily Beast](https://www.thedailybeast.com/why-is-chris-pratt-lying-about-his-religious-past) answered with a headline: "Why Is Chris Pratt Lying About His Religious Past?" In 2019 the actor Elliot Page had criticized his church as anti-LGBTQ. In 2022 Pratt said he had never been to Hillsong, the church he had been linked to, and attends Zoe Church, though not exclusively. The Daily Beast reported he had been photographed at a Hillsong service in Los Angeles in 2017, and Men's Health reported that Zoe's founding pastor executive-produced a 2017 film that equated "sexual brokenness" with "same-sex attraction." Asked why he hadn't set the record straight at the time, Pratt said, "I'm gonna, like, throw a church under the bus?"

Even the commitment has a Seven texture. He got through the whole Bible by listening on Hallow, he said, because when he reads books like Leviticus, "my brain starts wandering four seconds in."

The Seven reading explains the broken altar promises easily and the kept vow with more effort. Sevens can commit hard when a commitment opens onto something larger than the next pleasure, and he describes faith as the one thing that fills the hole. A skeptic will add that faith audiences are a market too. Even so, more than a decade of public faith, held through the mockery, is the strand of Pratt the type explains least.

## The part that never had to be hurt

The type leaves other pieces loose. He dieted with discipline for years when roles required it, and he has been "money motivated" since a childhood when, as "the king of my school," he wouldn't ask for the $35 a yearbook cost.

In March 2026, on _Not Dead Yet_, he said he "had a meeting with my manager and agent recently" about his direction. After a run of serious roles, from James Reese in _The Terminal List_ to the January thriller _Mercy_, the plan was "trying to do more comedy, trying to bring fun and lightheartedness back," because "that's something I have."

In October 2026, promoting the second season of _The Terminal List_, he told The Playlist that he had recently met with _Parks and Recreation_ creator Mike Schur "just to talk about the future." He described the "main character dilemma," an idea he said Schur introduced him to. A lead needs a full arc. "They need to be vulnerable. They need to be hurt." Then he talked about Andy:

> "What I liked about Andy Dwyer was it was like being a jazz soloist, but not the lead singer. Like when it was my four measures, I can go and do my little comedy thing. It can be different every time."

"It was easy work," he said, "and I loved it."

Whatever comes of that meeting, he has described a Seven's dream job: four measures, different every time. In 2015 he called the fun of his heavy years an image he cast to convince people he was okay. Eleven years later he wants the fun back, and he has said what made it easy. The funny guy didn't have to be hurt.
