On August 14, 2019, Jay-Z sat beside NFL Commissioner Roger Goodell at Roc Nation's New York headquarters to explain why his entertainment company was going into business with the league. ([theGrio/Associated Press, August 15, 2019](https://thegrio.com/2019/08/15/jay-z-defends-nfl-deal-with-roc-nation-talks-kaepernick/))

The deal was awkward. Colin Kaepernick, the quarterback who began kneeling during the national anthem in 2016 to protest police killings of Black Americans, had not played in the league since 2017, and Jay-Z had been, in theGrio's words, "among the biggest supporters of Kaepernick." He had also turned down invitations to play the halftime show, "even rapping about it in a song."

That day he gave his own reason for the refusals. He "didn't like the process": "You take four artists and everyone thinks they're playing the Super Bowl, and it's almost like this interview process." It "could have been more definite." When talks began, he said, he told Goodell, "If this is about me performing at the Super Bowl, then we can just end this conversation now." He also confirmed objecting to Travis Scott's guest spot in that year's show, not over Kaepernick: "I didn't see any reason for him to play second fiddle to anyone that year."

The usual picture of Jay-Z is hip-hop's great achiever: the Brooklyn dealer who became, by Forbes' June 2019 estimate, the first hip-hop artist with a fortune of $1 billion ([CNN via WTVR, June 3, 2019](https://www.wtvr.com/2019/06/03/superstar-jay-z-has-become-first-billionaire-rapper-says-forbes)). His career began with _Reasonable Doubt_ in 1996, he is married to [Beyonce](/personality-analysis/beyonce-knowles), and by October 2026 he had won 25 Grammys. His 2019 explanation says he refused something narrower than the Super Bowl: being one of four names waiting to be chosen. That reads two ways: a man who will not let others set his terms, or a star who will not be ranked beside three others, as "second fiddle" suggests.

On October 26, 2026, the NFL, Apple Music and Roc Nation announced that he would headline the Super Bowl LXI halftime show on February 14, 2027, a show Roc Nation was producing under its 2019 partnership with the league. Goodell said that when the league reached that deal he had made "just one request: that Jay-Z take the stage himself one day, whenever the moment felt right to him." ([theGrio, October 26, 2026](https://thegrio.com/2026/10/26/jay-z-super-bowl-lxi-halftime-show-announcement/))

The stage on his terms or not at all. The question this profile tries to answer is what that pattern protects: an image, or something he counts as his.

A note on the evidence. Most of what follows is Jay-Z describing himself in friendly or promotional settings. His 2017 New York Times interview is quoted through Time and his memoir _Decoded_ through a Village Voice review, because neither primary source could be opened for this profile. Testimony from people who know him is thin and partly hostile.

## What is Jay-Z's personality type?

Jay-Z most likely fits Enneagram Type 8, at medium confidence. Explaining his emotional wall in 2009, 2017 and 2018, he pointed to a father who let him down, to survival and to a rule against crying, never to winning. He also insists on setting the terms for his work and his family. Type 3 is the strongest alternative.

A [Type 8](/enneagram-corner/enneagram-type-8) organizes life around not being controlled or made vulnerable, and around protecting the people and territory it claims. The Eight reading of Jay-Z rests on the reason he gives for his own coldness and on his insistence on terms whenever something he counts as his is at stake. Counterphobic Type 6 is tested below too. Wing and subtype are left open here. The record does not support a confident guess.

## "When I get big"

Shawn Carter grew up in the Marcy Houses in Bedford-Stuyvesant, Brooklyn. His father, Adnis Reeves, left the family when he was 11. Walking through Marcy with Oprah Winfrey in August 2009, he was asked what his 11-year-old self had felt. "Anger," he said. Then, after describing a father as a boy's "superhero": "Once you've let yourself fall that in love with someone, once you put him on such a high pedestal and he lets you down, you never want to experience that pain again. So I remember just being really quiet and really cold. Never wanting to let myself get close to someone like that again." ([O, The Oprah Magazine, October 2009](https://www.oprah.com/omagazine/oprah-interviews-jay-z-october-2009-issue-of-o-magazine))

The same answer contains a job description: "I remember telling her, 'Don't worry, when I get big, I'm going to take care of this.' I felt like I had to step up." The loss, he said, "made me not express my feelings as much. I was already a shy kid, and it made me a little reclusive. But it also made me independent. And stronger."

At 12 he shot his older brother, Eric, in the shoulder. Asked whether his father's leaving had turned him into "the kind of angry kid who would end up shooting his brother," he said, "Yes—and my brother was dealing with a lot of demons." Eric, about 16, was "doing a lot of drugs. He was taking stuff from our family. I was the youngest, but I felt like I needed to protect everybody." Eric did not press charges, and the brothers made up ([theGrio, November 22, 2010](https://thegrio.com/2010/11/22/jay-z-admits-to-shooting-his-brother-when-he-was-12)).

These accounts are his own, and a frightened child in a violent neighborhood is not a personality type. What matters for the type question is the shape of the adult's answer. He accepts the anger. Protection is the context he adds to it, not a replacement for it.

He was selling crack by about 13. Once someone he calls a friend shot at him three times at close range. "You want to shoot back. Well, maybe not everyone, but I did. I was angry." The friendship was over: "you can't be friends after that—unless the guy is your brother."

He also drew lines that cost him. When his mentor, Jaz-O, took him to London on a record deal, Jay-Z first arranged his drug business so he could go back to it, "because I always had a fear that this music thing wouldn't be successful." When the label tried to sign him instead: "Jaz was the one who'd brought me in, and I felt that signing wouldn't be loyal to him. So I told them no." He traced the code to Marcy: "So it was here in the projects that I learned loyalty."

## The wall, described three times

The most useful evidence in Jay-Z's record is a sentence he keeps repeating in different decades to different interviewers.

In 2009 he told Oprah, "I always had that wall up. And whenever someone got close to me, I would shut down." Marriage, he said, was not the biggest change: "Reconnecting with my father changed me more than anything. Because it allowed me to let people in." His mother arranged the meeting in 2003. His father did not come the first time. The second time he did, and Jay-Z did not open with forgiveness. "I told him how I felt the day he left," he said. His father answered, "Man, you knew where I was." Jay-Z's reply: "I was a kid! Do you realize how wrong you were? It was your responsibility to see me." Then: "He finally accepted that." Reeves died a couple of months later. "I got him an apartment, I was buying furniture," Jay-Z said. "And he passed away."

In November 2017, after Beyonce's 2016 album _Lemonade_ had put infidelity in their marriage on record and his own 2017 album _4:44_ had answered it, he gave The New York Times' T Magazine a second source for the same mechanism. As Time quoted it: "So you go into survival mode, and when you go into survival mode what happen? You shut down all emotions. So even with women, you gonna shut down emotionally, so you can't connect. And then all the things happen from there: infidelity..." ([Time, November 29, 2017](https://time.com/5041732/jay-z-interview/))

In 2018, on David Letterman's Netflix show, he named a third: where he grew up, "Our emotional cue is 'Be a man. Don't cry.'" Of "Song Cry," from his 2001 album _The Blueprint_ ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Song_Cry)), he said, "It was my way of saying I want to cry, I want to be open, I want to have the emotional tools that it takes to keep my family together." Then: "We did the hard work of going to therapy." And: "I'm proud of the father and the husband that I am today." His father's brother had been killed in the projects, he explained, and hunting the killer consumed his father. He now has "compassion for what (my dad) went through, which was anger." ([The Washington Post via Bangor Daily News, April 7, 2018](https://www.bangordailynews.com/2018/04/07/arts-culture/jay-z-opens-up-to-david-letterman-about-cheating-on-beyonce-his-mothers-sexuality/))

Across nine years the sources he names shift, from a father who let him down to danger to a rule about crying, but the mechanism holds: the wall goes up wherever feeling would be exposure. In none of the three accounts does he describe it as a way to keep performing or to keep winning. That fits an Eight's armor, though a Three would rarely describe himself that way either. What leans Eight is what he did when the wall was finally tested: face to face with his father, he made the older man hear it. The limit is plain: the 2017 and 2018 versions came in the years after _Lemonade_, when repairing a public image was also in his interest. Both readings agree that he changed. They disagree about why.

## The masters are not for sale

In the mid-1990s, disenchanted with the small label that had signed him, he co-founded his own, Roc-A-Fella Records, with Damon Dash and Kareem "Biggs" Burke. In 2004 he became president of Def Jam, the larger label that owned a big share of Roc-A-Fella. In October 2023 Gayle King asked him on CBS Mornings whether he would ever sell his masters, the original recordings of his albums. "No," he said. "I get why people do it, I've been fortunate enough to make money in this place, but for me, it was the fight of my life." He described the condition he says he set when he took the Def Jam job: "Okay, I'll do this job and part of this job is my masters has to be reverted back to me." ([Express & Star/PA, October 26, 2023](https://www.expressandstar.com/whats-on/arts-and-entertainment/jay-z-says-he-will-never-sell-his-masters-it-was-the-fight-of-my-life-5840914))

The rule is narrower than ownership in general. He sells businesses: in March 2021 he agreed to sell a majority of the streaming service Tidal to Jack Dorsey's Square for $297 million in cash and stock, keeping a stake and joining Square's board ([theGrio, March 4, 2021](https://thegrio.com/2021/03/04/square-inc-buy-tidal-jay-z/)). The recordings are where he stops. One reading, offered as interpretation, is that he treats the masters as part of himself and the companies as assets. The limit is that this is his 2023 position, and artists of his generation sell catalogs all the time.

Not everyone remembers 2004 as principle. In a 2022 interview on The Art of Dialogue, Dash called it betrayal: "Like, how would you feel if your brother just betrayed you for money? Would it hurt?" He also called it an industry pattern: "They make one sell out the other, their friend, divide and conquer, that's a normal story." ([Okayplayer, August 24, 2022](https://www.okayplayer.com/dame-dash-addresses-his-relationship-with-jay-z-how-would-you-feel-if-your-brother-just-betrayed-you/768057)) Dash is a hostile witness; in 2021 Roc-A-Fella sued him for trying to auction _Reasonable Doubt_ as an NFT, a dispute settled in June 2022. The sources checked for this profile contain no public answer from Jay-Z to the charge. Taken at face value, Dash describes money and a bigger seat, which fits a Three at least as well as an Eight, and it sits awkwardly beside the Jaz-O story, where loyalty beat a record deal. Either the code gave way once his own catalog was at stake, or it was never as consistent as his telling of it.

## Where the line is

On November 19, 2016, Kanye West stopped a concert in Sacramento to talk about him. "Jay Z, call me, bruh. You still ain't calling me," West said, and a few lines later, "Just call me. Talk to me like a man." The two were already not speaking. In the same speech West told Beyonce that he "was hurt." ([The Fader transcript, November 20, 2016](https://www.thefader.com/2016/11/20/kanye-west-transcript-sacramento))

He answered first on his 2017 album _4:44_, then on the Rap Radar podcast that August. He said West had "talked about me 100 times," but "what really hurt me" was that West had brought his wife and children into it: "But you brought my family into it. Now it's a problem with me. That's a real, real problem." ([The Fader, August 18, 2017](https://www.thefader.com/2017/08/18/jay-z-kanye-west-444-tidal-rap-radar-podcast-interview)) By November 2017 he said he had talked to West "just to tell him, like, he's my brother. I love Kanye."

That was not the end. In March 2025 West posted, then deleted, a slur about Jay-Z and Beyonce's younger children on X ([Business Report, March 19, 2025](https://businessreport.co.za/entertainment/celebrity-news/2025-03-19-kanye-west-faces-backlash-for-insensitive-remarks-about-beyonc-and-jay-zs-children)). On April 10, 2025 he posted, "I'm sorry Jay Z," while complaining that no one in rap had stood by him ([Billboard, June 2, 2025](https://www.billboard.com/music/rb-hip-hop/kanye-west-jay-z-apology-1235987732/)). The sources checked for this profile record no public reply from Jay-Z.

The family line was not always his own rule. In 2001, feuding with Nas, he released "Super Ugly," with bars aimed at Carmen Bryan, Nas's then-girlfriend. He apologized on Hot 97 shortly afterward. "Mom put in a call and said, 'That went too far,'" he said. "She's never, ever called me about music." He added, "I felt like I didn't think about women's feelings." ([Complex, April 9, 2024, citing HipHopDX's 2001 report](https://uk.complex.com/music/a/backwoodsaltar/jay-z-nas-apology-super-ugly-j-cole-dissing-kendrick-lamar)) The line he defends is his own family, and he learned part of it from his mother after crossing it himself.

On February 4, 2024, accepting the first Dr. Dre Global Impact Award at the Grammys with his daughter Blue Ivy beside him, he turned on the institution honoring him. "I don't want to embarrass this young lady but she has more Grammys than everyone and has never won Album of the Year," he said. "So even by your own metrics that doesn't work." He softened it with "we love y'all" and "obviously, it's subjective," but said it on their broadcast. ([NBC New York, February 5, 2024](https://www.nbcnewyork.com/entertainment/entertainment-news/jay-z-beyonce-grammy-awards/5105432/))

## Two disputed episodes

Two parts of the record are disputed. One bears on anger versus protection; the other says nothing about personality.

In December 1999 the record executive Lance "Un" Rivera was stabbed at the Kit Kat Club in Manhattan, during a dispute over the bootlegging of Jay-Z's album _Vol. 3_. According to the police report summarized by Rolling Stone in December 2001, Jay-Z stabbed Rivera twice. In October 2001, Rolling Stone reported, he "admitted to the stabbing and changed his plea to guilty," in a deal that secured three years' probation. ([Rolling Stone, December 7, 2001](https://www.rollingstone.com/music/music-news/jay-z-gets-probation-244400/)) _Decoded_, as the Village Voice quoted it, calls the episode "a mindless assault" with "the potential of being behind bars for years." Then in May 2023 Rivera told DJ Vlad: "No. JAY-Z was not the guy that actually stabbed me that night." ([The Fader, May 22, 2023](https://www.thefader.com/2023/05/22/lance-un-rivera-jay-z-did-not-stab-him)) The court record and the victim's later account do not agree, and this profile does not choose between them.

If the plea is taken at face value, the episode cuts against the protective reading rather than for it. An assault in a dispute over bootlegged copies of his own album looks like anger on behalf of his work, not protection of anyone. Rivera's denial is why it carries little weight here in either direction.

In December 2024 an anonymous plaintiff, represented by the Texas attorney Tony Buzbee, added Jay-Z to a civil suit accusing him and Sean Combs of sexually assaulting a 13-year-old girl at an afterparty following the 2000 MTV Video Music Awards. Both men denied it. On February 14, 2025, she voluntarily dismissed the suit with prejudice. His lawyer, Alex Spiro, said "he never settled, he never paid 1 red penny." Jay-Z called the claims "frivolous, fictitious and appalling." ([ABC News/Good Morning America, February 14, 2025](https://www.goodmorningamerica.com/news/story/civil-case-accusing-jay-z-diddy-rape-dismissed-118847148)) An allegation and a denial tell us nothing about his personality, and they are noted here only for accuracy.

## The case for Type 3

The [Type 3](/enneagram-corner/enneagram-type-3) reading deserves full weight. A Three needs to be valued, so it becomes what the room admires and makes success visible. Much of Jay-Z's public life looks like that work.

He says plainly that "Jay-Z" is a construction. In _Decoded_, as the Village Voice's Zach Baron quoted it in November 2010, he writes, "What the rapper is doing is creating a character that, if you're lucky, you find out about more and more from song to song," and, "I never had to reject Shawn Carter to become Jay-Z." Baron noted that his wife is not named and that Shawn Carter "remains stubbornly distant." ([Village Voice, November 17, 2010](https://www.villagevoice.com/decoding-jay-zs-decoded/)) He and Beyonce keep a pact not to discuss each other in public.

He measures himself against the top. Of his rivalry with [Kanye West](/personality-analysis/kanye) he told the Times in 2017, "It's always been like a little underlying competition with your big brother," and, "everyone wants to be the greatest in the world." Of the end of his dealing years he told Oprah, "it was beyond survival. I was successful... I had become addicted to the lifestyle."

He also staged a famous exit. _The Black Album_ (2003) was promoted as his final release. He returned with _Kingdom Come_ on November 21, 2006 ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kingdom_Come_(Jay-Z_album))). Leaving at the top and coming back on his own schedule fits a Three managing a legend. It fits the "on his terms" pattern just as well, so it does not discriminate.

He explains toughness as a cover, too. On Fresh Air in 2010, speaking of rappers generally and then of his own first show, he called bravado stage fright: "So that bravado is an act of, I am so nervous right now, and I'm scared to death. I'm going to act so tough that I'm going to hide it." ([NPR Fresh Air, November 16, 2010](https://www.npr.org/transcripts/132438048)) That cuts both ways. Toughness over fear is how an Eight's armor works, and also how a performer keeps the show going.

And he adapts to powerful institutions. Asked at the 2019 press conference whether he would kneel or stand, he said, "I think we've moved past kneeling and I think it's time to go into actionable items." He added, "I support protest across the board," and gave his reason for the deal: "the kneeling was not about a job, it was about injustice," and "the NFL has a huge platform and we can use that huge platform." Eric Reid, who had sued the league alongside Kaepernick, criticized the deal. Asked why Kaepernick was not part of it, Jay-Z said, "You'd have to ask him. I'm not his boss. I can't just bring him into something. That's for him to say." He said the two had talked about the deal.

A Three engineers a persona, keeps the private self out of view, competes to be the greatest, exits and returns on a schedule that protects the legend, and partners with whoever offers the bigger platform. Every one of those fits.

## What separates them

The Three reading explains the product. It is weaker on why he says no.

The first test is what the shutdown protects. Given three chances over nine years, he ties it to a father who let him down ("you never want to experience that pain again"), to danger ("survival mode") and to a learned rule ("Be a man. Don't cry"). The repair he credits is relational, not success: confronting his father, therapy with his wife. A Three could give a similar account, so this test is consistent with Eight rather than proof of it. The bravado quote is the closest he comes to the Three habit of hiding feeling to keep performing, and it is about the stage, not the people close to him.

The second test is what he does when someone else would set the terms, and an honest count matters here because his stated reasons are mixed. For the masters he gives control: "the fight of my life," and a condition he says he set for the Def Jam job. For West he gives family. For the halftime refusals he gives the process. But on Travis Scott he gave rank, and at the Grammys he argued about accolades on Beyonce's behalf. Most of these choices also cost him little: they cost him with institutions while raising his standing with artists and fans, a trade a Three makes all the time. The test leans Eight on terms and is weaker on costs than it first looks.

The one choice that clearly cost him with his own side, the 2019 NFL deal, is the strongest case against this reading. "We've moved past kneeling" can read as a Three choosing the bigger platform. Under the same test, it is also a man accepting disapproval from his own side for a seat with power. An Eight reading can say that he chose a seat with power over a gesture, but that is an interpretation, and it should be held loosely.

The rival this comparison can miss is counterphobic Six, the classic Eight lookalike. Much of the early material fits it: a wall built after a trusted figure let him down, toughness worn over fear, a loyalty code, a backup plan in case music failed. What separates them is authority. A counterphobic Six tests authority and looks for backing it can trust. Jay-Z keeps becoming the authority. He says he made his masters a condition of running Def Jam, and he opened talks with the NFL by telling its commissioner what was off the table. At the 2019 press conference, when a reporter's question seemed aimed at both men, he asked, "Are you asking me?" Then: "I'm not the commissioner yet." The room laughed. It was a joke, and the pattern in a line. Six remains a fair reading of the boy; the adult leans Eight.

No single choice settles it. Across four decades, the insistence on terms leans Eight, at medium confidence.

## Where the Type 8 reading strains

The Eight reading has to explain the quiet. Classic Eights take up space. Jay-Z describes himself as a shy kid made "a little reclusive," a daydreamer who "took my mind out of my environment, to the point where I wasn't paying attention to what was happening around me. I still do that now." He answered West's 2016 attack first through an album and spoke about it only months later. Those traits sound more like a withdrawn Nine or Five than the confrontational Eight of the textbook.

One possible answer, offered as interpretation rather than proof, is that he learned early that showing feeling was exposure, so his control runs through ownership, terms and timing rather than volume. The answer fits, but it is also convenient, and a reader can reasonably weigh the quiet more heavily than this profile does.

The halftime show is the other open question. He refused it for years, then in 2026 accepted it, on a stage Roc Nation was producing, thirty years after _Reasonable Doubt_. His statement framed it as a career summit: "From 'Reasonable Doubt' to standing on the Super Bowl halftime stage, this has been a journey." ([KMPH/The National News Desk, October 27, 2026](https://kmph.com/news/entertainment/hip-hop-artist-jay-z-picks-himself-to-headline-super-bowl-lxi-halftime-show-february-14-inglewood-california-sofi-stadium-nfl-apple-music-roc-nation-entertainment-commissioner-roger-goodell)) On the Eight reading, nothing changed but who sets the terms. On the Three reading, he waited until the moment would look best. The record available on November 6, 2026 cannot separate the two.

## Up to them

The promise he describes making at 11, "when I get big, I'm going to take care of this," turns up again in what he said about his masters in 2023. He said then that he would not sell them, and he named who gets to decide after him: "I want my kids to see my work and if they decide to sell it, then it's up to them."

He has spent his career refusing to let a label, a league or an audition decide the terms of his work. The one group he names as free to decide is his children.
