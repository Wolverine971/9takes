In early December 2025, five days before the fifth Streamer Awards, the annual awards show for online streamers that she created, QTCinderella sat on a couch in her Los Angeles living room and showed a GQ reporter the seating chart she had built: hundreds of streamers sorted into dinner tables. Some guests, she said, were "social liquid" who do well anywhere. "But then you have people that haven't met anybody. Perfect example: Jenn McAllister," she said. "So I'm gonna sit her next to Valkyrae, because she's the sweetest muffin in the world." Valkyrae is a streamer and her podcast cohost. ([GQ, December 17, 2025](https://www.gq.com/story/qtcinderella-streamer-awards-profile))

About two weeks earlier, the streamer Deshae Frost had reacted to that year's nominations on his own stream by yelling for her to "shut the f\*\*\* up," as Dexerto printed it. The report does not say what set him off. She replied on X (formerly Twitter) on November 18, 2025:

> "How incredibly kind. Congrats on his nomination. I'll try to shut up during the show while all the streamers eat the dinner I paid for, be celebrated at the show I spent months working on, and enjoy their night hanging out with eachother."

Frost apologized on stream hours later: "I was being inconsiderate." She did not uninvite him. ([Dexerto](https://www.dexerto.com/twitch/qtcinderella-tearfully-lashes-out-streamer-awards-backlash-3284367/))

Both are things a host does. One finds the guest who knows nobody a kind neighbor. The other reads the bill out loud.

QTCinderella goes by Blaire and does not publish her surname. She was born in 1994 and grew up in Washington state, the youngest of four ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/QTCinderella); [GQ](https://www.gq.com/story/qtcinderella-streamer-awards-profile)). Her mother died in 2016.

She began streaming in 2018 on Twitch, the live-video platform, and on March 12, 2022 put on the first Streamer Awards, which she produces, hosts and, by her account, pays for. As of December 2025 she co-hosted the podcast Wine About It with Valkyrae. In October 2026 she married the streamer Ludwig Ahgren, her partner since 2020 ([Sportskeeda](https://www.sportskeeda.com/us/streamers/news-mr-mrs-coworkerwig-hasanabi-streamers-react-ludwig-qtcinderella-s-wedding)).

She built the room where her industry meets once a year. During that same December 2025 visit she told [GQ](https://www.gq.com/story/qtcinderella-streamer-awards-profile) of the people in it: "Nobody gives a shit about what's keeping me up at night."

So what is the hosting for: a place among people, or a stage she could win?

## What is QTCinderella's personality type?

QTCinderella reads as Type 2, the Helper, at medium confidence. In 2020 she said she bakes so people will be nicer to her and that care has to be earned. She has given out invitations, seats and trophies since, and when hurt she lists what others got. Types 3 and 6 are live rivals. Most evidence is self-report.

A [Type 2](/enneagram-corner/enneagram-type-2) secures a place with people by meeting their needs and finds it hard to ask for anything back. A [Type 3](/enneagram-corner/enneagram-type-3) builds worth through achievement and how it is received.

Running an awards show pays in gratitude and in status at once, so the job cannot separate the two. The test this profile uses is narrower: what she said the giving was for before there was a show, and what she counts when she is hurt. None of this is a diagnosis.

## "Maybe they'll come now"

She says she "was raised to be in someone's shadow, just being a supporting actor and second thought." Her parents were "mostly focused on getting divorced," she told GQ, though she also said they "definitely weren't neglectful." GQ reported that her family was and remains in the Mormon Church, which she left at 18. ([GQ](https://www.gq.com/story/qtcinderella-streamer-awards-profile))

The sources opened here hold two party stories, each told once. In sixth grade, she told GQ, she made 20 caramel apples for a birthday screening of The Incredibles and nobody came. In high school, she told Inven Global in 2022, she organized an Alice in Wonderland murder-mystery dinner, and by the outlet's telling only half the invited guests showed. "I was super bullied in high school. I didn't necessarily fit in," she said. ([Inven Global, 2022](https://www.invenglobal.com/articles/16950/qtcinderella-streamer-awards-shitcamp-twitch-landscape))

She draws the line from the parties to the career herself. Of a stalled stretch during COVID she told Inven Global: "I wasn't providing anything. I didn't feel driven." Then a friend, the streamer Malena Tudi, organized Sh\*tcon, a parody of the cancelled TwitchCon convention, and she went. She credited the friend: "it's really Malena that inspired me to get back into it. Maybe they didn't come to my parties when I was 16. But maybe they'll come now."

The stated reason has not changed since. Polygon in November 2023 called her a self-described "pathological people-pleaser" ([Polygon](https://www.polygon.com/23949548/qtcinderella-interview-twitch-controversy)). "For one night of the year, the negativity is silent," she told the BBC in December 2025 ([BBC](https://www.bbc.co.uk/news/articles/c3v1dd01x65o)).

A stated mission is also a brand. The girl nobody came to see who now throws the party is a very good origin story for an events business, and she may tell it because it works.

## "My cookies are my training wheels"

On June 20, 2020, by Inven Global's dating, she had just given notice at her day job when she spent about an hour and a half on stream with Dr. Alok Kanojia, the psychiatrist known as Dr. K ([HealthyGamerGG](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=De245b9hRw0)). The first Streamer Awards were almost two years away.

She told him baking had always been "an easy way to fit in." She brings cookies to a party, she said, becomes "the girl that brought cookies," and people are "nicer to me." When Kanojia compared a prop like that to training wheels on a bike, she finished the thought: "my cookies are my training wheels." ([34:55](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=De245b9hRw0&t=2095s))

"I just want them to be happy," she said of other people, and when he asked whether what she wanted was for them to like her, she allowed that maybe it was.

Then the part a Type 2 reading turns on. "I don't want to ask people to care about me," she said, because "it's selfish." And: "I don't think I deserve anything." She has to "work hard to earn it." Making other people happy gave a little back, she said, and "sometimes maybe that's not enough." ([59:27](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=De245b9hRw0&t=3567s))

This is one conversation, at a low point, with a psychiatrist steering it. It is also the only long account in this record from before she had events to promote.

## "I stayed at the hotel and I cried"

Her first major event, by Inven Global's account, was the Roast of Ludwig, an evening of insult comedy she organized for Ahgren. Sh\*tcamp, which she pitched at Sh\*tcon and ran in September 2021, was far more ambitious: nearly a week of shared streams with 16 streamers. It averaged more than 65,000 viewers, Inven Global reported. Her account of the last night:

> "When Sh\*tcamp ended, everyone went around the room, we popped champagne, we gave high-fives, they said, 'Let's go to the club!' I stayed at the hotel and I cried. I was exhausted. I felt like it was a failure because everyone was tired the last day, and so the last stream wasn't super hype. I felt like I didn't do good enough. I felt too many people didn't get the opportunity to stream."

The number was good, and it settled nothing for her.

Most of her reasons are a producer's, and she added, "I take it really personally because I just want it to be better." One reason is about who was left out. ([Inven Global](https://www.invenglobal.com/articles/16950/qtcinderella-streamer-awards-shitcamp-twitch-landscape))

The original plan for the awards, Inven Global reported, was to put the show on the channel of [xQc](/personality-analysis/xqc), then the biggest streamer on Twitch, with him as cohost. She kept it on hers. "I'm just highlighting another big male streamer and I'm giving them my thing," she said, crediting [Pokimane](/personality-analysis/pokimane) with the push. She hosted with Maya Higa instead. Higa closed the first show: "I know my name is all over it, but this is all QT."

Keeping "my thing" is a claim of ownership. Choosing Higa is a gift. A Two or a Three could have made that call.

Then there is the money. In March 2023 she told Entertainment Tonight (ET) that costs had tripled in a year and called the show "just a passion project" ([ET](https://www.etonline.com/qtcinderella-on-handling-online-attacks-and-what-keeps-her-streaming-exclusive-201807)). In December 2024 she said the show had been "almost profitable" until a sponsor "pulled out last second" over a nominee she did not name ([Dexerto](https://www.dexerto.com/twitch/qtcinderella-reveals-sponsor-pulled-out-of-streamer-awards-last-second-over-one-nominee-3014516/)). In December 2025 she told GQ: "I'm very unhappy to say, I've never made money from the Streamer Awards."

These are her figures and nobody outside has audited them. The show has had sponsors, and by the end of 2025 it had returned things a Three would count as profit: a GQ profile and an audience GQ put at over a million.

Five editions at a stated loss is still a costly choice made again and again. What it buys her is the question.

## "This is what pain looks like"

On January 30, 2023, the streamer Brandon Ewing, known as Atrioc, apologized on stream after inadvertently showing a browser tab for a website that sold AI-generated sexual deepfakes of women streamers, QTCinderella among them. He admitted he had bought access to the images, and he posted a longer written apology on February 1 ([Vice, 2023](https://www.vice.com/en/article/deepfake-qtcinderella-atrioc/)). GQ later described him as her "collaborator and friend."

The same day he apologized, she went live. "This is probably the stupidest thing I've ever done," she began. "And I'm sure everyone in the world would tell me not to go live right now." Then: "I wanted to go live because this is what pain looks like. This is what it looks like!" ([Sportskeeda](https://www.sportskeeda.com/esports/news-i-m-going-f-king-sue-you-qtcinderella-addresses-community-following-streamer-deepfake-controversy), transcribing the stream.)

She vowed to sue the site's creator. Two weeks later she told Vice that the lawyers she had consulted saw little more to do, since the site was already down.

In that interview she said the images had brought back an eating disorder and an older injury: "I was sexually assaulted as a child, and it was the same feeling." In December 2025 she told GQ more about that abuse, and why she speaks of it: "The most comfort I've ever found is when I hear other people talk about it."

That is her account, given twice on the record. This profile reports it in her words and draws no conclusion about her personality from it.

Nor does it read her type from that stream. Going live is how streamers answer a crisis, and Ewing's own apology that day was a live stream. She told Vice she did not want to talk about it, and that she did so hoping her nieces would not face the same thing in 10 years. ([Vice](https://www.vice.com/en/article/deepfake-qtcinderella-atrioc/))

Within two months she told ET, "I tell people I'm trying to forgive. I'm also healing from it." In January 2025 she said on stream, "I'm a forgiver," and also that she had skipped a New Year's party after learning he was invited ([Sportskeeda](https://www.sportskeeda.com/us/streamers/news-i-m-forgiver-qtcinderella-says-moved-atrioc-deepfake-controversy-refuses-meet)). He was part of her working circle, so keeping away had a cost and forgiving had a practical use, whatever her type.

Her forgiveness is her own to give and does not change the record of what happened.

## The dinner she paid for

Doubts about the show's fairness are as old as the show. At the first ceremony, on March 12, 2022, Streamer of the Year went to Ludwig Ahgren, the host's partner. She told GQ she cried the night before as she glued his name to the trophy, anticipating the backlash.

In November 2025, after the nominees for the fifth awards were announced, a viewer asked on her stream whether "problematic people" were "blackballed" from nominations. "No! They've just never nominated," she said. "Their audience doesn't show up."

Within hours the streamer [Asmongold](/personality-analysis/asmongold) answered on his own stream: "You're lying. You know you're lying. Everybody knows you're lying." He argued that she had "exercised editorial control" over the nominees to "get as many viewers as possible and maintain as many sponsors as possible" ([Dexerto](https://www.dexerto.com/twitch/qtcinderella-shuts-down-claim-that-streamer-awards-blacklist-problematic-streamers-3284164/)). GQ reported that some users went further and said she had rigged the awards against right-wing streamers such as him.

The reporting cited here presents no evidence of rigging. No vote counts are public either, so neither his claim nor her explanation can be checked from outside. Nominees are fan-voted, Dexerto reported. For the 2025 show, winners were decided 70% by public vote and 30% by an industry panel ([BBC](https://www.bbc.co.uk/news/articles/c3v1dd01x65o)) that GQ called secret.

On the November stream she cried. "I'm doing my f\*\*\*ing best over here. I'm just so tired. I'm tired of crying." Of the backlash from fellow streamers in the week she answered Frost, she said, per Dexerto:

> "Honestly, I expect the abuse from viewers, but my god does it feel like sh\*t from my peers."

About two weeks later, in her kitchen, she told GQ: "I am tired. But I'm too busy to be tired. And it's really hard 'cause I'm not a fucking hero for putting on an award show."

That can be read two ways. She wants the effort seen and disowns the wish in the same sentence. Or, more plainly, she feels she has no standing to complain about an awards show.

The show itself, on December 6, 2025, drew a second round of complaints. Both she and her cohost, the streamer Fanfan, delivered monologues built on insult jokes. Fanfan also compared [Kai Cenat](/personality-analysis/kai-cenat) to Sean Combs, the music mogul known as Diddy, who was convicted in a federal case that year. Afterward, in GQ's summary of the feed, unnamed posters said the jokes were racist and that streamers had acted homophobic.

The sharpest argument came from GQ's writer, Kieran Press-Reynolds:

> "It's hard to believe QT thought her and Fanfan's monologues, which mostly involved things like calling Caseoh fat and cracking Piker shock-collar jokes, would result in a feast of positivity."

Caseoh and Hasan Piker are streamers. Asked about her jokes before the show, she said she was working on it, he wrote, and gave her own example of why kind ones are harder: "A complimentary joke is so much harder to make than like, 'You're a pedophile!'"

Two days after the show she said on stream that a lot of the criticism was "really valid," Dexerto reported, without saying which parts. She said the show is "much bigger than it used to be" and that she has to "adjust for that" in its jokes and bits. She defended one of her own jokes, about the streamer Rakai's ban, as one the room understood and viewers at home did not. She promised "a very hard pivot" for 2026. ([Dexerto](https://www.dexerto.com/twitch/qtcinderella-promises-streamer-awards-changes-as-its-gotten-much-bigger-than-before-3291478/))

Her stated purpose for the night is that the negativity goes silent. Her own material that night was insult comedy, and her first major event was a roast. The Type 2 reading does not explain that.

## "A version of me"

In December 2025 GQ reported that she thinks of fellow streamers more as coworkers than as friends. "Everyone knows a version of me that I push 'Go Live' with," she said.

During the Wine About It recording the reporter watched, she said these streamers cannot be real friends because they do not know her last name or her birthday. That irritated Valkyrae, and GQ called the remark hyperbolic. Valkyrae, in the same article: "she's the best organizer, the best planner. She was my dream podcast cohost."

The counterpoint is already on the page: the surname is one she keeps private herself. No source opened here gives her reason. The same record includes three swattings, false emergency calls that send armed police to a home, and, by her account to the BBC, men turning up at her craft shop looking for her.

But she keeps naming the same absence. Of Ahgren she told GQ: "I'm so used to feeling like I'm the rainy cloud in everyone's room, and he's never been affected by that."

The woman who knows which guest will be alone at the table says nobody knows her.

That asymmetry does not pick a type. A Three would call "a version of me" a persona. A Six would call it caution. What it does show is which way her attention runs.

On the drive to the studio, GQ's reporter helped her brainstorm Christmas gifts for her podcast cohosts. She mentioned custom keyboard caps she once gave friends and thinks went unused. She pulled over for ambulances, wondering whether the person inside was okay.

A day with a reporter is a performance too, and empathy belongs to no one type. But the unused gift is the dinner again, remembered on a calm afternoon.

By October 2026 she was describing those coworkers differently. On October 22, after the couple called out four late wedding guests on a podcast and viewers went after them, she posted: "This job is weird cause you share your personal life online and then as you become more ingrained in it other content creators become your personal life." ([Dexerto](https://www.dexerto.com/twitch/qtcinderella-slams-parasocial-freaks-after-ludwig-reveals-which-streamers-were-late-to-their-wedding-3420953/))

## The case for Type 3

In the 2020 conversation she said, as Inven Global quoted it, "Going into Twitch, knowing that I'll never be at the top is very daunting." And:

> "I find my self-worth 100% in 'Oh, she is successful.' And now I'm not that. And so it's like, 'Well, who am I?'"

Five years later: "My work ethic has been my defining feature in my life," she told GQ. "It's the only thing that I've ever found valuable in myself."

On this reading she is a worker whose worth has always been her output. She knew she would never top the viewer charts, so she found the one lane in streaming where she could be the best: producer and host of the industry's central night. The show pays in standing what it costs in cash. The talk of inclusion is a host's brand, and the tears over the 2025 backlash are what an image under attack costs.

The rest of the 2020 conversation complicates it. Kanojia's summary was that she valued herself only with proof of being better than other people. That formulation is his. Asked where the standard came from, she said nobody had pushed her. Her brothers and sister were varsity athletes and she was cut from the volleyball team: "no one put pressure on me besides myself," and "I just wanted to fit in with my own family." ([1:16:00](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=De245b9hRw0&t=4560s))

By her own account, then, being the best was a way to belong, and work is how care gets earned. Read beside that, the 2025 line about work ethic sounds less like a scoreboard and more like the cookies.

So the lean toward Type 2 over Type 3 rests on three observations, each with a Type 3 answer.

First, the 2020 account came before the awards existed, so it cannot be a host's brand. But it is one conversation, and "100%" is hers too.

Second, when a peer attacked her in 2025 she listed what the streamers would receive, not ratings, rank or anything she had won. But a list of what she did is also a list of accomplishments.

Third, she has the admiration a Three works for and does not cite it when she is hurt. What she says is missing is thanks for the giving. A Three can want thanks too.

A family and a church with a high bar for being good could also teach these habits to a child of any type, and in 2020 she tied not fitting in to her family's religion. A Three with a strong helping streak would leave much of this record. That is why the confidence is medium.

## The case for Type 6

In the same 2020 conversation she said she had dealt with anxiety for about six years, severe at its worst. She would ask people "are you mad at me, do you not like me" and then decide "they lied." "I need reassurance way too much," she said. ([1:10:15](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=De245b9hRw0&t=4215s))

In December 2025 GQ reported that she tries to predict every objection to what she does. "I'm just tired of the internet being mad at me," she said.

Some of the vigilance has a plain outside cause, and anxiety alone does not make a Six. GQ reported in December 2025 that she was diagnosed with dissociation after being swatted three times in three months. But the timeline does not let those events carry all of it. The doubt and the reassurance-seeking were there in June 2020, before the deepfakes. GQ dates the first swatting only as "a few years ago."

What separates a Two from a Six here is what the doubt is about and what she does with it. A [Type 6](/enneagram-corner/enneagram-type-6) asks what can be relied on. The reassurance she described seeking was about being liked, and when Kanojia asked where she got the idea that people hated her, she traced it to herself: "since I don't like myself why would anybody else like me."

In Enneagram terms that is shame about her own worth, the ground Types 2 and 3 share, more than fear about what can be relied on. And her remedy is not to find someone to lean on. She said asking for care was selfish, and she bakes.

It is a narrow separation. A Six who buys safety by being useful would also bring cookies, and "nicer to me" can be read as protection. Type 6 ranks about level with Type 3 as a rival.

Some of her fits none of the three. "I am a perfectionist," she told Inven Global, which sounds like the inner critic of a [Type 1](/enneagram-corner/enneagram-type-1). But her critic is aimed at her productions, and what she says she wants from people is to be liked, not to be right.

"The rainy cloud" is [Type 4](/enneagram-corner/enneagram-type-4) material, but a Four leans into being different, and her stated aim in 2020 was to fit in. The insult comedy is explained by none of them.

## What the record cannot show

Nearly all of the evidence about motive is her own account, given to reporters between 2022 and 2025, often in weeks when she was promoting an event or defending one. The exception is the 2020 conversation, whose quotations were checked against the recording.

The two people quoted praising her were her cohosts when they spoke, Higa at the 2022 awards and Valkyrae on the podcast in December 2025. They speak to her effort and her planning, not to why she does it. As of November 10, 2026, this profile found no named account of her motives, and no critical one, from a former collaborator, a crew member or anyone else who has worked with her.

Several things went unreviewed: years of her podcast, a video in which she discusses her health, and her exit from the esports organization TSM. The skeptical view that her standing was borrowed from Ahgren's larger audience is not tested here. The record closes on November 10, 2026, before the sixth Streamer Awards, so the "very hard pivot" she promised is untested.

They come to her parties now, hundreds of them, to tables she arranged. By her own count in December 2025, an exaggeration even to the reporter who heard it, the people at those tables do not know her last name or her birthday. She seats them anyway, and puts the one who knows nobody next to someone kind.
