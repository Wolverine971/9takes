In the summer of 2018, between high school and college, the parties were at Josie Totah's house. It was her parents' house, which is why the host stayed sober: she was "so afraid to drink," she said on her podcast in April 2023, in case anyone ruined something there. She was 16 or 17, and she worked the room with props. "So I would just walk around with literally a fake prop movie cigarette and a glass of wine," she said, one "that I didn't drink from. Like Great Gatsby, while everyone was partying, getting drunk at my house." ([Dare We Say, April 6, 2023](https://crooked.com/podcast/drinking-with-christian-weissmann/))

Eight years later a castmate described her at work. Alison Thornton, who plays opposite her in Mike Flanagan's Carrie, said in November 2026, "Almost in every scene, Josie was improvising and adding things that nobody was expecting." ([Louisville Public Media, November 4, 2026](https://www.lpm.org/music/2026-11-04/alison-thornton-and-josie-totah-theres-a-lot-more-to-being-the-mean-girl-than-just-being-mean))

Totah is 25 as of November 2026, an actress, writer and producer who has worked since she was ten: Disney Channel's Jessie, Glee, NBC's Champions, then Lexi, the queen bee of Peacock's Saved by the Bell reboot, which she also produced, and Mabel on Apple TV+'s The Buccaneers. On October 7, 2026, Prime Video released Carrie, with Totah as Tina Blake, the bully's eager second.

The first scene has a plain explanation: a teenager guarding her parents' house. But the pairing recurs. From 2012 to 2026 her record shows someone outlandish wherever the work invites it and careful about two things: alcohol and being known. This profile reads the appetite as the engine and most of the care as circumstance. A Type 3 would show the same split, so the case is made elsewhere.

## What is Josie Totah's personality type?

Josie Totah's personality type is Enneagram Type 7, the Enthusiast. Sevens keep life lively and open and keep moving past pain. She says she began performing to make "a shocking scene," listed "the fun" among her job tests in 2024, and tells her hard stories as comedy. Confidence is medium. Type 3 is the closest rival; Type 6 is live.

[Type 7](/enneagram-corner/enneagram-type-7) is one of the three head types, with Five and Six, and fear is the emotion all three are built around. A [Six](/enneagram-corner/enneagram-type-6) looks for something solid to lean on: a trusted person, a rule, a plan for the worst case. A Seven moves on to the next plan before this one goes flat. The gifts are speed, humor and nerve. The cost is that hard feelings get outrun instead of felt.

Medium confidence is the honest ceiling. Nearly everything below is her own account, given in promotional interviews or on a podcast she hosted with two close friends. Research ran through November 10, 2026.

One limit matters more than the rest. Totah is a transgender woman, and being trans is not evidence of any personality type. She came out publicly in 2018, at 17, and much of what she has said since about hiding, fear and freedom describes what was done to a closeted child. None of that is counted here. What counts is what she chose when a choice cost something, and how she tells her own bad news.

## "Literally making a shocking scene"

Before there was a career there was a kitchen. "Whether it was in my kitchen pretending to fake fall or spit take or force my brother to stand at the top of the stairs and hold a flashlight pointed at me, I've never not acted," she told Wonderland in 2023. ([Wonderland, March 31, 2023](https://www.wonderlandmagazine.com/2023/03/31/josie-totah/))

In 2025 she gave the Harvard Crimson the motive. "I think initially, it was about entertaining and making a scene — not like making a written scene, but just literally making a shocking scene," she said. "I loved making people laugh." ([The Harvard Crimson, September 1, 2025](https://www.thecrimson.com/article/2025/9/1/josie-totah-buccaneers-jessie-mabel-honoria-queer-representation/)) The flashlight is also a spotlight, and a Three would have wanted that too.

She is the third child of Suheil and Christine Totah and grew up in Northern California. "My mom is Lebanese, my dad is Palestinian," she has said. She did stand-up at the Hollywood Improv at ten and moved with her mother to an apartment near Hollywood while her father and brother stayed north. "And the process is so fun. I love auditioning," the ten-year-old told her hometown paper. "You have to accept when you don't get it, that something else will come up," she said of rejection. ([Davis Enterprise, June 18, 2012](https://www.davisenterprise.com/news/j-j-totah-making-good-in-hollywood/article_ef19181d-7ef6-5d7f-826f-89cd3b01a443.html))

A ten-year-old in a proud local feature is weak evidence of adult motive. The vocabulary is already there, though: fun, the next thing, a shrug at rejection.

The same article holds the only criticism of her work this profile found as of November 2026. In 2012 she starred in AwesomenessTV's "Lil' Dictator," a web spoof of Sacha Baron Cohen's The Dictator, and the paper reported that some viewers called the videos racist. Her family answered that they are Arab-American and that it was a spoof. "It's their opinion," she said. She was ten, and the series was made and released by adults.

## "Jump on or jump off"

Through Disney Channel, Glee and Champions, the [Mindy Kaling](/personality-analysis/mindy-kaling) comedy in which she played a teenage boy, reporters and the public took her for a gay boy. On August 20, 2018, at 17, she published a first-person essay in Time saying she is a transgender woman. "I almost felt like I owed it to everybody to be that gay boy," she wrote. "But that has never been the way I think of myself." The assumption was other people's, the essay says, and she had only "halfway corrected people." ([Time, August 20, 2018](https://web.archive.org/web/20230810074045/https://time.com/5371387/josie-totah-transgender/))

At 14, the essay says, she was watching the docuseries I Am Jazz with her mother and told her, "This is me. I'm transgender. And I need to go through this." Her mother answered, "Okay, let's do it." Three days later she was at the pediatrician.

Who she is belongs to her. How she said it is fair to read, and she planned it.

The essay ran the week she left for college, after Champions had ended. "I just wanted to have my strings tied up and have a fresh start and experience my true self with a clean slate," she told Teen Vogue that fall. ([Teen Vogue via Yahoo, November 5, 2018](https://www.yahoo.com/lifestyle/21-under-21-josie-totah-124000605.html)) The tone she settled on was not a plea.

> "So, listen up y'all: You can jump on or jump off. Either way this is where I'm heading."

She has been plain about what the waiting cost. "I developed serious anxiety as I hid who I was," the essay says, and she told Teen Vogue she had been "really, really depressed." Those are the effects of a circumstance, one that ended, and they do not type her.

The years before the essay say nothing about her temperament. Its timing and tone were choices, though at 17 she is unlikely to have made them alone. A Three would stage a disclosure this carefully. The stance is what stands out: she announced a direction and left the audience to decide whether to come along.

## One condition

Tracey Wigfield, showrunner of the Saved by the Bell reboot, wrote its queen bee for her and told Variety in 2021 that Totah "just has comedy in her bones." ([Variety via Yahoo, June 3, 2021](https://www.yahoo.com/entertainment/josie-totah-shapes-path-trans-161556623.html)) That is praise for talent. It says nothing about motive.

When Wigfield's offer came, Totah was 18, a film major at Chapman University, shopping at Nordstrom with her grandmother when her agent called. She had one condition.

"I wasn't willing to do the show had I not been given a producer role," she told the Television Academy's emmy magazine. "So to pull me away from my education, which is really important to me, it had to be something that was worth it." ([Television Academy, 2021](https://www.televisionacademy.com/features/news/mix/worth-and-wisdom)) Every telling of this is hers. Variety reports only that Wigfield agreed.

She says the credit was not the point: "I didn't care about that." ([HuffPost UK, March 3, 2021](https://www.huffingtonpost.co.uk/entry/moxie-film-josie-totah-trans-actor-interview_uk_603cdd95c5b617a7e40f46a6)) A person who cared a great deal would say the same thing, and it can't be checked.

She kept writing on a job where no credit asked her to. On a Buccaneers night shoot, "I broke out my computer and started writing a scene," her character's coming-out scene. Two castmates told her to send it to the show's creator, Katherine Jakeways. "I was scared to do it because I have the utmost respect for Katherine and our writers," she said. So she phoned "in a roundabout way," with a cover story: "So I was journaling this morning and I had a few ideas for the scene…" By her account, "The majority of those ideas got integrated." ([Nylon, December 13, 2023](https://www.nylon.com/life/josie-totah-the-buccaneers-interview))

She cannot leave the material alone, and that night she got her way with a fib about journaling and an open laptop.

College is the nearest thing on record to a costly choice, and it is not a clean one. She stayed enrolled at Chapman while she starred in and produced the show, joined a sorority, and graduated in 2022. The commute, she told Wonderland, cost her "7 flat tires in four years." Professionally it cost nothing. She kept the lead, the credit and the degree.

Her reason for staying, given to Cero in 2025:

> "I love learning. I love being in class. But I also think as actors we have a mandate to kind of live our lives to the fullest extent."

([Cero Magazine, July 31, 2025](https://ceromagazine.com/articles/josie-totah-buccaneers-mabel-actor-interview))

That reason arrived three years after graduation, and there are plainer ones. Her mother's house rules, per a 2015 San Francisco Chronicle profile, were good grades and chores, so college may never have been optional. And having come out at 17, she felt behind. "I wish I had more of my teen years, you know?" she said the week she turned 21. Catching up on a delayed adolescence is circumstance, not temperament.

## Where the wildness goes

Back to the glass of wine. Its career reason is older than the parties. At about 14 she was invited to a birthday where there would be alcohol and told her mother. In Totah's retelling on that April 2023 episode, a paraphrase performed for laughs, her mother said that if it got out her career would end, and then: "So you can go ahead and do that shit, but you'll ruin your life. And I'll just sit here and watch it happen."

"I was like, holy fucking shit, I'm never having alcohol again."

"I've never really drank, to be honest," she said at 21, moments after saying she "always went to the bar with a fake I.D." in college. ([Dare We Say, August 11, 2022](https://crooked.com/podcast/were-crowning/))

So the restraint is narrow, and circumstance accounts for most of it: a host guarding her parents' house, a child actor told a drink could end her career. The Seven read neither needs it nor explains it. What stays open is why the caution outlasted its reasons, and that question belongs to Type 6, below.

She has said where the rest of it goes. "I think about the craziest stuff I'd want to do in my regular life and just do it on the show," she told Variety in 2021, about Lexi.

On The Buccaneers, Alisha Boe told her in a 2025 joint interview: "You'd crack a joke right before we have to film and you say the most outlandish stuff, and then you'll just lock in." ([Brit + Co, August 6, 2025](https://www.brit.co/the-buccaneers-interview-josh-dylan-alisha-boe-josie-totah-richard-conchita-mabel/))

Carrie tested the pattern, by her account. "I was told on day one, they told me, you know, Mike doesn't like Tina," she told Louisville Public Media, meaning Flanagan. "That didn't put me down. It put a fire under my butt, and I was like, 'I'm going to make him love Tina.'"

Her method, according to Thornton, was to add things. Thornton is the only witness to the improvising, in [two interviews](https://comicbookmovie.com/horror/carrie-interview-alison-thornton-josie-totah-on-toxic-relationship-and-iconic-shower-scene-exclusive-a230226), both with Totah beside her. Totah says Flanagan came around after the first rehearsal and "gave me so much to play with that he didn't need to." That is her report, and this profile found no statement from Flanagan about her.

Wanting to make a director love your character is approval-seeking, and winning him over with your best skill, in a way that enlarges your part, is what a Three would do too. The episode does not separate the two types.

## "So what can you give me?"

In August 2022 she launched a podcast, Dare We Say, with two friends: Alycia Pascual-Peña, her castmate in [Amy Poehler](/personality-analysis/amy-poehler)'s Moxie, and Yasmine Hamady, a sorority sister. She was turning 21 that week, and she opens the first episode wired. "The reason why I have this much energy is because I am on a burst prednisone pack," she says, and then explains how she got it.

She had woken up, in her words, "at my death," too sick to work, and ended up at her college health center being tested for everything. The verdict, as she tells it: "she said I was exhausted and depressed and she told me to go home."

> "I said, so what can you give me? And she was like, you just need to sleep."

([Dare We Say, August 11, 2022](https://crooked.com/podcast/were-crowning/))

By her telling she kept talking, about a steroid that had helped on an earlier shoot and about the drama she was about to film in Scotland (The Buccaneers), until the clinician prescribed one. "But you are also like trying to finesse this doctor," Pascual-Peña said. Totah delivered the punchline: "what did I want? I wanted drugs. When did I want it? I wanted it now."

It is a bit, the drugs are a prescribed steroid, and a Three with a shoot coming would refuse to rest too. The form is the evidence: by her telling, she was told she was depressed and should stop, she negotiated for a way to keep going, and the story became the first thing her new audience heard, as comedy.

Her mother's warning is a routine too, and the answer that closes this profile is a punchline. It is a pattern in how she tells stories on a comedy show. The counterweight is that she tells them at all: across her record she has reported anxiety, depression and fear in public.

The cost side came out in the podcast's last episode, on April 27, 2023, when she came back from a break in the recording. "I did go to the bathroom and feel like I wanted to cry," she said. "Only because I just feel like I'm so bad with being vulnerable." She went on, "I just want to be an actor that like, sits at home and no one knows anything about. But I have too loud of a mouth." ([Dare We Say, April 27, 2023](https://crooked.com/podcast/dare-we-say-goodbye/))

In October 2026 she told Interview that a short film she wrote and directed is "the most personal thing that I've ever done, which is really nice and cool because I don't usually open up in that way." ([Interview, October 26, 2026](https://www.interviewmagazine.com/culture/josie-totah-carrie-mike-flanagan-face-card)) Threes, Sevens and Eights all claim a fear of vulnerability, so it settles nothing about type. It does suggest what the jokes may be standing in front of.

## Why not Type 3?

The case for [Type 3](/enneagram-corner/enneagram-type-3), the Achiever, is strong enough that this typing is contested. A Three's worth runs through being seen to succeed, and the feeling underneath is shame at being seen as less.

In 2016, she told Wonderland, "my dad nearly forced me to attend the Sundance Film Festival for a film where I was literally in two scenes," and that weekend led, with the film's producer Naomi Scott, to an NBC sale. That October, at 15, she was attached as star and producer to an NBC comedy ([Deadline, October 31, 2016](https://deadline.com/2016/10/jj-totah-star-nbc-comedy-adam-naomi-scott-1201844962/)). At 18 she made a producer credit the price of a lead.

In the same written answers, in 2023, on being a trans woman in the industry, she sounds like a hustler: "I'm constantly texting and emailing people I look up to asking for coffee, lunch, or ways we can work together." "These are my cards and I'm still going to win," she wrote. She attached the win to other people in the next sentence ("Not for myself"), but the sentence stands.

From the age of ten, she says, her parents coached her for the ethnicity question in casting rooms ("never tell them you're Palestinian"), and until 16 or 17 her answer was "I'm Italian." A child actor from an Arab-American family had practical reasons to follow that script. The reason she gives is her own: "I think because I was afraid of my own identity being controversial." ([Dare We Say, March 23, 2023](https://crooked.com/podcast/kids-from-immigrant-families/))

Embarrassment is one of her most frequent words. "I think actors are the most embarrassing thing on planet Earth," she said in 2022. Called a frequent poster in 2026, she told Interview, "I'm embarrassed and ashamed." Of high school she said, "People were mean to me when I was a teenager, and I was definitely mean back," and that being on television "gave me a cachet that allowed me to survive." Shame about how one is seen is a Three's home ground, and a comic's self-mockery can manage an image as well as polish does.

A choice would settle it: a moment when advancement and enjoyment pulled apart and she gave one up. None is on record. At Chapman she kept both. So the difference has to be read from what she says she weighs, which is weaker evidence than what she does.

Her stated test for a job, in January 2024: "I examine the three main factors: the material, the people, and the fun." ([Content Mode, January 7, 2024](https://contentmode.com/josie-totah/)) By June 2025 the answer had career logic in it. "I weigh the director and material, but also where it might take me and who I might get to work with," she told Numéro Netherlands, adding, "I'm also such a believer in work begets work." ([Numéro Netherlands, June 25, 2025](https://www.numeronetherlands.com/in-conversation-with/in-conversation-with-josie-totah))

The later answer moves toward Three. What still leans Seven is small and specific. Fun is a test in 2024, and "the experience" is one of two factors in 2025. Her advice to her college self that year was to steer by "what you like and what you don't like," which is appetite, not a goal. And off the clock she spends on experience, not things. "I will pay a lot of money for a nice dinner," she said in 2026. "But the idea of owning another thing gives me insane anxiety."

In that 2024 answer she also said she had little choice ("I am lucky anytime a great audition or Zoom comes through my email"), so stated criteria may flatter necessity. The read would weaken if a director or showrunner described her tracking billing, reviews or status, or if her choices after Carrie visibly favored prestige over enjoyment.

## "For me, love is safety"

Type 6 is the other head type in play. A Six's motive is security: people, rules and plans solid enough to lean on.

She kept to her mother's worst-case warning for years, recalling it, she said, "at every instance." She tried the Buccaneers scene on two castmates and was scared to send it up. In 2024 she called her parents her "primary sounding board." She defined love for Galore that January as protection: "for me, love is safety. Being a safety net for another person unconditionally." In the same Q&A, asked what would surprise people, she said, "I have a fear of grocery stores. I can't go alone," adding that not wearing her glasses is part of it. ([Galore, January 11, 2024](https://galoremag.com/josie-totah-is-hollywoods-next-big-act/)) Nine months earlier she had told her podcast about going alone anyway: "a little risky for me," she said, and then, "It was so nice."

Someone who told Cero in 2025 she is "really down for anything" also describes a mundane errand as a risk, and a freedom motive does not predict that.

What separates Six from Seven is what a person does with fear: look for support and prepare, or reframe it and move to the next thing. She checks with her parents and her castmates. Then she acts, and the bad news becomes material. This profile leans Seven because the reframing turns up in more places over more years: the ten-year-old's "something else will come up," the clinic story, a disliked character heard as a dare. Six remains live, and some Sixes also walk straight at what scares them.

Type 8, whose motive is to stay in control of one's own life, has a claim too. She was "very frank with Tracey" about the producer condition ([The Hollywood Reporter, June 5, 2021](https://www.hollywoodreporter.com/tv/tv-news/saved-by-the-bell-bowen-yang-josie-totah-1234962271/)), and she told Galore, "I'm quite loud and don't take well to authority." But she kept her parents' audition answer for about six years and approached Jakeways sideways. Eight is unlikely here, not excluded.

Then there is who has not spoken. Apart from Wigfield's one sentence, no director or showrunner describes her in their own words. This profile found nothing from her parents after 2015, and nothing from her siblings, from anyone at her high school, or from anyone with a reason to be critical. That is the record as of November 10, 2026. The testimony that exists is from friends and castmates, in promotional interviews, mostly with her in the room.

## Music always has to be playing

Two weeks later, at the end of another episode, the hosts held a mock trial, with the show's producer as bailiff, on whether music should always be on. She ruled for music. "I cannot go, like, an hour of silence without music, because then I have to confront my own thoughts." Then she told a story about the first day of a horror-film class, when the professor asked everyone in the room what scared them most. It is a comic segment, and both lines were played for laughs. A transcript cannot say how much she meant them. ([Dare We Say, August 25, 2022](https://crooked.com/podcast/do-you-have-pretty-privilege/))

> "And I said myself. And then it was very awkward."

"And so music always has to be playing," she said. The transcript records a gavel. Then she announced she was taking the bailiff out to dinner.
