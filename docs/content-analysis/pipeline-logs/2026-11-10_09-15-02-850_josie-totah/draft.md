The summer after high school, the parties were at Josie Totah's house. She was the host, and by her own account she was acting. "I was so afraid to drink because I didn't want anyone to ruin anything," she said on her podcast in April 2023, so she worked the room with props. "So I would just walk around with literally a fake prop movie cigarette and a glass of wine," she said, one "that I didn't drink from. Like Great Gatsby, while everyone was partying, getting drunk at my house." ([Dare We Say, April 6, 2023](https://crooked.com/podcast/drinking-with-christian-weissmann/))

Set that beside what a castmate says. Alison Thornton, who plays opposite her in Mike Flanagan's Carrie, said in November 2026, "Almost in every scene, Josie was improvising and adding things that nobody was expecting." ([Louisville Public Media, November 4, 2026](https://www.lpm.org/music/2026-11-04/alison-thornton-and-josie-totah-theres-a-lot-more-to-being-the-mean-girl-than-just-being-mean))

Totah is 25 as of November 2026, an actress, writer and producer who has worked since she was ten: Disney Channel's Jessie, Glee, NBC's Champions, then Lexi, the queen bee of Peacock's Saved by the Bell reboot, which she also produced, and Mabel on Apple TV+'s The Buccaneers. On October 7, 2026, Prime Video released Carrie, with Totah as Tina Blake, the bully's eager second.

So the cast's improviser is also the one holding a glass she won't drink from. Fourteen years of interviews suggest where the appetite goes and what the control is for. The wildness goes into the work. The discipline protects the place where she gets to play.

## What is Josie Totah's personality type?

Josie Totah's personality type is Enneagram Type 7, the Enthusiast. Sevens are driven to keep life open, lively and enjoyable, and to keep moving past pain. Totah picks jobs for "the fun," mocks her own fame, and turns bad news into a bit. The read is held at medium confidence, with Type 3 the closest rival.

[Type 7](/enneagram-corner/enneagram-type-7) sits in the head triad, the three types organized around fear. A Seven's fear is of being trapped: in pain, in boredom, in a life with no exits. The answer is motion. A Seven keeps options open, finds the enjoyable angle on almost anything, and has the next plan ready before the current one goes flat. The gifts are real: speed, humor, nerve. The cost is that hard feelings get outrun instead of felt.

Her own version, offered in 2025 as advice to her college self:

> "It's okay to not know what you want to do. I think it's more important to know what you like and what you don't like. And I think if you can just follow that and be present with that, you're gonna be A-okay."

([The Harvard Crimson, September 1, 2025](https://www.thecrimson.com/article/2025/9/1/josie-totah-buccaneers-jessie-mabel-honoria-queer-representation/))

Medium confidence is the honest ceiling. Nearly everything below is her own account, given in promotional interviews or on a podcast she hosted with two close friends.

One limit matters more than the rest. Totah is a transgender woman, and being trans is not evidence of any personality type. When she talks about boxes, hiding and freedom, she is describing what was done to a closeted child, so none of that language is counted here. What counts is what she picks when a choice costs something, and what she does with bad news.

## "Literally making a shocking scene"

Before there was a career there was a kitchen.

"I've always acted," she told Wonderland in 2023. "Whether it was in my kitchen pretending to fake fall or spit take or force my brother to stand at the top of the stairs and hold a flashlight pointed at me, I've never not acted. It just so happened that when I ended up being able to monetize it, it became a career." ([Wonderland, March 31, 2023](https://www.wonderlandmagazine.com/2023/03/31/josie-totah/))

Two years later she gave the Harvard Crimson the motive in plainer terms. "I think initially, it was about entertaining and making a scene — not like making a written scene, but just literally making a shocking scene," she said. "I loved making people laugh." Neither account mentions being admired or being the best. The payoff she names is disruption and laughter.

She is the third child of Suheil and Christine Totah and grew up in Northern California. "My mom is Lebanese, my dad is Palestinian," she has said. She followed her sister Camille into a local musical theater company before kindergarten, did stand-up at the Hollywood Improv at ten, and moved with her mother to an apartment near Hollywood while her father and brother stayed north. Her hometown paper interviewed her that year. "And the process is so fun. I love auditioning," the ten-year-old said. "You have to accept when you don't get it, that something else will come up," she said of rejection. ([Davis Enterprise, June 18, 2012](https://www.davisenterprise.com/news/j-j-totah-making-good-in-hollywood/article_ef19181d-7ef6-5d7f-826f-89cd3b01a443.html))

The same article holds the only criticism of her work this profile found. In 2012 she starred in AwesomenessTV's "Lil' Dictator," a web spoof of Sacha Baron Cohen's The Dictator, and the paper reported that some viewers called the videos racist. Her family answered that they are Arab-American and that it was a spoof. "It's their opinion," she said. She was ten, and the series was made and released by adults.

A ten-year-old in a proud local feature is weak evidence of adult motive. The vocabulary is already there, though: fun, the next thing, a shrug at disapproval.

Her employers describe the same instinct from outside. "I wrote the character of Lexi for Josie Totah," showrunner Tracey Wigfield told Variety in 2021. "Josie is so deeply funny. She understands the rhythm of jokes and just has comedy in her bones." ([Variety via Yahoo, June 3, 2021](https://www.yahoo.com/entertainment/josie-totah-shapes-path-trans-161556623.html)) That is praise for talent. It says nothing about motive.

## Two answers she gave for years

From the age of ten, when a casting room asked her ethnicity, she had an answer ready, and it was false. Her parents, she says, told her "never tell them you're Palestinian." So she didn't. "They'd say what is your ethnicity and I would say I'm Italian. And so I didn't start telling people I was Palestinian until I was like until I was like 16, 17 years old, maybe." "I think because I was afraid of my own identity being controversial," she explained in 2023. ([Dare We Say, March 23, 2023](https://crooked.com/podcast/kids-from-immigrant-families/))

The second answer ran just as long. Through Disney Channel, Glee and Champions, the press and the public took her for a gay boy, and she let them. On August 20, 2018, at 17, she published a first-person essay in Time saying she is a transgender woman. "I almost felt like I owed it to everybody to be that gay boy," she wrote. "But that has never been the way I think of myself." ([Time, August 20, 2018](https://web.archive.org/web/2023/https://time.com/5371387/josie-totah-transgender/))

Who she is belongs to her. How she said it is fair to read, and she planned it.

She waited for Champions to end. "I didn't think it was right to come out during that when I was playing a male character," she told Teen Vogue that fall. "I just wanted to have my strings tied up and have a fresh start and experience my true self with a clean slate." ([Teen Vogue via Yahoo, November 5, 2018](https://www.yahoo.com/lifestyle/21-under-21-josie-totah-124000605.html)) The tone she settled on was not a plea.

> "So, listen up y'all: You can jump on or jump off. Either way this is where I'm heading."

She has been plain about what the waiting cost. "I developed serious anxiety as I hid who I was," the essay says, and she told Teen Vogue she had been "really, really depressed." Those are the effects of a circumstance, one that ended, and they do not type her.

Read coldly, two managed presentations kept up for years and retired on her own schedule look like image management, the signature of a Type 3. That reading gets a full hearing below. Two things temper it here. Both scripts were handed to a child by adults, with real safety and employment reasons behind them. And when she dropped the second, she announced a direction and left the audience to decide whether to come along.

## One condition

When Wigfield's offer came, Totah was 18, a film major at Chapman University, shopping at Nordstrom with her grandmother when her agent called. It was a lead role, written for her, on a title everyone knew. She had one condition.

"I wasn't willing to do the show had I not been given a producer role," she told the Television Academy's emmy magazine. "So to pull me away from my education, which is really important to me, it had to be something that was worth it." ([Television Academy, 2021](https://www.televisionacademy.com/features/news/mix/worth-and-wisdom)) Every telling of this is hers. Variety reports only that Wigfield agreed.

She says the title was not the point: "I didn't care about that." ([HuffPost UK, March 3, 2021](https://www.huffingtonpost.co.uk/entry/moxie-film-josie-totah-trans-actor-interview_uk_603cdd95c5b617a7e40f46a6)) A person who cared a great deal would say the same thing, and it can't be checked. Her account of where the time went is at least consistent. By season two she was in the writers' room weekly, where "I'll either be revising a script or breaking a new episode, which is just so much fun."

She kept doing it on a job where no title asked her to. On The Buccaneers she drank coffee by accident at 8 p.m. on a night shoot, and "I broke out my computer and started writing a scene." It was her character's coming-out scene. She tried it on two castmates first. Then, "in a roundabout way," she phoned the show's creator, Katherine Jakeways, with a cover story: "So I was journaling this morning and I had a few ideas for the scene…" By her account, "The majority of those ideas got integrated." ([Nylon, December 13, 2023](https://www.nylon.com/life/josie-totah-the-buccaneers-interview))

She cannot leave the material alone, and she gets her way with a fib about journaling and an open laptop.

The college decision cost more. She stayed enrolled at Chapman while she starred in and produced the show, joined a sorority, and graduated in 2022. Senior year, she told Wonderland, meant six courses packed into Tuesdays and Thursdays and filming on the other three weekdays. The commute cost her "7 flat tires in four years."

Career logic says drop out. Her reason for staying, given to Cero in 2025:

> "I love learning. I love being in class. But I also think as actors we have a mandate to kind of live our lives to the fullest extent."

"In order to have stuff to pull from, I have to make memories," she added. ([Cero Magazine, July 31, 2025](https://ceromagazine.com/articles/josie-totah-buccaneers-mabel-actor-interview))

That reason arrived three years after graduation, and there are plainer ones. Her mother's house rules, per a 2015 San Francisco Chronicle profile, were good grades and chores, so college may never have been optional. And in 2022, the week she turned 21, Totah gave a sadder version of wanting experiences. Having come out at 17, she felt behind: "I wish I had more of my teen years, you know?" Catching up on a delayed adolescence is circumstance, not temperament.

## Where the wildness goes

Back to the glass of wine. She traces the restraint to a conversation at about 14, when she was invited to a party where there would be alcohol and told her mother. In Totah's retelling on that April 2023 episode, a paraphrase performed for laughs, her mother said that if it got out her career would end, and then: "So you can go ahead and do that shit, but you'll ruin your life. And I'll just sit here and watch it happen."

"I was like, holy fucking shit, I'm never having alcohol again."

She gave a second reason in the same episode, and it has nothing to do with a career: "I always felt so outside of my body and I felt like drinking would take me so much further." "I've never really drank, to be honest," she said at 21. ([Dare We Say, August 11, 2022](https://crooked.com/podcast/were-crowning/))

She is the same way about objects. "I will pay a lot of money for a nice dinner," she told Interview in October 2026. "But the idea of owning another thing gives me insane anxiety." ([Interview, October 26, 2026](https://www.interviewmagazine.com/culture/josie-totah-carrie-mike-flanagan-face-card))

So the restraint has a shape. She refuses what could cost her the work or weigh her down, and she pays for experience. There are ordinary explanations too: a Disney-era career gave a 14-year-old real reasons to behave, and an actor who is "never really in one place" has reasons to own little.

She has said where the rest of it goes. "I think about the craziest stuff I'd want to do in my regular life and just do it on the show," she told Variety in 2021, about Lexi.

Castmates describe the switch. Alisha Boe, from The Buccaneers, told her in a joint interview: "you say the most outlandish stuff, and then you'll just lock in." ([Brit + Co, August 6, 2025](https://www.brit.co/the-buccaneers-interview-josh-dylan-alisha-boe-josie-totah-richard-conchita-mabel/))

Carrie tested the pattern. "I was told on day one, they told me, you know, Mike doesn't like Tina," she told Louisville Public Media. "That didn't put me down. It put a fire under my butt, and I was like, 'I'm going to make him love Tina.'"

Her method, according to Thornton, was to add things. In another interview Thornton said Totah "would just make us all laugh with her improv all the time, and just brought things that were not on the page." "So it wasn't allowed?" Totah cut in. ([Comic Book Movie, October 4, 2026](https://comicbookmovie.com/horror/carrie-interview-alison-thornton-josie-totah-on-toxic-relationship-and-iconic-shower-scene-exclusive-a230226)) Totah says Flanagan came around after the first rehearsal. That is her report, and this profile found no statement from Flanagan about her.

Wanting to make a director love your character is approval-seeking, and a Three would want it just as much. What points to Seven is the means. She took discouragement as an invitation and answered it with play.

## "So what can you give me?"

In August 2022 she launched a podcast, Dare We Say, with two friends: Alycia Pascual-Peña, her castmate in [Amy Poehler](/personality-analysis/amy-poehler)'s Moxie, and Yasmine Hamady, a sorority sister. She was turning 21 that week, and she opens the first episode wired. "The reason why I have this much energy is because I am on a burst prednisone pack," she says, and then explains how she got it.

Feeling awful, she had gone to her college health center and been tested for everything. The verdict, as she tells it: "she said I was exhausted and depressed and she told me to go home."

> "I said, so what can you give me? And she was like, you just need to sleep."

([Dare We Say, August 11, 2022](https://crooked.com/podcast/were-crowning/))

She kept talking, about the show she was about to shoot in Scotland, until the clinician offered a steroid. "But you are also like trying to finesse this doctor," Pascual-Peña said. Totah agreed and delivered the punchline: "what did I want? I wanted drugs. When did I want it? I wanted it now."

It is a bit, and she may be exaggerating. A Three with a shoot coming would refuse to rest too. Look at the form, though. Told she is depressed and should stop, she negotiates for a way to keep going, and the story becomes the first thing her new audience hears, as comedy.

The same reflex shows up as a principle. On Saved by the Bell, she told Variety in 2021: "you shouldn't only be talking about the struggles of trans people, because trans people live joyful lives and have the ability to live joyful lives." On The Buccaneers, she told the Crimson in 2025: "I wanted to make sure that it was a story of positivity and love, because a lot of queer stories can be traumatic." It is also a common conviction among queer writers her age, and she keeps a caveat: struggle "is important to tell to an extent."

The cost side came out in the podcast's last episode, on April 27, 2023. "I did go to the bathroom and feel like I wanted to cry," she said. "Only because I just feel like I'm so bad with being vulnerable." She went on, "I just want to be an actor that like, sits at home and no one knows anything about. But I have too loud of a mouth." ([Dare We Say, April 27, 2023](https://crooked.com/podcast/dare-we-say-goodbye/))

In October 2026 she told Interview she had written and directed a short film she has not yet introduced: "It's the most personal thing that I've ever done, which is really nice and cool because I don't usually open up in that way." Threes, Sevens and Eights all claim a fear of vulnerability, so it settles nothing about type. It does say what the jokes are standing in front of.

## Why not Type 3?

The case for [Type 3](/enneagram-corner/enneagram-type-3), the Achiever, is strong enough that this typing is contested. A Three's worth runs through achievement and a well-managed image, and her record supplies both.

At 15 she was attached as star and producer to an NBC comedy ([Deadline, October 31, 2016](https://deadline.com/2016/10/jj-totah-star-nbc-comedy-adam-naomi-scott-1201844962/)). At 18 she made a producer credit the price of a lead. In written answers to Wonderland in 2023 she sounds like a hustler. Asked who inspires her to succeed, she named her family, her community, and "the island I plan to buy by 30." "These are my cards and I'm still going to win," she wrote. She attached the win to other people in the next sentence ("Not for myself"), but the sentence stands.

Add the two answers she kept up for years, the staging of the essay, and her read on high school in 2026, where being on television protected her: "That gave me a cachet that allowed me to survive." A Three is easy to write from this.

The difference shows in what she picks when advancement and experience pull apart, and in what she does with status once she has it.

Her stated test for a job, in 2024: "I examine the three main factors: the material, the people, and the fun." "I'm no Viola Davis," she had just said, picturing Davis hanging scripts on a war-room wall. ([Content Mode, January 7, 2024](https://contentmode.com/?p=17793)) Her advice is to steer by "what you like and what you don't like," which is appetite, not a goal.

And she deflates her own standing where a Three would be expected to polish it. "I think actors are the most embarrassing thing on planet Earth," she said in 2022. "I don't think I was ever that big as a kid, or now," she said in 2026. Her dream roles, listed in one breath, are "a period war drama like 1917" and "a Big Mama's House remake." A wish list built for image would point somewhere. Hers has range.

The limits are real. She admits she has had little choice ("I am lucky anytime a great audition or Zoom comes through my email"), so stated criteria may flatter necessity. Self-deprecation is a comedian's working register. And every item but the castmates' testimony is self-report. The read would weaken if a director or showrunner described her tracking billing, reviews or status, or if her choices after Carrie visibly favored prestige over enjoyment.

Two other types have a claim. Type 8 has the producer ultimatum and "jump on or jump off." But what she reports feeling, again and again, is fear and embarrassment, and her method with people in charge is to pitch by fib. Type 6 has the anxieties described below and a tight, loyal circle. But no single emotion makes a type, and in fourteen years of interviews there is almost no second-guessing or worst-case planning. Discouragement gives her an appetite.

## What the Seven reading leaves out

She cannot go to a grocery store alone. "I have a fear of grocery stores. I can't go alone. I just get so overwhelmed and for some reason my social anxiety just comes out," she told Galore, adding that not wearing her glasses is part of it. ([Galore, January 11, 2024](https://galoremag.com/josie-totah-is-hollywoods-next-big-act/)) Someone who calls herself "really down for anything" will not run a mundane errand by herself, and a freedom motive does not predict that.

She is also the caretaker. "That's me, the mama. I'm very much the kind of person who will text the group chat and be like, 'I have a reservation on this day.' It's cute," she told Cero in 2025. She defined love for Galore as protection: "for me, love is safety. Being a safety net for another person unconditionally." Being alone is the state she has to practice: "I'm working on having more solo self-care days since I know those are important, too."

She wants to be the actor nobody knows anything about, and she co-hosted a confessional podcast and said in 2026, "I do have a relationship with social media, and it's not healthy."

Then there is who has not spoken. Apart from Wigfield's one sentence, no director or showrunner describes her in their own words. This profile found nothing from her parents after 2015, and nothing from her siblings, from anyone at her high school, or from anyone with a reason to be critical. The testimony that exists is from friends and castmates, in promotional interviews, mostly with her in the room.

## Music always has to be playing

Two weeks after the steroid story, she described a week on set when things had built up. She was "feeling insecure and, like, tired of being scared in this world," and also just exhausted. She kept going to the bathroom at work to cry and couldn't: "I knew I needed to get out, but I could not get it out." It came later, at home, in the shower, with Oasis playing. ([Dare We Say, August 25, 2022](https://crooked.com/podcast/do-you-have-pretty-privilege/))

At the end of that episode the hosts held a mock trial on whether music should always be on. She ruled for music. "I cannot go, like, an hour of silence without music, because then I have to confront my own thoughts." Then she told a story about the first day of a horror-film class, when the professor asked everyone in the room what scared them most.

> "And I said myself. And then it was very awkward."

"And so music always has to be playing," she said. The transcript records a gavel. Then she announced she was taking the bailiff out to dinner.
