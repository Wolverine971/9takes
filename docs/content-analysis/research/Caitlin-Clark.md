## 2. First-Person Quotations (subject)

### On what she is actually obsessed with — **strongest evidence in the file**

> "I'm obsessed with the work. And then you get in the game and then you're competitive with it because you've been so obsessed with the time that you've put in. I come in here every single day and I want to put in the work and get everything done because I want to be the best player in the world and I want to help this team win. **I don't think it's like an obsession with winning. I think that comes because you're obsessed with all the time you put in.**"
> — Caitlin Clark, ESPN's _NBA Today_ sit-down, 2026. Transcript: YouTube `arXekQ8W4_c`.

### The self-rating

> Interviewer: "On a scale of 1 to 10, 1 being still trying to find your footing and 10 being the best player that you can be in this league, where do you think you fall right now?"
> Clark: "That's a great question. Um, honestly, I think **I'm at a six, probably.** I've only played 60 games in this league and I've played two playoff games… I'm only 24. Like I still feel like I have so much room to improve."
> — ESPN's _NBA Today_, 2026. Transcript: `arXekQ8W4_c`.

### On being coached

> "I think I'm easy to coach and Steph would say the same thing. And I think at times maybe difficult too because I don't think there's many people that get guarded the way I do… what I love about her the most is her competitive fire is the same exact as mine. We'll go at it, but at the end of the day we love each other so much."
> — ESPN's _NBA Today_, 2026. Transcript: `arXekQ8W4_c`.

### The hotel room (2025 playoffs, watching)

> "I think the toughest moments for me were like the night before the games. You're in your hotel room and it's like you're on a road trip and it's like, 'Man, my team is gearing up to play in game five of the semifinals to maybe go to the finals and I know I don't get to put my uniform on.' Like that makes me emotional right now. Like that just ate at me. Like I hated that."
> — ESPN's _NBA Today_, 2026. Transcript: YouTube `hNBUneM3YqM`.

> "I try to approach every preseason game the way I'm going to approach the game on May 9th or the game on August 12th, whatever it is. They're all the same to me. Same amount of fire, same amount of wanting to win than the one before it or the one that will be next."
> — ESPN's _NBA Today_, 2026. Transcript: `hNBUneM3YqM`.

## 4. Accomplishments, Failures, Controversies, Counterevidence

**Accomplishments**

- 2024 WNBA Rookie of the Year (66 of 67 votes); rookie records for single-season assists and rookie scoring.
- Fastest player in WNBA history to 500 career assists (59 games; prior mark Sue Bird, 82 games).
- Fastest to 200 career three-pointers (74 games; prior mark Katie Smith, 81 games).
- July 17, 2026: 45 points, 10 assists, 4 steals, 2 blocks on 11-of-18 shooting (6-of-10 from three). First 40-point/10-assist game in WNBA history.
- Leading All-Star vote-getter, 2026.

**Failures and counterevidence to any "flawless" reading**

- **223 turnovers in 2024 — the most in a single WNBA season in league history, shattering a record of 137.** This is the central factual counterweight to the perfectionism narrative and must be engaged, not buried.
- 2025: played 13 of 41 games. Three separate soft-tissue and bone injuries.
- Rookie-year shooting efficiency was mediocre by elite standards; Swoopes' critique was not baseless.

**Controversies**

- Persistent culture-war proxy status: race, audience growth, media coverage volume, officiating.
- Rookie-season flashpoints with veterans; the "is she targeted?" discourse. Clark herself said in December 2024: "I don't think I was being targeted."
- Recurring accusation that she is entitled to calls and visibly petulant with officials and teammates.

## 6. Type Hypothesis (labeled hypothesis — not a verdict)

**Leading hypothesis: Enneagram Type 1, wing 2 (1w2).**

Supporting evidence:

- Named a perfectionist independently by two head coaches (Bluder, White) using near-identical language.
- Anger directed first at herself, then leaking outward at teammates, coaches, and officials — to the point of being removed from her own practice.
- Needed a sports psychologist to instruct her to smile, indicating her default expression was reading as judgment to others.
- "I care more than all of you" is the Type 1 sentence almost verbatim (the 9takes Type 1 pillar renders the same thought as "Why am I the only one who cares?").
- Explicitly locates her obsession in the work rather than the win — the standard is about correctness of effort, not outcome.
- Treats her platform as a debt owed to the league's history rather than as personal validation.
- Every game weighted identically. Preseason equals August. Standards do not flex for context.

**Strongest alternative: Type 3.** Marketing major; enormous endorsement portfolio; record-chasing; comfort with spotlight. Real, and not fully explained by Type 1.

**Named tiebreaker:** the ESPN "obsessed with the work, not with winning" quote, paired with Bluder having to eject her from practice. A Three optimizes for the visible result and manages the room to protect the image; Clark disclaims the result as the driver and burned room-management capital holding teammates to her standard.

**What the type does not explain:** her ease inside the commercial machine and her fluency with self-promotion. Best available reconciliation is that she frames the platform as service to the league rather than as personal reward, but this remains the softest seam in the typing.

**Disconfirming evidence to keep visible:** the 223-turnover season. A conventional perfectionism read predicts risk-aversion; Clark's game is the highest-variance style in the league. The typing only holds if the standard is understood as _correctness of read_, not _avoidance of error_.

**Confidence: medium-high.**

- The Letterman quotes are sourced from secondary write-ups; the Netflix episode has no fetchable transcript. Quotes are consistent across at least three independent outlets, but not primary-verified.
- The ESPN sit-down transcripts are auto-generated captions. Wording is reliable at the sentence level; punctuation is inferred.
- The interviewer in `arXekQ8W4_c` / `hNBUneM3YqM` is not named on-transcript. Attributed as ESPN's _NBA Today_ rather than to a named journalist. The separate "false characterization" quotes are firmly attributed to Malika Andrews (July 2026).
- No direct quotes located from Clark's brothers Blake or Colin in primary sources; the ESPN+ documentary reportedly contains them.
- No teammate testimony located that is substantive rather than congratulatory (Boston and Martin quotes found were social-media reactions and do not qualify as analytical testimony).
- Bluder's "Caitlin is a perfectionist" quote appears across multiple outlets without a single consistent primary venue and date. Treat the venue as unconfirmed.
