## First-person quotations (book-sourced — VERIFIED 2026-08-13 by research-analyst agent against full MDR text)

All grep-verified against Memories, Dreams, Reflections (Winston & Winston trans.); cite by chapter.

- Prologue: "My life is a story of the self-realization of the unconscious."
- The manikin (First Years, ~age 10): "At the end of this ruler I now carved a little manikin, about two inches long, with frock coat, top hat, and shiny black boots. I colored him black with ink, sawed him off the ruler, and put him in the pencil case, where I made him a little bed." Hidden on a beam in the forbidden attic with a painted Rhine stone; fed with tiny scrolls "in a secret language of my own invention"; forgotten until age 35. "This possession of a secret had a very powerful formative influence on my character; I consider it the essential factor of my boyhood." "The episode with the carved manikin formed the climax and the conclusion of my childhood."
- The fainting neurosis (School Years, age 12): father overheard: "I have lost what little I had, and what will become of the boy if he cannot earn his own living?" Jung: "I was thunderstruck. This was the collision with reality. 'Why, then, I must get to work!'" The self-cure over Latin grammar: "'Devil take it, I'm not going to faint,' I told myself." "The whole bag of tricks was over and done with! That was when I learned what a neurosis is." "I saw clearly that I myself had arranged this whole disgraceful situation." Study habit after: "Regularly I would get up at five o'clock in order to study."
- The mist (School Years, ~11): "Suddenly for a single moment I had the overwhelming impression of having just emerged from a dense cloud. I knew all at once: now I am myself!... Previously I had existed, too, but everything had merely happened to me. Now I happened to myself."
- Two personalities (School Years): "Somewhere deep in the background I always knew that I was two persons." "The play and counterplay between personalities No. 1 and No. 2, which has run through my whole life, has nothing to do with a 'split' or dissociation in the ordinary medical sense."
- Mother (MDR): "There was an enormous difference between my mother's two personalities. That was why as a child I often had anxiety dreams about her. By day she was a loving mother, but at night she seemed uncanny... Archaic and ruthless; ruthless as truth and nature."
- Freud first meeting (Sigmund Freud chapter): "We met at one o'clock in the afternoon and talked virtually without a pause for thirteen hours. Freud was the first man of real importance I had encountered."
- Freud faints (MDR, both incidents PRIMARY): Bremen Aug 1909; Munich Nov 1912 ("At that moment Freud slid off his chair in a faint... he looked at me as if I were his father").
- Post-break (Confrontation with the Unconscious): "After the parting of the ways with Freud, a period of inner uncertainty began for me. It would be no exaggeration to call it a state of disorientation. I felt totally suspended in mid-air, for I had not yet found my own footing."
- Oct 1913 vision: "I saw the mighty yellow waves, the floating rubble of civilization, and the drowned bodies of uncounted thousands. Then the whole sea turned to blood." "I drew the conclusion that they had to do with me myself, and decided that I was menaced by a psychosis. The idea of war did not occur to me at all."
- Red Book years: "The years when I was pursuing my inner images were the most important in my life — in them everything essential was decided."
- Bollingen (The Tower): "At Bollingen I am in the midst of my true life, I am most deeply myself." "I have done without electricity, and tend the fireplace and stove myself... These simple acts make man simple; and how difficult it is to be simple!" "I had started the first tower in 1923, two months after the death of my mother." The 1950 cube stone: "No, that is my stone, I must have it!"
- Retrospect (loneliness — use THIS wording, not the internet paraphrase): "As a child I felt myself to be alone, and I am still, because I know things and must hint at things which others apparently know nothing of, and for the most part do not want to know."
- Letters: "Who looks outside, dreams; who looks inside, awakes." (to Fanny Bowditch, 22 Oct 1916, Letters Vol. 1 — GENUINE despite fake-quote reputation.) To Freud 18 Dec 1912: "You go around sniffing out all the symptomatic actions in your vicinity... Meanwhile you remain on top as the father, sitting pretty."
- BBC follow-up (The Listener, 21 Jan 1960): "I did not say in the broadcast, 'There is a God.' I said 'I do not need to believe in God; I know.'"

## Preliminary hypotheses (labeled as hypotheses)

- **Working type hypothesis: Enneagram 5 (wing 4)** — withdrawal as baseline (loneliness quote, tower, vault, private notebooks), knowledge-as-safety ("I know" epistemology), difficulty with feeling by his own account, the fortress objects (manikin → tower → vault as one lifelong gesture), competence-armor (self-cured neurosis by forcing study).
- **Strongest alternative: 9w1 (Riso-Hudson)** — mediator epistemology (holding opposites, transcendent function), "both sides" temperament, long deferral to Freud before rupture, buried anger surfacing explosively (the seven-boys fight), nature-merger at Bollingen, "peace of mind through wholeness" language.
- **Disconfirming evidence for 5:** he was no minimalist recluse — large household, decades of daily clinical practice, love affairs, public lecturing; gut-level physicality (stone carving, sailing, the fight).
- **Disconfirming evidence for 9:** no self-forgetting — ferocious self-assertion in the Freud break; his conflict was principled and pursued, not avoided; energy management and privacy hoarding look nothing like merging.
