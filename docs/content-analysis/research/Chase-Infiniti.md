### Counterevidence / skeptical readings

- **The "carried by the room" case.** A debut performance directed by Paul Thomas Anderson, opposite DiCaprio, Sean Penn, Regina Hall, Benicio del Toro and Teyana Taylor. The skeptical read is that the performance is a product of casting and direction. She concedes adjacent ground herself: "I know that I've had a privileged entrance into Hollywood."
- **Bruce Miller's unpredictability quote cuts against a pure composure/control read.** He describes not knowing whether she'd go left or right. That is not the description of a performer optimizing for the safest legible choice.
- **She names her own nerves on the record**, repeatedly and unprompted ("I remember being very nervous that day"; the scaredy-cat framing). Image-managing performers usually edit that out. This is real counterevidence to a naive Type 3 read and should be engaged, not ignored.
- **Very short public record.** Years active: 2023–present. Two lead roles. There is no long arc to test claims against, no documented failure, no controversy. Any typing is a reading of a three-year window.
- **No adversarial press.** No hostile interviews, no scandal, no public conflict located. The "critic pressure" available is the Oscar omission and the structural skepticism about newcomers, not a named detractor.

## 6. Preliminary Type Hypothesis (labeled hypothesis, not fact)

### Leading hypothesis: **Type 3, the Achiever. Wing 2. Social-dominant instinct.**

**Strongest case for Type 3**

- Her stated mantra is a competence affirmation, not a safety scan: "I'm capable of doing everything. I'm not here by accident."
- She converted a six-month unpaid audition limbo into a curriculum: "I treated every single callback and chemistry read like a master class... be a sponge." A person in that limbo describing the syllabus rather than the uncertainty is orienting toward advancement.
- Her standard for competence is legibility, stated outright: "I'm at a point now where it's convincing on camera." Not _learned_. Convincing.
- She cannot land on her own work: "For me personally, it's hard for me to say I'm happy with what I did."
- She manages the frame around her own arrival: "I know that I've had a privileged entrance into Hollywood." Naming the critique first is how you keep it from being made about you.
- Corroborated composure under acute pressure: Kulukundis's razor account — shaking underneath, no visible beat missed.
- Her name is an assignment she has explicitly internalized and restated as a personal creed: "chasing greatness."

**Strongest case against / strongest alternative: Type 6, the Loyalist**

- She narrates herself through mentors and authorities constantly: Gyllenhaal taught her, Negga told her not to be scared, Anderson and DiCaprio were "leaders and guides," Moss is "an encyclopedia."
- "I shadowed anyone who would let me watch."
- Self-described scaredy-cat; covers her eyes at horror films; "I try to remember to breathe."
- Warm, protective ensemble loyalty: "I wanted to help the other girls whenever I could."

A secondary alternative is **Type 9** (adaptive, accommodating, belonging-oriented) and a distant **Type 4** (the biracial "missing a part of yourself" language). Both are weaker: she is too explicitly driven for 9 and too oriented to being _legible_ rather than _distinct_ for 4.

**Named tiebreaker**
The razor screen test, read against her own mantra. Handed a razor by a director she had been auditioning for for months and told to shave Leonardo DiCaprio's face, she delivered a take. Kulukundis: "I'm sure she was shaking, but she held it all in and didn't look like she missed a beat." A Six under that pressure typically shows the anxiety or reaches for the authority's reassurance; the anxiety is the material. She produced a surface instead, and the sentence she uses to steady herself is about capability ("I'm not here by accident"), not about backup. The Six markers are real, but they read better as a Three's integration line to Six — loyalty, mentorship, ensemble care — than as a Six core.

**One important behavior the type does not explain**
She names her fear on the record, repeatedly and unprompted, including in the same interviews where she is otherwise polished. Threes usually delete the shaking from the story. Bruce Miller's "I didn't know if she was going to go left or right" points the same direction: something in her working process is genuinely uncontrolled. That is unresolved.

**Confidence: medium.** The type call is well-supported inside a three-year public record with two roles, no failures, and no adversarial press. It should be revisited after a documented setback.
