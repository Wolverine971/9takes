Test this across sources; don't assume it.

Altman disputes the confrontation. Use it to corroborate, not to anchor, and attribute it carefully. In about 20 hours of her own speech she shows no first-person anger at anyone. - **The Two material is real; place it.** Her warmth, the "classic do-gooder" identity and her people-first management run consistently from 2022 to 2026. Read them as the 2 wing plus a people-and-operations job that rewards them. Show them on the page; don't hide them to make the case cleaner.

- **Format:** standard page, not an open case. The record is now deep: 15+ long-form first-person sources.

## Leads for Type 1

1. **The Altman "coup" confrontation.** Altman claimed "good authority" that the siblings were plotting a coup. Daniela "lost it," per Dario's notes, called in the executive he'd named, who denied it, and corrected Altman on the spot ("You just said that"). The WSJ version: "prompting the Amodeis to begin shouting angrily at him." Sources: New Yorker 2026-04-13 (Dario's notes plus one person briefed); WSJ 2026-03-28 (unnamed). Altman: "not quite his recollection."
2. **A position over a friendship.** She kept Brockman off the language project and "offered to step down as head of the project rather than allow Brockman on it." Brockman had recruited her and gave a toast at her 2017 wedding. Sources: WSJ 2026-03-28; The Atlantic 2026-09-29 (unnamed).
3. **Correction, not repair.** Brockman's 2020 peer review accused her, in the **WSJ's paraphrase, not a Brockman quote**, of using "bureaucratic processes" to get her way. She "delivered a long response, rebutting him point by point." Source: WSJ (reported).
4. **Moral judgment of a rival that Dario softens.** Isaacson 34:17 (above).
5. **Costly choices justified by what's right.** Pentagon: "the co-founders felt like it was just the right thing to do… These are the right red lines." No ads, against investors' "quiet forces": "sorry, that's just not how we're going to do things. We'll find another way." Source: Oprah podcast 2026-05-19, ~32:29 and ~35:42.
6. **Rules she applies to herself.** Peer feedback: "I made everyone go do that and they had to Mark in the spreadsheet." Board reviews: "that includes me and dar." Going behind colleagues' backs: "We have no tolerance for that." Challenged on a "culture interview that we have perfected" ("Perfected?"), she answered "Yes, yes." Sources: Kleiner Perkins *Grit* ~91:00 and ~94:03; Sixth Street 2026-02-19 ~30:10 and ~34:06.
7. **She hires for principled stands.** Values that don't create tension are "probably too bland." "You need to be able to back up any value with 10 examples." She asks candidates how they defended beliefs "in kind of uncomfortable situations… because you felt it was the right thing to do." Source: Vanta 2025-06-13, ~08:19 and ~13:48.

## Strongest alternative: Type 2

**Her own words:**
- "I'm a supportive manager I really want people to succeed" (Stanford eCorner 2024; GSB 2026)
- "a classic do-gooder" (Isaacson; FLI 2022)
- "I'm an expert in people" (Kleiner)
- "People love people. People are going to want to report to me" (GSB). This is a joke she frames as a bias she's "extremely guilty of," so don't let it carry weight.

**Standing beside the star, and tending the bond:**
- She went into global health to "understand the things that he was working or interested in" (FLI).
- "The smartest person that I know is Dario" (co-founders video, probable).
- She wants to "water that relationship" (GSB); "Dario comes over, he plays with my kids" (Oprah).

**Reputation:** she was seen at OpenAI as "her brother's high‑EQ counterpart" (Atlantic).

**Why it loses:**
- Under stakes she moves toward the standard, even against a friend.
- Users appear only as the people the standard serves: "We want the product to be good… we're standing up for our customers" (Code with Claude 2026-05-06).
- She rejects being indispensable: "my number one goal is getting myself out of that job" (Kleiner ~85:09).
- She wants Claude to have "professional warmth… approachable, but distant" (*The Circuit*, probable).
- The Two material sits in self-description and job description. The One material sits in behavior.

**Other types:**
- **9:** she won't arbitrate; she and Dario "meet in the middle" "80 to 90% of the time"; she never names OpenAI. It fails on the friend-blocking and the point-by-point rebuttal.
- **6:** "scary because we can lose it at any time… Are we accidentally making things worse?" (Atlantic); "what I want is no surprises" (Kleiner). Her fear centers on being wrong, not on losing security, and she acts decisively.

## Evidence against Type 1 (show it, don't bury it)

- Asked "what's the thing that triggers you" (Kleiner), she names nothing. She skipped Arianna Huffington's question about mistakes.
- She's light about her own mistakes: "we messed up multiple times" (payroll), a "70% hit rate," "I need an adult."
- Pluralism (Isaacson 10:28).
- She retired a company value because "if not everyone loves it… people are not going to use it" (Vanta).
- She left Capitol Hill because it was "slow and bureaucratic."
- She's diplomatic about leaving OpenAI: "running towards something versus running away," said at four or more venues.
- "Right thing" and "right way" are house vocabulary; Dario says them too, so discount them.

**What would change the call:**
- **Toward 2:** her own account of the OpenAI fights framed as protecting Dario or her team.
- **Toward 9:** the WSJ / New Yorker episodes contradicted.
- **Toward high confidence on 1:** a named colleague describing her reaction when someone cuts a corner.
