## Timeline (facts relevant to the thesis)

- **1946-09-05** — Born Farrokh Bulsara, Stone Town, Zanzibar, to Parsi-Indian parents Bomi and Jer Bulsara.
- **1954/55 (age 8)** — Sent to St Peter's boarding school, Panchgani, India. Freestone (2025 interview): "He left home at 8 years old and went back at 17, and I think he saw his parents maybe once"; school friends began calling him Freddie; he kept the name.
- **1964** — Family flees the Zanzibar Revolution to Feltham, Middlesex, England.
- **1969–70** — Meets Mary Austin around the time Smile becomes Queen; they live together ~7 years; engaged.
- **1970–71** — Renames the band Queen; adopts the surname Mercury; designs the Queen crest himself (Ealing Art College graphic-design training).
- **1975** — "Bohemian Rhapsody"; nine consecutive weeks at UK #1.
- **1976** — Tells Mary Austin he is bisexual (her account); relationship converts to lifelong friendship; he keeps her on payroll/nearby for life.
- **1979** — Dave Marsh in Rolling Stone (Jazz review) calls Queen "the first truly fascist rock band" (critic-pressure anchor). NME had earlier (1977) run the Tony Stewart interview headlined "Is this man a prat?"
- **1979–85** — Munich years: lives around clubs, Barbara Valentin, large entourage, heavy spending/gift-giving.
- **1985-07-13** — Live Aid, Wembley (72,000 in stadium): 21-minute Queen set, widely voted/cited since as the greatest live performance in rock.
- **1985** — David Wigg interview, Munich (transcript on file): mirrors quote, scars quote, hides-behind-work quote, Mary-only-friend quote.
- **1986** — Jim Hutton gives Freddie a plain gold band for his birthday; they wear matching rings (Hutton, Mercury and Me).
- **1987** — Records "The Great Pretender" cover; Rudi Dolezal films his final on-camera interview ("all this is a pretence"). Hutton dates the AIDS diagnosis to spring 1987 (reported, never publicly confirmed by Mercury).
- **1987–88** — Barcelona with Montserrat Caballé.
- **1990** — Records "The Show Must Go On" vocal while too weak to stand (May's account).
- **1991-11-23** — Public statement confirming AIDS, released ~24h before death.
- **1991-11-24** — Dies at Garden Lodge, age 45, of bronchopneumonia resulting from AIDS. Mary Austin at bedside.
- **1991-12-25** — Elton John receives the Henry Scott Tuke painting Freddie arranged before dying, note signed "Melina."
- **1992-04-20** — Freddie Mercury Tribute Concert, Wembley (Bowie quote).
- **~1993–94** — Mary Austin secretly places his ashes per his instruction; location never disclosed.
- **2018-12** — "Bohemian Rhapsody" certified most-streamed song from the 20th century (1.6B streams, Universal Music Group / Guinness World Records).
- **2026-09-05** — 80th birthday; estate's "The Great Pretender's Gala" at Casino Barrière Montreux; Freddie Days festival Sept 2–6 (freddiemercury.com, Jan 2026; ABC News syndication).

## Named third-party testimony

1. **Brian May** (recounting the 1990 "The Show Must Go On" session, MusicRadar/Total Guitar): "I said, 'Fred, I don't know if this is going to be possible to sing.' And he went, 'I'll fucking do it, darling' — vodka down — and went in and killed it."
2. **Elton John** (Love Is the Cure, 2012): "Here was this beautiful man, dying from AIDS, and in his final days, he had somehow managed to find me a lovely Christmas present." Note read "Dear Sharon, I thought you'd like this. Love, Melina." (their drag names)
3. **Mary Austin** (OK! magazine, 2000): "When he died I felt we'd had a marriage. We'd done it for better or worse, for richer for poorer, in sickness and in health." Also relays Freddie: "I've left you the house because you would have been the woman I would have married."
4. **Jim Hutton** (Mercury and Me, 1994): "No matter how ill Freddie felt, he never grumbled to anyone or sought sympathy of any kind. It was his battle, no one else's, and he always wore a brave face." Reply to the diagnosis: "Don't be stupid. I'm not going anywhere." Also: Freddie "treated cats like his own children," phoned home from tour to talk to them.
5. **Peter Freestone** ("Freddie and his privacy," his YouTube channel, 2021): "Freddie never wanted anything to take away from the image of Queen... he was 25% of Queen, that's what he was proud of." "For Freddie, sex never entered into his music, his work. That was his private life." "He just cared more about other people than he did himself in the end."
6. **Peter Freestone** (dopeYEAH talk interview, 2025): "He left home at 8 years old and went back at 17, and I think he saw his parents maybe once." "People get the idea that Freddie was all lonely and sad and depressed... but Freddie loved laughing. People ask me my favorite memory of Freddie... it's just watching him at home with a group of friends laughing." Also: took Mary to every lunch with his mother; kept family away from Garden Lodge and shows; "he became Freddie at school... never went back to Farrokh." On teeth (widely quoted elsewhere): "He always covered his teeth with his top lip or raised his hand to cover them."
7. **David Bowie** (1992 Tribute Concert): "Of all the more theatrical rock performers, Freddie took it further than the rest... he took it over the edge... he was definitely a man who could hold an audience in the palm of his hand."

## Accomplishments / failures / controversies / counterevidence

- **Accomplishments:** "Bohemian Rhapsody" (9 weeks UK #1 1975–76; most-streamed 20th-century song, 1.6B streams, UMG/Guinness Dec 2018); Live Aid 1985; Barcelona with Caballé; wrote in one band for 20+ years.
- **Failures/flops:** Mr. Bad Guy (1985 solo) underperformed; Hot Space backlash; late-70s/early-80s critical savaging (Marsh "the first truly fascist rock band," Rolling Stone 1979; NME "Is this man a prat?" 1977).
- **Controversies:** the 1991 near-total public silence about AIDS drew criticism from some AIDS activists/commentators after his death (he announced it only ~24h before dying); Munich-era excess; Sun Cit... (did not find Queen Sun City well-sourced enough to use — Queen played Sun City 1984 and were criticized for breaking the apartheid cultural boycott; solid, usable if needed).
- **Counterevidence to the "sad lonely Freddie" frame:** Freestone 2025 (laughing at home, hated the mopey myth); the Wigg transcript is full of jokes; band testimony about his humor. Any draft must carry this pressure, not flatten him into a tragedy.

## Hypotheses (interpretation — not fact)

- **Preliminary type: Enneagram 4, wing 3 (sexual/intimate subtype).** Withdrawal when hurt ("you do shut yourself off"), envy of ordinary belonging ("you're normal underneath"), identity as a constructed artwork (Farrokh → Freddie → Mercury, self-designed crest), romantic idealization and serial disappointment, intensity-seeking, persona explicitly held at distance ("all this is a pretence").
- **Strongest alternative: 3w4.** The showmanship, image discipline, productivity, and "smile stays on" stoicism. Weakened by: he protected the private self _from_ the audience rather than fusing with the image; hurt produced withdrawal (Garden Lodge, cats, tight circle), not harder performing; he framed the persona as a pretence blocking love, where a 3 seeks love through the image.
- **Second alternative: 7w6.** Munich hedonism, "every day is Fasching." Weakened by: he articulated and marinated in the pain (scars quote, the loneliness ballads); 7s reframe away from it. The hedonism reads as intensity-seeking anaesthetic, and it ended in withdrawal, not reframing.
- **Named tiebreaker:** what he did when hurt. Hurt 3s perform harder; hurt 7s reframe and move; hurt 4s withdraw and aestheticize. Mercury withdrew (post-Munich retreat to Garden Lodge, the guarded circle) and aestheticized (the loneliness catalog, The Great Pretender as self-commentary).
- **What the type does NOT explain:** 20+ years of pragmatic band loyalty and business discipline ("why kill the goose that laid the golden egg," Wigg 1985) — uncharacteristic of textbook 4 volatility; likely w3 pragmatism, noted as a genuine anomaly.
- **Stress/security mapping (hypothesis):** stress → 2-ish clinging generosity (Munich gift-buying, entourage, "I buy a lot of things for a lot of people; most of the time I get them back a few years later," Wigg 1985); growth → 1-ish discipline (Caballé collaboration's classical rigor, the methodical final-year vocal stockpiling, the dignity of the last statement).
- **Confidence: high** (multiple independent intimate witnesses converge on the same private pattern; the subject repeatedly self-reports the mechanism).
