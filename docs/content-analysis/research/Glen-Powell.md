## Working psychological hypothesis (interpretation, not fact)

Central question: how does someone who remembers being professionally expendable decide which opportunities to refuse once people finally want him?

Working tension: deliberately managing a long career while fearing that any pause could end it. The scarcity decision is a better anchor than interpreting fictional disguises as his psychological identity. Type 3 remains a medium-confidence reading: achievement and external reception are recurrent considerations, but ordinary economic precarity, professional competence, and pleasure in filmmaking explain substantial portions of the same behavior.

Strongest alternative: Type 7, given enthusiasm and appetite for experience; Type 2 is also plausible from reported people-pleasing. The tiebreaker to examine is his explicit discussion of self-presentation, the struggling actor who remains inside, and fear-driven career choices in S01. Do not invent the proposition that love in his family was conditional on success. Sources instead describe family support. Do not declare a wing or instinct stack established, and do not describe returning to Austin as pathological withdrawal. Public interviews cannot establish a stress arrow. His family attachment and craft interests exceed what a career-status explanation alone can explain.

### S01 — Interview Magazine, Matthew McConaughey interviewing Powell

- URL: https://www.interviewmagazine.com/film/glen-powell-and-matthew-mcconaughey-tell-the-truth-about-movie-stardom
- Publication: 2024-06-10 (independently verified on page); conversation explicitly dated 2024-03-13. Primary, edited written Q&A; full text available. Web summary budget: 200 words.
- Powell recalls approximately $35,000 for *Hidden Figures*, his only movie that year, and refusing available guest roles while broke to alter his career prospects. This is his retrospective estimate, not an audited salary or proof that he had no other income. Exact selected fragment: “take supply out of it and hope demand would follow.”
- Later in the same exchange he describes the lingering struggling-actor identity and “fear-based decisions.” He also discusses disappointing acquaintances when protecting time for close family/friends.
- Named witness option: McConaughey, “That was a very proactive move.” Verified surrounding exchange: he is praising Powell creating *Hit Man* rather than relying on available acting offers. Do not substitute general advice addressed to actors for testimony about Powell.
- Editorial use: selective refusal with a concrete cost; preserve uncertainty about whether the gamble caused his success.

## Pre-edit claim audit and cut instructions

- **Opening Linklater recognition scene:** attribution not verified and conflicts with the located father/assistant anecdote in S03. Replace the hook or report the correct anecdote with attribution. Do not merge these into an invented scene.
- **Denzel episode:** S03 supports the core witnessed successful-take anecdote. Old draft's precise ages, exact set dialogue, and premiere-versus-set introduction timing need consolidation. An October 1988 birth means he was 18 during most 2007 filming, not automatically 17.
- **Jurassic Park age:** an October 1988 birth makes him four in summer 1993. Remove the old age-five calculation or verify what viewing date he actually described.
- **Spy Kids age:** sources vary between 13 during filming and 14 at release; use early teens or film year until filming dates are established.
- **Parents in every movie:** unsupported absolute; use recurring cameos/set visits. Do not build subtype inference on this absolute.
- **Notebook:** retain only the limited fact in S14. No evidence that entries exclude script ideas or record every collaboration.
- **Boot flask:** S02 supports behavior. It does not establish shame, deception, or exact overlap with the *Hidden Figures* refusal period.
- **Scarcity:** S01 supports planned refusal, not proof he singlehandedly engineered demand or uniquely differed from all other struggling actors.
- **Rom-com resurrection:** do not credit one performer with reviving an entire genre or infer sole marketing authorship. Draft's box-office statistics and quoted strategy require original sourcing if retained.
- **Fictional identity-switching:** remove as diagnostic proof. Authorship may show interests when tied to an explicit first-person statement, but cannot establish psychological identity loss.
- **AV Club rebuttal:** acknowledge that a performance can feel overmanaged regardless of whether its performer experiences insecurity. Empathy explains a possible cost; it does not invalidate criticism.
- **Current relationship status:** old single/unavailable quotations are dated statements, not current facts. No new partner speculation needed. Relationship allegations, if included, must preserve disagreement and avoid adjudication.
- **Austin:** do not turn a move toward family into proof of collapse, integration, or total disappearance from Hollywood. Exact home distance, motive quotes, and relocation date require sourced attribution.
- **Wing/subtype/arrows:** several old passages assert unobservable motives (admiration rather than affection; sexual instinct last; security rather than status). Remove certainty, explain evidence limits, and keep advanced theory inside the rabbit hole.
- **Ending:** no evidence that an identity crisis has resolved, that all charm became effortless, or that he does not know which self is real. Those are the old writer's inventions.
