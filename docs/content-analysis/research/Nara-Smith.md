### On the origin of the from-scratch project

- "I got diagnosed with an autoimmune disease and my eczema started flaring up really bad to the point where I genuinely felt crippled by it because I couldn't really go out. I couldn't use my hands. I couldn't brush my hair."
- "Not being from America, I didn't quite know what it meant eating processed food here because a lot of ingredients in our food are not good for us. So then I kind of did so much research to the point where I was like, 'Okay, well, I guess I'll just have to make everything from scratch because that feels the safest to me.'"
- "The bob also came from that because I was like, I can't do my curls anymore because of all these health issues."

### On the whisper

- "The voice came from me having put my baby down. And I was like, I've just spent so much time putting you down. There's no way I'm going to jeopardize waking you up, but Mom's got to work."
- "I got to get this video up. I take no days off."

### On the business

- "It's basically a one-man show, woman show." (She films, picks outfits, picks recipes, shoots on a tripod, edits, records voiceover after the kids are asleep.)
- "There was no other option in my mind cuz if I do something, I do it."
- "I'm a very ambitious and driven and hardworking woman to where there was no option but to film and get the video up. So, it wasn't even a conversation where I was asking for permission."
- "Well, got to watch the kids, hun, cuz I got to film."
- "A lot of what I do is tongue and cheek. I push buttons. I follow instincts." (from her book *Homemade*, quoted back to her by Alex Cooper)

### On the tradwife label

- "They kind of used me as the poster child for the tradwife movement, which was very bizarre to me."
- "With that definition and those points, I'm none of that. And we can go through it point by point."
- "I don't prioritize being a homemaker. I'm a working mom. Like, I am out in LA. I travel a lot."
- "Yeah, I like to cook. Okay, sue me."
- "He does the dishes. I don't touch the dishes. There's no way you're going to see me washing and scrubbing the dishes."
- "No, we both work like that. That doesn't make any sense. Like I provide for our family."
- "I find it very concerning almost that we need to put women in very specific very narrow boxes in order to make sense of them."
- "Why can't we be a working mom that enjoys cooking, that enjoys being at home and taking care of our kids, but also is on the next plane out to LA to take business meetings with big executives?"
- "I realized that a lot of the people that are very very stuck in this narrative do not want to be corrected."
- "I don't think a trad wife would be cooking in like Schiaparelli, you know, or a Mugler look straight off the runway."
- "I've kind of detached myself from what the public perception is because I know I'm not that."
- "Let them think what they're going to think."
- "I'm not the issue. Something that is happening for you is the issue."
- "I'd be like, what is happening? Did they not know that I worked as a model? I've been working since I've been 14."

It would light up like a world globe and he would spin the globe and then he'd like stop it and then he'd like point to this. He's like, 'What is this country? What's the capital? - "I don't want them to be followers. I want them to be leaders, strong leaders."

### On postpartum anxiety (fourth child)

- "I want to be seen as this very like collected strong woman to where I don't want people to think I'm unwell."
- "It genuinely felt like everything was a dream. I told Lucky, I was like, it feels like I'm going to wake up tomorrow and I would have never been pregnant. I don't have a baby. I don't — this doesn't feel real."
- "I told him like I don't feel like I'm here. Am I here? … I looked at him one night and I'm like, did I die?"
- "Every morning I'd wake up looking at this baby being like, 'Who are you?'"
- "I didn't tell my midwife."
- "Three weeks after she was born, I was shooting cookbook images … I just had to keep it pushing."
- "I was so disconnected from myself … it felt like I was living this like warped reality."
- On the postpartum photo the internet celebrated: "That picture wasn't me at my best and my normal. I was crying that same morning. I was wearing a diaper in that picture. I was wearing linen pants because I didn't fit a single one of my denim pants that I had. I was way above my usual weight."

### On the eight months of Whimsy's treatment

- "I was still posting my videos. I was still posting stories and being like, 'Today my kids were —' while internally I was like crumbling. Truly crumbling. And I was like zipping up my dresses being like, 'Okay, let's just go do it. Let's just take the meeting. Oh, next flight out to here.'"
- "Deadlines for the book. Like there was no messing around."
- "When I'm faced with a crisis, whatever that crisis may be, I'm like, okay, there's no time for emotions. There's no time for anything. It's like zoning in and doing it."
- On the pediatrician going silent: "In that moment, like my heart just dropped and there was this like weird gut feeling of being like, 'Oh, she has cancer. I know she has.'"
- On the phone ringing a week later: "It was this like weird gut feeling. I'm like, 'Oh, it's the hospital. They're going to tell us she has cancer.' I looked at Lucky and I'm like, 'This is it. Let's just brace ourselves.'"
- Context for the premonition: "My mom before that, she'd gone through a year of chemo treatments for breast cancer. And it was gruesome … there was always this lingering fear of this like sickness that would come."
- "My fear — I always had this like lingering fear. Well, what if someone takes a video of us in the hospital and they put it out?" (Strangers approached them in the hospital for selfies. "Obviously, I smiled and said, 'Of course you can.'")
- "To me, her safety and privacy was the most important thing."
- "I always came back to the fact like I don't want to share it. I don't want to talk about it. I don't want her out there."
- "I waited until we were [in remission] because I couldn't stomach going through everything at the same time."
- "I can't give up hope. If I give up hope, Whimsy will. And I need to show up for my daughter. And I need to make sure she's all right. And she will be all right. I'm going to make sure of that. And if it's not this treatment, it will be next treatment. There was never the thought of giving up emotionally or physically to me."
- "Maybe if I use my platform to make even just one person feel less alone, I can do that."
- On finding others: "The more time I spend in the hospital and kind of seeking that community myself, the more I realize that there's actually a lot more parents going through the exact same thing."

### Miscellaneous signature detail

## 7. Type hypothesis (interpretation — labeled as hypothesis)

**Leading hypothesis: Enneagram Type 8, self-preservation dominant, 9 wing. Confidence: high.**

Supporting evidence:
- Gut-center certainty, twice, under maximum pressure: "there was this like weird gut feeling of being like, 'Oh, she has cancer. I know she has.'" She trusts an instant read over process, and was right.
- Refusal to be defined. The specific injury she reports is not being disliked but being *categorized*: "we need to put women in very specific very narrow boxes in order to make sense of them."
- She does not ask permission: "It wasn't even a conversation where I was asking for permission."
- Refusal of the servile task: "I don't touch the dishes."
- Direct confrontation over image repair: she read the dictionary definition on air and dismantled it clause by clause, then closed with "I'm not the issue. Something that is happening for you is the issue."
- Concealment of vulnerability until it is resolved: hid the fourth pregnancy, hid the postpartum depersonalization from her own midwife, hid an eight-month cancer diagnosis until remission. Stated motive: "I want to be seen as this very like collected strong woman to where I don't want people to think I'm unwell."
- Self-preservation instinct: the entire project is food, home, provision, body, resources, and the safety of what enters them. "That feels the safest to me."
- 9 wing: unhurried affect, the whisper, "let them think what they're going to think," withdrawal from the internet rather than escalation.

**Named tiebreaker behavior:** she withheld her daughter's cancer diagnosis for roughly eight months and released it only in the same breath as the remission. A Three hides a setback because the setback spoils the record; an Eight hides it because being seen mid-collapse is exposure. Her own stated reason is the Eight reason — she could not "stomach going through everything at the same time," and her primary fear was a stranger filming them in the hospital and taking the disclosure out of her hands.

**Strongest alternative: Type 3.** She is a model since 14, an image professional, explicitly "ambitious and driven," running a personal brand, and highly attuned to how she is read. A Three reading explains the polish and the work ethic well.

**Disconfirming evidence for Type 3:** her response to reputational damage was not adjustment but escalation — she leaned harder into the aesthetic ("I don't think a trad wife would be cooking in Schiaparelli") and detached from the audience's verdict ("let them think what they're going to think"). Threes recalibrate the image toward what the audience rewards. She refused the correction and reframed the argument as the audience's problem. She also declines the flattering read of her own postpartum photo rather than accepting the win.

**Disconfirming evidence for Type 8 (name honestly):** the depth of the postpartum anxiety — depersonalization, intrusive thoughts, "did I die?" — reads more anxious-type than the textbook Eight. Two mitigations: it is a documented hormonal-crash phenomenon rather than a dispositional trait, and her *handling* of it (tell no one, keep working, present as collected) is the Eight pattern even if the symptom is not. Log as unresolved.

**Arrows (Rabbit Hole material, not body):**
- Stress 8 → 5: withdrawal, secrecy, information hoarding. "I kind of took a step back from the internet." "I kind of put myself in this little bubble." Concealing the pregnancy, the diagnosis, the anxiety.
- Growth 8 → 2: opening after remission, seeking community in hospital corridors and on Reddit, disclosing in order to make other parents "feel less alone."
