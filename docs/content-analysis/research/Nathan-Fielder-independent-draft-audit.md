## Publication-blocking findings

### 1. Performed fake-Kor dialogue is presented as genuine participant testimony

**Lines 65 and 202; high confidence.** The draft identifies the closing complaint about being made to look fake as a real participant describing harm, then explicitly insists that it is a person's actual experience. The [Variety review](https://au.variety.com/2022/tv/reviews/nathan-fielder-the-rehearsal-review-hbo-4203/) itself clarifies in its next paragraph that an actor playing the subject delivers the complaint in another rehearsal. Its correction note also addresses the speaker's identity. This is an error in evidence category, not merely an omitted caveat.

**Required correction:** remove the purported participant testimony or identify it as fictionalized dialogue and self-criticism within the episode. D'Addario's editorial objection remains a real critical response. Kor Skeete's actual GQ interview remains separate participant evidence. Do not count the actor's words as a qualifying firsthand participant quote.

Remove its downstream use in the table, persona comparison, perfectionism, and alternative-type analysis. ## Major interpretive overreach

### 6. The pilot explanation suppresses a motive he explicitly gave

**Lines 147, 149 and 222.** The interview window **17:50–20:55** offers both learning/understanding and credibility with pilots. The draft ranks understanding below credibility, then writes as though he did not express the former. That is a selective paraphrase. It also turns a production-specific concern into a total account of his personality.

**Correction:** preserve both stated motives, attribute them, and make the relative psychological weight an interpretation. An editing reveal to viewers does not mean the participating pilots were kept ignorant; the same window says he discussed his training with them. Also do not imply he possessed every later rating for the entire production.

### 7. The persona and empathy sections assert inaccessible inner states

**Lines 121, 127, 186–194, 208, 220–228.** No source establishes a numerical proportion of real awkwardness; that all his anxiety is socially switched on and off; that he experiences only rehearsed contact as survivable; that he never considers whether participants want the intervention; or the ultimate benign purpose of a particular set. The categorical claim that the screen character enters with no plan also poorly describes shows built around conspicuous plans.

**Correction:** delete the invented percentage and absolutes. Distinguish his self-reported difficulties, deliberate comic exaggeration, documented work habits, and the author's hypothesis. Preserve participant criticism without inventing either malicious or purely generous intent. The type and wing should be a reading of incomplete public evidence, not a settled fact. Rejecting Type 3 because people of that type supposedly would not publicly acknowledge insecurity is an unsupported stereotype, not a discriminating counter-test.

### 8. The opening and ending add unverified biographical continuity

**Lines 86–110, 169, 239–245.** The curse anecdote is his retrospective account in a promotional Q&A. The private thought paragraph, stranger's smile, precise mid-2000s timing, lasting inadequacy of reassurance, and all later work being repetitions of that encounter are not established facts. The ending also credits Fielder with complete control over a festival screening and unlimited documentary access without evidence.

**Correction:** attribute the anecdote, mark any imagined interiority, remove unsupported scene details and chronology, and present the thematic connection as a possible reading. Distinguish roughly three years making a film from continuous filming or unrestricted access. Shared filmmaking and festival decisions should not silently become individual psychological evidence. September 6 is supported by the later dated reporting in the biography packet; the problem is the extra control narrative.
