## Type-challenge memo (required pre-draft artifact)

**Original creator hypothesis: Enneagram Type 6, wing 6w5, social instinct dominant. Original confidence: medium-high. This confidence requires reassessment after the supervisory corrections above.** Public personality typing remains interpretation. No type's response to a curse, admission of insecurity, need for credibility, or creative career can be predicted categorically.

1. **Strongest case for 6.** The method is anticipatory rehearsal of feared outcomes, which is the Six defense rendered as a production budget. He states the motive for the pilot rating in credibility terms and names the feeling ("I felt insecure"). The Authority Paradox is on the record: he earned aviation's own credential, then argued publicly with the FAA when it declined to agree. The $20 is a Six hedging a risk he explicitly rates at zero. 9takes' own stress-line description for Six — credentials and proximity to competence functioning as proof of safety, "who can I trust" becoming "nobody can dismiss me" — matches the pilot-license account almost word for word.
2. **Strongest case against (Type 5).** Flat affect, encyclopedic preparation, observational remove, documented difficulty with unstructured social time, the analytic premise of _Nathan for You_. Emma Stone's "observer of human behavior" is a Five sentence. A Five's competence-before-engagement explains the same preparation.
3. **Proposed tiebreaker, challenged.** The creator proposed preparation directed toward an audience as decisive. The pilot account actually names learning and credibility together, and public interviews disproportionately expose public work. The curse anecdote supports his reported discomfort and return; it cannot establish how every Type 5 would behave. The jury should test whether a Type 6 interpretation survives these limitations.
4. **One behavior the type does not explain.** How readily he gives authorship away. John Wilson: "he took a chance on me" — Fielder backed a show he could not control on limited proof of concept and let staged material go. He rewrote a character on _The Curse_ around a performer's improvisation, and keeps details he has said remain confusing. The growth-to-Nine line covers some of this, but not the ease of it.
5. **Confidence: medium-high.** Named tiebreaker present, repeated across years and unrelated projects. Not high, because the strongest evidence is his own public self-explanation, which is a remark in context and not access to motive.

**Explicitly not established:** any clinical diagnosis; a childhood cause; the identity of the ex-partner or any current partner; cause of the divorce; net worth; that his aviation proposals prevent crashes or drove legislation. Fictional Asher is not biographical evidence.
