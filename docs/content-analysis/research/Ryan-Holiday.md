## 2. First-person quotations

### "Two Sides," ryanholiday.net, 2011-07-05 (fetched and verified)

The single most important document in this file. Written at 24, while at American Apparel, a year
before _Trust Me, I'm Lying_, and three years before the Stoicism pivot.

- "I have always felt pulled in two conflicting directions. It has not been pleasant. It's made me feel miserable when I should feel happy, afraid when I should feel secure–a constant tension and uncertainty that takes an exhausting toll."
- "There is the part of me drawn to discipline. To work and do great things in this brief bit of time I have been given."
- "There is another part of me pulled towards recklessness. To simply live. To hate the things that most people seem to value."
- "All this is to say that there is a tension in me that I have trouble grappling with. It is the feeling of living in a house divided against itself."
- "The few moves I've made in my life that could be considered daring or even reckless–dropping out of school, packing up and moving across the country–were almost immediately overwhelmed by the responsible side."
- **"And yet I think they are both equal parts fear, the fear of simply _just being_."**

### Chase Jarvis LIVE, 2012-07-03 (transcript fetched)

- "I'm good at quitting actually… I can see several times in my life where I've dropped out of something that I think other people would have been comfortable with or kept doing, and every time I've done that I've moved on to something better and cooler and more interesting and made more money at."
- "I look at my education as having really started the day I dropped out of school, cuz now it was on me. It was my full-time job and no one was going to demand that I turn in papers… And so for me, that's when education became real and I really valued it because it wasn't, you know, a 9:00 a.m. class and then a noon class and then a 3 p.m. class. It was all the time, every day."
- "When I left school, I felt like my education started. It was like now I'm in a classroom of one person. I'm the only student."
- On the subtitle _Confessions_: "here's what I do and here's how you can do it, and then sort of here's, like, you know, not an apology, but here's sort of an **admission of some guilt** in some sense."
- On his American Apparel-era boss: "he didn't care what people said about him so long as they talked about him… you've got nothing to lose because the alternative is obscurity and that's really scary."

### Talks at Google, _Ego Is the Enemy_, 2016-07-20 (transcript fetched)

- "I do all my books on these four by six note cards."
- The book's cycle, in his words: "you're aspiring to get some taste of success, then you succeed, maybe you plateau, then you begin to get complacent, and you fail and then you're back to aspiration."
- On why he changed the subject from humility to ego: "instead of talking about humility, I would talk about ego."

### Diary of a CEO, 2023-08-24 (transcript fetched)

- **The daily verdict (signature detail):** "I try to run or swim or bike every day. I'm not training for a marathon. I'm not trying to set any records. **I'm trying to make a promise to myself and keep that promise.** And it doesn't matter how badly the writing goes, it doesn't matter what news I just got from my agent, it doesn't matter how my business is doing…"
- On discipline: "discipline is not the marine sergeant telling everyone else what to do how to be… the Stoics are talking about the virtue of self-discipline, which is you have your standards that you selected for yourself that you are living up to. **It's not a weapon that you wielded into other people.**"
- Quotes Marcus Aurelius: "remember, tolerant with others, strict with yourself."
- On his parenting question: "does this matter? Like, why does this thing matter? Is this thing that I'm about to argue with them or force them to do or feel insecure about — does it actually matter? And the answer is almost always no. It doesn't matter. It's made up."
- **Childhood pressure, in his own words:** "early on in my life I felt a lot of pressure and I was demanded or asked a lot of myself, and the ability to be like, I did it… now every extra minute or day that I get is extra."
- "I wake up with this sense of living in the bonus that it's all extra."
- On his wife pushing back: "I remember my wife said to me one time… she said 'I can't frustrate you' — she was basically saying we're responsible for our own emotions." _(Holiday recounting Samantha; does NOT qualify for Gate 1.)_

## 4. Accomplishments, failures, controversies, counterevidence

**Accomplishments.** 13 books, 2012–2025. Founded The Painted Porch Bookshop, Bastrop, TX. The Daily
Stoic and Daily Dad newsletters/podcasts. Five years lecturing midshipmen at the Naval Academy.

**Sales figures — attribute or omit.** His own /about page says "more than 2 million copies in thirty
languages" and describes "10 books" (stale). His current bio and Amazon say "more than 5 million" in
"more than 40 languages." One secondary (bestwriting.com) says 10 million. **Wikipedia carries no
total-sales figure**, only "By December 2016, [_The Obstacle Is the Way_] had sold more than 230,000
copies." Every large figure traces to author/publisher marketing copy.

**Controversies / pressure points.**

- The Ivanka Trump video, April 2026 — the strongest critic-pressure anchor and the most recent.
- Scholarly objection that he has commercialized Stoicism, knows neither Greek nor Latin, and
  flattens the philosophy into self-help.
- The American Apparel / Dov Charney era. **Legally sensitive.** His self-reported tactics are fair
  game; Charney's conduct and the company's collapse are not this page's subject.

**Counterevidence to the Type 1 read (recorded honestly).**

- He is a formidable self-marketer with three media properties, a tour, and a bookstore that extends
  his brand. That is Type 3 surface behavior.
- He was a professional image-manufacturer, and image fluency is the Three's native language.
- He has a Five's habits: 4x6 notecards, a research practice, withdrawal to a 40-acre ranch, an
  obsessive reading life.
- **What resolves it:** he explicitly rejects outcome metrics for his own daily practice ("not
  training for a marathon… not trying to set any records"), and Greene's description of his
  self-concept lists _conscientious_ and _a good person_, not _successful_. Per the internal lens,
  "Type 1 is not mainly trying to look successful. They are trying to make the situation right."

## 6. Type hypothesis (hypothesis, not verdict)

**Leading: Enneagram Type 1, wing 9, social subtype. Confidence: high.**

**Named tiebreaker.** The daily swim. "I'm not training for a marathon. I'm not trying to set any
records. I'm trying to make a promise to myself and keep that promise." A Three trains for the
record. Holiday explicitly discards the record and keeps the verdict. The exercise is not
performance; it is a daily ruling on whether he is still the kind of person who does what he said.

**Corroborating.** Greene's list of his self-concept (conscientious, good person, good father, not
"successful"). The four-virtue publishing series as a self-administered report card. "Tolerant with
others, strict with yourself," repeated across at least three independent sources, as his most-cited
maxim. Childhood in a household of two professional rule-enforcers — a police detective and a high
school principal. "Early on in my life I felt a lot of pressure and I was demanded or asked a lot of
myself."

**Strongest alternative: Type 3.** See counterevidence above. Resolved by the outcome-metric
rejection and by Greene's testimony.

**Also considered: Type 5** (notecards, ranch, reading) — fails because Fives withdraw from the
audience rather than build three of them. **Type 8** (the Naval Academy confrontation, the American
Apparel power tactics) — fails because the confrontations are consistently moral rather than
territorial.

**One behavior the type does not fully explain.** The genuine relish of the American Apparel
manipulation years — not merely tolerated as a means to an end, but performed with evident skill and
enjoyment, and marketed afterward. A One's guilt engine explains the confession; it does not fully
explain the six years of fluent, inventive lying that preceded it. The 2011 "Two Sides" essay is the
best available evidence that he experienced those years as a genuine internal split rather than a
strategy.

**Arrows.** Stress 1→4: self-doubt, withdrawal, aggrievement. The April 2026 episode fits — Bates:
"Holiday seems to want everyone to know how upset he is about all of this." Growth 1→7: "I wake up
with this sense of living in the bonus that it's all extra."

**Emotional thesis, in his vocabulary.**

- **Core fear:** "the fear of simply _just being_" (2011). Not failure. Rest.
- **Core desire:** "how to be a good person and how to live a good life" (2025).
- **Childhood message:** "I felt a lot of pressure and I was demanded or asked a lot of myself."
- **Daily mechanism:** "make a promise to myself and keep that promise."
