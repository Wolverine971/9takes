## Timeline (evidence, dated)

- 1996-04-02 — Born on a U.S. Navy base in Okinawa, Japan. Father Dewayne Bryan (Navy ~24-25 years, master chief, later a recruiter in Oklahoma); mother Annette DeAnn Bryan (Navy veteran, later a CNA). Grandfather also Navy. Raised in Oologah, Oklahoma. Parents divorced when Zach was ~12; father had custody (SCMP, Biography.com).
- ~2010 (age 14) — First guitar; decides "I'm going to be in the Navy, that's all I want to do" (JRE #2015).
- 2013 (age 17) — Enlists; father, then a Navy recruiter, helps recruit him. Diver billet falls through; becomes an Aviation Ordnanceman (AO). Deployments include Djibouti and Bahrain (JRE #2015).
- ~2014-2016 — Trains ~2 years for SEAL selection (BUD/S) with "Senior Chief Lanquist out of Omaha"; calls his mother daily, she asks how far he ran/swam (JRE #2015).
- 2016-08-03 — Mother Annette DeAnn Bryan dies at 49. Per JRE: "the day my package came back for the SEAL, the BUDS thing, my mama died." He abandons the SEAL path afterward ("I bitched out for sure and I wish I wouldn't have sometimes"). His chief: "you want to go out there and die or something, why do you want to do this so bad?" (JRE #2015).
- 2017 — Starts posting song videos to Twitter from the Navy; "five or six likes... it was kind of my validation in the world of I can write a song at least" (JRE #2015). Didn't believe in therapy: "that's crazy in the Navy."
- 2019 — DeAnn (debut album, named for his mother) recorded and self-released while active-duty.
- 2020 — Elisabeth (second album).
- 2021-10 — Honorably discharged after ~8 years (2013-2021) to pursue music; his Gunner told him "you got to get out of the Navy," Bryan answered "I'd rather not." Discharge processing took 8 months; he compares himself to Elvis as the last such discharge-to-music case (JRE, self-aware "don't quote me"). NOTE: sources split 7 vs 8 years; he himself says both "eight years" and "nine years" in JRE. Phrase as "eight years (2013-2021)" or "nearly a decade."
- 2022-05 — American Heartbreak (triple album, debuts #5 Billboard 200).
- 2022-11/12 — Ticketmaster war: Nov 15 tweet "until there is a serious change in the system all my homies will continue to hate Ticketmaster"; Dec 25 surprise live album "All My Homies Hate Ticketmaster" with statement: "I have met kids at my shows who have paid upwards of four-hundred bucks to be there and I'm done with it" (Variety, CNN, Dec 2022). 2023 tour: registered fans, IDs at venue, ~$150 cap (JRE: "tickets going to be 150 bucks no matter what... backfired on me because people were just so angry at me about it and I was just trying to do the right thing"; asked if people were happy or angry: "hard to tell").
