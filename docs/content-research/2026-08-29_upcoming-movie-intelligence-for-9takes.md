# Upcoming Movie Intelligence for 9takes

**Audience:** DJ / 9takes editorial planning  
**Date:** August 29, 2026  
**Decision:** Which upcoming movies, actors, and psychology themes deserve early coverage?

## Scope and assumptions

The supplied Rotten Tomatoes capture contains 243 release entries from September 2026 through 2031, including re-releases and one duplicate. This report treats September 2026 through August 2027 as the actionable window, with later films included only when early indexing is strategically useful. It is U.S.-release-centric, but gives extra weight to stories capable of crossing language and cultural boundaries. Re-releases are excluded from the ranked opportunity list.

The rankings optimize for 9takes, not box office alone. The main criteria are: probable cultural attention, psychology/personality fit, a defensible first-mover angle, actor-profile opportunity, evidence depth, and timing. “Breakout” is a forecast, not a claim of certainty.

## Executive answer

The safest cultural-event bets are **The Hunger Games: Sunrise on the Reaping**, **The Social Reckoning**, **Dune: Part Three**, **Avengers: Doomsday**, **Narnia: The Magician's Nephew**, and **The Legend of Zelda**. They will be large; the editorial question is whether 9takes can enter them through a distinctive psychological lens instead of producing generic franchise coverage.

The best asymmetric “maybe this surprises everyone” bets are **Clayface**, **Hope**, **Whalefall**, and **Other Mommy**. **Appofeniacs** is the best small-film/big-idea opportunity: its limited distribution caps mass reach, but its deepfake, apophenia, confirmation-bias, and manufactured-reality themes fit 9takes almost perfectly.

The strongest new adult actor opportunities are **Austin Abrams**, **Tom Rhys Harries**, **Joseph Zada**, **Mia Tharia**, and **Anthony Ippolito**. **Bo Bragason** is the best early 2027 seed. The highest-ROI immediate people action is not a new profile: finish and publish the strong existing **Jeremy Allen White** draft before **The Social Reckoning**, then refresh the existing **Mikey Madison** page during the press wave.

The broadest coming trend is **epistemic anxiety**: stories about whether a person, image, memory, narrative, or relationship is real. The second is **identity horror**: faces, bodies, roles, artificial friends, doppelgängers, and public selves that stop matching the private self. Those two trends give 9takes an editorial lane that can connect many releases without chasing every title individually.

## Priority ranking: what to act on

### 1. The Social Reckoning — October 9, 2026

**Projection:** Near-certain cultural conversation; very high psychology fit; low new-actor value.

Sony describes Aaron Sorkin's film as a companion to _The Social Network_, following Facebook engineer Frances Haugen and reporter Jeff Horwitz as they expose the platform's guarded internal information. The cast includes Mikey Madison, Jeremy Allen White, Wunmi Mosaku, Betty Gilpin, Billy Magnussen, Bill Burr, and Jeremy Strong. The story is not merely “tech news”; it is about institutional loyalty, moral courage, persuasion, incentives, groupthink, and the gap between a company's public identity and private knowledge. [Sony Pictures synopsis and cast](https://www.sonypictures.co.uk/movies/social-reckoning)

The real-world source material has unusually strong primary evidence. Haugen's written U.S. Senate testimony argued that Facebook's incentives traded safety, including children's safety, for growth. That gives 9takes a factual spine for psychology coverage without treating the movie as evidence. [U.S. Senate testimony](https://www.commerce.senate.gov/wp-content/uploads/meetings/A04578DB-ADD9-41A7-BF1C-A22703DAEA02/Frances%20Haugen%20Written%20Testimony.pdf)

**Write:** “Why Good Employees Become Whistleblowers,” “The Psychology of Institutional Loyalty,” or “When the Group's Story Becomes More Important Than Reality.”  
**People move:** Publish Jeremy Allen White; refresh Mikey Madison in late September; use Bill Burr and Jeremy Strong as internal-link support rather than commissioning new profiles solely for this movie.  
**Risk:** The subject may polarize along political/technology lines, and Sorkin's dialogue-first style may create more discourse than mass fandom.

### 2. Clayface — October 23, 2026

**Projection:** Highest 9takes-native movie; high breakout upside; strongest new-lead opportunity.

DC's official campaign calls the film a horror thriller about a rising actor's descent into a revenge-filled monster, explicitly naming loss of identity and humanity, corrosive love, and scientific ambition. Tom Rhys Harries carries the title role, with Naomi Ackie, David Dencik, Max Minghella, and Eddie Marsan. [Official Clayface site](https://www.clayfacemovie.com/)

This is franchise scale wrapped around body horror and psychological unraveling. It is positioned to attract superhero, horror, and prestige-genre audiences at once. Harries is a comparatively unfamiliar adult actor carrying a DC film after an exhaustive casting process, which should create predictable “who is Tom Rhys Harries?” demand.

**Write:** “What Happens When Your Face Is Your Identity?”, “Clayface and the Terror of the Ideal Self,” or a character-based Enneagram analysis after release. Self-discrepancy research provides a useful non-diagnostic framework: across 70 studies, larger gaps between actual and ideal/ought selves were associated with more negative emotion and psychopathology. [Self-discrepancy meta-analysis](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/30640499/)  
**People move:** Research Tom Rhys Harries now; publish by mid-September if the interview evidence supports a defensible type. Keep Matt Hagen's psychology separate from Harries' personality.  
**Risk:** R-rated body horror narrows the family audience, and franchise affiliation does not guarantee broad affection.

### 3. The Hunger Games: Sunrise on the Reaping — November 20, 2026

**Projection:** Near-certain global conversation; very high new-actor value; very high psychology fit.

The source novel sold more than 1.5 million English-language copies in its first week. Suzanne Collins says it was inspired by David Hume's idea of implicit submission and by the use of propaganda and control of narrative. This is unusually direct authorial permission to write about conformity, authoritarianism, moral injury, trauma, and collective memory. [Scholastic sales and author statement](https://www.scholastic.com/newsroom/all-news/press-release/sunrise-on-the-reaping-new-novel-in-the-worldwide-bestselling-hu.html)

Scholastic and Lionsgate place rising stars Joseph Zada and Whitney Peak beside an A-list ensemble. Zada is the clearer first-mover opportunity because he inherits young Haymitch, a role already associated with Woody Harrelson, while still having a shallow general biography field. [Scholastic movie tie-in and cast release](https://www.scholastic.com/newsroom/all-news/press-release/scholastic-to-publish-trade-paperback-and-movie-tie-in-editions-.html)

**Write:** “How Propaganda Rewrites Personal Memory,” “Haymitch and Moral Injury,” “Why the Many Submit to the Few,” or a post-release Haymitch personality analysis.  
**People move:** Build Joseph Zada's evidence file now and publish by late September; keep Whitney Peak on watch for a substantive profile or featurette.  
**Risk:** The movie is not a surprise hit; first-mover advantage comes from owning the actor and psychology queries before the press tour, not predicting that the franchise will be popular.

### 4. Resident Evil + Whalefall — September 18 and October 16, 2026

**Projection:** The best two-release actor breakout ladder; one mainstream franchise and one high-concept sleeper.

Sony's _Resident Evil_ makes Austin Abrams the central protagonist in a new survival story from Zach Cregger. [Official Resident Evil site](https://residentevil.movie/) Four weeks later, _Whalefall_ puts Abrams almost alone inside a sperm whale, searching for escape after diving for his dead father's remains. [20th Century Studios synopsis](https://www.20thcenturystudios.com/movies/whalefall)

The second film supplies the deeper psychology. The source novel centers guilt after a father's suicide; its publisher describes the story as both survival adventure and a father-son reckoning. Research on suicide bereavement consistently identifies guilt, self-criticism, stigma, and meaning-making as distinctive burdens. [Simon & Schuster book page](https://www.simonandschuster.com/books/Whalefall/Daniel-Kraus/9781665918176) [2023 meta-synthesis](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/35996970/)

**Write:** A single Austin Abrams profile before September 18, then use _Whalefall_ for “Why Grief Makes Us Attempt the Impossible” or “The Difference Between Remembering a Parent and Escaping Their Voice.”  
**People move:** Austin Abrams is the most time-sensitive new profile.  
**Risk:** _Resident Evil_ can generate franchise interest without converting into actor-name demand; _Whalefall_ could be dismissed as a one-gimmick movie if the emotional story does not land.

### 5. Klara and the Sun — October 23, 2026

**Projection:** Highest AI/attachment relevance; strong prestige interest; moderate mass-breakout uncertainty.

Sony's adaptation stars Jenna Ortega as an Artificial Friend who bonds with Josie, played by Mia Tharia, and begins to heal a family marked by loss. Amy Adams, Aran Murphy, Steve Buscemi, and Natasha Lyonne round out the cast. [Official Sony synopsis and cast](https://klaraandthesun.movie/)

The obvious article is not “Is AI conscious?” but “Can an attachment be psychologically real when one side is artificial?” A four-week randomized controlled study from MIT Media Lab found no significant effect from assigned chatbot mode or conversation type, but heavier voluntary use was associated with worse psychosocial outcomes; greater trust and social attraction toward the AI were associated with emotional dependence and problematic use. The authors explicitly caution that this raises questions rather than proving simple causation. [MIT Media Lab study](https://www.media.mit.edu/publications/how-ai-and-human-behaviors-shape-psychosocial-effects-of-chatbot-use-a-longitudinal-controlled-study/)

**Write:** “Can You Form a Secure Attachment to Something That Cannot Need You?”, “Why Care From a Machine Can Still Feel Real,” or “Klara vs. the Human Need to Be Chosen.”  
**People move:** Refresh Jenna Ortega rather than commission another general profile. Start a Mia Tharia evidence file; at 20 and already identified in a British Vogue rising-actor portfolio, she is a plausible adult breakout, but wait for substantive press before fixing an Enneagram type.  
**Risk:** Cerebral science fiction may produce prestige conversation without mass fandom; Taika Waititi's recent reception may split audiences.

## Trends 9takes can own

### 1. Epistemic horror: “How do I know what is real?”

_The Social Reckoning_, _Verity_, _Appofeniacs_, _The Influencer Project_, and _Sunrise on the Reaping_ all revolve around controlled narratives, manipulated evidence, or uncertainty about truth. This is the most coherent cross-film trend and the best bridge between pop culture and psychology.

### 2. Identity horror replaces the simple monster

_Clayface_, _Klara and the Sun_, _Other Mommy_, _Victorian Psycho_, and _Dune: Part Three_ ask whether the self is a body, a role, a memory, a relationship, or a story other people believe. The “monster” is often a mismatch between public form and private reality.

### 3. Prestige horror moves inside blockbuster machinery

DC body horror, auteur _Resident Evil_, Robert Eggers' _Werwulf_, and Na Hong-jin's Cannes monster epic show horror functioning as the language for franchise reinvention and serious cultural anxiety, not a separate low-status genre.

### 4. Trauma is being placed inside a clean high-concept hook

_Whalefall_ is suicide bereavement inside a one-hour survival trap. _Josephine_ is childhood trauma through a child's viewpoint. _Other Mommy_ turns attachment insecurity into a doppelgänger. The best articles should explain the human problem beneath the marketing premise.

### 5. Old IP is being relaunched through unfamiliar young faces

_Hunger Games_, _Clayface_, _Narnia_, _Zelda_, and parts of _Dune_ use recognizable worlds to install actors without household-name status. That creates a repeatable 9takes tactic: index the adult newcomers before the release, then update only when the press tour adds real personality evidence.

### 6. The next global surprise is more likely to be culturally hybrid

_Hope_ combines Korean genre filmmaking, Cannes legitimacy, and international stars. _Children of Blood and Bone_ packages African-inspired fantasy for IMAX. _Zelda_ is Japanese IP, Hollywood distribution, and globally recognizable game language. The pattern resembles the border-crossing mechanism behind _Squid Game_ and _KPop Demon Hunters_ more than a conventional domestic sleeper does.

## Limitations and confidence

Release dates and distribution plans can change; the supplied Rotten Tomatoes snapshot already conflicts with Nintendo's primary-source _Zelda_ date. “Trending” projections use franchise scale, source-material demand, trailer/press momentum, festival reception, distribution, and 9takes' existing content inventory. They are not box-office forecasts. Unreleased films without reviews remain especially uncertain.

Early reviews exist for _Hope_, _Victorian Psycho_, and _Appofeniacs_, but most fall titles are still pre-review. A great premise can fail in execution, while a low-profile release can explode after a trailer, song, scene, or meme. Recheck the watchlist at final-trailer, festival, review-embargo, and direct-name-trend milestones.

The actor recommendations concern editorial opportunity, not confirmed personality types. Character psychology, role choice, and publicity copy are not evidence of an actor's Enneagram. Profiles should use attributed first-person interviews and third-party testimony, state uncertainty, and avoid clinical claims.
