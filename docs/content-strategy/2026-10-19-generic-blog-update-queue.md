Use the generic audit to locate passages worth reading, then choose work by editorial weakness, search opportunity, recent changes and the consolidation plan. Do not turn every score above 15% into a full rewrite.

## What the audit found

The October 19 run covered **159 published non-people posts** of at least 300 analyzed words. The median generic score was **12.8%**; **62 posts scored at least 15%**, including **23 with zero clicks in the audit's GSC lookup**. Among 453 published people posts, only three crossed 15%. The broad Enneagram library is the larger concentration of flags, though those genres are not directly comparable.

The percentage is the share of analyzed words in flagged sentences. It is not a probability of AI authorship, a factual accuracy score or a prediction of search performance. The rules catch stock phrasing, repeated frames, unanchored generalizations, advice and abstract language. Some are useful warning signs; others flag necessary explanations or cautions.

Two concrete limitations matter:

- The neurodivergence guide's warning that the framework cannot identify which types are likely to have ADHD or autism is flagged as a generalization. Removing that limit to improve the score would weaken the article.
- The parser omits tables and does not expand rendered components or their props. The strengths rewrite uses several visual components, so its score covers only part of the reading experience. Quotes, numbers, names and first-person wording can also exempt weak-rule matches without proving that a sentence is true or useful.

The generic audit's click lookup combines some URL variants. The search figures below instead use exact base URLs from the fresh export, consistent with the [two week search check](./2026-10-19-search-cleanup-two-week-check.md).

### Depression

The 159-word “Why Depression Varies by Enneagram Type” section scores 74.8%. The central problem is the claim that type changes triggers, symptoms and which healing approaches work. The medication section similarly says type dramatically shapes medication behavior. These need a source check and a narrower claim where the evidence does not support the wording.

Review clinical and treatment claims before polishing voice. Preserve the direct search answer, crisis information, prescriber guidance and evidence limits. This is a bounded accuracy edit on a page with existing demand, not an invitation to make stronger claims or restructure it around personal anecdotes.

### Dating apps

The opening says apps were built by and for specific personality types. That is a sweeping explanation without support in the passage. The article also makes broad claims about algorithms and recommends app features that need current verification.

Build the rewrite around a specific problem: what happens between a match, an ambiguous message and the decision to meet. Show how different motives can produce different reactions to the same situation, and give a usable response. Use DJ's actual observations where available, or label illustrative scenarios honestly. Verify app-specific claims rather than treating every app as the same product. Keep the existing URL and answer the existing search question early.

### Careers

The introduction calls the Enneagram the “cleanest map” of work motivation; the Type 2 section says a person in a solitary role “will rot” and broadly tells them to avoid coding and accounting. Strong wording is not the same thing as a supported insight.

Replace occupation-by-type prescriptions with specific work conditions and tradeoffs. The same occupation can involve isolation, collaboration, client contact or autonomy. Explain those differences while still answering career-choice and careers-to-avoid intent. This page was edited October 7, so keep the recent search structure and focus on the claims and examples.

### Relationships

This is not an empty article: it already contains useful conflict scenes and partner scripts. Preserve them. Its weak close, general advice and overlap with the attachment, love-language and compatibility guides dilute its purpose.

Give it one clear job, such as recognizing recurring conflict and practicing repair. Link out where other guides own the answer. The consolidation plan mentions a possible relationship-page merge but explicitly leaves it unresolved and outside that plan's approved scope. Do not assume a merge is decided.

### Parenting and wings

Parenting already has some concrete scenes. Improve the deterministic opening and generic type promises, then use specific parent-child situations to show the distinction. Avoid implying that type inevitably determines a child's outcome. Its smaller search opportunity puts it behind the first four.

Wings needs less work. Sharpen the comparisons between neighboring wings and trim the generic close. Its 16.1% score does not justify replacing the whole guide.

## Repeatable workflow

1. Shortlist by search demand, then review the flagged passages:

   ```bash
   pnpm audit:generic -- --blogs --by clicks --min 15 --top 25
   pnpm audit:generic -- src/blog/enneagram/why-dating-apps-are-harder-for-certain-personality-types.md
   ```

2. Check recent edits, the page's intended search question and any consolidation decision. Choose a full rewrite, a few specific section edits, a merge input or no action.
3. Write one sentence explaining what the reader should understand or do after reading. Select the two or three passages that most obstruct that result.
4. Add original observation, a sourced fact, a concrete tradeoff or an honestly labeled scenario where it improves the explanation. Cut redundant claims. Do not invent quotes, experiences or statistics to satisfy the scorer.
5. Follow the [blog quality check](../writing-system/05-blog-quality-check.md): metrics, heading skim, full read and factual verification. Rerun generic and readability audits as diagnostics. Check the rendered page when components carry part of the argument. There is no zero-percent target.
6. Record the actual publication date and preserve comparable GSC windows. Let the revision accumulate data before another structural change; correct factual errors immediately when identified.

This approach also matches [Google's helpful content guidance](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/fundamentals/creating-helpful-content): original information and useful analysis matter more than superficial freshness or a preferred word count.

### Follow-up prose and flow review

The first pass improved substance but left too much formal explanation around the examples. Some passages repeatedly introduced a “Type X lens,” explained an already-clear scene, then ended on another caveat. Dating apps had a particularly uniform run of medium-length paragraphs despite passing the readability thresholds. Parenting and wings also needed related fragments joined and repeated labels removed.

Three editors revised two posts each, followed by another integration read and phone-width preview checks. The changes preserve the factual corrections and useful scenes while tightening sentences, breaking paragraphs at changes of thought, and removing repeated explanations. The wings portraits retain all 18 combinations but drop occupation inventories and checklists that repeated their prose. Sample dating messages now stand apart from the explanation.

Two examples of the sentence-level change:

- Careers: “The Type 8 pattern makes ownership and the ability to act especially relevant” became “An impressive title can still come with a manager who approves every decision.”
- Relationships: “They make the boundary legible” became “Say when you'll return, then come back at that time.”
