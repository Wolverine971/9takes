# Situational Series: framing (2026-11-04)

Hyper-specific posts built around one moment a reader is about to walk into (or just walked out of).
Status: framing only. Nothing drafted yet. DJ to answer the riff questions at the bottom before drafting.

## Why this series fits 9takes

Every one of these situations is the brand promise in miniature: one moment, a room full of people
reading it nine different ways. The aunt asking "so, anyone special?" is reacting to her own alarm,
not to a defect in you. That is level 5 of the message hierarchy almost word for word:
"Stop mistaking someone else's alarm for a defect."

**The gap we exploit.** What ranks today for these searches is Bustle / Elite Daily / Bumble listicles:
"15 things to say when relatives ask why you're single." They hand you lines. Nobody explains _why
the question lands the way it does_, _why that relative asks it_, or _why your reaction differs from
your sibling's_. That "why" is what 9takes does and they don't.

## The underlying theme (reverse-engineered)

Strip the specifics and every situation is the same thing: **you're about to be read, and you're
dreading the reading.** The dread is a prediction, and the research says the prediction is usually wrong:

- Only ~11% of Americans expected a political argument at Thanksgiving 2025 (YouGov), while about
  half said they wanted to avoid politics (Emerson, Nov 2025). The fear is much bigger than the fight.
- Spotlight effect (Gilovich, Medvec & Savitsky, 2000): people overestimate how much others notice
  their embarrassing moments.
- Liking gap (Boothby et al., 2018): after a conversation, people underestimate how much the other
  person liked them.
- Perception gap (More in Common, 2019): people overestimate how extreme the other side's views are.

So each post runs one arc: **here's the moment → here's what's actually going on underneath (yours and
theirs) → here's how each type tends to blow it → here's exactly what to do and say.**

## Post template (every piece)

1. **The scene** (second person, concrete): the table, the pause, the look across the mashed potatoes.
2. **What's actually happening**: the emotion under your reaction and the emotion under theirs.
3. **Nine ways the room reads it**: a tight type grid. One line each. This is the 9takes signature.
4. **Your move**: tactics grouped by triad (gut / heart / head) so it's actionable, not nine essays.
5. **Exact words**: 3-5 lines to say, plus 1-2 to never say.
6. **CTA into a live 9takes question** tied to the moment ("Answer before the crowd").
   This is the activation hook: the blog is the doorway, the question is the room.

Target ~1,800-2,500 words. Short, specific, skimmable. Home: `/how-to-guides` (`src/blog/guides/`).

## Lean vs ambitious (DJ picks)

- **Lean:** six blog posts in `/how-to-guides`, each ending in a link to an existing question.
- **Ambitious:** each post gets its own **seeded 9takes question** at the exact moment ("What's the
  Thanksgiving question you dread most?", "Your side won. What do you want to say to your family that
  you won't?"), a **"nine reactions" carousel** for IG/X cut from section 3, and a small
  **Thanksgiving hub** linking all of them plus the live questions. Same writing effort; the
  difference is the posts feed the give-first loop instead of ending at "thanks for reading."
