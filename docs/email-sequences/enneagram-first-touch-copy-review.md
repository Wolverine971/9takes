# Enneagram campaign: first-message copy review

This is a founder introduction for existing 9takes account holders who may not remember the product or have heard much from DJ. The immediate goal is a saved Enneagram type. A first useful interaction matters more than introducing every feature at once.

The canonical first email is in `src/lib/email/enneagram-type-prompt-content.ts`. The local preview is `artifacts/email-campaign/enneagram-type-pilot-preview.html`. This review changes the draft and its image; it does not activate a campaign, enroll anyone, or schedule follow-ups.

## What changed

- Kept DJ's requested “Life is hard…” opening verbatim.
- Added an honest reminder that DJ is behind 9takes and the reader created an account. The email no longer asks an existing member to join again.
- Changed the subject to **“Why we read people differently”**, paired with **“One question for your next tense conversation. A note from DJ at 9takes.”** This is an editorial choice to test, not a proven winner.
- Added one useful reflection: **“What am I trying to protect right now?”** Independence, connection, and safety give the reader something to consider before clicking. This is a reflection prompt, not a test that assigns a type.
- Explained anonymous questions, comparing types, and answering before seeing others in one paragraph.
- Kept **Add my type** as the only button and placed it before the image. The button uses 9takes amber.
- Moved the beginner guide into the P.S.; the image links to that same guide. There are two distinct content destinations: the account and the guide.
- Saved the separate invitation to answer a question for a later message.
- Replaced small open arrowheads with filled heads and thicker warm-ivory shafts. The colored triad sections remain in the approved amber palette.

## How the four articles apply

### Hormozi: the closest match

[Newsletter Email Marketing in 39 Minutes](https://build-os.com/blogs/source-analyses/hormozi-newsletter-email-marketing-39-minutes) explicitly distinguishes subscriber/lifecycle email from cold outreach. Its useful principle here is to reward reading with one practical idea, then connect the CTA to that idea. The revised message gives the reflection first and makes adding a type the next step. The deliberately written preheader and secondary P.S. also fit.

The article's frequency and performance anecdotes are not forecasts for this small, infrequently contacted audience. Keep the approved illustration as supporting content and preserve a complete plain-text alternative.

### Connor Murray: directness without sales pressure

[Less Is More Cold Email](https://build-os.com/blogs/source-analyses/connor-murray-cold-email-assumptive-cadence) offers a useful sequence: identify the sender, establish relevance, make a clear request. That supports the short founder introduction and direct button. Its meeting-oriented assumptions and rapid four-touch cadence do not fit this invitation. The email should not imply that the reader owes DJ a response.

### Austin Schneider: relevance and restraint

[Engagement-First Cold Email](https://build-os.com/blogs/source-analyses/austin-schneider-engagement-first-cold-email-2026) supports a concrete benefit for a well-defined audience. Here the audience is existing members without a saved type. Its caution about repeated follow-ups supports starting conservatively. Do not import cold-outreach domain rotation, automated warm-up, vendor volume rules, or claims that particular engagement signals guarantee delivery.

### Florin Tatulea: packaging and a small next step

[Reply-Method Cold Email](https://build-os.com/blogs/source-analyses/florin-tatulea-reply-method-cold-email-showdown) separates subject/preview, body, and style. That is a useful editing pass: promise a relevant idea, deliver it in short paragraphs, and ask for one manageable action. Keep DJ's personal voice. Do not manufacture social proof, urgency, personalization, or an internal-looking subject that disguises the sender relationship.

## Recommended follow-up approach

These are proposed drafts, not additions to the active one-step sequence. Start with the revised message to the reviewed eligible audience. Evaluate saved types as the main outcome, with genuine site visits and clicks as supporting signals. Do not rely on opens alone or interpret a tiny subject test as statistically decisive.

Consider at most one relevant follow-up after 7–10 days, respecting the existing seven-day shared email buffer, suppression, current consent/eligibility, and other sequences. Choose the next message from what the person actually did. Do not claim they saved a type until the profile confirms it. If there is no useful new message for non-engagers, stop rather than sending a generic bump. The timing is an editorial recommendation, not a scheduled action or a proven optimum.

### A. Confirmed type saved: invite participation

**Subject:** Your type is only the beginning

You've added your type. Here's where it gets interesting.

Think of a time someone misunderstood you. What did they see—and what were you actually trying to do?

On 9takes, those different readings can sit next to each other. Pick a question, give your take before seeing the other answers, then compare perspectives by Enneagram type.

Your answer might be the perspective someone was missing.

[Find a question](https://9takes.com/questions)

DJ

### B. Engaged but still no type: help with discovery

**Subject:** Start with the reason, not the behavior

Two people can both go quiet in an argument. One is holding back anger. Another wants time to think.

The behavior looks the same. The reason may be different.

When you read about the Enneagram, try asking “Why do I tend to do this?” Look for a motivation that keeps showing up across situations, rather than deciding from one reaction.

The guide gives you a place to start comparing those patterns.

[Explore the type guide](https://9takes.com/enneagram-corner/beginners-guide-to-determining-your-enneagram-type)

DJ
