**You are building:** one 26-second, voiceover-led, faceless Instagram Reel about Robert Greene for @9takesdotcom.

## 0. How to work (read this twice)

This is a **step-by-step build**, not a one-shot. DJ is refining the flow itself while you work, so each stage is a checkpoint where the process gets corrected, not just the output.

### The hard rule

> **Produce the current stage. Stop. Wait for DJ's response. Do not begin the next stage.**

Even if the next stage seems obvious. Even if you have time. Even if you already know what you would write. The point of the gate is that DJ sees each layer in isolation and can change the method before it compounds into six more Reels.

### The stages

| Stage | Deliverable                                             | Gate                                  |
| ----- | ------------------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------- |
| **1** | **VO script only.** Words. Nothing else.                | ← **START HERE.** Stop after this.    |
| 2     | Beat map: script assigned to the 5 movements, timecoded | Stop.                                 |
| 3     | On-screen text cards, synced to the beat map            | Stop.                                 |
| 4     | Visual plan: shot list, framing per beat, the one move  | Stop.                                 |
| 5     | Audio plan: bed in/out, the hit, room tone, VO notes    | Stop.                                 |
| 6     | Package: caption, pinned comment, alt text + gate run   | Stop. Then DJ approves or sends back. |

**You are on Stage 1.** Deliver a VO script and nothing else. No visuals, no timings beyond the word budget, no caption. If you find yourself writing a table with a "Visual" column, you have gone too far.

### What a good stage delivery looks like

- The deliverable itself, clean and copy-pasteable.
- **Two or three real alternatives** where there is genuine design space, with a recommendation and one sentence of why. DJ picks. Do not present one option as if it were the only one.
- A short "what I was unsure about" note. Name the calls you were not confident in. This is more useful than confidence.
- Nothing else. No summary of this document back at DJ. He wrote it.

## 2. The north star (non-negotiable)

Every post in this series obeys this. It governs your script.

**The goal: build curiosity about what makes this person tick.** Stack specific, insider, lore-rich facts and let the viewer assemble the psychology themselves. The series wins when someone thinks _"I want to know more about this person,"_ not _"that was a clever post."_

**The three words.** Fail any one and it is not a series post:

1. **Lore.** Every fact contributes to a portrait of who this person actually is. Not a hot take, not a moral verdict, not a meme.
2. **Insider.** Things people probably do not know. If 80% of his readers could recite it from his Wikipedia opener, cut it.
3. **Curiosity.** The post must leave a _question_, not a conclusion. **The post is the question. The blog is the answer.**

**Five tests every fact must pass, five for five or cut:**

1. **Insider** — a casual fan would not already know this.
2. **Engine-revealing** — it hints at _how_ he became this person.
3. **Specific** — real name, real date, real number, real quote. "5,127 prototypes" beats "thousands of prototypes."
4. **Scroll-stopping alone** — read in isolation, someone pauses. If it needs setup, it is a paragraph, not a beat.
5. **Opens a question** — it creates a _why_. It does not resolve one.

## 3. The spine — "THE CASE FILE"

Five movements. This is the shape your script must fill.

| #   | Movement          | Window | Job                                                             |
| --- | ----------------- | ------ | --------------------------------------------------------------- |
| 1   | **Cold open**     | 0–4s   | One specific fact that does not fit the person's public image.  |
| 2   | **The frame**     | 4–7s   | Who they are, and the tension. The name lands here, not before. |
| 3   | **The record**    | 7–18s  | Three dated evidence beats. Irregular rhythm.                   |
| 4   | **The turn**      | 18–23s | The reversal _in the evidence_, placed on the retention cliff.  |
| 5   | **Open question** | 23–27s | The question, held. Loops back to frame one.                    |

### Why each movement exists

- **Cold open.** Sound-off viewers decide in about half a second. A title card says "this is an ad." A strange specific fact says "wait, what."
- **The frame.** Only now does the viewer learn who this is. Withholding the name for four seconds is the whole trick; it converts recognition into curiosity.
- **The record.** Three beats, because two feels thin and four overruns the budget. **The rhythm between them must be uneven** (long, short-short, long). Three evenly spaced beats is the loudest machine-made signature there is.
- **The turn at 18s.** There are two retention cliffs: the 1-second scroll-past, and a second drop at 40–60% where the viewer thinks "I get it." The turn is placed exactly on the second cliff to defeat it.
- **The open question.** Ends unresolved, on the frame-one image, so it loops. Loops count as views.

### Rules that keep it from reading as generic

- **No title card. No logo sting. No "Personality Analysis" intro.**
- **The cold open is a fact, never a claim.** "He had eighty jobs before he had one" works. "Robert Greene is a textbook Type 5" does not, and it violates the north star.
- **The turn is an evidence reversal, not a lesson.** Never "here is the trap of Type 5." Show the fact that reverses the picture and let the viewer draw the conclusion.
- **No CTA card.** No "send this to the Type 5 in your life." The CTA lives in the caption where it costs no watch-time.
- **The type label is chrome, not content.** The Reel never argues the type. The blog does that.

### BANNED: the old template

There is a file called `reel-scripts.md` in this directory with a hook → callout → proof → catch → CTA template. **It is superseded. Do not use it, do not imitate it.** It is a growth-funnel shape that _resolves_ the tension, which is backwards for this series. Its fact lists are fine; its structure is not.

## 4. Timing physics — your Stage 1 budget

This is the constraint that decides everything. It is not a guideline.

**Voiceover runs at 2 words per second of elapsed reel time**, pauses included. Conversational speech is faster than that, but a Reel needs air; beats have to land in silence.

> ### **26 seconds = ~52 words. That is your entire script.**

Suggested distribution (adjust, but stay near 52 total):

| Movement      | Window | Words |
| ------------- | ------ | ----: |
| Cold open     | 0–4s   |   6–8 |
| The frame     | 4–7s   |   5–7 |
| The record    | 7–18s  | 20–24 |
| The turn      | 18–23s |  8–10 |
| Open question | 23–27s |   6–8 |

**Hard fail: more than 57 words.** (The gate trips at 2.2 words/sec.) An overstuffed script forces a rushed read, and a rushed read is the fastest way to sound synthetic.

Write for the ear. Contractions. Fragments are fine. Short words beat correct words. Read it out loud before you deliver it; if you run out of breath, it is too long.

### Boundaries you must not cross

These were decided during fact-checking. Violating one is a hard fail, not a note.

- **Do not claim the stroke created his interest in the sublime.** He says the idea predated it by decades. The stroke changed the _terms_, not the subject.
- **Do not present the wasp sting as a cause of the stroke.** Greene called it one element in "a kind of a perfect storm" that also included elevated blood pressure and prednisone. Safest move: leave the sting out entirely.
- **Do not use the father-journalist revelation.** Fact-checking marked its sourcing insufficient.
- **Do not invent a year for the Hollywood incident.** The source gives no calendar year. It happened before the 1995 pitch. That is all you may say.
- **Do not use a specific card count** (the drafted "3,000–4,000 cards" was cut). "Thousands" only.
- **Do not claim one childhood experience explains a life.** Place facts beside one another; let the viewer connect them.
- **Never state the Enneagram type as fact.** It is an editorial interpretation, and the Reel does not argue it at all.

### The turn is already in the material

You do not need to invent the reversal. It is fact 13 + 14 against facts 7–9:

> He spent decades making himself the world's most ruthless student of power. His next book is about wonder, and it had to be the kind you can reach from a bed.

That is the spine of movement 4. Your job is to land it in 8–10 words.

### Two constraints you must design around

1. **Aspect mismatch.** Assets are 3:4. Reels are **1080×1920 (9:16)**. You cannot crop 3:4 to 9:16 without losing a third of the width. Text-heavy slides (02, 03) will not survive a crop; anything from them must be **re-typeset**, not cropped.
2. **The cover cannot be frame one.** `01.png` is a title card (name plus label), and the spine explicitly bans opening on a title card. **Build frame one from the portrait instead**: `static/types/5s/Robert-Greene.webp` is the source cutout, unmodified and reframeable.

**Note the eye bar.** The portrait treatment puts a hand-drawn violet scribble across his eyes. It is genuinely handmade and it is distinctive on the grid, but eyes are the single thing that makes a human face stop a scroll. **This is an open A/B DJ has not run yet.** If your visual plan wants frame one without the bar, say so at Stage 4 and make the argument.
