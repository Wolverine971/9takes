## 9takes Opportunity

- **Pattern observed:** Typology content almost always runs one-type-at-a-time or all-nine-at-once. The middle move — **grouping types by a shared mechanism and treating the group as the unit** — is underused and it changes the reader's job from self-identification to other-navigation. That is a different (and larger) intent: people search "how to deal with X" far more than "am I X."

- **Audience this lands with:** People trying to decode someone else, not themselves. Partners, managers, siblings. This is a colder, larger, more practical audience than the self-typing crowd, and it maps to the "decode / navigate / read" verbs in our brand voice.

- **Possible format:** Port the skeleton to the Enneagram's own native groupings, which are better-established than Berens' Interaction Styles:
  - **Hornevian triads** — assertive (3, 7, 8) / compliant (1, 2, 6) / withdrawn (4, 5, 9). The direct analog: three types who all move the same way toward what they want.
  - **Harmonic triads** — positive-outlook (2, 7, 9) / competency (1, 3, 5) / reactive (4, 6, 8). Three types who all handle disappointment the same way.
  - **Centers** — gut (8, 9, 1) / heart (2, 3, 4) / head (5, 6, 7).
    Same layout: shared-mechanism paragraph, one panel per type, closing row of concrete do-this lines. Three or four panels, **not nine** — the constraint is what makes it legible.
  - **Fix his mistake:** carousel it. One panel per slide. His single-frame version is unreadable in-feed, which is very likely why a 105-like post got 4 comments.

- **Source blog/person tie-in:** Type pages `/personality-analysis/type/N`; the Enneagram Corner type blogs; the "nine scans" framing. A Hornevian-triad post routes cleanly to three type pages at once instead of one.
