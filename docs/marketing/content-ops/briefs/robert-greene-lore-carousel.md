### Evidence and boundaries

- “My parents kind of left me alone a lot” and “almost kind of raised me” are from _The Diary of a CEO_, published March 23, 2023. The mother/anxiety wording is from _Tetragrammaton_ in 2023.
- The Hollywood sequence, “master observer,” and “It’s all material” are from Greene’s 2010 Yale speech, published on his official site. The event itself occurred before his 1995 book pitch; the source does not provide a calendar year, so the slide does not invent one.
- Greene worked at Fabrica in Italy in 1995 and met Joost Elffers there; _The 48 Laws of Power_ was published by Viking in 1998.
- The stroke date, August 17, 2018, is stated by Greene in a later interview. The 2023 _Tetragrammaton_ transcript supplies the independence/dependence and accessible-sublime lines.
- _The Law of the Sublime_ is scheduled for publication by Viking on November 10, 2026. Greene told Rick Rubin that the idea predated the stroke by decades; the carousel does not claim the stroke created his interest in the subject.
- Greene described the wasp sting as one element in a “perfect storm” that also included elevated blood pressure and prednisone. The carousel does not present the sting as a proven single cause.
- The January 9, 2019 _My Morning Routine_ interview explicitly says the stroke was about four months earlier and supplies the breakfast/dishes quote.
- The Ryan Holiday line is taken from Holiday’s own post. The unverified claim that he “never saw Greene yell” was removed during fact-checking.
- The research-process line uses Greene’s directly documented “thousands” of 4×6 cards. The previously drafted 300–400 books / 3,000–4,000 cards range was removed because accounts vary and no clean primary citation was located for the exact range.
- Greene has described the pre-success period with different job totals in different tellings. The slide preserves the exact context of the 2023 statement: at age 37–38, he called the Italy post “one of my 80 different jobs.”
- The closing question is the only 9takes interpretation. Every preceding sentence is a dated event, direct quote, or tightly bounded paraphrase.
- Do not use the father-journalist revelation on Instagram; the intro-arc fact check marked its sourcing insufficient.

## Caption

```text
Robert Greene spent decades writing about power. On November 10, 2026, Viking is scheduled to publish The Law of the Sublime.

Long before the books, Greene says his parents “kind of left me alone a lot.” His sister, he recalled, “almost kind of raised me.” Later, while working in Hollywood, he says he reacted emotionally to a superior—and made himself a promise: “I am just going to become a master observer of the game of power.”

That vow became a way of working. By 37–38, he was deeply discouraged and said “suicidal thoughts” were floating through his mind. Then came the opportunity that became The 48 Laws of Power.

On August 17, 2018, a stroke left his left side paralyzed. The sublime was not a new interest—he says the idea had been with him for decades—but the stroke changed how he approached it. What had once meant distant adventures now had to be accessible “from your chair, from your bed, in your daily life.”

This carousel does not claim that one childhood experience explains a life. It places Greene’s documented words and dated moments beside one another, then leaves the question open:

What makes someone study power this closely—then spend his next chapter studying wonder?

Our 9takes reading is Enneagram Type 5, the Investigator. That is an editorial interpretation based on public material—not a diagnosis or a type Greene has claimed for himself.
```
