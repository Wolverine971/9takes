# “A Window Inside” — Evidence-Led Personality Lore Slide 3

## Purpose

Slide 1 shows the person. Slide 2 builds the lore. Slide 3 lets the public record reveal something about the person’s inner world.

This is not a diagnosis, a neat origin story, or a list of personality conclusions. It is a short evidence trail: dated moments, exact first-person language, visible choices, and—when the record supports it—a change over time. The reader should reach the insight a beat before 9takes names the question.

## Three-slide story

1. **See the person** — recognizable cover, name, and personality lens.
2. **Meet the lore** — specific facts that create tension or contradiction.
3. **Follow the evidence** — formative moment → consequential choice → repeated method → documented evolution.

The caption can carry the link and direct CTA. The third slide ends with a question, not a verdict or marketing furniture.

## The evidence trail

Use four to six chronological records. Every record should contain most of these parts:

1. **When and where** — an exact date when verified; otherwise use an honest range such as `1960s`, `before 1995`, `post-stroke`, or `recalled in 2023`.
2. **What happened** — one concrete event, decision, routine, or consequence.
3. **What they said** — a short exact quote from a primary interview, speech, or their own writing.
4. **What changed next** — the next observable action, practice, work, or shift in subject.
5. **Source cue** — identify the interview, speech, book, or publication year in small type on-slide.

The records should accumulate. Do not translate each one into “wound,” “adaptation,” “strength,” “fear,” “shame,” “anger,” or “superpower.” Those ideas can guide research, but the audience should see the behavior and infer the pattern.

## Interpretation rule

- Keep at least 80% of the slide factual and observable.
- Use no more than one interpretive statement, preferably the closing question.
- Never assign private causality the person has not assigned themselves.
- If a date is inferred, mark it `c.` or use a range.
- If a quote is lightly shortened, use an ellipsis and preserve its meaning.
- Pair claims of evolution with before-and-after evidence; do not merely announce that the person changed.

## Closing question

End by connecting two facts that sit in productive tension:

```text
What makes someone [documented long-term pursuit]—
then [documented later turn]?
```

A strong question is respectful, positive or neutral, and leaves multiple explanations open. It should create curiosity about the person, not tell the reader what is secretly wrong with them.

## Evidence bar

Before the slide advances to design:

- At least two first-person anchors from primary interviews, speeches, or writing.
- At least three verified dates or honest date ranges.
- At least one concrete decision or practice between the early and late moments.
- At least one documented before/after contrast if evolution is claimed.
- Every quote checked word-for-word against its source.
- Every source attached in the production brief, even if the final image uses compact source cues.
- No diagnosis, moral verdict, or certainty about private motives.
- The person would plausibly recognize every stated fact, even if they rejected the implied pattern.

## Voice

Prefer:

- `1960s · Baldwin Hills — Recalling childhood in 2023…`
- `After a superior felt outshone, he made a vow…`
- `Six months later…`
- `In 2023, five years after the stroke…`
- `What makes someone…?`

Avoid:

- `Early weather`
- `Possible adaptation`
- `The strength it built`
- `Where it is turning`
- `Their trauma made them successful.`
- `Deep down, they secretly…`
- therapy language, flattering labels, or a childhood-origin story that explains everything.

## Visual contract

- 1080x1440 (3:4), current Streetlamp V5 system, using `instagram-feed-safe-zones.md`. Reflow to 1080x1350 only for a scheduler compatibility export.
- Eyebrow: `A WINDOW INSIDE`.
- Chronological evidence trail, documentary ledger, or quote mosaic—not analytical panes.
- Every record has a visually distinct date and a compact source cue.
- Four to six records; one event per record.
- Exact quotes are typographically differentiated from surrounding facts.
- Final question receives the only solid amber field.
- Small footer: `Public record, arranged by 9takes.`
- No hard CTA on this slide.
