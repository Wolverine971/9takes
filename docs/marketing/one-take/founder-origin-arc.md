# One Take — Founder Origin Arc

**Status:** Four scripts ready to film
**Created:** 2026-08-13
**Owner / speaker:** DJ Wayne
**Format:** Existing **One Take** series; this is a founder-origin branch, not a new brand concept
**Message source:** [`docs/brand/messaging-hierarchy.md`](../../brand/messaging-hierarchy.md)
**Primary CTA:** Answer one real question before reading the room

## Why these four

The four scripts establish one coherent story without explaining the entire Enneagram:

1. **The marriage loop** makes the personal payoff tangible.
2. **The killed prototype** proves 9takes chose self-discovery over diagnosis.
3. **The Reddit insight** explains the product ritual and movement belief.
4. **The empathy surprise** demonstrates the brand promise through DJ’s own changed mind.

Together they answer: Why DJ? Why this product? Why this mechanic? Why does it matter?

## Production rules

- Phone, eye level, one continuous take, burned-in captions.
- 35–60 seconds. No cinematic intro, logo sting, stock footage, or template animation.
- Start on the first sentence. No “hey guys,” biography, or setup paragraph.
- Speak from the outline; do not memorize punctuation.
- Keep one small human imperfection if the meaning remains clear.
- The Enneagram is the map in the background. The human situation is the foreground.
- End on a question the viewer can answer, followed by the existing product ritual.
- Do not name or type DJ’s wife. Do not identify the original decision or disclose private conflict details.

## Episode 1 — Two different emergencies

**Hierarchy job:** Personal payoff → brand promise  
**Target length:** 50–60 seconds  
**On-screen opening:** `She was afraid of my anger. I was angry at her fear.`

### Script

> Early in my marriage, my wife and I kept having the same kind of fight.
>
> I would see her worried and try to override it. I was angry. I wanted to push through, get the decision over with, and move forward.
>
> The more forcefully I moved, the less safe she felt. The more fearful she became, the angrier I became.
>
> She was afraid of my anger. I was angry at her fear.
>
> Underneath that, I wanted her to trust my judgment. She wanted me to slow down enough to address what she was worried about.
>
> Same situation. Two different emergencies.
>
> When someone you trust is afraid of a decision you believe is right, what do you do?
>
> Answer before the crowd. Link in bio.

### Caption

Your strongest alarm feels like reality. Someone else’s can look like a defect.

I did not need my wife to become less fearful. I needed to understand what her fear was trying to protect. She did not need me to stop feeling anger. She needed evidence that my urgency still had room for her safety.

One question, nine perspectives. Answer before reading the room.

## Episode 2 — I killed the first version

**Hierarchy job:** Personal payoff → value system  
**Target length:** 40–50 seconds  
**On-screen opening:** `The first version of 9takes was personality Facebook. I killed it.`

### Script

> The first version of 9takes was basically personality Facebook. I killed it.
>
> The idea was that you would type people you knew and build a social graph around personality.
>
> The more I worked on it, the more dystopian it felt.
>
> I had spent years in personality forums watching strangers tell other strangers who they were. I had done my share of that too. It never made people curious. It made them defensive.
>
> People do not want you to hand them a label. They want the chance to recognize themselves.
>
> So I stopped building around verdicts and started building around questions.
>
> Personality is a door that opens from the inside.
>
> What is something people often assume about you that they get completely wrong?

### Caption

Self-discovery over diagnosis. Questions over verdicts.

The Enneagram should make people more human, not easier to dismiss.

### Destination

Route to the closest live question about being misunderstood. If none is sufficiently specific and populated, create that question before posting the Reel.

## Episode 3 — One perspective with an audience

**Hierarchy job:** Movement belief → product ritual  
**Target length:** 40–55 seconds  
**On-screen opening:** `Reddit does not always show you what people think.`

### Script

> Reddit does not always show you what people think. It shows you which answer won the room first.
>
> The early comments collect the votes. The best-positioned answer becomes the language everyone else reacts to. Most people read without saying anything. A thoughtful answer that arrives later can disappear at the bottom.
>
> That does not mean the winning answer is wrong. It means popularity is not the same thing as completeness.
>
> The internet collapses nine perspectives into one winning take.
>
> That is why 9takes hides the room until you answer. Your perspective gets a chance to be yours before the crowd can shape it.
>
> Answer first. Then see what you missed.

### Caption

One question, nine perspectives.

The crowd is not the enemy. The design problem is anchoring: once you know what the room rewards, your untouched first read is gone.

### Destination

Route directly to a specific live question, not the homepage or a general blog index. Default: the question currently featured in the homepage hero's `#live-question` section (that is the closest thing to a "featured question" mechanism as of 2026-08-13); confirm it has real answers before posting.

## Episode 4 — The analysis changed me first

**Hierarchy job:** Brand promise → movement payoff  
**Target length:** 40–50 seconds  
**On-screen opening:** `I started profiling famous people as a growth hack.`

### Script

> I started writing personality analyses of famous people as a growth hack.
>
> Then something happened that changed what the work was for.
>
> I would pick someone I did not like, study their childhood, their losses, the contradictions in their life, and the emotional strategy underneath their behavior.
>
> By the end, I almost always liked them more.
>
> Understanding did not mean agreeing with them. It meant their behavior stopped looking random or inhuman.
>
> The analysis generated empathy in me first.
>
> That is what “see the emotions behind every take” means. Before you decide someone is broken, ask what their behavior is trying to protect.
>
> Who is someone you understand better now than you used to?

### Caption

Understanding over judgment does not require agreement. It requires enough curiosity to find the emotional logic.

### Destination

Route to one strong personality analysis whose closing question turns the lens toward the reader. Do not route to a category index.

## Batch-shoot plan

1. Put the four opening lines in a phone note.
2. Record one natural take of each script.
3. Re-record only factual errors or meaning-breaking stumbles.
4. Burn captions and trim dead air; make no other edits.
5. Publish Episode 1 first because it contains the entire 9takes thesis in one human loop.
6. Space the remaining posts 4–7 days apart.

## Measurement

Primary:

- Qualified visits to the linked question
- Answer starts and completed contributions
- Chorus reveals
- Shares or invitations after reveal

Secondary:

- Replies that describe a personal situation
- Saves and sends
- Follows from non-followers
- Completion rate

Likes are diagnostic of distribution, not the definition of success.

## Acceptance gate

- [ ] The destination is a live, populated question or a specific high-quality analysis.
