## What DJ could learn

Joanne describes using the Enneagram to explore recurring emotional and relationship patterns alongside other therapy tools. Her website distinguishes therapy from coaching, and also describes coaching without geographic restrictions. Her latest email confirms only the California therapy offering, so the listing does not advertise broader service availability. [Her approach](https://www.olivemecounseling.com/enneagram-therapy-enneagram-therapist-los-gatos-california).

The useful interview question is what actually changes in the room: what a therapist asks, notices, or does differently when the client brings an Enneagram framework. A hypothetical example could follow someone who automatically agrees to requests, feels resentful afterward, and struggles to name their own needs. Ask Joanne how she would explore that pattern without assuming a type or diagnosis. This is an interview prompt, not an account of her clinical work.

A 2021 systematic review of 104 independent samples found mixed evidence for Enneagram reliability and validity. An article should distinguish a practitioner's experience from evidence that an intervention improves clinical outcomes. [Hook and colleagues, Journal of Clinical Psychology](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/33332604/).

## Suggested next conversation

Start with a 20–30 minute informational conversation for DJ's own understanding. Discuss a possible article openly, and agree on recording and quotation permission before using either. No client identities or identifiable case histories are needed; hypothetical examples are enough.

Questions:

1. What does using the Enneagram actually look like during a session?
2. Can you walk through a hypothetical stuck pattern and explain what you would ask next?
3. What changes when someone knows their type, is unsure, or has mistyped themselves?
4. When is the framework useful, and when do you put it aside?
5. How do you distinguish personality patterns from symptoms requiring clinical assessment?
6. How do you tell whether the work is helping beyond the client recognizing themselves in a description?
7. Where do you draw the boundary between coaching and therapy, and what should a coach refer out?

Possible article angle: **“What Actually Happens When a Therapist Uses the Enneagram?”** Build it around concrete questions and a hypothetical session, followed by limitations and how readers can find appropriate support. This would add something different from the existing type-by-type therapy guides.
