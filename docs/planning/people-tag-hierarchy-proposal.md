# How to group people on 9takes

Editorial proposal for DJ’s review before Jev classifies the catalog.

## The unit of classification

A person can have several **field → specialty** paths. A field answers “What kind of work do people know them for?” A specialty answers “Whose work within that field makes a useful comparison?” Start with the work that made the name recognizable and consider any later career with an independent audience.

Each path carries one of three weights.

| Weight | When to use it | Where the person appears |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Primary | The first career you would use to introduce the person to a reader | The default group on a Type page and the field and specialty pages |
| Joint primary | Two careers could each lead the introduction on their own. Mark both paths joint primary and name a body of work or achievement for each | Both fields have equal standing on the profile and in browse. Pick one display home so an unfiltered Type page counts the person once |
| Secondary | A sustained, publicly recognized second line of work that matters to understanding the career | The profile and the relevant field or specialty filter |

Use another specialty under the same field when appropriate. Someone who acts in and directs films can have both specialties and still count once under Film & TV. A guest appearance, endorsement, former day job or social media account alone does not establish another career.

**Music has two useful ways in.** Default to genre when readers browse musicians. Give musical roles their own filter for vocalist, rapper, instrumentalist, songwriter, composer, DJ or producer. A singer-songwriter can sit beside a producer in the same genre, and a reader can find producers across genres. Add narrower styles such as dance-pop beneath a genre when there is a useful comparison group. A cross-genre artist can have multiple genres. A producer whose work spans many styles can qualify for Music through the producer role alone.

**Creators and hosts share platforms.** If the audience knows the person for teaching, vlogging or gaming, start with Digital creators. If it knows them for conducting interviews, reporting or presenting a show, start with Journalism, broadcasting & hosts. The same distinction puts a sports broadcaster under broadcasting with a sport as their subject. Playing professionally earns a separate Sports path.

## Tags that travel across fields

| Question | Tag examples | How a reader would use it |
| --- | --- | --- |
| What role did they perform? | Founder, executive, talent manager, host, producer, coach | Find founders who work in fashion, technology or film. Record the venture, team or work the role belongs to |
| What format do they regularly make? | Podcast, video interview, livestream, newsletter | Find people who host podcasts across several fields. Appearing as a guest is insufficient |
| Where do they publish? | YouTube, Twitch, TikTok | Find YouTubers across education, entertainment and interviewing |
| What is their subject or industry? | AI, business, sport, beauty, productivity | Find someone who reports on AI alongside someone who builds it, while preserving each person’s profession |

Enneagram type stays an independent filter. A reader can start at Type 3 and narrow to a field, or start at podcast hosts and filter for Type 3. Set each field and format from evidence of the person’s work. A topic, platform, executive title or level of fame is insufficient on its own to establish another field.

## Timeline

Give readers two independent ways to narrow an occupation or personality type by time. **Born in** finds peers by birth year. **Active in** finds people whose defining work belongs to a particular time and place. Both apply to historical and contemporary figures.

| Filter | How to assign it | Examples a reader can select |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Born in | Derive a single birth cohort from a sourced year. Display the year range beside every modern generation name | Silent Generation (1928–1945) · Baby Boomers (1946–1964) · Gen X (1965–1980) · Millennials (1981–1996) · Gen Z (1997–2012). Offer a birth-year range across the whole timeline |
| Active in | Record the dated work, office or achievement that establishes a period. Use its century for broad browsing and an optional named period for closer comparisons | 2nd century CE · 19th century · Ancient Greece → Classical Athens · Ancient Rome → Roman Empire · U.S. history → American founding · U.S. history → Civil War |

Use the modern generation names as shorthand for fixed birth-year bands. They come from a U.S. demographic convention. Show the years for international comparisons and keep other years available by date. A named historical period needs a geographic context and a short scope definition. Two people working in the 19th century can share the century while belonging to different historical settings. [Pew Research Center’s cohort ranges](https://www.pewresearch.org/social-trends/wp-content/uploads/sites/3/2021/03/RE_2021.03.25_Black-Americans_FINAL.pdf)

**How the examples would read**

| Person | Timeline placement | Why a reader might use it |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Marcus Aurelius | Ancient Rome → Roman Empire · 2nd century CE | Find him through government as emperor, or philosophy through *Meditations*. A period label on the result distinguishes him from modern writers. [Stanford Encyclopedia of Philosophy](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/marcus-aurelius/) |
| Robert Greene | Born 1959 · Baby Boomer · active in the late 20th and 21st centuries | A reader comparing writers can select a working period or deliberately compare across centuries |
| George Washington and Abraham Lincoln | U.S. history → American founding for Washington · U.S. history → Civil War for Lincoln | A U.S. history filter brings them together. Its child periods distinguish Washington’s revolutionary command and presidency from Lincoln’s Civil War presidency. [Mount Vernon](https://www.mountvernon.org/george-washington/constitutional-convention/washingtons-constitution) · [National Park Service](https://www.nps.gov/articles/abraham-lincoln-the-war-years-1861-1865.htm) |
| Friedrich Nietzsche | 19th century · German-speaking Europe | The same century filter can find both Nietzsche and Lincoln. Adding Philosophy narrows it to the work the reader wants |
| Martha Stewart, Brad Pitt, Jennifer Lawrence and Ashby Florence | Silent Generation (1941) · Baby Boomer (1963) · Millennial (1990) · Gen Z (2000), respectively | These are birth cohorts. People from different cohorts can have careers in the same decade. Stewart is a local draft and is excluded from the published counts below |

**Dates and uncertainty.** Reuse an existing birth date when available. The timeline only needs a year and can also accept a sourced approximate year, a range or a BCE date without inventing a birthday. Use a cohort only if the entire plausible birth range fits inside it. Preserve the source and precision for each estimate. A person with a documented reign and an uncertain birth year can still appear under the appropriate historical period. Attach multiple active periods when distinct, dated work supports each one. A historical subject in a modern author’s book does not place the author in that period.

## What Jev should do after approval

For each person, propose the paths, their weights and the applicable tags. Give a one-sentence rationale naming the work behind each path and a source for a disputed placement. If two fields are equally strong, mark them joint primary and nominate a display home. When the vocabulary fails, record the nearest existing path and propose the missing label, its parent and the people it would help group. Keep those proposals together for editorial review so one new spelling or one unusual career does not silently create a new category.

For the timeline pass, derive birth cohorts from recorded years first. Have Jev research unresolved years and propose active periods with a dated work and source for each. Start with the historical profiles and a few cross-generation careers to review the period vocabulary. Once approved, apply it across occupations and build the coverage table for editorial planning.
