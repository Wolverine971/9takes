# Seed Takes: The "Friend Criteria" Question (v1 draft, T-41 item G)

Question: "What are your criteria for considering someone a friend?"
Backing question: id 203, `/questions/whats-criteria-considering-someone-friend`

## What these are

Nine anonymous, type-labeled takes for the post-answer reveal on celebrity pages (and anywhere else the chorus for this question renders). Same rules as wave 1 (`docs/content-research/2026-07-15-wave1-seed-takes.md`): no usernames or avatars, never posted as comments or fake accounts, and seeds are scaffolding that the best real take per type replaces over time (DJ curates). Each take is written from a hidden persona (the italic note on the `Type N` line, never displayed; the seed script drops it).

The question text above is the punctuated `question_formatted` from the row; the stored `question` lost its punctuation in the create UI (the asker complained about it in their own answer). The seed script stores this line as `nine_takes.situation`, which the mirror prompt prefers.

## Grounding (read-only production data, 2026-10-03)

8 live human answers on q203. Real criteria cluster on reciprocity ("someone who contacts me to see me, not that the initiative is always from me"), being able to be yourself, being known ("they know what's happening in my life and show they care"), and depth of conversation. Lengths run from one line to a long paragraph, and the long ones are the most self-aware. The seeds below aim for nine visibly different tests for the same word, which is the reveal's whole point: the reader's own test fails most of the room.

**Type 1** _(persona: civil engineer, late 40s, answers like it is going in the record)_

They keep their word on small things. If you say you'll be there at seven, be there at seven, or text at 6:40. Anybody can show up for the big moments; I learn more from whether you returned the ladder. And they will tell me when I am wrong, privately, which I resent for about a day and then respect for years.

**Type 2** _(persona: dental hygienist, early 40s, hosts every holiday)_

Oh, my bar is pretty low honestly, if you let me feed you, you're in. I'll remember your mom's surgery date, I'll drive you to the airport at 5am, that part's easy. Okay but who I'd actually count? Whoever notices when I've gone quiet and asks a second time. Most people take "I'm fine!" and move on, which is fair, I say it very convincingly. Whoever doesn't take it, I'd do anything for. I'd do anything for the others too, but that's different.

**Type 3** _(persona: brand strategist, early 30s, replied from an airport gate)_

Easy to be around, quick to call me out, and happy for me when things go well without making it weird. That last one cuts the list way down. Plenty of people I'd call friends at a dinner. I can name three I'd call if things went badly.

**Type 4** _(persona: hospice music therapist, mid-30s, writes letters by hand)_

Whether I can bring them the unfinished version of a thought. Most people want the edited one, the one with a point, and I've gotten very good at producing it. A friend is someone I can call at eleven at night and say "the light in the kitchen made me sad today and I don't know why," and they don't rush to fix it or tell me to get some sleep. They just ask what kind of sad. I could tell you the exact moment I knew, with each of them. One was in a parked car outside a Waffle House in 2014, which is not where you expect your life to change. Everyone else I love is real too. They just get the edited version.

**Type 5** _(persona: research librarian, 40s, answered in one pass)_

Doesn't require frequent contact. Can sit in the same room doing separate things. Says something I didn't predict at least once a visit. Requirement one eliminates most people. That suits me.

**Type 6** _(persona: 911 dispatcher, late 30s, same three friends since college)_

Would they show up at 2am. That's the whole test, and yes I've had to find out the hard way (flat tire, middle of nowhere, long story). Also how they talk about their other friends when those friends aren't in the room, because I assume I'm getting the same treatment. My sister says I run people through a probation period. Six months, roughly? I don't think that's weird. It might be a little weird. But if you made it through, I'd go to war for you.

**Type 7** _(persona: travel nurse, late 20s, answering in group chat mode)_

anyone who says yes to a dumb plan on 20 minutes notice. criteria met, welcome to the club, we have jackets (we do not have jackets). ok real answer, the keepers are whoever's still in the car when the plan falls apart and we're just sitting in a gas station parking lot sharing gummy worms. that's kind of it. anyway who's free saturday

**Type 8** _(persona: restaurant owner, mid-50s, answered under protest)_

Tell me the truth to my face. Don't fold when it gets hard. I'll do the same for you. I can count them on one hand and I've never needed the other hand.

**Type 9** _(persona: middle school band teacher, early 40s, edited this answer a few times)_

I don't know if I really have criteria? I kind of get along with everybody, my coworkers, the guy at the coffee place, my neighbor who gives me way too many tomatoes. they're all sort of friends. but ok if I'm honest there's a difference between people I like and people I don't have to rearrange myself around... like where I can say "actually I don't want to go" and nothing gets weird. there are maybe two of those. I can disagree with them and the room stays the same temperature. anyway the tomato neighbor is probably a real one too. she just doesn't know I don't like tomatoes.
