## 2. The psychology questions this refresh must answer

Type 8. **Gut center, core emotion anger.** Core fear: being controlled, being harmed, being seen as weak. Core desire: to protect himself and be strong enough that nobody can. **Stress → 5. Integration → 2.**

### The three the audience is literally searching for

**A. "Does iShowSpeed have anger issues?": 402 impressions, and nobody is answering it well.**

This is the highest-value question on the page and it deserves a real answer, not a dodge. The 8's relationship to anger is not a defect to be diagnosed; it is the type's native fuel and its native trap. Frame it as: what is the anger _for_? An 8's rage is usually protective, fast, and over quickly. It discharges rather than accumulates. Is that the pattern in his documented outbursts, or do they look like something else?

Answer honestly. If the record shows escalation that outlasts the moment, say so. **Do not diagnose a disorder**: that is a hard line in the doctrine. Personality pattern, not pathology.

**B. "Is iShowSpeed kind?": this is the integration question in disguise.**

Type 8 integrates to Type 2: the armored protector becomes openly caring. The World Cup year gives you material, the FIFA partnership, the global stadium moments, the way he handles fans in person versus on stream. Is there evidence of the 8-to-2 move, or is the visible warmth still a performance that stays inside the persona's control?

**C. "How does he have so much energy?": this is the 8's body-center relationship to life force.**

Gut types run on physical energy and 8s in particular experience life as something to be met with maximum force. Connect the observable stamina to the type's engine rather than treating it as a personality quirk.

### The event-specific ones

**D. What does the World Cup stage do to an 8's core fear?**
His fear is being controlled and being small. He just performed at the closing ceremony of the largest sporting event on earth, in partnership with FIFA, institutions that control the terms. What does an 8 feel walking into an institutional frame he does not own? Is this arrival, or is it a cage with a good view?

**E. Add to the story, or subtract from it?**
Speed's whole arc is "the loud kid nobody could contain became too big to ignore": that is the existing page's thesis and it ranks. Institutional legitimacy (TIME100, FIFA, WWE) is what happens _after_ you win that fight. Does an 8 know what to do once the fight is over? That tension is the strongest thing available here, and it is the throughline that keeps the piece readable in twelve months.

**F. What did it cost him?**
The 8 buys invulnerability with the refusal to be soft, and pays for it in exhaustion and isolation. If the hospitalization detail verifies, that is a body-center type's body sending the bill. Handle it seriously and without speculation about his health.

**G. Inner dialogue.**
Write the line he is plausibly telling himself standing on that closing-ceremony stage. In his idiom, not yours. This is the most quotable sentence in the piece.

**H. What would the other eight see?**
At minimum: how a **5** reads that stage (exposure, depletion, why would anyone), how a **3** reads it (arrival, the metric finally cashed), and how a **9** reads it (noise). Speed is the most polarizing figure in the corpus by type. Use it.

## 4. Doctrine, condensed

The full version is in [the batch README](README.md) §1. Read it if you have not.

**News is the door. Psychology is the room.** If a paragraph would sit comfortably in People magazine, cut it.

Answer all eight: (1) the feeling underneath, tied to the gut center; (2) his inner dialogue, in his idiom; (3) whether this is evidence **for, against, or complicating** the Type 8 read, say it in the published text, not just your notes; (4) stress (→5) or integration (→2); (5) does it add to or subtract from his arc; (6) what it cost him; (7) what the other eight types would see; (8) what the reader does with it.

**Observable behavior is evidence. Feelings are interpretation. Keep them visibly separate.** "He did X, which reads as Y": never "he felt Y."

No hedge words (`perhaps`, `somewhat`, `might`, `tend to`). No armchair diagnosis. No moralizing. Write so it still reads in twelve months: date-stamp the World Cup once, then stop saying "recently."

## 5. Mechanics

**The push is a two-step. The bare command is a DRY RUN and writes nothing.**

Step 1, preview the diff:

```bash
node scripts/personBlogParser.js IShowSpeed
```

This prints `Dry-run previewing...`, the field-level diff, an expected content hash, and an approval token of the form `--approve-fields=content,description`.

Step 2, apply exactly what you reviewed:

```bash
node scripts/personBlogParser.js IShowSpeed \
  --apply \
  --expected-content-hash=<hash from step 1> \
  --approve-fields=<token from step 1>
```

Both flags fail closed. A hash mismatch or an approved-field list that does not exactly match the dry-run diff aborts the write, which is the guard against a stale preview overwriting someone else's concurrent edit. `--apply` requires an explicit single person slug and cannot be combined with `--dry-run`.

**If you stop after step 1, nothing has been saved.** The dry run's success message is not a confirmation that the page was updated.

Preserves `lastmod`. **Never `--publish`** on a live page: it is the first-release workflow and rewrites `lastmod`, which breaks DJ's manual-lastmod rule. Never hand-edit `lastmod`.

Zero em-dashes. No `<!-- QUALITY GRADE:` or `QUALITY_FEEDBACK` comments left in the file. Valid YAML in FAQ frontmatter (`''` not `\'`). A bad draft fails the whole build.

**Do not retitle toward keywords.** Tested across all 391 pages: keyword titles convert _worse_ (0.391% vs 0.585%). Leave the persona title alone.

No `git stash`, no bulk operations. DJ and other agents are in this repo concurrently.
