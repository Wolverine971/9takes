## ⚠️ Read this before anything else: the neutrality guardrail

This is the most politically charged page in the batch. **9takes does not take political sides, and this refresh must not become a referendum on his politics or his critics'.**

The rules for this page specifically:

- **Analyze the pattern, not the position.** The question is never whether he is right. It is what the _shape_ of his behavior under sustained pressure reveals about how he is wired.
- **Describe controversies neutrally and factually.** "X happened, Y organizations objected, he responded with Z." No adjectives that carry a verdict.
- **Do not adopt either side's framing.** Not his, not his critics'. Both are available in abundance and both are arguments, not evidence.
- **Do not editorialize about the subject matter of the controversies**: ICE, Israel/Palestine, Cuba, 9/11. Name what happened, attribute claims to whoever made them, move to the psychology.
- **If you cannot write a sentence that a reader on either side would call accurate, cut the sentence.**
- **Legal exposure is real here.** This corpus has already published one legal-risk claim. Every factual assertion needs a source, and characterizations of his statements must be attributed, not asserted.

An 8 under sustained institutional attack is genuinely interesting psychological material. That is the piece. Nothing else.

## 2. The psychology questions this refresh must answer

Type 8. **Gut center, core emotion anger.** Core fear: being controlled, being harmed, being made vulnerable by someone else's power. Core desire: self-protection through strength, and protection of whoever he has decided is his. **Stress → 5. Integration → 2.**

**A. The spine: what does an 8 do when punishment is continuous rather than episodic?**

Seven bans. A federal inquiry. A congressional resolution. That is not a controversy; it is a sustained institutional campaign, and it is the exact stimulus an 8's core fear is built around, being controlled by a power he cannot fight directly. The interesting question is not whether the punishment is deserved. It is whether **escalation is the response or the cause**, and whether an 8 in this position can tell the difference.

**B. Does he escalate _after_ punishment, or does punishment follow escalation?**

Build a dated timeline of ban → next action → next ban. This is the highest-value research in the tasker. If provocation reliably increases immediately after each sanction, that is an 8's counter-dependence made visible: the type that cannot let being controlled stand. If the pattern is not that clean, say so. That complicates the read and the doctrine requires you to publish the complication.

**C. Protection of others as the 8's actual engine.**

The January ban happened while he was talking about a nurse killed by federal agents. Whatever one thinks of the framing, the _shape_ is an 8 defending someone weaker against institutional force. That is the type's integration direction, 8 to 2, the protector who genuinely cares, expressed at maximum volume. Is the care the point and the aggression the vehicle, or the reverse? Both readings are defensible; pick one and evidence it.

**D. Stress → 5. What does withdrawal look like for him?**

An 8 under stress goes to 5: isolating, hoarding information, cutting people off, going cold rather than hot. Look for the quiet stretches, after the January ban especially. For an 8, the silences are more diagnostic than the outbursts, and almost nobody covering him looks at those.

**E. The 9/11 display. This is where you must be most careful and most precise.**

Report the object, the date it was noticed, and who objected. Then ask the psychological question and only that: what does it mean for a gut-center type to keep a permanent artifact of the thing he is most attacked for in the frame he broadcasts from every day? An 8 does not remove the thing you told him to remove. That is a type-level observation about defiance, not an endorsement or a condemnation of the object.

**F. What did it cost him?**
The 8 buys autonomy with confrontation and pays for it in permanent siege. Seven bans is a platform relationship in ruins. A federal inquiry is a life change. Name the cost concretely.

**G. Inner dialogue.** The sentence he tells himself the morning after a ban. His idiom is combative and articulate; do not make it self-pitying.

**H. What would the other eight see?** At minimum the **9** (why invite this), the **6** (the institutions exist for a reason and he is burning his own safety), and the **1** (there is a right way to do this and it is not this). This is the most productive nine-takes moment in the batch precisely because readers arrive with a side already picked.

**I. The reader's mirror.** The reader is the person whose response to being told to stop is to do it harder. That is the universal 8 pattern under the politics, and it is what makes the piece useful rather than a news recap.

## 4. Doctrine, condensed

Full version in [the batch README](README.md) §1.

**News is the door. Psychology is the room.** On this page that rule is also the safety rail: the further you stay from the politics and the closer to the pattern, the better the piece and the lower the risk.

Answer all eight: (1) the feeling underneath, gut-center anger and what it is protecting; (2) inner dialogue; (3) evidence **for, against, or complicating** the Type 8 read, in the published text; (4) stress (→5) or integration (→2); (5) arc; (6) cost; (7) what the other eight see; (8) the reader's mirror.

**Observable behavior is evidence. Feelings are interpretation.** On this page, also: **claims are attributed, never asserted.**

No hedge words. No pathology. No moralizing. Which here means no verdict on his politics _or_ on the reaction to them.

## 5. Mechanics

Step 1, preview the diff:

```bash
node scripts/personBlogParser.js Hasan-Piker
```

This prints `Dry-run previewing...`, the field-level diff, an expected content hash, and an approval token of the form `--approve-fields=content,description`.

Step 2, apply exactly what you reviewed:

```bash
node scripts/personBlogParser.js Hasan-Piker \
  --apply \
  --expected-content-hash=<hash from step 1> \
  --approve-fields=<token from step 1>
```

Both flags fail closed. A hash mismatch or an approved-field list that does not exactly match the dry-run diff aborts the write, which is the guard against a stale preview overwriting someone else's concurrent edit. `--apply` requires an explicit single person slug and cannot be combined with `--dry-run`.

**If you stop after step 1, nothing has been saved.** The dry run's success message is not a confirmation that the page was updated.

Preserves `lastmod`. **Never `--publish`** on a live page: it is the first-release workflow and rewrites `lastmod`, which breaks DJ's manual-lastmod rule. Never hand-edit `lastmod`.

Zero em-dashes. No quality-comment markers. Valid YAML in FAQ frontmatter.

Do not retitle toward keywords. Tested corpus-wide, keyword titles convert worse.

### The doctrine conflict worth resolving

The news-refresh doctrine and the creator spec pull opposite ways. A refresh is additive by construction: eight questions to answer, new events to cover. The creator spec is subtractive: cut, compress, one idea. Run refreshes repeatedly against the same page and it inflates every time, which is precisely how this page reached 8,881 words. **The refresh doctrine needs a subtraction step:** what comes out to make room? Recommend adding a word-count ceiling and a "name what you cut" line to the batch README's §1 doctrine before the remaining eleven taskers run.

It runs the other way: the April 14 line preceded the resolution citing it, the MP's campaign preceded the entry ban, and the framed newspaper went up before anyone objected because he announced it himself. Sanctions follow behavior; behavior does not follow sanctions. The read that survives is not "he doubles down" but "the consequence never enters the arithmetic." The framed newspaper is a pre-commitment, not a reaction, which is a stronger claim and better evidenced.
