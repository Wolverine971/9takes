## How this comparison was made

We evaluated each test on five things that can be checked:

1. **What is free:** whether “free” means a usable result or merely starting the questionnaire.
2. **Price transparency:** whether a visitor can see the paid price before investing time.
3. **Test format:** item count, response format, and estimated completion time.
4. **Result depth:** all-type scores, wings, instincts, or workplace guidance.
5. **Evidence transparency:** whether the provider publishes enough methodology to examine its claims.

We did not convert those dimensions into a fake percentage. We also did not assume that more questions automatically means a more accurate type. Item quality, scoring, response bias, and the construct being measured matter too.

There is a second reason for caution. A peer-reviewed [systematic review of 104 independent samples](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/33332604/) found mixed evidence for the Enneagram's reliability and validity. Some individual instruments publish promising results, but that is not the same as proving that every online test identifies a person's “true type” at a known success rate.

## 1. Truity: the best-documented free starting point

[Take the Truity Enneagram test](https://www.truity.com/test/enneagram-personality-test)

Truity says its test has approximately 105 questions and takes 10–15 minutes. The basic result is free and shows how you scored across all nine types. A more detailed report is optional and paid, but the public test page does not state the current price before you begin.

The strongest reason to start here is transparency. Truity publishes an eight-page [technical document](https://www.truity.com/sites/default/files/uploads/EnneagramTechnicalDocument.pdf) based on anonymous data from more than 2.3 million users. It reports internal-consistency statistics for its underlying constructs and tests whether scores predict several relationship-preference variables above chance.

That document is useful, but it has limits:

- It is provider-produced rather than an independent peer-reviewed comparison.
- Its constructs are anonymized by letter because the implementation is proprietary.
- Predicting relationship responses is not the same as proving the top-scoring Enneagram type is correct.

**Choose Truity if:** you want a polished free result, want to see all nine scores, and care about having some methodology to inspect.

**Skip or pause before upgrading if:** your top two or three scores are close. Read those type descriptions first; the free chart may already tell you what to investigate.

## 2. Eclectic Energies: best free option for wings and instincts

[Take the Eclectic Energies tests](https://www.eclecticenergies.com/enneagram/test)

Eclectic Energies offers two free tests. The classical test adapts as you answer: its own methodology page says it asks between 78 and 126 questions, retaining adjacent types so it can estimate a wing. A second, faster test uses pairs of traits and also indicates an instinctual subtype.

The site also explains how it has revised items using response statistics. That is more disclosure than many free quizzes provide, though it is still a provider account rather than an independent validation study.

The tradeoff is presentation. The interface is dated, and the two tests use different formats. If they return different types, that is not proof that one is broken. It is a sign to compare the motivations behind your top results.

**Choose Eclectic Energies if:** you want a usable result without paying, especially if your immediate question is “What is my wing?” or “What is my instinctual subtype?”

**Do not interpret:** agreement between its two tests as a measured probability that the result is correct. The site does not publish a defensible accuracy percentage for that conclusion.

## 3. Cloverleaf: best free workplace-oriented result

[Take the Cloverleaf Enneagram test](https://cloverleaf.me/assessments/enneagram/)

Cloverleaf's current assessment page says the individual test is 100% free, takes about 12 minutes, and provides instant results covering type, wings, triads, growth and stress arrows, and workplace guidance.

This makes Cloverleaf useful for a different question from the other free options. It is designed to turn a result into communication and teamwork advice. If your goal is preparing for a difficult meeting or understanding a team pattern, that emphasis may be more useful than a long personal-growth report.

Cloverleaf says its test is built on the RHETI model and describes it as validated. The public page does not provide the test's item count or a technical manual specific to Cloverleaf's scoring, so this comparison does not translate that marketing claim into an accuracy score.

**Choose Cloverleaf if:** you want a free result framed around work, communication, and collaboration.

**Choose another option if:** you want the clearest public psychometric documentation or a long personal-development report.

## 4. RHETI: the established paid Riso-Hudson option

[View the RHETI test and current price](https://tests.enneagraminstitute.com/orders/create)

The Riso-Hudson Enneagram Type Indicator version 2.5 uses 144 forced-choice pairs and takes about 40 minutes. As of this review, one test code costs $20 and includes a report. The Enneagram Institute says the report contains scores for all nine types and expanded descriptions for the top three.

Two corrections matter:

- The RHETI is not $12 for an individual test today. The $12 figure on the old page was stale volume pricing.
- The $20 RHETI does not include the Instinctual Variant Questionnaire. The IVQ is a separate $20 test; the provider sells the pair for $36.

The Enneagram Institute describes the RHETI as independently validated. The broader peer-reviewed systematic review found mixed evidence for RHETI reliability and validity across versions and studies, so “established” is more defensible than declaring it the undisputed gold standard.

**Choose RHETI if:** you specifically want the Riso-Hudson instrument, prefer forced-choice questions, and value expanded descriptions of your top three scores.

**Do not buy it expecting:** a wing-and-instinct bundle for $20 or certainty that a close score resolves your type.

## 5. iEQ9: the professional-depth option

[View iEQ9 individual reports](https://www.integrative9.com/GetYourType/)

The iEQ9 is a dynamically adaptive 175-question assessment. Its provider estimates 20–35 minutes and sells a 22-page standard report for $60 or a 44-page professional report for $120. The reports cover more dimensions than the other options here, including subtype, wing influence, centers, integration, and stress.

Integrative Enneagram Solutions publishes a [2019 reliability and validity summary](https://www.integrative9.com/media/articles/35/testing-the-ieq9-reliability-and-validity) based on a 5,910-person sample and reports acceptable-to-strong internal consistency across its nine scales. The company commissioned the analysis and distributes the full statistical manual itself.

Its marketing pages also use a 95% figure while referring variously to accuracy, validity, and a confidence interval. Those are not interchangeable statistical concepts, so we do not use “95% accurate” as a head-to-head comparison number.

**Choose iEQ9 if:** a coach or organization will use the detailed report and the extra dimensions justify the price.

**Skip it if:** you only want a first-pass type hypothesis. A free all-nine score is a more proportionate starting point.

## What “most accurate” can honestly mean

We found no independent, current head-to-head trial showing that one commercial Enneagram test identifies core type more accurately than every other test.

You can still compare evidence quality:

- A public technical document is stronger than an unexplained “research-backed” badge.
- Independent peer review is stronger than a provider testimonial.
- Reliability does not automatically prove validity.
- A large sample does not fix a biased or self-selected sample.
- A confidence interval is not an individual correct-typing rate.

The practical goal is not to crown a universal winner. It is to find a small set of plausible types and then test those hypotheses against your repeated motivations, defenses, and reactions over time.

## Red flags when choosing a test

Be cautious when a test:

- promises a precise accuracy percentage without defining how “correct type” was established;
- hides whether the useful result is paid until after you finish;
- calls itself the only official Enneagram test;
- gives no information about its format, scoring, or result depth;
- treats a type result as a mental-health diagnosis;
- recommends using an Enneagram type as a hiring filter.

Short tests are not automatically worthless, and long tests are not automatically valid. A short test can be a useful conversation starter. The problem is presenting a lightweight result as certainty.

## How to use your result without getting trapped by it

1. **Keep your top three scores.** A close second-place type often contains the real question.
2. **Compare motives, not adjectives.** “Helpful,” “ambitious,” and “private” can arise from several different motives.
3. **Answer for your normal pattern.** A crisis, new relationship, or unusually demanding job can distort self-report answers.
4. **Read disconfirming evidence.** Ask what does not fit instead of collecting only flattering matches.
5. **Retest later if the scores are close.** Stability matters more than one dramatic result.
6. **Use observation to validate.** Our [beginner's guide to finding your Enneagram type](/enneagram-corner/beginners-guide-to-determining-your-enneagram-type) walks through that process.

If wings are the confusing part, use the [complete Enneagram wings guide](/enneagram-corner/enneagram-wings-complete-guide). If instinct results are driving the disagreement, read the [instinctual subtypes guide](/enneagram-corner/enneagram-instinctual-subtypes) before treating a stacking label as settled.

## Frequently asked questions

### What is the best free Enneagram test?

Truity is the best-documented free starting point in this comparison because it provides basic scores across all nine types and publishes technical documentation. Eclectic Energies is the better free choice if you specifically want a wing or instinctual-variant result.

### What is the most accurate Enneagram test?

We found no independent head-to-head study proving that one current commercial test is the most accurate. RHETI, Truity, and iEQ9 publish different kinds of supporting material, but their statistics are not directly comparable. A 2021 systematic review found mixed evidence for Enneagram reliability and validity overall.

### Is the Cloverleaf Enneagram test actually free?

As of August 1, 2026, Cloverleaf's public page says its individual Enneagram test is 100% free and includes instant results for type, wings, triads, arrows, and workplace insights.

### How much does the RHETI Enneagram test cost?

As of August 1, 2026, one RHETI test code costs $20. The separate Instinctual Variant Questionnaire also costs $20, or both can be purchased together for $36.

### Is the Truity Enneagram test free?

Truity is free to take and provides basic results showing scores across all nine types. It offers an optional paid report, but its public test landing page does not state the current report price before you begin.

### Why do different Enneagram tests give different results?

Tests use different items, response formats, and scoring rules. Your mood, self-image, interpretation of a question, and closeness between several types can also change the result. Treat disagreement as a reason to compare motives and observe patterns, not as proof that one result must be accepted.
