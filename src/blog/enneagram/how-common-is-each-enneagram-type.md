<QuickAnswer question="How common is each Enneagram type?">
Nobody knows for the general population: we found no study that tested a representative sample. In the two large public samples that report all nine types, every type lands between 4.6% and 16.5% of test-takers. <strong>Most common: Type 9</strong> in the 200,000-person enneagram-personality.com sample (16.5%). Truity's sample of 54,000+ puts Type 8 first (15%) and Type 9 second (13%). <strong>Rarest: Type 5</strong> in the 200,000-person sample (4.6%), Type 7 at Truity (9%). Each chart describes the people who took that one test.
</QuickAnswer>

<p class="firstLetter">Take Truity's free Enneagram test, score as an Eight, and you've joined the most common group Truity measured: about 15% of more than 54,000 test-takers. Take the test at enneagram-personality.com, score Eight again, and you're in that site's second-rarest group: 6.3% of 200,000.</p>

Same type, opposite verdicts. Neither site made a mistake. Each one counted the people who came through its own door, and different doors draw different crowds.

That's the catch inside every Enneagram statistic, and it's why the answer to "what's the rarest type?" changes with the page you land on. Below are the type percentages we could verify, the places the charts agree, the five reasons they split, and what 9takes' own 450 public-figure profiles can add. (Less than you'd hope, and we'll say exactly why.)

---

## Enneagram type percentages: the two complete public charts

We found two sources that publish a percentage for all nine types along with a sample size.

**Truity** reports each type's share on its type pages, from what it calls "a Truity study of more than 54,000 respondents." Its articles on that survey date to October 2022. **enneagram-personality.com** publishes a full table from "100,000 men and 100,000 women" who took its test between January 20, 2025 and April 4, 2026. The page was last updated April 7, 2026.

## Where the charts agree, and where they flatly don't

**They agree on two things.** No type dominates: the biggest share in either chart is 16.5%, fewer than one test-taker in six. And [Type 9](/enneagram-corner/enneagram-type-9) is common in both, at 13% and 16.5%.

**They disagree on three types, by a lot.**

- **[Type 8](/enneagram-corner/enneagram-type-8):** Truity's most common type (15%) is enneagram-personality.com's second rarest (6.3%). Truity's figure is more than double.
- **[Type 5](/enneagram-corner/enneagram-type-5):** 10% at Truity, 4.6% at enneagram-personality.com. Truity's own rarest-type article still calls Fives rare, at "around 10 percent, to as low as 6 percent on some online forums and surveys."
- **Type 7:** Truity's rarest type (9%) is the third most common at enneagram-personality.com (13.6%).

Then there's gender. Both sources split their numbers by women and men, and they don't agree on which types lean which way.

Truity finds no gender gap among Ones, and enneagram-personality.com finds a five-point one.

One finding survives both charts: **Type 5 is the rarest type among women** in each, at 7% and 4%.

## Why the charts disagree: five ways a percentage moves

### 1. Different people walk in the door

Every large Enneagram dataset is self-selected. It counts the people who found that site and chose that test, or who were asked to take it at work or in class. How a test gets found shapes who shows up.

Cloverleaf, whose test is built around teamwork and workplace results, shows what a different door does. It reports Type 1 at 15% and Type 2 at 14% of more than 883,560 Cloverleaf Enneagram respondents. Both figures are higher than Truity's (10% and 11%) and enneagram-personality.com's (11.5% each). Cloverleaf publishes only those two numbers, so its other seven types can't be ranked.

A workplace tool samples people at work. A free quiz site samples people curious enough to search for one. Neither group is "everyone."

### 2. Different tests sort people differently

Each vendor writes its own questions and its own scoring. A [2021 systematic review](https://doi.org/10.1002/jclp.23097) of 104 independent samples in the _Journal of Clinical Psychology_ found mixed evidence for the Enneagram's reliability and validity. Factor analyses typically found fewer than nine factors, and as of that review, no study had used clustering methods to derive the nine types. So every chart on this page starts by assuming nine bins, then counts how full each one is. (More on what the research does and doesn't support in our [look at the Enneagram's scientific validity](/enneagram-corner/mental-health/enneagram-science-mental-health).)

The test itself can swamp everything else. In a [2025 study](https://doi.org/10.3390/bs15030312), researchers typed 164 computer-engineering students at a Spanish university, about four in five of them men, with a two-question short form of the Riso-Hudson test. Helpers came out on top: 50 of the 164, about 30%. Investigators numbered 8, about 5%. Set that beside Truity's finding that 7% of men are Twos.

Maybe that classroom really was full of Helpers. Our read is simpler: a two-question test has a shape of its own. If you're picking a test, our [honest comparison of free Enneagram tests](/enneagram-corner/enneagram-test-comparison-2026) covers what each one actually measures.

### 3. Different rules for who gets counted

The enneagram-personality.com sample is filtered three ways: unique IP addresses, anti-bot checks, and a top type score that must beat the second-highest by more than 6%. That's a reasonable quality rule. It also throws out every close call.

We don't know how many results that rule removed or which types they belonged to. But if some types tend to produce close scores, a rule that drops close scores will undercount them, and the chart will look tidier than the people who took the test.

### 4. A different mix of women and men

Gender alone can move a type several points. On enneagram-personality.com's own numbers, an all-female sample would show Type 4 at 15%, and an all-male sample would show it at 9%. Type 1 runs the opposite way, 9% versus 14%.

The enneagram-personality.com sample sidesteps this with exactly 100,000 of each. Truity doesn't publish the gender mix of its 54,000 respondents, but its numbers hint at one. For the three types with the biggest gender gaps (Two, Five and Eight), Truity's overall share lands at or near the midpoint of its women's and men's figures, which is what a roughly even split would produce.

If that holds, gender explains little of the gap between these two charts. It matters more when one sample skews hard one way, like the Spanish classroom above, which was about four-fifths men.

### 5. A different year

Other sites still quote an older version of the enneagram-personality.com table, built from 189,957 test-takers. They cite it at enneagram.bz, an address that now redirects to the current distribution page. Hiring-software blogs were already quoting it by October 2023. Here is how it compares with today's version:

## What 9takes' 450 public-figure profiles show, and what they can't

Start with the limit. **The 9takes corpus does not tell you how common any type is.**

Nobody in it took a test. Each profile is our read of a public figure, built from interviews, decisions and the public record, so it's a typing by 9takes rather than a self-report. And fame did the sampling: we write about people the public is already searching for, which filters for visibility before any typing starts.

Our own lens is in the data too. If our typing tends to read visible ambition as Type 3, the corpus overcounts Threes, and we can't rule that out from inside our own numbers.

With that on the table, here is the split across the 450 profiles published as of early October 2026:

What the corpus can show is which types each kind of fame puts in front of an audience, in our reading:

- **Comedians:** 15 of 35 read as Sevens (43%). The patterns behind that are in our [comedy Enneagram breakdown](/pop-culture/comedy-kings-enneagram-analysis).
- **Musicians and artists:** 31 of 83 read as Fours (37%). Here's [why so many musicians type as Fours](/pop-culture/what-enneagram-type-are-most-musicians).
- **Tech, founders and business:** 19 of 80 read as Fives (24%).
- **Film and TV:** 5 of 175 read as Fives (3%). Music is thinner still, with 1 Five in 83.

The most interesting contrast sits at the bottom of the table. Ones and Twos are our rarest types, 29 profiles each. Cloverleaf's workplace sample, meanwhile, puts them at 15% and 14%, above both big quiz-site samples.

One reading: the habits that make someone a steady colleague rarely make them famous. Another: our typing undercounts Ones and Twos. Both fit the data, and we wouldn't bet on either yet.

So treat the corpus as a source of patterns worth asking about. Counting the population is a job it can't do. The live numbers, refreshed on every deploy, are on the [9takes corpus stats page](/corpus-stats).

## So what is the rarest Enneagram type?

If you need one answer, **Type 5 is the type named rarest most often.** It's the rarest in enneagram-personality.com's 200,000-person sample (4.6%), and the rarest among women in both large samples.

It isn't a clean win. Truity puts Fives at 10%, ahead of its rarest type, Seven, at 9%. Among men, the two samples split: Truity's rarest is Type 2 (7% of men), while enneagram-personality.com's is Type 5 (5%).

"Rare" is relative, too. The lowest share in either big sample, 4.6%, is still about one test-taker in 22.

Search "rarest Enneagram type" and page after page names a single winner, usually Type 5, sometimes Type 7. Several honest measurements get collapsed into one winning take, and the disagreement, which is the most informative part, disappears.

## Why a type can feel rare in your own life

Maybe you landed here because there's a type you almost never seem to meet. Two things are worth checking before you decide it's rare.

**Your life is a self-selected sample too.** Your job, your city and your friend group filter who you meet, the same way a test site filters who takes its test. If our profiles are any guide, a comedy club and a room full of founders would put you around very different types.

**What you see is behavior, and behavior hides the type.** The same behavior runs on different motives. The quiet coworker you'd file as a Five might be a Nine keeping the peace or a Six watching for risk. "I never meet Fives" can mean "I never meet anyone who acts like my picture of a Five."

That's why you can't type someone from one behavior, and why our [guide to finding your Enneagram type](/enneagram-corner/beginners-guide-to-determining-your-enneagram-type) starts with motives.

**Try this today:** name the type you think you almost never meet. Then think of two people you know well whose reasons (what they protect, what they chase) might fit it, even if their manners don't. Ask one of them which type they'd guess for themselves. The "rare" type may have been in the room all along, filed under a different number.

## How to check an Enneagram statistic before you repeat it

1. **Who took it?** Online volunteers, employees, students, and famous people typed by someone else are four different populations.
2. **How many, and when?** The same website put Type 4 at 15.0% in one snapshot and 12.2% in the next.
3. **Which test?** A two-question short form and a full-length test won't sort people the same way.
4. **What got filtered out?** Close calls, bots and repeat visitors all change the shape.
5. **What's the gender mix?** It can move a type several points on its own.
6. **Does it claim to describe "the population"?** A self-selected sample can't speak for one.

## Frequently asked questions

### What is the most common Enneagram type?

Type 9 is the most common type in the largest public sample that reports all nine types: 16.5% of 200,000 test-takers at enneagram-personality.com (2025 to 2026). Truity's sample of more than 54,000 puts Type 8 first at 15%, with Type 9 second at 13%. Both are self-selected online test-takers, so neither number describes the general population.

### What is the rarest Enneagram type?

It depends on the sample. Type 5 is the rarest at enneagram-personality.com (4.6% of 200,000 test-takers). Type 7 is the rarest at Truity (9% of more than 54,000). Type 5 is the type most often named rarest, but we found no representative population study that settles it.

### What is the rarest Enneagram type for females?

Type 5. It is the rarest type among women in both large public samples: 7% of women at Truity and 4% of women at enneagram-personality.com. The two samples disagree about men. Type 2 is the rarest among men at Truity (7%), and Type 5 is the rarest among men at enneagram-personality.com (5%).

### What percentage of people are each Enneagram type?

Nobody knows for the general population. In the two large public samples that report all nine types, every type falls between 4.6% and 16.5% of test-takers. Type 9 runs from 13% to 16.5%, Type 8 from 6.3% to 15%, and Type 5 from 4.6% to 10%, depending on the sample.

### Why do Enneagram type statistics disagree?

Each dataset counts a different group of people with a different test. Samples differ in who chooses to take the test, how the test is written and scored, which results get filtered out, the mix of women and men, and the year the data was collected. Any one of those can move a type's share by several points.

### Does the 9takes corpus show how common each Enneagram type is?

No. As of October 2026, the 9takes corpus is 450 public figures typed by 9takes from their public record. Type 3 is the largest group (18.0%), and Types 1 and 2 are the smallest (6.4% each). That describes who gets famous enough to profile, as 9takes reads them. It says nothing about how common any type is in the population.

## The disagreement is the useful part

Every Enneagram chart is an honest answer to a narrower question than the one you searched: how did this test sort these people? Put two of them side by side and you learn more than either one tells you alone, because the gaps show where the measuring does the work.

9takes runs on the same idea at human scale. One question, nine perspectives. You answer before the crowd, so the room can't pick your answer for you, and then you see how other types read the same thing. If the nine types are real, the takes should sound different by type, and that's something you can check one question at a time.

So check it. Answer this before you read anyone else's take:

**Which type do you almost never meet, and why do you think that is?**
