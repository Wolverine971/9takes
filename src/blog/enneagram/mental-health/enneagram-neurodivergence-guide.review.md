# Safety Review Record: Enneagram and Neurodivergence Guide

**Review date:** 2026-08-13

**Scope:** Body, QuickAnswer, disclaimer, visible FAQ, FAQPage JSON-LD, frontmatter, related links, metadata sidecar, and social sidecars

**Method:** Two cold editorial passes against current independent clinical, employment, and autistic-led sources

These are adversarial editorial reviews, not a medical, legal, or lived-experience endorsement. No clinician, lawyer, or external disability-community reviewer participated in this implementation.

## Pass 1: Skeptic and clinical-boundary review

### Questions applied

1. Could any sentence be read as saying an Enneagram type causes, predicts, intensifies, masks, confirms, or rules out a condition?
2. Does any behavior example infer ADHD, autism, dyslexia, DCD, sensory status, giftedness, or a type?
3. Is a community or education term presented as a formal diagnosis?
4. Does the article recommend medication, treatment, evaluation, disclosure, or an accommodation for an individual?
5. Is a U.S. workplace rule presented as universal or guaranteed?
6. Do the QuickAnswer, body, FAQ, and structured data make the same claims?
7. Do social drafts restore a mapping or prescription removed from the article?

### Findings and dispositions

- **Type-condition mapping:** None remains. The page directly rejects type-based diagnosis and prevalence claims.
- **Behavioral inference:** None remains. Examples list multiple possible functions and interpretations, then tell the reader not to infer the cause from the behavior.
- **Diagnostic terminology:** ADHD, autism, dyslexia, and DCD are presented as formal conditions. Executive dysfunction is a functional description. Sensory processing differences are descriptive. 2e is an education term. RSD is community shorthand. Masking is a variably defined research and community term.
- **Medication and treatment:** Properly bounded. The page says not to start, stop, or select medication by type and routes individual decisions to a qualified prescriber.
- **Evaluation:** Properly bounded as an option that may help. The page does not promise certainty, require testing, or treat access as equal.
- **Accommodation:** Examples are explicitly options to discuss, test, or adapt by function. They are not represented as automatic legal entitlements or type-specific prescriptions.
- **Disclosure and law:** Properly labeled as U.S. federal guidance, with personal choice, confidentiality, documentation limits, jurisdictional variation, and legal-advice boundaries.
- **Autism stereotypes:** No claim remains that autistic people lack empathy, social understanding, flexibility, or a particular personality. Eye contact, directness, routine, sociability, and emotional intensity are explicitly rejected as diagnostic shortcuts.
- **Cross-surface consistency:** Six visible FAQ questions and answers exactly match six valid JSON-LD entities. The QuickAnswer and body use the same non-diagnostic boundary.
- **Social sidecars:** No diagnosis invitation, type-condition profile, medication advice, or universal accommodation claim remains.

**Pass result:** No diagnostic, causal, predictive, or prescriptive overreach found after the rebuild.

## Pass 2: Disability-language and representativeness review

### Questions applied

1. Does the article describe disabled people as broken, deficient, inspirational, or superhuman?
2. Does it use functioning labels, euphemisms, or moral judgments for access needs?
3. Does it universalize one autistic or neurodivergent experience?
4. Does it treat masking, sensory needs, communication, empathy, giftedness, or rejection distress as uniform?
5. Does it invent first-person experience or use advocacy as clinical evidence?
6. Does it acknowledge access barriers and individual language preferences?

### Findings and dispositions

- **Identity language:** The page uses autistic identity-first language in line with autistic self-advocacy and explicitly respects individual preferences.
- **Labels and euphemisms:** No high-functioning, low-functioning, different abilities, special needs, lazy, broken, or superpower framing remains.
- **Support needs:** Needs are described by function and context. Lack of a formal diagnosis is not used to invalidate difficulty or support needs.
- **Representativeness:** Variation in communication, sensory experience, behavior, abilities, and support needs is explicit. Masking definitions and samples are described as limited rather than universal.
- **Stereotypes:** The guide does not assign empathy, social understanding, rigidity, intelligence, or giftedness to a condition or Enneagram type.
- **Source roles:** Official and peer-reviewed sources support clinical claims. Autistic-led sources support language choices and disability framing. One is not substituted for the other.
- **Lived experience:** No fabricated first-person account or claim of community consensus appears.
- **Access:** Cost, geography, race, gender, age, clinician bias, and masking are named as possible recognition and assessment barriers without claiming identical effects for every person.

**Pass result:** No demeaning, universalizing, or type-linked disability language found after the rebuild.
