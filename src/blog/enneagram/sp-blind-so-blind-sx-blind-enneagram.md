<QuickAnswer question="What do SP blind, SO blind and SX blind mean?">
"Blind" names the last instinct in an Enneagram stacking. <strong>SP blind</strong> (so/sx or sx/so) means self-preservation comes last: money, sleep, food and health admin slide until they turn into a crisis. <strong>SO blind</strong> (sp/sx or sx/sp) means the social instinct comes last: group standing goes untracked until a decision gets made without them. <strong>SX blind</strong> (sp/so or so/sp) means the one-to-one instinct comes last: life runs steady, and very few people ever see them at full intensity. The instinct still exists. It just waits for a crisis before it gets attention.
</QuickAnswer>

<p class="firstLetter">Three people, same Tuesday. One opens a shutoff notice from the electric company while planning a friend's surprise party. One learns the team picked a new lead last week, and everyone seems to have seen it coming except him. One hears her partner of six years say, quietly, "I don't think I've ever seen you really want anything." None of them is careless or cold. Each one has a blind spot in a different instinct.</p>

The Enneagram's [instinctual subtypes](/enneagram-corner/enneagram-instinctual-subtypes) describe three survival drives: self-preservation (SP), social (SO) and one-to-one, also called sexual (SX). A stacking ranks them, and the last one is the blind spot. The overview page covers the basics and a fix for each. This page covers what the overview doesn't: the head-to-head comparisons, four checks for finding yours, public figures we've read with each blind spot, and where the theory is weaker than its fans admit.

| Blind spot   | Stackings    | What gets neglected                                      | How it usually surfaces                                  | Who notices first              |
| ------------ | ------------ | -------------------------------------------------------- | -------------------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------ |
| **SP blind** | so/sx, sx/so | Money, sleep, meals, checkups, paperwork                 | An overdraft, a burnout, a health scare                  | A partner or roommate          |
| **SO blind** | sp/sx, sx/sp | Reputation, alliances, unwritten group rules             | Passed over, left out, "how did I miss that?"            | Coworkers, the friend group    |
| **SX blind** | sp/so, so/sp | Intensity, chemistry, the conversation that goes too far | A relationship that works on paper and runs at room temp | A partner, or nobody for years |

## What "blind" means, and three things it doesn't

Katherine Fauvre, who says the three-part stacking came out of her 1994 study, describes the third instinct as one that "is often neglected or avoided." The Enneagram Institute's instinct questionnaire ranks them "from your most developed Instinct to your least developed Instinct." Least developed is the key phrase. The blind instinct still works. It never volunteers.

That clears up the three most common misreadings.

**Blind doesn't mean bad at it.** An SP-blind accountant can run a company's books perfectly and forget to file her own taxes for two years. Skill shows up when someone else's system demands it. The blind spot shows up in what a person does with no reminder.

**Blind doesn't mean dislike.** Fauvre notes that someone can be "positively identified" with their least preferred instinct. The SO-blind man who talks about wanting a tight neighborhood like the one in his childhood has muted the block's group chat since March.

**Blind doesn't mean absent from a life.** Plenty of SP-blind people have tidy finances because a parent or partner runs them. The gap shows when that person leaves. A breakup, a move or a new roommate often reveals a blind spot that was there the whole time.

## SP blind: the bill arrives all at once

[IShowSpeed](/personality-analysis/ishowspeed) is the loudest public example we've profiled. We read him as an sx/so Eight with self-preservation last: he has lit fireworks indoors, streamed 35 days straight across America and taken a spear through a table on camera. A self-preservation Eight guards his body and resources first. Speed spends his to keep the arena full.

The private version is quieter. Here is what it looks like in a kitchen, not on a stream:

- **Open loops pile up.** Unopened mail with a bank logo. A car registration that expired two months ago. A prescription that ran out Thursday, refill still not called in on Monday.
- **The body runs on borrowed time.** Coffee for breakfast, a phone at 1 a.m., a dentist last seen two jobs ago.
- **Calm in the emergency, gone for the upkeep.** The burst pipe at midnight gets handled. The weekly budget review never happens.
- **The last forty dollars goes to a friend's cover charge.** The weekend meant for rest goes to helping someone move.

**Worry is not the same as attention.** An SP-blind person can lose sleep over a bill they still haven't opened. The tell is the gap between how much they worry and how little they act.

[Madison Beer](/personality-analysis/madison-beer) shows what the fix looks like from outside. We read her as an sx/so Four, and her self-preservation reads as structure she built on purpose: therapy three times a week, a 10 p.m. bedtime, phone-free swims. That is the overview page's advice in practice. The instinct didn't wake up. She bolted rails onto her week so it wouldn't have to.

## SO blind: you find out where you stood after the vote

[Pete Davidson](/personality-analysis/pete-davidson) is the clean case. We read him as an sx/sp Nine. He is off social media entirely, skips Hollywood's networking rituals, and has called the whole scene "a trap" where "everybody can smell it on you." When an SO-blind Nine pulls back from the room, he pulls back all the way.

[Joe Rogan](/personality-analysis/joe-rogan) shows the working version. We read him as an sp/sx Eight whose social instinct is weakest. He built the Mothership comedy club, then ran it as a stage for his specific friends instead of as a scene. He endorses candidates but doesn't stay inside any movement for long. Loyal to Joey Diaz, Cam Hanes and Tom Segura, indifferent to "the comedy community" as a thing.

Off camera, SO blindness looks like this:

- **Knowing the org chart only one level up.** Ask who really sets the department's budget and get a shrug.
- **Learning the rules by breaking them.** The reply-all that shouldn't have been. The blunt comment in a meeting where everyone else agreed beforehand to stay quiet.
- **Hearing their reputation secondhand.** "Aloof," "intense" and "not a team player" arrive after they've already stuck.
- **Missing when the mood turns.** A group sours on a plan for six weeks. To the SO-blind person it happens on a Thursday.

**Introverts aren't automatically SO blind.** An introvert can be fiercely SO-dominant: quiet at the party, and still able to tell you on the drive home who came with whom and who left early. **Neither are contrarians.** Opposing the crowd takes close study of the crowd. An SO-blind person usually can't say what the crowd thinks at all.

## SX blind: plenty of warmth, very little voltage

SX blindness (sp/so and so/sp) is the hardest to spot, because nothing looks broken. The SX instinct runs chemistry, fascination and the pull toward one person or one obsession. When it comes last, life runs steady and a little flat.

[Sydney Sweeney](/personality-analysis/sydney-sweeney) is our most interesting example, because her public image is the opposite of her stacking. We read her as an sp/so Three: the water-only rule, the financial anxiety, the production company built as a financial moat. The SX instinct sits last. She has repeatedly described her body as a cage, with Cassie's nudity in _Euphoria_ becoming her permanent public identity. It reads like an SX-blind person watching the world treat her as SX-dominant.

At home, SX blindness shows up as:

- **Dating by checklist.** Kind, stable, good with money, same values. Then a vague sense that nothing caught fire.
- **Managing intensity down.** Long eye contact, a fight that needs finishing, a conversation going somewhere raw at midnight: a joke, a subject change, a suggestion that everyone get some sleep.
- **No obsession to name.** Asked what they could talk about for three hours, they list hobbies. None of them is a fixation.
- **A partner saying "I never get all of you."** The SX-blind person is baffled, because they show up for every dinner and every bill.

**SX is not sex drive.** An SX-blind person can have an ordinary sex life. Someone asexual can be strongly SX-dominant about a friendship, a craft or an idea.

**The bill comes slowly.** SP blindness bills in a crisis and SO blindness in a single bad decision. SX blindness bills in years: a fine marriage, fine friendships, then a restlessness that surfaces sideways as an out-of-character crush or a sudden urge to quit everything.

## SP blind vs SX blind vs SO blind, head to head

The fastest way to separate them is the pair of instincts a person does use.

| If they're... | They run on                    | Life centers on                           | They'd sacrifice                    |
| ------------- | ------------------------------ | ----------------------------------------- | ----------------------------------- |
| SP blind      | Social + One-to-one            | People: the room and the person in it     | Sleep, money, meals                 |
| SO blind      | Self-preservation + One-to-one | Home and a few people: the private world  | Reputation, networking, group roles |
| SX blind      | Self-preservation + Social     | Stability and community: the steady world | Intensity, chemistry, deep merging  |

### SP blind vs SX blind

Both can be friendly, socially busy people. The difference shows at 1 a.m., when a conversation with someone new is getting real and work starts at eight. The SP-blind person stays until three and drags through the next day. The SX-blind person says "let's continue this sometime," goes home and sleeps eight hours. They rarely continue it.

SP-blind people trade upkeep for intensity. SX-blind people trade intensity for upkeep. Friends who nag about health point to SP blind. Partners who say "you're holding back" point to SX blind.

### SP blind vs SO blind

Here the confusion comes from a shared SX-dominant start: sx/so (SP blind) and sx/sp (SO blind) both pour themselves into one person or one passion. IShowSpeed and Pete Davidson make the contrast. Speed takes the intensity out into the biggest room he can find and spends his body doing it. Davidson took his into a quieter life outside New York, with rehab pauses, DBT workbooks and a decade-long tattoo removal.

The test: a free Saturday with their person. The SP-blind one reaches for the party. The SO-blind one reaches for the couch and a locked door.

### SX blind vs SO blind

These two often share an SP-dominant start: sp/so (SX blind) and sp/sx (SO blind). Both are practical and both keep a stable home base. Watch them at a dinner party of eight.

The SX-blind guest works the table, checks that everyone has a drink and keeps the talk broad, warm and safe. The SO-blind guest finds the one interesting person in the corner and stays there for three hours. On the drive home, the SX-blind guest knows how everyone is doing. The SO-blind guest knows one person's whole life story and couldn't name who else was there.

## How to tell which one you are

A dominant instinct is hard to find because it feels like plain reality. A blind spot leaves receipts. Four checks, from most to least reliable:

**1. The bill test.** Look back three years. Which of these cost real money, health or an opportunity?

- A late fee, a skipped checkup that wasn't skippable, a burnout. That points to SP blind.
- A promotion, invitation or vote that went the other way without warning. That points to SO blind.
- A relationship that drifted flat, or someone who said they never really knew you. That points to SX blind.

**2. The complaint test.** Write down what the closest people in your life complain about, in their exact words. "Did you eat today?" points to SP. "People think you're arrogant" points to SO. "Where are you, really?" points to SX.

**3. The boredom test.** Retirement accounts and meal prep. Office politics and who's feuding with whom. A long talk about what two people really mean to each other. The one that makes attention drift is usually the blind instinct.

**4. The admiration check.** Some people idealize their least-used instinct, so envy of the friend who has it easy is weak evidence alone. It counts when it matches checks one through three.

**Three things fake a blind spot:**

- **Life stage.** New parents look SX blind for a year. Founders and med students look SP blind on purpose.
- **Forced competence.** A kid who ran the household budget at twelve may grow up good with money and still never check the balance unprompted.
- **Core type.** Some types imitate a blind spot on their own, covered next.

## How each blind spot shows up in different types

Core type and blind spot combine. These pairings are our read, not measured findings, and the [core type](/enneagram-corner/enneagram-tldr) still comes first.

### SP blind across types

- **[Type 7](/enneagram-corner/enneagram-type-7):** [Caleb Hearon](/personality-analysis/caleb-hearon), whom we read as so/sx, splits his time between Brooklyn and Kansas City instead of settling one base, and gives his appetite to the room. A self-preservation Seven protects his own comforts first. Hearon hosts. Impulsive spending alone doesn't make a Seven SP blind. A Seven who keeps the pantry stocked and the travel points maxed probably isn't.
- **[Type 2](/enneagram-corner/enneagram-type-2):** knows when every friend last ate and skips lunch three days running. The crash usually lands right after a stretch of caring for someone sick.
- **[Type 4](/enneagram-corner/enneagram-type-4):** spends the rent on the guitar and calls it integrity: "I'm not going to live for a paycheck."
- **[Type 8](/enneagram-corner/enneagram-type-8):** treats the body as a tool that doesn't break. The doctor's visit happens when a partner books it.

### SO blind across types

- **[Type 9](/enneagram-corner/enneagram-type-9):** Pete Davidson's full retreat from social media and industry events, above.
- **[Type 5](/enneagram-corner/enneagram-type-5):** does the analysis, sends it to one colleague, and watches the person who presented it get the credit.
- **[Type 1](/enneagram-corner/enneagram-type-1):** corrects the error in the shared doc on principle, two meetings after the group stopped listening.
- **[Type 3](/enneagram-corner/enneagram-type-3):** an unusual mix. SO-blind Threes perform for one person or one standard, then get stunned when the wider field ranks them lower.

A Four who feels like an outsider isn't necessarily SO blind. Measuring yourself against the group is social attention, painful but present.

### SX blind across types

- **Type 3:** Sydney Sweeney, above. The intensity goes into the work and the fortress, and the public's version of her is the instinct she uses least.
- **[Type 6](/enneagram-corner/enneagram-type-6):** shows up, plans ahead, keeps every promise, and still keeps a partner at a careful distance because closeness is one more risk to manage.
- **Type 9:** everyone's peacemaker and nobody's confidant. Asked "what do you want?", they honestly don't know.

A reserved Five can look SX blind and be the opposite. An SX-dominant Five holds back with most people and pours enormous intensity into one person or one obsession.

## The honest part: stacking theory is contested

Instinct stacking is one of the most useful ideas in the Enneagram, and one of the least tested.

**No study we could find tests it.** A [2021 systematic review in the Journal of Clinical Psychology](https://doi.org/10.1002/jclp.23097) looked at 104 independent samples and found "mixed evidence of reliability and validity" for the Enneagram overall. It describes the three instinctual subtypes but reports no study testing them. It did find tests of two other secondary ideas, one study on [wings](/enneagram-corner/enneagram-wings-complete-guide) and two on stress-and-growth movement. None supported the theory, though the authors note the studies were few and flawed.

**Its origins are practitioner research.** Fauvre says the three-instinct stacking emerged during her 1994 study, which sent questionnaires to members of the Enneagram community and used qualitative methods. That's real observation without independent replication.

**Teachers disagree about where the weakness lives.** Most writing on "SP blind" puts the vulnerability in the last instinct. Fauvre's own research page says the opposite: "our dominant instinctual drive is, in actuality, our area of greatest weakness." Both can be useful. The dominant instinct is where people overdo it and get hurt. The blind spot is where they underdo it and get billed. They're still different claims, and the teachers haven't settled them.

**Some teachers think stacking is the wrong focus.** Mario Sikora argues people should stop fixating on their stack and watch how the dominant bias flavors everything they do: "Preservers navigate like Preservers, not like Navigators."

**Our read:** a blind spot is a hypothesis to check against the record. The late fees, the reputation surprises and the relationships that went flat are all checkable. When the record and the label disagree, the record wins. For the wider debate, see [Enneagram criticisms](/enneagram-corner/enneagram-criticisms) and [what the science says](/enneagram-corner/mental-health/enneagram-science-mental-health).

## FAQ: SP blind, SO blind and SX blind

### What does SP blind mean?

SP blind means self-preservation is the last instinct in the stacking (so/sx or sx/so). Money, sleep, food, health and paperwork go untracked until something forces them. Problems build quietly and arrive as an overdraft, a burnout or a health scare.

### What does SO blind (social blind) mean?

SO blind, also searched as social blind, means the social instinct is last in the stacking (sp/sx or sx/sp). Group standing, alliances and unwritten rules go untracked. The person often learns where they stood only after a decision gets made without them.

### What does SX blind mean?

SX blind means the one-to-one (sexual) instinct is last in the stacking (sp/so or so/sp). Life tends to be stable and socially connected, with intensity, chemistry and deep merging kept at a distance. Partners often say they never get all of the person.

### Is SO blind the same as being introverted?

No. Introversion is about energy. The social instinct is about tracking the group's map. A quiet introvert can be strongly SO-dominant and know who holds influence in every room. An outgoing person can be SO blind and talk to everyone without noticing the hierarchy.

### Can SX-blind people have good relationships?

Yes. Many SX-blind people are steady, loyal partners. The common complaint is that the relationship runs warm but not hot. SX blindness also has nothing to do with sex drive. It describes how much intensity and merging a person seeks, in romance, friendship or work.

### Which is the most common blind spot?

We don't know of reliable population data on instinct stackings, so we can't say. Be skeptical of any chart that claims to rank them.

### Can your blind spot change?

Our read is that stacking stays fairly stable, and the blind instinct develops through deliberate practice rather than switching places. There's no controlled research either way. Life stages can also make a blind spot look like it has moved when it hasn't.

### Is "SP blind" scientifically validated?

No. A 2021 systematic review of Enneagram research found mixed evidence for the system overall and reported no study testing the instincts. Stacking is a model for naming patterns a person can check against their own record, not a measured trait.
