<QuickAnswer question="Which Enneagram type is the hardest to love?">
<strong>Type 4 is the hardest to love. Type 8 is the most difficult to deal with.</strong> Those are two different questions. "Hard to love" is about closeness: a Four's attention goes to what's missing, so the love already in front of them can feel less real. "Difficult" is about daily contact: an Eight pushes, takes over and says the blunt thing. Runners-up are Type 5 (hard to love) and Type 1 (hard to deal with). The easiest are Type 2 to love and Type 9 to deal with.
</QuickAnswer>

<p class="firstLetter">Katy Perry spent four years on and off with John Mayer, through reconciliation after reconciliation, before the final split. Amazon executives used to get forwarded customer complaints from Jeff Bezos with one character added: "?". Both men get called "hard." Only one of them is hard to love, and it isn't the one with the question mark.</p>

The two lists don't line up, and that's the useful part. An Eight can be exhausting to deal with and easy to love once you're inside its circle. A Four can be easy to work beside and hard to love.

## Hardest to love: Type 4

The Four's core fear is having no identity or significance. Its attention goes to what's missing, in itself and in the people around it. The traditional Enneagram passion for the type is envy.

In love, that produces a recognizable loop. The Four idealizes someone who feels out of reach. When that person gets close, the flaws come into focus and the longing fades. When the person pulls back, the longing returns. Love that is already _here_ can feel less real than love still out of reach.

Our [John Mayer](/personality-analysis/john-mayer) profile, a Four, traces that loop through his public relationships: intense pursuit, emotional overwhelm, withdrawal. With [Katy Perry](/personality-analysis/katy-perry), his longest relationship, the cycle ran four years. Mayer has said, "I hate being the heartbreaker." The pattern kept happening anyway.

[Joaquin Phoenix](/personality-analysis/joaquin-phoenix), whom we type as a Four, used his 2020 Best Actor speech for _Joker_ to audit himself in front of the whole industry. He called himself "selfish," "cruel at times" and "hard to work with." Our profile reads that merciless self-audit as the core of his Four pattern: the flaw gets named first, before anyone else can name it, at the exact moment the room is handing him its biggest compliment.

**What helps:** consistency without chasing. A Four learns that love is real by watching it survive the distance phase, unpunished and unrushed.

### Runner-up: Type 5

The Five's core fear is being drained or overwhelmed, so it rations how much of itself anyone can reach. The love is often there, but it arrives in small, carefully measured amounts. You get the long, thoughtful email; you don't get them on the phone when you're crying.

Fours make your love hard to _land_. Fives make it hard to _get in_. We rank the Four first because a Five who trusts you usually stays reachable, while a Four's longing can restart even after trust is built.

### Easiest to love: Type 2

Being loved by a Two is easy, because the Two does most of the work. [Kristen Bell](/personality-analysis/kristen-bell), a Two on our site, built a life around service: a nurse mother, a baby-products company, years as a UN humanitarian advocate.

The catch is that loving one _well_ is hard. Bell has said she didn't realize she was codependent until her mid-30s, when her husband, Dax Shepard, told her "that's not a compliment." The Two's own needs stay hidden, sometimes from the Two. If you love one, ask what they need, and keep asking after they say "nothing."

## Most difficult to deal with: Type 8

The Eight's core fear is being controlled or harmed, and its strategy is strength: take charge before someone else does, and push to find out what holds. For an Eight, confrontation is a way of making contact. A good argument can feel like closeness.

For everyone else, that means daily friction. Eights decide fast and expect objections out loud. In our [CEO data post](/enneagram-corner/what-enneagram-type-are-most-ceos), [Jeff Bezos](/personality-analysis/jeff-bezos) forwarded customer complaints to his executives with a single character, "?", and let the silence do the rest. Efficient. Also the reason people dread opening their email.

The other side shows up inside the circle. Our [Gordon Ramsay](/personality-analysis/gordon-ramsay) profile, an Eight, makes the case that the intensity contestants dread on television is the same thing that makes him a fiercely devoted father and husband. That's why Eights rank low on "hard to love" and high on "difficult."

**What helps:** say the direct thing first. "I disagree, here's why" works better with an Eight than three rounds of hints.

### Runner-up: Type 1

The One's core fear is being bad, wrong or corrupt, and its strategy is a standard that's never fully met. Work handed to a One comes back with notes, and the notes are usually right, which makes them harder to take.

[Hayao Miyazaki](/personality-analysis/hayao-miyazaki), a One on our site, has called AI-generated animation "an insult to life itself." His own former staff describe the cost of that standard from the inside: one former Ghibli employee, Hirokatsu Kihara, said people who worked there "leave quite fast." To a One, the gap between how things are and how they should be feels like _their_ gap to close, and you're standing in it.

We rank the Eight first because an Eight's friction comes at you directly. A One's mostly comes at your work.

### Easiest to deal with: Type 9

Nines are easygoing, accommodating and hard to offend. Day to day, they're the easiest type to share an office or a kitchen with.

They're also the hardest type to have a real argument with. A Nine goes quiet, says "I'm fine" and lets the issue drift. If you need a decision or a fight that clears the air, the Nine's calm becomes its own kind of difficult.

## The nine takes: what makes each type hard

Every type makes love hard, or daily life hard, in its own way. Each one has an alarm underneath that the type doesn't experience as a flaw.

### Type 1: The standard you can't meet

**Hard because:** criticism comes before warmth. **The alarm:** being wrong or bad. You hear "the edges are crooked" before you hear "it's beautiful." The One usually means both and only says one.

### Type 2: The need they won't name

**Hard because:** the giving comes with expectations they won't say out loud. **The alarm:** being unwanted. You find out you missed something they needed weeks after it mattered, in the middle of an unrelated fight.

### Type 3: The person behind the performance

**Hard because:** you're not sure you've met the real one. **The alarm:** being worthless without results. A Three can answer "how are you?" with a work update and mean it as an honest answer.

### Type 4: The longing that restarts

**Hard because:** present love can feel less real than missing love. **The alarm:** being ordinary or unseen. Covered above. The tell is "you don't really get me," said to the person who gets them most.

### Type 5: The door that closes

**Hard because:** access is rationed. **The alarm:** being drained or intruded on. You plan a weekend away, and they need a full day alone after it to recover from it.

### Type 6: The loyalty test

**Hard because:** trust has to be re-earned under pressure. **The alarm:** being left without support. A Six asks "are you sure you're not mad?" three times, and on bad days reads a late reply as proof of the worst case.

### Type 7: The exit when it gets heavy

**Hard because:** commitment can feel like a closed door. **The alarm:** being trapped in pain or limited. [Kate Hudson](/personality-analysis/kate-hudson), a Seven on our site, married Chris Robinson seven months after they met; they separated six years later. Our profile calls it the commitment paradox: every yes to one thing registers as a no to everything else.

### Type 8: The push

**Hard because:** daily contact feels like a contest. **The alarm:** being controlled or vulnerable. Covered above. The tell is that the Eight is most aggressive with people it hasn't decided about yet.

### Type 9: The disappearing "I"

**Hard because:** the fight gets postponed until it explodes. **The alarm:** conflict and separation. [Pete Davidson](/personality-analysis/pete-davidson), a Nine on our site, texted Kanye West "I want everything to be smooth," absorbed months of public attacks, then cut through it in one message: "I'm in bed with your wife."

## The honest "it's complicated"

A healthy Four is one of the most loving partners there is. An unhealthy Two or Nine can be impossible to live with. Health and maturity predict "hard to love" far better than type does.

The other person is part of the equation. An Eight is hardest on someone who avoids conflict; a Four is hardest on someone who needs steady reassurance. Reading the alarm lets you stop mistaking someone else's alarm for a defect, and still decide what you can live with.

With that said, the picks stand: Type 4 is the hardest to love, Type 8 is the most difficult to deal with.
