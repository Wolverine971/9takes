<QuickAnswer question="Which Enneagram type is the most emotional?">
<strong>Type 4.</strong> In nine studies that compared Enneagram types with the Big Five, Type 4 scored higher on neuroticism, the trait for how easily distress hits, in eight of them, and psychiatrist Claudio Naranjo called the Four "the most sensitive in the enneagram." <strong>Type 6 is the runner-up</strong>, with the same result in eight of nine studies, though its emotion runs mostly as anxiety. <strong>Type 8 is the least emotional</strong>, the one type that tested lower on neuroticism. Eights feel plenty. They just let out anger and keep the rest behind it.
</QuickAnswer>

<p class="firstLetter">Two people get the same bad news at the same table. One cries before the sentence is finished and needs to talk about it for an hour. The other asks a practical question, pays the check, and then can't sleep for three nights. Ask the table which one is "the emotional one" and they'll point at the first. They might be wrong about the second.</p>

Most pages that answer "which Enneagram type is the most emotional" won't name one. They say every type has feelings, which is true, and then they stop. But this is one of the rare superlatives where the theory, the research and the surveys line up. You can name a type at the top and a type at the bottom, and show why.

---

## What "emotional" means when you search it

People use the word three ways, and the answer shifts a little with each:

- **Feels intensely.** Emotions hit hard and stay. This is "sensitive."
- **Swings fast.** Small things set off big reactions. This is "reactive."
- **Shows it.** The feeling is visible on the face and in the room. This is "expressive."

The pick, Type 4, wins the first two. The third has a different answer, Type 2, and we'll get to why.

## How we picked: three sources, one direction

**The research.** A 2021 systematic review in the _Journal of Clinical Psychology_ pulled together nine studies that measured both Enneagram type and the Big Five personality traits. The authors counted a pattern as consistent when it showed up in more than half of them. On neuroticism, only three types had a consistent result: Type 4 scored higher in 8 of 9 studies, Type 6 scored higher in 8 of 9, and Type 8 scored lower in 6 of 9 ([Hook et al., 2021](https://doi.org/10.1002/jclp.23097)). The APA describes neuroticism as a long-term tendency toward emotional instability and vulnerability to distress ([APA Dictionary of Psychology](https://dictionary.apa.org/neuroticism)). <!-- corpus-ok: figures from the Hook et al. 2021 review, not the 9takes corpus -->

**The theory.** Naranjo, whose clinical type descriptions shaped the modern Enneagram, put the two ends side by side: "Just as the envy-centered character is the most sensitive in the enneagram, ennea-type VIII is the most insensitive" ([Naranjo, _Character and Neurosis_, ch. 4](https://www.claudionaranjo.net/pdf_files/not_catagorized/character_and_neurosis_nine_types_english/character_neurosis_4_english.pdf)). The "envy-centered character" is his name for Type 4.

**The self-reports.** A Psychology Junkie survey of 1,024 readers found that "over 93% of Fours say they're empathetic," the most of any type, and about 45% said they absorb other people's emotions at least most of the time. Fives (52.09%) and Eights (52.38%) came in lowest ([Psychology Junkie, 2024](https://www.psychologyjunkie.com/the-enneagram-types-most-likely-to-be-empathic/)). Empathy isn't the same as being emotional, and a reader survey isn't a study, but it points the same direction. <!-- corpus-ok: figures from a Psychology Junkie reader survey, not the 9takes corpus -->

Three sources with different methods put Type 4 at the top and Type 8 near the bottom. That's as much agreement as you get on an Enneagram superlative.

## The pick: Type 4

The [Four's](/enneagram-corner/enneagram-type-4) basic fear is having "no identity or personal significance," and feelings are where it goes looking for one. The Enneagram Institute lists two of the Four's key motivations as "to maintain certain moods and feelings" and "to take care of emotional needs before attending to anything else." At average health, it describes Fours who "interiorize everything, taking everything personally," and become "moody and hypersensitive" ([Type 4](https://www.enneagraminstitute.com/type-4)).

What sets a Four apart is how long it stays with a feeling. Naranjo compared the Four to the type next door, the detached Five: "dry avarice is apathetic, wet envy, most intense; if the one is a desert, the other is a marsh."

You can see it in public. After Justin Bieber, her childhood idol, sang to [Billie Eilish](/personality-analysis/billie-eilish) on stage at Coachella, the singer we type as a Four posted a tear-streaked selfie captioned "can't stop crying." She didn't tidy the feeling up first.

The pattern shows in what Fours do for a living, too. Among the {{corpus.domain.music.total}} musicians we've profiled, {{corpus.domain.music.type.4.share}} are Type 4, more than any other type. We type those profiles ourselves, so read it as our pattern, not a census. The full breakdown is in [what Enneagram type most musicians are](/pop-culture/what-enneagram-type-are-most-musicians). <!-- corpus-assert: domain.music.top.type = 4 -->

## The runner-up: Type 6

The research gives Type 6 the same neuroticism result as Type 4, 8 of 9 studies. The difference is which emotion it is. "Sixes exhibit the most fear of all three types, largely experienced as anxiety," the Enneagram Institute writes, and at average health they become "highly reactive, anxious, and negative" ([Type 6](https://www.enneagraminstitute.com/type-6), [Enneagram Institute](https://www.enneagraminstitute.com/how-the-enneagram-system-works)). <!-- corpus-ok: figure from the Hook et al. 2021 review -->

A Four's feelings are about who it is. A [Six's](/enneagram-corner/enneagram-type-6) are about what might happen. [Tom Hanks](/personality-analysis/tom-hanks), whom we type as a Six, has described the start of a workday as "the complete total terror of losing the battle against self-loathing," and the job as "still very scary" after four decades. That's an emotional life running at full volume behind one of the steadiest faces in Hollywood.

We put the Six second because its emotions tend to sit inside one channel, worry, while the Four's run across the whole range. For where that worry loop comes from, see [why each type overthinks](/enneagram-corner/why-you-cant-stop-overthinking-enneagram).

**Why not Type 2?** If you mean whose feelings you can see from across the room, it's the Two. The Enneagram Institute describes average Twos as "emotionally demonstrative" ([Type 2](https://www.enneagraminstitute.com/type-2)), and Type 2 was linked to extraversion in all nine Big Five studies. But Twos had no consistent neuroticism result, and their feelings point outward, at you. [Dolly Parton](/personality-analysis/dolly-parton), whom we type as a Two, cried over telling Elvis Presley's manager no when he wanted half the publishing on "I Will Always Love You." Then she kept the copyright. Big feeling, aimed at the relationship, and the feeling didn't make the decision. <!-- corpus-ok: figure from the Hook et al. 2021 review -->

## The least emotional: Type 8

[Type 8](/enneagram-corner/enneagram-type-8) is the only type that tested lower on neuroticism in most of the studies (6 of 9), the type Naranjo called "the most insensitive," and second-lowest on the empathy survey. On every measure we have, Eights are the hardest type to rattle. <!-- corpus-ok: figure from the Hook et al. 2021 review -->

Hard to rattle isn't the same as empty, though. The Enneagram Institute is blunt about what's behind the armor: "Beneath the tough façade is vulnerability," and Eights "have trouble admitting their vulnerability to themselves, let alone to anyone else" ([Type 8](https://www.enneagraminstitute.com/type-8)). Anger is the feeling an Eight gives itself permission to show. Hurt and fear stay behind it.

Our profiles are full of Eights cracking at odd moments. [Winston Churchill](/personality-analysis/winston-churchill) wept openly at funerals and walking through bombed neighborhoods. [Denzel Washington](/personality-analysis/denzel-washington) broke on _The Late Show_ while talking about his mother, asked Stephen Colbert for a tissue, and said: "Didn't cry at her funeral. I guess I saved it up for you." The feeling was there the whole time. It came out on the Eight's schedule.

## Nine takes: how each type handles a feeling

Every type feels. The difference is what each one does with the feeling, and the alarm that decides it.

### Type 1: "I'm not upset. I'm right."

**The alarm:** being bad or wrong. Ones are in the gut triad, where the Enneagram Institute names anger as the core emotion, and a One's anger feels like a moral failure. So it comes out as correction instead: the tight jaw, the "it's fine," and the dishwasher reloaded the right way.

### Type 2: "How are _you_ doing?"

**The alarm:** being unwanted. Twos feel strongly, but they feel about you. Ask a Two how they are and you'll get a sentence about themselves and a paragraph about you. The feelings they don't voice tend to come back later as resentment.

### Type 3: "I'll feel it after the launch"

**The alarm:** being worthless without results. The Enneagram Institute says Threes "are potentially the most out of touch with underlying feelings of inadequacy." When a 2020 leak backed up what [Taylor Swift](/personality-analysis/taylor-swift), whom we type as a Three, had said for four years about her feud with Kanye West, she posted: "Instead of answering those who are asking how I feel about the video footage proving I was telling the truth, I'm going to be supporting organizations that help supply hungry children with meals." The feeling went straight into a task.

### Type 4: "I need to sit with this"

**The alarm:** having no identity. A Four treats a feeling as information about who it is, so it won't rush one. That's why a Four can still be grieving something you forgot happened, and why they're often the first to name what everyone else in the room is feeling.

### Type 5: "Let me think about it and get back to you"

**The alarm:** running out of energy and capacity. The Enneagram Institute says Fives have "fear about the outer world and about their capacity to cope with it." Naranjo notes that Fives "often have trouble in crying." The Five feels it fine. It just processes the feeling alone, hours or days later, and then sends you the thoughtful text.

### Type 6: "What if it goes wrong?"

**The alarm:** losing support. Most of what a Six feels arrives as worry about the future. You'll see it in the questions: the third "are you sure?" and the backup plan for the backup plan.

### Type 7: "Okay, but here's the good part"

**The alarm:** being trapped in pain. Sevens, per the Enneagram Institute, "have fear about their inner world," so a heavy feeling gets reframed before it can land. They feel joy bigger than anyone. Sadness gets a ten-minute window and a plan for the weekend. Our [Type 7 guide](/enneagram-corner/enneagram-type-7) covers what happens when the reframe stops working.

### Type 8: "I'm fine. Who did this?"

**The alarm:** being controlled or hurt. An Eight's hurt turns into anger almost instantly, because anger feels like strength and hurt feels like exposure. If an Eight lets you see them sad, you're in the inner circle.

### Type 9: "It's not a big deal"

**The alarm:** loss and separation. Nines "do not want to be affected," so feelings get turned down to keep the peace. They don't disappear. [Keanu Reeves](/personality-analysis/keanu-reeves), whom we type as a Nine, has spent decades quietly carrying loss: "Grief changes shape, but it never ends." The Nine version of a big feeling is steady and low and lasts for years.

## The honest "it's complicated"

**Neuroticism only covers part of "emotional."** The research measures negative emotions like worry, sadness and irritability. It doesn't measure joy, love or excitement, where Sevens and Twos would score higher. If "emotional" to you means big, warm and loud, the Two or the Seven is your answer.

**The studies are few.** Nine studies is a short shelf, they used different Enneagram tests, and the review calls the overall evidence for the Enneagram "mixed." A consistent result in 8 of 9 is a real pattern, but it's not proof that every Four is more emotional than every Eight. <!-- corpus-ok: figure from the Hook et al. 2021 review -->

**"Highly sensitive person" is a different question.** If you searched for the HSP trait, we found no study that measured it across the nine types.

The pick still stands. The type the theory calls most sensitive is the same type the research finds highest on negative emotion and the same type that most often calls itself empathetic. If you want one answer to "which Enneagram type is the most emotional," it's Type 4.

If feelings have turned into something heavier and longer-lasting, our [guide to depression patterns by type](/enneagram-corner/depression-patterns-by-enneagram-type) covers what that looks like in each type and when to get help. For what each type does under pressure in general, see [the Enneagram types in stress](/enneagram-corner/enneagram-types-in-stress).

## Frequently asked questions

### Which Enneagram type is the most sensitive?

Type 4. Naranjo wrote that the envy-centered character, his name for Type 4, is "the most sensitive in the enneagram," and that Type 8 is the most insensitive. Fours also lead the Psychology Junkie empathy survey.

### What is the least emotional Enneagram type?

Type 8. It was the only type to score lower on neuroticism in most of the Big Five studies. Eights are hard to rattle, not empty: anger is the feeling they let out, and hurt or fear stays behind it. Type 5 is a close second, because Fives process feelings later and alone.

### Which Enneagram type shows emotion most openly?

Type 2. Twos are described as emotionally demonstrative, and Type 2 was linked to extraversion in all nine Big Five studies. Their feelings point outward, at other people, so they're the most visible even when Fours feel more.

### Are Type 4s too emotional?

No. Fours feel intensely and stay with a feeling instead of moving past it, which other people can read as too much. The same trait is why Fours can name what a room feels. Trouble starts when a Four treats a mood as the whole truth about who they are.

### Is Type 6 or Type 4 more emotional?

They tie on the research. Both scored higher on neuroticism in 8 of 9 studies. We rank the Four first because its feelings cover the whole range, while the Six's mostly arrive as anxiety about what might happen. <!-- corpus-ok: figure from the Hook et al. 2021 review -->
