<QuickAnswer question="Which Enneagram type is the most intelligent?">
<strong>Type 5, if you mean the type built around its mind.</strong> The Enneagram Institute says a Five's "identity is built around 'having ideas.'" But no Enneagram type has a higher IQ, and intelligence isn't a type trait. <strong>Type 7 is the runner-up</strong>: it's the one type linked to openness, the personality trait most tied to intelligence, in all nine studies that checked. <strong>Type 9 is the least likely to get called the smart one</strong>, and the most underestimated. Type tells you how someone uses their mind. It doesn't tell you how much mind they have.
</QuickAnswer>

<p class="firstLetter">There's a group chat with an argument in it. One friend replies with three studies and a link. One fixes the actual problem while everyone else is still typing. One reads the mood, sends the line that ends the fight, and nobody notices that was the smartest move in the thread. Ask the chat who the smart one is and they'll point at the first friend.</p>

That's the trouble with "which Enneagram type is the most intelligent." The question assumes the Enneagram measures brainpower. It doesn't. It measures what a person is afraid of and what they do about it, and that turns out to decide something real: what each type _uses_ its intelligence for, and which kind of smart gets noticed.

So here's the pick, the catch that comes with it, and how all nine types use their heads.

---

## The plain truth: type is not IQ

Start with what the research actually says, because most pages on this skip it.

**We found no study that has measured IQ across the nine types.** A 2021 systematic review of Enneagram research, covering 104 samples, lists none either ([Hook et al., 2021](https://doi.org/10.1002/jclp.23097)). Any "most to least intelligent Enneagram" list you've seen is a guess. <!-- corpus-ok: sample count from the Hook et al. 2021 review -->

**Personality and intelligence are only loosely linked.** The largest meta-analysis on the question pooled 272 studies and 162,636 people. Openness was the Big Five trait most related to intelligence, at ρ = .20, followed by neuroticism at ρ = −.09 ([Anglim et al., 2022](https://doi.org/10.1037/bul0000373)). A correlation of .20 accounts for about 4% of the differences between people. Personality tilts the odds a little and decides almost nothing. <!-- corpus-ok: figures from the Anglim et al. 2022 meta-analysis -->

So the answer has to come from a different question: **which type's core fear runs through its mind?** That one the Enneagram can answer.

---

## The pick: Type 5

The [Five's](/enneagram-corner/enneagram-type-5) basic fear, in the Enneagram Institute's words, is "being useless, helpless, or incapable," and its basic desire is "to be capable and competent." Its key motivation is to "possess knowledge, to understand the environment, to have everything figured out." Then comes the line that settles the pick: Fives value knowledge "because their identity is built around 'having ideas'" ([Type 5](https://www.enneagraminstitute.com/type-5)).

Every type thinks. For the Five, thinking is the strategy itself. Understanding the world from a safe distance is how a Five feels capable enough to step into it. The Enneagram Institute describes healthy Fives as having "searching intelligence: nothing escapes their notice," and Fives who "attain skillful mastery of whatever interests them."

You can see the pattern in who becomes famous for ideas. Among the {{corpus.domain.technology.total}} tech figures we've profiled, {{corpus.domain.technology.type.5.share}} are Type 5, more than any other type, against {{corpus.type.5.share}} across all {{corpus.total}} people we've typed. Type 5 also leads our {{corpus.domain.authors-thinkers.label}} field. We pick and type those profiles ourselves, so treat that as our pattern, not a census. The people are on our [tech category page]({{corpus.domain.technology.url}}). <!-- corpus-assert: domain.technology.top.type = 5 --> <!-- corpus-assert: domain.authors-thinkers.top.type = 5 -->

[Demis Hassabis](/personality-analysis/demis-hassabis), whom we type as a Five, shows what the Five's mind looks like up close. For roughly a decade he ran a second workday from late evening to 4 a.m., after the company day was over. In August 2026 he gave up the chief executive title at Google DeepMind "so that I have the time and space to focus on the big picture." The Five's mind wants time alone with the problem more than it wants the corner office.

### The catch

The research doesn't back the Five's reputation the way you'd expect. In the nine studies that compared Enneagram types with the Big Five, Type 5's only consistent result was introversion, lower extraversion in all nine. It showed no consistent link to openness, the one trait the meta-analysis ties to intelligence ([Hook et al., 2021](https://doi.org/10.1002/jclp.23097)). <!-- corpus-ok: figures from the Hook et al. 2021 review -->

So the Five is the type most organized around its mind. That's different from being the type with the most of it. A Five who's afraid of being incapable may study a subject for ten years before saying a word about it. That looks like intelligence, and often is. It's also a defense.

## The runner-up: Type 7

If you go by the data instead of the theory, the [Seven](/enneagram-corner/enneagram-type-7) has the better case. Type 7 was linked to openness in all nine Big Five studies, the only type with a perfect record on it. Type 4 came next, at six of nine ([Hook et al., 2021](https://doi.org/10.1002/jclp.23097)). And the openness facets most tied to intelligence in the meta-analysis were "intellectual engagement and unconventionality," which reads a lot like a Seven's resume. <!-- corpus-ok: figures from the Hook et al. 2021 review -->

The Seven's mind is fast and wide. Its fear is being trapped, so it keeps generating options, and it's often the first person to connect two ideas nobody else put together. [Paul Graham](/personality-analysis/paul-graham), whom we type as a Seven, quit academia, painting, his own company and eventually Y Combinator, each time for something newer. In between he wrote more than 200 essays and funded Airbnb partly because of its founders' cereal boxes.

One honest wrinkle: the same meta-analysis found that excitement seeking ran slightly negative with crystallized intelligence, the knowledge kind. Sevens tend to score high on that too. The Seven's mind is broad, and it doesn't always stay put long enough to go deep.

## The least likely to be called smart: Type 9

Nobody should call any type the least intelligent, and we won't. But one type's intelligence gets noticed least, and it's the [Nine](/enneagram-corner/enneagram-type-9).

The Nine's basic fear is "loss and separation," and its strategy is to blend in and keep the peace ([Type 9](https://www.enneagraminstitute.com/type-9)). Showing off a mind creates distance. Winning an argument creates friction. So Nines tend to hold their best thinking back, give the room what it needs, and let someone else take the credit.

Watch what that looks like at the top. [Sundar Pichai](/personality-analysis/sundar-pichai), whom we type as a Nine, runs Google. In meetings he goes around the table one by one to draw out the quieter voices, absorbs every view, then offers the idea that pulls them together. [Mira Murati](/personality-analysis/mira-murati), another Nine on our shelf, was chief technology officer at OpenAI. Neither one gets talked about the way the loud geniuses do. That's the Nine's kind of smart: synthesis, done quietly.

## Nine takes: how each type uses its mind

Each type below has a real mind at work and a fear steering it.

### Type 1: the mind as an editor

**The alarm:** being wrong or corrupt. A One's intelligence goes to finding what's off and fixing it.

On the day _The New York Times_ reported that [Geoffrey Hinton](/personality-analysis/geoffrey-hinton) had quit Google to talk about AI risk, Hinton, whom we type as a One, publicly corrected the paper's framing in Google's favor. He gave up the more flattering story to get the details right. That's the One's mind: accuracy first, even when it costs you.

### Type 2: the mind as a people reader

**The alarm:** being unwanted. A Two's intelligence goes to other people: what they need, what they fear, what will move them.

Before the 1978 Camp David talks, [Jimmy Carter](/personality-analysis/jimmy-carter), whom we type as a Two, pored over intelligence files to learn Menachem Begin's and Anwar Sadat's characters and red lines. For 13 days he shuttled between two leaders who couldn't stand to share a room. The breakthrough came from knowing the people, not from a clever argument.

### Type 3: the mind as a strategist

**The alarm:** being worthless without results. A Three's intelligence goes to what works and how it will land.

[Tina Fey](/personality-analysis/tina-fey), whom we type as a Three, figured out in middle school that humor made people like her, and spent decades refining it into a system. Her self-deprecating jokes read as vulnerability. They work as strategy. Threes are often underrated as thinkers because they make the thinking look easy.

### Type 4: the mind as a depth finder

**The alarm:** having no identity. A Four's intelligence goes looking for meaning, often in several fields at once.

[Robert Oppenheimer](/personality-analysis/robert-oppenheimer), whom we type as a Four, led the laboratory that built the atomic bomb, and he also studied Sanskrit and the Bhagavad Gita, returning to one verse from it for two decades. To a Four, an idea that doesn't mean something hasn't been understood yet.

### Type 5: the mind as a fortress

**The alarm:** being incapable. The Five's intelligence goes to understanding the system well enough to feel safe inside it.

You know the Five in your life by the one subject they know cold and the party they left early to get back to it. They'll often know more than anyone in the room and say the least.

### Type 6: the mind as a risk scanner

**The alarm:** losing support. A Six's intelligence goes to what could go wrong and who can be trusted when it does.

[Dario Amodei](/personality-analysis/dario-amodei), whom we type as a Six, runs one of the leading AI labs and told _60 Minutes_ he is "deeply uncomfortable with these decisions being made by a few companies, by a few people." That's the Six's mind: build the thing, then build the checks around it, including on yourself.

### Type 7: the mind as a connector

**The alarm:** being trapped. A Seven's intelligence is fast and wide, built for the next idea and the link between two old ones. See Paul Graham above.

### Type 8: the mind as a lever

**The alarm:** being controlled. An Eight's intelligence goes to strategy and power: where the pressure point is, and how to move first.

[Jeff Bezos](/personality-analysis/jeff-bezos), whom we type as an Eight, banned slide decks from Amazon meetings in favor of six-page memos read in silence, and kept an empty chair at the table for the customer. Both rules force everyone into his way of thinking. That's an Eight's intelligence: designing the room so the decision goes where it should.

### Type 9: the mind as a synthesizer

**The alarm:** loss and separation. A Nine's intelligence goes to holding every view at once. See Sundar Pichai above. The cost, as one of Pichai's former colleagues told him, is that "he agonizes over decisions." Seeing every side makes picking one hard.

## The honest "it's complicated"

**Smart people mistype as Fives all the time.** The Five description sounds like a compliment to anyone who likes ideas. But the Enneagram sorts by motive, not ability. A brilliant Three or Nine uses the same intelligence for a different reason, and the reason decides the type. If you're not sure, our [guide to finding your type](/enneagram-corner/beginners-guide-to-determining-your-enneagram-type) walks through how to tell.

**Intelligence comes in more than one kind.** IQ tests capture reasoning and knowledge well. They don't capture reading a room, which is where Twos and Nines tend to shine. The 2021 review lists no study comparing emotional intelligence across the nine types either.

**Where the smart types end up is a different question.** If you want to know which types run companies, our [CEO breakdown](/enneagram-corner/what-enneagram-type-are-most-ceos) covers it, and [careers by type](/enneagram-corner/enneagram-types-and-career-choices) covers the rest.

Back to the pick. No type is smarter. But if you mean the type that lives in its head, builds its identity on knowing things and feels most at risk when it doesn't, that's Type 5.

## Frequently asked questions

### Is there a link between Enneagram type and IQ?

No study has measured IQ across the nine types. Personality and intelligence are only loosely connected in general: in a 2022 meta-analysis of 272 studies, openness was the trait most related to intelligence, at a correlation of about .20. <!-- corpus-ok: figures from the Anglim et al. 2022 meta-analysis -->

### Are Type 5s smarter than other types?

Not as far as anyone has shown. Fives build their identity around understanding things, so they often look like the smartest person in the room. In the Big Five studies, though, Type 5's only consistent result was introversion, with no consistent link to openness.

### What is the most to least intelligent Enneagram type?

There's no honest ranking, because no study has compared intelligence across the nine types. What differs by type is what the mind gets used for. The table above lays it out.

### Which Enneagram type is the most underestimated?

Type 9. Nines hold their best thinking back to keep the peace and let others take the credit, so their intelligence often goes unnoticed. It shows up as synthesis: pulling a room's views into one decision.
