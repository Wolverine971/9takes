<QuickAnswer question="Which Enneagram type is the most jealous?">
<strong>Type 4.</strong> Envy is the Four's core passion in Enneagram theory, described by psychiatrist Claudio Naranjo as "a painful sense of lack and a craving toward that which is felt lacking." <strong>Type 2 is the runner-up</strong> for the relationship kind of jealousy, because a Two's worth rides on being the one you need. <strong>Type 9 is the least jealous</strong>, because its whole strategy is to want less. No study has measured jealousy across the nine types, so this is our read of the theory, and we show the work below.
</QuickAnswer>

<p class="firstLetter">Your best friend gets engaged and you're happy for her, mostly. Your partner laughs a little too hard at a coworker's text. A guy you barely knew in high school posts the house, the dog and the job you wanted. It's the same burn in your chest all three times, and it's actually two different feelings.</p>

Search "which Enneagram type is the most jealous" and the top results politely decline to answer. Every type can be jealous, they say, and then they list nine paragraphs without a winner. That's true, and it doesn't help you. Some types are built around comparison in a way others aren't, and you can say which ones and why.

So here is a pick, the reasoning tied to each type's core fear, and what jealousy looks like in all nine.

---

## Jealous or envious? Most searches mix them up

Psychologists separate the two. "Envy occurs when we lack a desired attribute enjoyed by another," writes social psychologist Richard H. Smith. "Jealousy occurs when something we already possess (usually a special relationship) is threatened by a third person." Envy involves two people. Jealousy involves three ([Smith, _Psychology Today_, 2014](https://www.psychologytoday.com/us/blog/joy-and-pain/201401/what-is-the-difference-between-envy-and-jealousy), citing Parrott & Smith, 1993).

In everyday speech, "jealous" covers both, which is why the friend's engagement and the partner's text feel like one emotion. The Enneagram has a name for only one of them. Envy is the traditional passion of Type 4. Romantic, three-person jealousy has no number of its own, so the pick has to come from asking whose core fear it threatens most.

That gives two questions:

1. **Whose core fear keeps them comparing?** That's who runs on envy.
2. **Whose sense of worth depends on holding one person's attention?** That's who runs on jealousy.

One study helps here, even though it never mentions the Enneagram. Across three studies, Zlatan Krizan and Omesh Johar found that envy tracks the shame-prone, vulnerable style of narcissism ("strongly and consistently related") and not the confident, grandiose style, which was "slightly negatively related" to it ([Krizan & Johar, 2012](https://doi.org/10.1111/j.1467-6494.2012.00767.x)). Envy, in other words, lives with shame. The Enneagram Institute puts shame at the center of Types 2, 3 and 4 ([Enneagram Institute](https://www.enneagraminstitute.com/how-the-enneagram-system-works)). Our top two picks come from that group.

## The pick: Type 4

The [Four's](/enneagram-corner/enneagram-type-4) basic fear, in the Enneagram Institute's wording, is "that they have no identity or personal significance" ([Type 4](https://www.enneagraminstitute.com/type-4)). A Four looks at other people to find out what it is missing, and it always finds something.

Naranjo, whose clinical descriptions shaped the modern types, gave the Four's passion its name. "The emotional state of envy involves a painful sense of lack and a craving toward that which is felt lacking," he wrote, describing a person who "identifies with that part of the psyche that fails to fit the idealized image" ([Naranjo, _Character and Neurosis_, ch. 3](https://www.claudionaranjo.net/pdf_files/not_catagorized/character_and_neurosis_nine_types_english/character_neurosis_3_english.pdf)). The Enneagram Institute's description of the average-to-unhealthy Four includes "self-pity and envy of others."

The giveaway is what a Four envies. It's rarely the car. It's the ease. [Miley Cyrus](/personality-analysis/miley-cyrus), whom we type as a Four, said it plainly to NPR in 2025: "I've always admired and somewhat envy people that know just exactly who they are." [Noah Kahan](/personality-analysis/noah-kahan), another Four, describes watching horses stand in a flooded Vermont field: "I just felt jealous of these horses that had the ability to stay. I was flying to LA the next week."

And when a Four falls for someone, envy can turn into the three-person kind. [John Lennon](/personality-analysis/john-lennon) said of the song "Jealous Guy": "I was a very jealous, possessive guy... because it makes me feel insecure." One person, both feelings, the same hole underneath.

## The runner-up: Type 2

If you mean jealousy in a relationship, the [Two](/enneagram-corner/enneagram-type-2) is the sharper answer. Its basic fear is being "unwanted, unworthy of being loved," and its strategy is to become the person you need. A rival is a direct threat to that position.

Teachers say so in plain words. The Enneagram Institute notes that Twos "typically have problems with possessiveness" and describes the average Two as "enveloping and possessive" ([Type 2](https://www.enneagraminstitute.com/type-2)). Naranjo calls the Two "over-involved in relationships and possessive," and he goes further: he reads the Two's pride as envy that has been pushed out of sight, "a transformation of envy through the joint action of repression and histrionic emotionalism" ([Naranjo, ch. 6](https://www.claudionaranjo.net/pdf_files/not_catagorized/character_and_neurosis_nine_types_english/character_neurosis_6_english.pdf)).

Here's what that looks like. You make a new close friend, and your Two friend suddenly gets more helpful: the extra texts, the favor nobody asked for, the dinner invitation for the same night. Then comes the hurt, unsaid at first. The Four envies who you are. The Two is afraid you'll stop needing them.

**Why not Type 6?** The Six has a strong claim. Its basic fear is being "without support and guidance," and one of its key motivations is "to test the attitudes of others toward them" ([Type 6](https://www.enneagraminstitute.com/type-6)). Naranjo lists "paranoid jealousy" among the fear-driven traits of the Six. We rank it third because a Six's jealousy is a trust problem, and it often settles once the Six gets real reassurance. A Two's jealousy is about its worth, and reassurance only buys time.

## The least jealous: Type 9

The [Nine's](/enneagram-corner/enneagram-type-9) basic desire is "inner stability," or "peace of mind." The average Nine, in the Enneagram Institute's description, does "not want to be affected" and starts "walking away from problems" ([Type 9](https://www.enneagraminstitute.com/type-9)). Jealousy means wanting something badly enough to fight for it. The Nine's strategy is to want less, so nothing has to be fought for.

That doesn't mean Nines never feel it. It means the feeling goes underground. [Post Malone](/personality-analysis/post-malone), whom we type as a Nine, listed his toxic traits on _Call Her Daddy_ as "drinking and sometimes jealousy" and "a quick temper." That's the Nine version: the jealousy doesn't get spoken, so it comes out sideways, in a drink, a sulk or a flash of anger nobody saw coming.

## Nine takes: what jealousy looks like in each type

Same feeling, nine different alarms underneath it. Read the alarm, not just the behavior.

### Type 1: "They didn't earn it"

**The alarm:** being bad, wrong or corrupt. Ones rarely feel allowed to envy, so the feeling comes out dressed as a principle.

In 2015 a professor wrote a _New York Times_ piece on Stoicism, the subject [Ryan Holiday](/personality-analysis/ryan-holiday) had made his own. Holiday, whom we type as a One, wrote down what he felt: "I also had an embarrassing human reaction: jealousy. Why did Professor Massimo Pigliucci get this opportunity and I didn't? Why are things so unfair?" Then he did the One thing and corrected himself. He emailed the professor and published a long interview with him.

### Type 2: "I was here first"

**The alarm:** being unwanted. The Two's jealousy is about position: who you call first, who you lean on.

Watch for extra helpfulness right after a new person enters your life, and for a list of favors that only gets read aloud during a fight. The Two rarely says "I'm jealous." It says "after everything I've done."

### Type 3: "That could have been me"

**The alarm:** being worthless without success. Threes envy wins, and they usually envy the peer closest to them.

[Charli XCX](/personality-analysis/charli-xcx), whom we type as a Three, told _Rolling Stone UK_: "When 'Royals' came out, I was super jealous of the success that that song got, and that Ella got." Then: "You create these parallels and think, 'Well, that could have been me.' But it couldn't have." The Three's move is to turn envy into a plan.

### Type 4: "Why do they get to be whole?"

**The alarm:** having no identity. The Four envies a way of being more than any thing someone owns.

You'll see it as longing more than spite: the friend who goes quiet after your good news because it reminded them of what they don't have. Expect them to bring it up later, often in a way that makes it about them.

### Type 5: "They're overrated anyway"

**The alarm:** being useless or incapable. Naranjo puts the Five right next to envy, sharing "a comparable sense of deprivation," with one difference: "just as avarice is resigned, envy is passionate."

So the Five's envy rarely burns. It cools into contempt. Listen for the dismissive review of a rival's work, delivered with a little too much detail, and then a retreat back into their own project.

### Type 6: "Who were you texting?"

**The alarm:** losing support. A Six's jealousy is a loyalty test, and it shows up as checking, questions and worst-case stories.

Sixes can also envy people who don't seem to carry the alarm at all. [Kendall Jenner](/personality-analysis/kendall-jenner), whom we type as a Six, said of her sister Kylie: "I was jealous that I didn't have as much of a free spirit as her at the time."

### Type 7: "A little, but I'm happy for him"

**The alarm:** being trapped in pain or missing out. Sevens feel the sting, especially as fear of missing out, and then reframe it fast.

When [Chris Hemsworth](/personality-analysis/chris-hemsworth), whom we type as a Seven, was asked whether he was jealous that his younger brother Liam got further in the Thor auditions, he said: "A little jealous maybe, but I was excited for him." Then he went back in, got the part, and turned the feeling into motivation.

### Type 8: "Nobody takes what's mine"

**The alarm:** being controlled or betrayed. Eights don't do jealousy often, and when they do it arrives as anger and control instead of hurt.

[Napoleon Bonaparte](/personality-analysis/napoleon-bonaparte), whom we type as an Eight, wrote letters to Josephine during his Italian campaign that lurch between adoration, jealousy and rage, sometimes in the same paragraph: "You never write me; you don't love your husband." He was commanding an army while he wrote them.

### Type 9: "It's fine"

**The alarm:** loss and separation. A Nine would rather not want something than risk a fight over it.

The jealousy is still there, just quieter. You'll see it as a Nine going vague and distant after a night out, then flaring over something small a week later. Ask what's wrong and the answer is "nothing."

## The honest "it's complicated"

Three things push against the pick.

**First, nobody has measured this.** We found no study that gave any envy or jealousy scale to people across all nine types. The ranking above is a theory-based read, the same kind of read Enneagram teachers make, and you should hold it that way.

**Second, instinct may matter as much as type.** Enneagram teachers describe three instincts, and the one-to-one instinct is the one focused on a single intense bond. Our read is that a Six or a Seven with that instinct dominant can look more jealous in love than a socially focused Four. Our [guide to the instinctual subtypes](/enneagram-corner/enneagram-instinctual-subtypes) explains how to tell which one leads for you.

**Third, jealousy is everybody's.** Every type above has a version, and plenty of people feel it more than their type would predict.

None of that changes the pick. Envy is the Four's defining passion in the Enneagram's own theory, and no other type has a feeling of lack built that deep into its core fear. If you're looking for the most jealous Enneagram type, it's Type 4, with Type 2 close behind in relationships.

## If jealousy is running you

1. **Name which one it is.** If you want what they have, it's envy. If you're afraid of losing someone to them, it's jealousy. Each one asks a different question.
2. **Find the alarm.** Read your type's section above and ask what the feeling is protecting. A Four's envy protects an identity. A Two's jealousy protects a role.
3. **Say the plain sentence.** "I felt jealous when you laughed at his text" works better than the silent treatment for every type on this list.

If jealousy has turned into checking a partner's phone or controlling who they see, our [relationship red flags by type](/enneagram-corner/toxic-traits-relationships-warning-signs) covers when a pattern stops being a feeling and becomes a problem. For how each type reads love in the first place, see [the Enneagram types in relationships](/enneagram-corner/enneagram-types-in-relationships).

## Frequently asked questions

### Which Enneagram type is the most envious?

Type 4. Envy is the traditional passion assigned to the Four. Fours tend to envy what other people seem to be, at ease, whole, sure of who they are, more than what they own.

### What is the least jealous Enneagram type?

Type 9. Jealousy means wanting something badly enough to fight for it, and the Nine's strategy is to want less so nothing disturbs the peace. Nines still get jealous. It tends to go underground and come out sideways.

### What is the difference between envy and jealousy?

Envy is about lacking something another person has. Jealousy is about the threat of losing something you already have, usually a relationship, to a third person. Richard H. Smith calls it a two-person situation versus a three-person one.

### Which Enneagram type is most jealous in relationships?

Type 2, with Type 6 close behind. The Two fears being replaced as the one you need. The Six fears betrayal, so its jealousy looks like suspicion and checking. Type 8 jealousy is rarer, and it comes out as possessiveness and anger.

### Are Type 4s toxic because of envy?

No. Envy is a feeling, and it's the Four's growth edge, not a verdict on the person. Healthy Fours often turn it into art or empathy. The trouble starts when the Four treats someone else's ease as proof that something is wrong with them.
