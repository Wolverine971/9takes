<QuickAnswer question="Which Enneagram type is the most selfish?">
<strong>Our pick is Type 7.</strong> The Seven's core fear is being trapped in pain, so its first move is to protect its own options, and the people around it feel that when plans change or the hard conversation gets dropped. <strong>Type 4 is the runner-up</strong>, because a Four's attention keeps circling back to its own feelings. <strong>Type 9 is the least selfish</strong>, because its whole strategy is setting its own wants aside. Every type has a selfish move. These three are about whose default strategy runs closest to the word.
</QuickAnswer>

<p class="firstLetter">A podcaster says she wants "to be selfish a little longer" before having kids. A rapper calls one of his own songs a catalog of "my insecurities and selfishness." A business professor looks back at the man who asked for a divorce and calls him "So. F\*\*\*ing. Selfish." [Alex Cooper](/personality-analysis/alex-cooper) is a Seven, [Kendrick Lamar](/personality-analysis/kendrick-lamar) a Four and [Scott Galloway](/personality-analysis/scott-galloway) an Eight. Three types, one word.</p>

Search "which Enneagram type is the most selfish" and the top pages decline to pick. They list nine flaws and stop. The quotes above come from three different numbers on the Enneagram, and the selfishness in each one has a different engine. Two of those engines sit much closer to the word than the rest.

So here's a pick, the reasoning behind it, and the version of selfishness each of the nine types actually does.

---

## How we picked

Our working definition: "selfish" is when one person's wants override another person's need, and the first person doesn't notice. The Enneagram question is narrower: **whose basic survival strategy requires putting their own experience first?**

Each type runs on a core fear and a strategy for managing it. For some types, that strategy points outward: keep the peace, be needed, be useful. For others, it points at their own experience: stay free, stay understood, stay in control. The closer a type's strategy sits to "my experience comes first," the more often its ordinary behavior gets called selfish by the people around it.

That gives a ranking at the top and bottom. The middle is a tangle of different kinds of selfishness, which is where the nine takes below earn their keep.

---

## The pick: Type 7

The Seven's core fear is being trapped in pain, boredom or deprivation. Its strategy is to keep options open and keep the next good thing in view. The traditional Enneagram name for the Seven's passion is gluttony: wanting more of the experience, more of the plan, more of what's next.

Nothing about that is malicious. But it puts the Seven's own experience at the center by design, and other people pay for it in specific, recognizable ways:

- The plan gets upgraded mid-week, and nobody asked you.
- The hard conversation gets reframed into something positive before you've finished saying what hurt.
- They're the best person to have at the party and the hardest to reach in the hospital waiting room.

Our [toxic traits guide](/enneagram-corner/toxic-traits-of-each-enneagram-type) calls the unhealthy Seven pattern "selfish escapism," and the word fits the outside view. From the inside, it feels like survival. Staying in the pain feels like it might never end, so the Seven leaves before it starts.

[Alex Cooper](/personality-analysis/alex-cooper), whom we type as a Seven, named this in herself in June 2025 while talking about delaying motherhood: "I want to grind at work and I want to be selfish a little longer." What makes that a healthy Seven moment is that she said it out loud. The unhealthy version would have quietly booked the next thing.

## The runner-up: Type 4

The Four's core fear is having no identity or significance, so it looks inward to find out who it is. That's where its attention lives: in its own mood, its own longing, its own story about what's missing.

The result is self-absorption more than taking. [Joaquin Phoenix](/personality-analysis/joaquin-phoenix), whom we type as a Four, used his 2020 Oscar speech for _Joker_ to call himself "selfish," "cruel at times" and "hard to work with." The Four's selfishness tends to be confessed in public and lived in private: the airtime, the mood that sets the room's temperature, the story that circles back to its own lack. The traditional Enneagram passion for the type is envy.

Sevens rank higher because their selfishness acts: it cancels, upgrades and leaves. A Four's mostly occupies the airtime. Both hurt. One costs you more plans.

## The least selfish: Type 9

The Nine's core fear is loss and separation, and its strategy is to merge with other people's agendas. Nines are famous for not knowing what they want for dinner, or for the next ten years, because wanting things creates friction. The [Enneagram Institute](https://www.enneagraminstitute.com/type-9) describes the average Nine "saying 'yes' to things they do not really want to do." That's the opposite of the problem this page is about.

Nines aren't saints. Their selfishness is quiet and stubborn: they say yes, then don't do it. But it rarely takes anything on purpose. [Pete Davidson](/personality-analysis/pete-davidson), a Nine on our site, described fatherhood to Theo Von as relief: "I get to be unselfish. I'm not the focal point of my life anymore. I'm so sick of myself." For a Nine, getting out of the center feels like rest.

**And Type 2?** Twos are most people's guess for least selfish, and Twos do give constantly. But the Two's passion is pride, the belief that it has no needs and others need it. When the help comes with an unspoken bill, that is the Two's version of selfishness, and it can cost you more than the Seven's canceled plans.

## The nine takes: what selfish looks like in each type

Same word, nine different behaviors, nine different alarms underneath. The point is to read the alarm instead of stopping at the label.

### Type 1: "My way is the right way"

**The alarm:** being wrong, or being blamed for something done badly.

The One's selfishness wears a halo. They redo the dishes someone else already washed, because their way is correct and correct isn't a preference. Ones rarely feel selfish, because they're sacrificing comfort for a standard. When [Tim Cook](/personality-analysis/tim-cook) offered [Steve Jobs](/personality-analysis/steve-jobs), a fellow One, part of his liver, Jobs refused and yelled at him. Cook's read later: "Somebody that's selfish, doesn't reply like that." Even dying, Jobs wouldn't take what he judged wrong to take. With his own life on the line, the standard still won.

### Type 2: Help with a receipt

**The alarm:** being unwanted or unloved.

The Two gives first and keeps a quiet ledger. You get the casserole, the airport ride and the long talk at midnight. Six months later, during an argument, you hear "after everything I've done for you." The help was real. So was the invoice. A Two's selfishness is needing to be needed, which means your independence can feel like a threat.

### Type 3: The room is an audience

**The alarm:** being worthless without results.

The Three's selfishness is about attention and time: the goal crowds everything else out of view. [Logan Paul](/personality-analysis/logan-paul), a Three on our site, described the change after his daughter was born in September 2024: "I'm no longer my first priority. It's made me less selfish." He ranked himself third, behind his daughter and his fiancée. For a Three, that's the tell: the first priority was the career, and he could say so only once something outranked it.

### Type 4: My feelings first

**The alarm:** being ordinary or unseen.

The Four's own feeling arrives louder than anyone else's, and the Four experiences saying so as honesty. On _To Pimp a Butterfly_, [Kendrick Lamar](/personality-analysis/kendrick-lamar) wrote "u," a song that screams every charge he has filed against himself, including the family he wasn't there for while he chased a rap career. He called it "one of the hardest songs I had to write": "All my insecurities and selfishness and let-downs." Nine tracks later, "i" makes the case that he deserves to love himself anyway. That's the Four's arc in two songs: the self-indictment first, then the search for an identity that survives it.

### Type 5: Time and energy, rationed

**The alarm:** being drained or overwhelmed.

The Five's selfishness is withholding. Its passion is traditionally called avarice, and it's mostly about time, energy and privacy rather than money. During _Oppenheimer_, [Cillian Murphy](/personality-analysis/cillian-murphy) never joined the cast for dinner, and Matt Damon called him "the worst dinner companion imaginable." Damon's explanation was that Murphy's brain was "just too full," and Murphy didn't argue: "I've always been like that." That's the Five in one scene: protecting a limited supply, and leaving other people to read the empty chair as coldness.

### Type 6: Mine first, just in case

**The alarm:** being without support or security.

The Six's selfishness is defensive: stock up, hedge, keep a backup plan. Sixes also turn the word on themselves. [Jennifer Lawrence](/personality-analysis/jennifer-lawrence), a Six on our site, has said, "It feels vain and selfish that I love being a creative person as much as I do." [Howard Stern](/personality-analysis/howard-stern), another Six, sums up his younger self as "I was a selfish prick."

### Type 7: Keep my options open

**The alarm:** being trapped in pain or missing out.

Covered above. The tell is timing: Sevens are most selfish at the exact moment something becomes heavy, boring or limiting.

### Type 8: Take what's needed

**The alarm:** being controlled or vulnerable.

The Eight's selfishness is appetite plus control. [Scott Galloway](/personality-analysis/scott-galloway), an Eight on our site, told the Rational Reminder podcast that years of working until 10 or 11 at night "cost me my first marriage." He asked for the divorce, and later called his early-thirties self "So. F\*\*\*ing. Selfish." The work was his choice and the cost landed on someone else. That's the Eight's version: they choose the terms.

### Type 9: Peace for me, at your cost

**The alarm:** conflict and disconnection.

Covered above. The Nine's selfish move is comfort: avoiding the hard conversation so _they_ don't have to feel the tension, even when you needed the conversation. "I'm fine" protects the Nine's peace and leaves you alone with the problem.

---

## The honest "it's complicated"

Health matters more than type. A healthy Seven is generous and present; an unhealthy Nine can freeze out a whole family. Type tells you the _shape_ of someone's selfishness, not how much of it they have.

Self-protection also isn't selfishness. A Five who says no to a fourth social event, or a Two who finally asks for something back, is doing the growth work, even if it looks selfish to people used to the old pattern.

With that said, the pick stands: the Seven, with the Four close behind.

<InsightBox tone="info" title="The short version">
Sevens protect their options, Fours protect their feelings, and Nines protect everyone else's comfort at the expense of their own. Every type has a selfish move. Read the alarm under it before you decide what it says about the person.
</InsightBox>

## What to say to each type's selfish move

Naming the alarm works better than naming the flaw. One line per type:

<div class="scroll-table">

| Type | Their selfish move            | Try saying                                                                   |
| ---- | ----------------------------- | ---------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| 1    | Overrides your way            | "I know your way works. Mine works too, and I'd like it to stand."           |
| 2    | Brings up what they gave      | "I'm grateful. What do you need from me right now? Ask directly."            |
| 3    | Turns your moment into work   | "Tonight is mine. Can the work wait two hours?"                              |
| 4    | Pulls the focus to their mood | "I want to hear that. Can we finish mine first?"                             |
| 5    | Withholds time                | "I'm not asking for all of it. One hour on Thursday?"                        |
| 6    | Vetoes the risky plan         | "What would make this feel safe enough to try?"                              |
| 7    | Leaves when it gets heavy     | "I don't need you to fix this. I need you to stay in it for ten minutes."    |
| 8    | Decides for everyone          | "I'm pushing back. I want a vote on this one."                               |
| 9    | Avoids the hard talk          | "Your 'fine' leaves me alone with it. I'd rather fight about it than guess." |

</div>
