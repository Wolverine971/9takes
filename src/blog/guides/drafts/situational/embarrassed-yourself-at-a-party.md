## The scene

- next morning. The phone is face down. You don't want to look
- the replay: the joke that wasn't one, the thing you said about [someone], the volume
- [DJ: your night]

## What they said → what they were really thinking

- laughter in the moment → mostly with you; some of it nervous
- "you were funny last night" → it's fine
- one person went quiet → that's the only one you need to talk to

## What's going on for them

- they barely remember. They were managing their own night [CHECK: spotlight effect, Gilovich 2000]
- the one person you hurt is waiting to see if you'll mention it

## What's going on for you

- shame, replaying on loop
- you think everyone saw it. A couple of people did
- you like them less because you assume they like you less [CHECK: liking gap, Boothby 2018]

## The nine the morning after (carousel)

1. writes a moral verdict on themselves
2. apologizes to everyone, including people who didn't notice
3. spins it into a funny story before anyone else can
4. spirals for a week
5. disappears from the group chat
6. polls friends: "was it bad?"
7. already planning the next party
8. "I said what I said," minus the one line that crossed
9. acts like nothing happened, still thinking about it

## Defuse it

- sort it: who was hurt vs who was there. Only the first group needs anything
- one apology, in person or by call, within a day: name it, no excuses, stop
- everyone else gets nothing or a light line: [DJ: line]
- don't keep bringing it up. Every mention reminds them
- don't: a group-chat apology essay
