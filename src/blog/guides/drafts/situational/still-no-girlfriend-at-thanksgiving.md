## What they'll say → what they're really asking

- "anyone special?" → are you okay?
- "you're too picky" → I don't understand your life
- "is there something you want to tell us?" → I'm scared of what I don't know
- "you'll find someone" → I'm worried you won't

## What's going on for them

- their own timeline anxiety, projected
- your mom: fear she did something wrong / won't get grandkids
- your being single feels like a problem they're supposed to solve

## What's going on for you

- the question isn't the hit; the look is
- "what's wrong with me?" runs in the background all weekend
- their alarm ≠ your defect

## The nine at this table (carousel)

1. defends his standards a little too hard
2. jokes it off, then helps in the kitchen all night
3. talks about work wins to change the scoreboard
4. hears "something is wrong with you"
5. gives a one-word answer, disappears to the garage
6. waits for the question all day
7. funny answer, new topic, gone
8. "why, you got someone for me?" Ends it
9. "not yet" and lets it wash over

## Defuse it

- answer before the room fills the silence: short, warm, done
- give them something true and boring: "not right now, I'm focused on [X]"
- turn it back with a real question about them
- for the repeat offender: name it, smile: "you've asked that three times. I'm good"

- don't say: anything defensive or self-roasting. Both make them worry more
