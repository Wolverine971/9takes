> "Paul, in that screen test, literally handed her a razor and was like, 'Shave Leo's face.'"
>
> Cassandra Kulukundis, casting director, [TheWrap, 2025](https://www.thewrap.com/chase-infiniti-one-battle-after-another-interview/)

<p class="firstLetter">She had been auditioning for months by then. Karate lessons. Weapons practice. Chemistry reads, callbacks, costume fittings for a part that was not hers. Then Paul Thomas Anderson put a razor in her hand and pointed her at Leonardo DiCaprio.</p>

Chase Infiniti was twenty-three. She had exactly one job on her résumé, and she had never used an electric shaver in her life.

"I'm sure she was shaking," Kulukundis told TheWrap in 2025, "but she held it all in and didn't look like she missed a beat."

Kulukundis was guessing from the outside. Infiniti, describing the shave on Jimmy Kimmel's show in January 2026, was not guessing: "I was like shaking the entire time. I was like, 'I'm so sorry. I hope I don't do anything wrong.'"

She was shaking. Nobody in that room could tell.

Three years after that screen test, Anderson's <em>One Battle After Another</em> has earned her best-actress nominations from the Golden Globes, BAFTA, SAG and Critics Choice, and her second lead role a 2026 Emmy nomination. Every profile reaches for the same word. Poise.

She has a more useful word for it, and she used it about lace.

Here is what the poise story leaves out. She grew up biracial in Indianapolis, on the seam between the white neighborhoods and the Black ones, and she told <em>Dazed</em> in December 2025 that she understood Willa's isolation from the inside: "I can definitely understand feeling like you don't fully belong in your environment."

She built the ability to look like she belonged in a room years before anyone handed her a razor in one.

Her mantra, given to <em>Elle</em> in October 2025: "I ground myself and remind myself that I'm here for a reason. I'm not here by accident."

That is a reminder you only need if some part of you suspects otherwise.

## What is Chase Infiniti's personality type?

### Chase Infiniti Is an Enneagram Type 3 (The Achiever)

Chase Infiniti is an Enneagram Type 3, the Achiever, with a 2 wing. [Threes](/enneagram-corner/enneagram-type-3) measure themselves by output, and they learn young to read a room and hand it what it rewards. Underneath sits a suspicion that with the output stripped away, there would be nothing left there to love.

The audition that nearly consumed 2023 is the clearest case. Six months of callbacks with no contract, no salary and no guarantee. Most people describe that stretch as uncertainty. She described it as school.

"I treated every single callback and chemistry read like a master class," she told TheWrap in 2025. "I would observe them, and I would try to pick up everything that I could and be a sponge."

Then she trained in mixed martial arts for four straight months for a role she had not been given.

Ambition is the boring part. Every type has a version of it. The tell is what she converts pressure into. Handed a razor, she produced a performance. Handed six months of limbo, she produced a curriculum. Handed a Gilead handicraft she could not do, she produced this line for <em>W</em> magazine in June 2026: "I've struggled with embroidery and lace making. We all did. I'm at a point now where it's convincing on camera."

Convincing. She set the bar inside somebody else's eye.

**The pressure test.** Type 6 is the one reading that could displace this one, and it has evidence behind it. She narrates her career almost entirely through mentors: Anderson and DiCaprio were "incredible leaders and guides on that film," she told TheWrap in 2025, and Elisabeth Moss, her <em>Testaments</em> co-star and executive producer, is "an encyclopedia." At her IndieWire Honors tribute in 2025 she thanked the crew and said she shadowed anyone who would let her watch. She calls herself a scaredy-cat. That is a real case.

It breaks on where the standard sits. A Six steadies by preparing until the risk is covered, then checks that preparation against someone they trust. Her bar sits in the observer, and she says so out loud: convincing on camera. Six months of contractless limbo produced no vigilance and no hunt for reassurance, only a curriculum she graded herself against. The mentors are real; they read as a Three's healthiest instinct rather than a Six's core, and the full case is in the rabbit hole below.

One thing stays unexplained. First the caveat that governs everything above: nearly every word quoted in this piece was said to a reporter during one of two awards campaigns, filtered through an interviewer and an edit. Inside that record, one behavior refuses to fit. She never told her <em>Testaments</em> castmates she was in <em>One Battle After Another</em>. "I didn't tell anybody that I was in the movie," she told <em>Variety</em> in June 2026, "until the trailer started to play before <em>Sinners</em> at the movie theater." Handed the most standing-establishing credential a young actor can get, in front of a new peer group, she said nothing. Threes are legible on purpose. Something in her working process is not, and three years of public record, through mid-2026, is not enough to say what.

## Chase Infiniti's Childhood in Indianapolis

What is legible about her has an origin, and it starts with the name she was given.

Her parents watched <em>Batman Forever</em> and decided Nicole Kidman's Chase Meridian was the most formidable woman they had ever seen on a screen. For the middle name they took Buzz Lightyear's line about infinity. Then they handed the whole thing to a daughter and let her carry it.

She has thought about this. "I don't want things to hold me back or to stop me," she told <em>Elle</em> in 2025, about her own name. "I am pushing myself creatively and chasing greatness."

Most kids get a name. She got an instruction.

The house was loud with music. Her father had played drums in a jazz band. From 2016 she worked steadily with Summer Stock Stage in Indianapolis, seven credits by 2021, <em>Bonnie and Clyde</em> through <em>Godspell</em>.

The other half of that childhood is the half she brought to Willa. The seam ran through her schooling too: a French-immersion private school, then a big public high school. She has said she did not resemble the other mixed kids around her, and described the ache of "feeling like you're missing a part of yourself." It is quieter than drama: a low-grade, permanent calibration, reading a room to work out how much of yourself will fit in it.

<div class="pull-quote">"I will make sure that I fit. If it's a 38,000-point turn, I'm going to get it."</div>

She said that to <em>W</em> magazine in January 2026, about parallel parking. A gap on a street, a car that does not obviously go into it, and a driver who will saw the wheel back and forth however many times it takes rather than drive on and find a space that already fits her. Nothing in that sentence asks the space to change.

That calibration is where a Three's equation gets written. A child who senses that her standing in a room is provisional does not conclude that rooms are unsafe. She concludes that standing is something you earn on arrival, every time, by being good enough that nobody has to decide whether you belong. She has never described it that way herself, and this is a reading rather than her testimony.

Then she saw Zendaya. "I remember being like, this girl looks like me," she told <em>Elle</em>. "She is mixed. She is singing, acting, dancing, doing the thing that I want to do." What she took from it was proof of concept: the shape already existed, and a room had already let it in.

At Columbia College Chicago she majored in musical theatre and co-founded a K-pop cover-dance crew. Her first professional job arrived in early 2023, playing Jake Gyllenhaal's daughter on <em>Presumed Innocent</em>: five months alone in Los Angeles at twenty-two, the longest she had ever been away from home.

She kept the dance crew anyway, turning up in Tyler, the Creator's "Darling, I" in 2025, mid-campaign, and again in 2026, with an Emmy nomination behind her, in a video for the K-pop group Ateez. A Three's usual move is to shed whatever has stopped advancing the career; she kept all of it.

## The Master Class Chase Infiniti Was Never Enrolled In

Keeping everything is also how she got cast. She called the audition a master class. Take her literally and the six months have a syllabus.

Kulukundis, who ran the search, needed someone who could "kick some butt" and "run the gamut of emotions." Her reaction to the self-tape was simpler than that: "She's so special. She's so unique." Later in the same answer she gave the reason: "she just felt like such a real person."

Anderson's own summary, sent by email, is the sharpest anyone has offered: "Chase is extremely agile and quick on her feet. She is also able to fight the corner for her character and what she believes."

He is describing an adaptive instinct as a professional asset, and it is the same instinct she built on a neighborhood line in Indiana, running at full speed in a director's chair.

What reached the screen is smaller than the audition story makes it sound. Willa is a revolutionary's teenage daughter trying to have an ordinary adolescence under a name that is not hers. She was born Charlene Calhoun; Willa Ferguson is an alias, and she and the man who raised her lived under it off-grid for sixteen years. Infiniti plays most of her at the register of an ordinary kid: chewing her father out for drunk driving, correcting him on a schoolfriend's pronouns. Then federal agents take her and the whole film reorganizes around getting her back. Writing in <em>The FADER</em> in September 2025, David Renshaw said she gives Willa "a steeliness that makes you leave the theater thinking having faith in the kids isn't misplaced." She did her own action work, and the Alliance of Women Film Journalists gave her its stunt-performance award for it, on a first film, at twenty-three.

## What Chase Infiniti Does When She Gets Scared

Getting cast did not settle the question the razor asked. Her first day on the film, they shot the breakfast scene from the self-tape, the one she had been carrying since the cold open, now with DiCaprio across the table and Anderson behind the camera.

"I remember being very nervous that day," she said.

<p class="inner-thought">You know this scene. You have known it for a year. You did it alone in a room with nobody watching and it was good enough to get you here, which means the only variable left in it is you, sitting in this chair, in front of the person you grew up watching. Breathe. Do not let it into your hands.</p>

Watch what she does in those moments. She writes herself a frame. ## Why Chase Infiniti Thanks Everyone but Herself

## What "Convincing on Camera" Means to Chase Infiniti

The shaking never showed. She is the one who keeps telling people it was there.
