> ["I think it's a real weakness to want to be liked. A real weakness. I do not have that."](https://www.rev.com/transcripts/dealbook-summit-2023-elon-musk-interview-transcript)
>
> — Elon Musk, DealBook Summit, November 2023

<p class="firstLetter">Late 2017. Tesla's Model 3 line is failing, and Wall Street analysts are about to join an earnings call.</p>

Jon McNeill, then Tesla's president of global sales, opens a dark conference room. Elon Musk is lying on the carpet and doesn't respond. It takes McNeill about half an hour to get him into a chair, and then he covers the call.

Walter Isaacson's biography reports that the scene played out five or six times. Once, McNeill presented a website redesign lying on the floor beside him, because Musk wouldn't get up. McNeill has since [told the story himself](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/features/2026-04-02/tesla-s-former-president-shares-lessons-from-managing-elon-musk-s-chaos).

<EvidenceFigure evidenceId="elon-model-3-launch-2017" />

Most arguments about Musk come down to genius or menace. The man on the carpet raises a better question. Why does someone with almost unlimited resources keep building a life with no slack left in it?

Our answer starts with one fear: that the world will ask for more than he has. His lifelong move is to retreat into a problem until he can master it. Now the life is so big there's nowhere left to retreat to, so the problems keep getting bigger.

## What is Elon Musk's personality type?

### Elon Musk is an Enneagram Type 5

**Elon Musk is an [Enneagram Type 5](/enneagram-corner/enneagram-type-5), the Investigator, most likely 5w6.** Myers-Briggs sites usually call him an INTJ or INTP. The Enneagram asks a different question: what is all that thinking for?

Fives sit in the head triad, the thinking center, and their core emotion is fear. The mechanism is simple. When the world asks for more than a Five thinks they can handle, they pull back. They study the problem until they can master it. Then they come back out with a plan, and the plan becomes their protection. What they're really avoiding is helplessness.

Musk looks nothing like the quiet Five in the library. He's loud, combative and everywhere, which is why so many people call him an 8. We take that argument seriously in the Rabbit Hole below. But watch where he goes when things get bad.

He goes into the problem.

## How Elon Musk turns fear into a map

<span id="dj-reasoning"></span>

Musk has said he tries to minimize being ["confidently wrong"](https://lexfridman.com/elon-musk-4-transcript). His engineering method starts by questioning every requirement and deleting whatever shouldn't exist, and only then optimizing what's left. [The method is built to catch bad assumptions](https://lexfridman.com/?p=5969), his own included.

Before SpaceX, he didn't know how to build a rocket. Jim Cantrell, an early SpaceX executive, described him [absorbing rocket-science texts and interrogating people](https://time.com/4033395/elon-musk-learning/) until he could work inside the field himself. In a [Tsinghua University dialogue](https://www.sem.tsinghua.edu.cn/en/info/1135/2367.htm), he traced rocket prices back to raw materials, which is how reusability became the whole bet.

The Model 3 crisis is the cleanest test. In 2018, Musk admitted Tesla had leaned on too much automation and too much new technology at once. He tore out machinery, took over the line and slept at the factory.

Listen to what he said brought him back, though. He had only recently gained a ["clear understanding of the path out of hell"](https://www.cbsnews.com/news/elon-musk-tesla-model-3-problems-interview-today-2018-04-13/). His confidence returned when the map did.

That's the Five sequence: the map comes back, then the action speeds up. You'll see it in every crisis below.

## Elon Musk and Asperger's: running "human" in emulation mode

People joked about Musk being autistic for years. In May 2021 he beat them to it. Hosting *Saturday Night Live*, he told the audience he was ["the first person with Asperger's to host SNL"](https://www.thewrap.com/snl-no-elon-musk-isnt-the-first-host-with-aspergers-video/), "or at least the first to admit it."

Then came the line that tells you how he thinks about it. He wouldn't make a lot of eye contact with the cast, he said, but not to worry: he was "pretty good at running 'human' in emulation mode."

Asperger's is an older label, now folded into autism spectrum disorder. 9takes doesn't diagnose anyone. But autism and Type 5 are two different layers. Autism describes how a brain takes in social information. Type 5 describes what a person does with fear. Plenty of autistic people aren't Fives, and most Fives aren't autistic. (Our [neurodivergence guide](/enneagram-corner/mental-health/enneagram-neurodivergence-guide) explains why personality tests mix them up.)

In Musk, the two layers seem to stack. "Emulation mode" describes social behavior as software he runs on purpose. That's a man who learned people the way he learned rockets: from the outside, by building a model.

You can watch both layers in his 2023 BBC interview. The reporter said hateful content on Twitter had gone up. Musk asked for one specific example. Then he asked again. When none came, curiosity hardened into confrontation.

A literal mind wants the data point. A Five wants it because, without it, he's being asked to accept a claim he can't check. Later in the same conversation, Musk admitted the criticism of his leadership hurt, then immediately [reframed it as feedback he shouldn't lose](https://www.rev.com/transcripts/elon-musk-interview-with-the-bbc-4-11-23-transcript). The hurt turned into data within a sentence.

That's the context for the DealBook line at the top of this page. Not needing to be liked is an asset in a factory crisis. With the people closest to him, it's expensive.

## How retreat became Elon Musk's operating system

Musk's childhood gave withdrawal a job. After his parents divorced, he chose to live with his father, Errol, a decision he later described with deep regret. He was badly bullied at school. He read for hours at a time, taught himself to code, and sold a video game he wrote at twelve.

Isaacson's biography describes a boy who kept leaving an unpredictable human world for one that ran on rules. Decades later, his second wife, Talulah Riley, watched that past break through.

<EvidenceFigure evidenceId="elon-talulah-riley-childhood-quote" />

People could humiliate him, leave him or overwhelm him. A system could be studied. The boy who escaped into books became the man who can disappear into a broken production line while standing in the middle of the factory.

Isaacson's biography has a name for Musk's darkest, angriest state: "demon mode." Grimes, the mother of three of his children, described it this way: ["Demon mode is when he goes dark and retreats inside the storm in his brain."](https://fortune.com/2023/09/12/elon-musk-biography-video-games-polytopia-life-lessons/) Even his rage, in her telling, moves inward.

Comfort runs through the same channels. His humor is one. A meme makes contact without much exposure. If the joke lands, he gets instant connection. If it bombs, it was irony.

Games are the other. In April 2022, he and Grimes played *Elden Ring* until 5 a.m.; at 7:23 he tweeted that he had made an offer for Twitter. His favorite, the strategy game *Polytopia*, even came with "Polytopia Life Lessons." One reads like a Five's manual for fear: "You will lose. It will hurt the first fifty times. When you get used to losing, you will play each game with less emotion."

Even his downtime is practice at feeling less.

With an audience of millions, the stakes of a post go up. In August 2018 he posted that he was considering taking Tesla private at $420 a share, "funding secured." The SEC charged him with fraud and said he picked $420 partly because of the number's marijuana connotation. Musk maintained the funding was real. He and Tesla paid $40 million combined, he gave up the chair, and in 2023 a jury found him not liable to shareholders who had sued.

The posting habit survived all of it.

## Why Elon Musk bought Twitter

Start with the logic that built SpaceX. Humanity needs to become multiplanetary. Nobody is building cheap rockets. He can learn to build rockets. So he builds them.

Now run Twitter through the same machine. Civilization needs a place where people can speak freely. The platform that matters most isn't one he trusts. He can afford to buy it. So he buys it.

In April 2022, hours after making his offer, he said almost exactly that on the [TED stage](https://www.ted.com/talks/90833). The deal was "not a way to make money." His "strong intuitive sense" was that "having a public platform that is maximally trusted and broadly inclusive is extremely important to the future of civilization."

That's first-principles reasoning. We think there's also a layer underneath. Twitter was where Musk had fought his critics for a decade. It was where "funding secured" had cost him his chairmanship. A Five's deepest instinct is to control the inputs, and buying Twitter meant owning the room where people talked about him.

Then he tried to back out. Twitter sued in Delaware to hold him to the deal, and he closed it in late October 2022. Within days he cut about half the staff. On November 16, he emailed the rest. Twitter 2.0 would need to be ["extremely hardcore,"](https://fortune.com/2022/11/16/elon-musk-email-twitter-extremely-hardcore-long-hours-high-intensity/) with long hours at high intensity. Anyone who didn't opt in by the next evening got three months' severance. Hundreds more left.

A year later, the platform he bought hosted the post he would later call his worst. On November 15, 2023, a user posted a claim that Jewish communities push hatred against white people. Musk replied: "You have said the actual truth." White House spokesman Andrew Bates called it an ["abhorrent promotion of antisemitic and racist hate."](https://fortune.com/2023/11/17/elon-musk-biden-white-house-anti-semitism-tesla/) IBM, Apple, Disney and other major advertisers pulled back from X.

Two weeks later at DealBook, Musk apologized and cursed in the same interview. He called the reply foolish: ["Of the 30,000 it might be literally the worst and dumbest post I've ever done."](https://www.rev.com/transcripts/dealbook-summit-2023-elon-musk-interview-transcript) Then he turned to the advertisers. If they were trying to blackmail him with money, he said, "go fuck yourself."

Look at what he did with the two halves. The post was a bad output, so he ranked it: worst and dumbest. The advertisers leaving was people telling him what it cost, and he treated that as an attack on the system. The pattern doesn't excuse the post. It explains why his apologies so often arrive with a counterpunch attached.

## When every escape route becomes another company

Pressure does something odd to Musk: it makes his life wider.

August 2018 shows what that costs him. Nine days after "funding secured," he gave *The New York Times* an hourlong interview from his home in Los Angeles. He choked up several times. He said he had nearly missed his brother's wedding and had spent his birthday holed up at Tesla. ["This past year has been the most difficult and painful year of my career,"](https://www.cnbc.com/2018/08/17/musk-details-excruciating-personal-toll-of-tesla-turmoil.html) he said. "It was excruciating."

He said he had no plans to give up either role. But "if you have anyone who can do a better job, please let me know. They can have the job."

That's the Five bind in one breath. He's out of reserves, and the only relief he can picture is someone more capable taking the whole thing. Sharing the load doesn't come up.

So the systems grow instead. In March 2023, five months into the Twitter chaos, with advertisers gone and the company losing money, he quietly incorporated xAI. The expansion isn't random, though. By 2026, rockets, satellites, social data, AI models, chips, robotaxis and humanoid robots had been pulled into one connected bet. Count the companies and he looks scattered. Count the ideas and he looks relentlessly focused.

He answers too many demands by making himself more indispensable.

## Elon Musk and politics: from the stage to the checkbook

Politics is where the pattern got most expensive.

In 2024, Musk went all in. He appeared onstage at Trump rallies and spent [more than $290 million](https://ktvz.com/politics/cnn-us-politics/2025/02/01/elon-musk-spent-more-than-290-million-on-the-2024-election-year-end-fec-filings-show/) on the election, according to year-end federal filings. After the win, he fronted DOGE, the cost-cutting project that treated the federal government like a production line with too many parts.

On January 20, 2025, at an inauguration-day rally in Washington's Capital One Arena, Musk put his hand on his chest and swung his right arm out, palm down, twice. "My heart goes out to you," he told the crowd.

Ruth Ben-Ghiat, a historian of fascism, [wrote](https://www.salon.com/2025/01/21/it-was-a-nazi-salute-historian-dismisses-claim-that-musks-raised-arm-was-mere-awkward-gesture): "It was a Nazi salute and a very belligerent one too." The Anti-Defamation League called it ["an awkward gesture in a moment of enthusiasm"](https://www.aljazeera.com/news/2025/1/22/adl-faces-backlash-for-defending-elon-musks-raised-arm-gesture) and not a Nazi salute. Musk's reply: "Frankly, they need better dirty tricks. The 'everyone is Hitler' attack is sooo tired."

He didn't explain what he meant. He filed the accusation under bad-faith inputs, as he had with the BBC reporter. That explains his response. It doesn't settle the argument about the gesture.

The partnership broke fast. Musk left DOGE at the end of May 2025. In early June he called Trump's spending bill a "disgusting abomination," then posted, and later deleted, a claim tying Trump to the Epstein files. On June 11 he wrote: ["I regret some of my posts about President @realDonaldTrump last week. They went too far."](https://www.semafor.com/article/06/11/2025/musk-says-he-regrets-some-of-his-posts-about-trump) A third party, the America Party, came and went that summer.

Then he rebuilt the bridge. In September 2025 he sat beside Trump at [Charlie Kirk's memorial](https://kutv.com/news/nation-world/trump-musk-shake-hands-at-charlie-kirks-memorial-service) and shook his hand. In January 2026 he posted about a [Mar-a-Lago dinner](https://www.foxnews.com/politics/trump-musk-share-lovely-dinner-mar-a-lago-after-public-feuding) with the Trumps: "2026 is going to be amazing!" In May he flew to Beijing with the president on [Air Force One](https://wtop.com/national/2026/05/group-of-top-executives-including-musk-and-cook-invited-to-join-trump-on-trip-to-china/).

That's the most patient person-to-person repair in his public record. He can mend a relationship when he decides it matters. It also ran to the head of the government that awards SpaceX's largest contracts.

In July 2026 he told *The Economist*: ["I got a little too involved in politics, got carried away, frankly."](https://elonmuskarchive.org/video/economist-elon-musk-2026-07-23) In the same interview he defended DOGE's foreign-aid cuts: ["Zero died because of DOGE. Zero. Point. Zero."](https://newrepublic.com/article/213533/elon-musk-economist-interview-doge-cuts-killed-zero-children) Researchers writing in *The Lancet* had [projected](https://ph.ucla.edu/news-events/news/research-finds-more-14-million-preventable-deaths-2030-if-usaid-defunding) that the cuts could cause more than 14 million deaths by 2030, including more than 4.5 million young children. He could admit he'd misallocated his time. Sitting with someone else's account of harm was harder.

### The 2026 midterms: money instead of microphones

Six days later, [Axios reported](https://axios.com/2026/07/29/musk-pac-midterm-election) that Musk was planning a major push for Republicans in the midterms. By mid-October he was the biggest known donor of the cycle, including [$158 million to the Senate Leadership Fund](https://www.pbs.org/newshour/politics/elon-musk-quietly-becomes-biggest-known-donor-in-midterm-contests-as-he-tries-to-save-republicans). By election day, he had spent [at least $445 million](https://www.wrdw.com/2026/11/05/elon-musk-spent-least-445-million-boost-republicans-midterms/), according to the AP. Yet he ["generally kept a lower profile than in 2024,"](https://www.pbs.org/newshour/politics/elon-musk-quietly-becomes-biggest-known-donor-in-midterm-contests-as-he-tries-to-save-republicans) the AP noted. No rallies. Just money.

Two motives fit. One is the rebuilt alliance and his stated belief. On New Year's Day he posted: ["America is toast if the radical left wins."](https://kutv.com/news/nation-world/tesla-space-x-billionaire-elon-musk-backs-republicans-warns-against-radical-left-ahead-of-2026-midterms-president-donald-trump-doge-one-big-beautiful-bill-act-political-donor)

The other, in our read, is insurance. House Democrats had been [demanding investigations](https://ross.house.gov/2025/2/democrats-demand-investigation-into-musk-s-alleged-conflicts-over-billions-in-fed-contracts) of his DOGE role and his companies' federal contracts since February 2025, and a Democratic majority would have subpoena power. When Politico reported on their plans in October, he replied: ["Live by the sword, die by the sword."](https://www.foxbusiness.com/politics/musk-warns-democrats-he-retaliate-use-congressional-power-investigate-him) Mapping a threat and spending resources to shrink it is a very Five move. The record can't fully separate protecting his companies from believing what he posts.

It didn't work. Democrats took Congress, and Ken Paxton, the Texas Senate candidate his biggest donation was used to support, lost. The next day Musk posted: ["Guess my election-interfering space lasers didn't work this time."](https://www.aljazeera.com/news/2026/11/4/musk-makes-light-of-defeats-after-record-spend-on-republican-candidates) A little later: "Data centers in space looking pretty appealing right now."

A joke to hold the loss at arm's length, then straight back into the next system.

## Elon Musk in 2026: what he revises and what he defends

2026 was full of public corrections, and they don't all look alike.

**March: rebuild xAI.** As xAI's co-founders left one after another, Musk wrote that ["xAI was not built right first time around, so is being rebuilt from the foundations up."](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-03-13/musk-pledges-to-rebuild-xai-as-another-co-founder-departs) By the end of the month, [all eleven were gone](https://thenextweb.com/news/xai-all-cofounders-departed-musk-spacex-rebuild). Reality beat attachment to the old design, but the diagnosis stopped at the architecture. The people who left stayed outside the explanation.

**May: lose to OpenAI.** On May 18, a jury in Oakland found Musk had waited too long to sue OpenAI and Sam Altman, and the judge dismissed his claims. Musk called it ["a calendar technicality"](https://theringer.com/2026/05/21/tech/elon-musk-sam-altman-openai-trial-verdict), posted that the judge was a "terrible activist Oakland judge," then deleted the post. His lawyer said he would appeal. (Our [Musk vs. Altman trial breakdown](/pop-culture/musk-vs-altman-trial-personality-dynamics) covers the clash itself.)

**June: fold the ventures together.** SpaceX's [public offering](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1181412/000162828026036936/spaceexplorationtechnologi.htm) presented the newly acquired xAI as a core pillar, with the money funding launch, satellites and compute. More systems now [depend on one person's model](https://www.nasdaq.com/newsroom/spacex-ipo-rocket-company-launches-historic-ipo).

**July: crown a rival.** On July 9, after months of attacking the company, Musk wrote that he had been ["clearly wrong about Anthropic"](https://www.businesstoday.in/technology/artificial-intelligence/story/i-was-clearly-wrong-about-anthropic-elon-musk-praises-mythos-and-fable-models-542266-2026-07-10) and called it the current AI leader. By then Anthropic was also renting SpaceX's computing power, and in the same thread he promised not to cut it off. The praise went to a paying customer, but the ranking still moved.

Line them up and the boundary shows. When a benchmark says he's behind, he updates the ranking. When an org chart fails, he rebuilds it. When a court rules against him, he argues the procedure.

When a system fails, Musk reaches for a rebuild. When a person describes the cost, he argues the inputs.

## The difference between showing up and taking over

Musk's best leadership moments put his body next to the consequence. After the third Falcon 1 failed in 2008, he stood in front of his employees and promised to keep going. The fourth rocket reached orbit. During the Model 3 crisis, he slept at the factory. During the Twitter takeover, he slept at headquarters. The demands were brutal, but the man making them was in the building.

Taking over looks different: an early-morning email with a next-day deadline, or a late-night post that thousands of people have to clean up. Speed goes up, and so does the distance between his decision and the people living with it.

Twitter had both in the same month. The man sleeping at HQ also sent the "hardcore" ultimatum.

His growth edge is the one thing money can't shorten: the walk from his decision to the people who live with it.

### Why not Type 8?

That's a lot of Eight energy. Here's why the call is still Five.

**Where does he go when it's too much?** Eights under overload push harder or blow outward. Musk's documented collapses go inward: on the floor of a dark conference room, crying at night over things his father said, choking up through a newspaper interview. Even "demon mode," in Grimes's words, means retreating "inside the storm in his brain." An Eight armors against vulnerability. Musk's worst moments look like withdrawal.

**What comes first, the force or the analysis?** Eight anger arrives first and the reasons come later. Most of Musk's fights follow a model: a cost curve, a threat map, a demand for one specific example. But not all of them. "Pedo guy" had no model behind it, and neither did firing Twitter engineers who corrected him in public. This is the Eight case's best ground. **Which emotion is driving?** Eights live in the gut triad with anger at the core. Musk's anger usually sits on top of a threat: AI risk, falling birth rates, a hostile Congress. That's fear doing the steering.

The connecting line explains the healthy version. Type 5's growth line runs to Type 8: a Five at his best becomes decisive, physical and bold, which is Musk after Falcon 1 or on the Model 3 floor. ### Why not Type 6?

A counterphobic Six is the sharper rival. Nearly every argument against Eight points here too: fear underneath, threat maps, insurance against a hostile Congress, striking first at whatever looks dangerous. Counterphobic Sixes handle fear by charging at it, which fits the fights.

The difference is where certainty comes from. Sixes look outward for something to trust or test: an authority, an ally, a team, a rulebook. Musk's certainty comes from his own model. He reads the textbooks, rebuilds the cost from raw materials and trusts that over any expert. When his xAI co-founders walked out, he didn't look for a new authority to trust. He rebuilt the company around his own design. A Six asks who can be trusted. Musk asks what the physics says. The 5w6 wing is where the two overlap.
