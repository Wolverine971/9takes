> ["I think it's a real weakness to want to be liked. A real weakness. I do not have that."](https://www.rev.com/transcripts/dealbook-summit-2023-elon-musk-interview-transcript)
>
> — Elon Musk, DealBook Summit, November 2023

<p class="firstLetter">Autumn 2017. Tesla's Model 3 line is failing, and Wall Street analysts are about to join an earnings call.</p>

Jon McNeill, then Tesla's president of global sales, opens a dark conference room. Elon Musk is lying face-down on the carpet and doesn't respond. McNeill spends half an hour getting him into a chair, then covers the call.

Walter Isaacson reports that scenes like this happened five or six times that autumn. Once, McNeill presented a website redesign while sitting on the floor beside him, because Musk wouldn't get up.

<EvidenceFigure evidenceId="elon-model-3-launch-2017" />

Most arguments about Musk come down to genius or menace. The man on the carpet raises a better question. Why does someone with almost unlimited resources keep building a life with no slack left in it?

Our answer starts with one specific fear: that the world will ask for more than he has. Musk's lifelong move is to retreat into a problem until he can master it. He has now built a life so big that there's nowhere left to retreat to. So the problems keep getting bigger instead.

## What is Elon Musk's personality type?

### Elon Musk is an Enneagram Type 5

**Elon Musk is an [Enneagram Type 5](/enneagram-corner/enneagram-type-5), the Investigator, most likely 5w6.** Myers-Briggs sites usually call him an INTJ or INTP. The Enneagram asks a different question: what is all that thinking for?

Fives sit in the head triad, the thinking center, and their core emotion is fear. The mechanism is simple. When the world asks for more than a Five thinks they can handle, they pull back. They study the problem until they can master it. Then they come back out with a plan, and the plan becomes their protection. What they're really avoiding is helplessness.

Musk looks nothing like the quiet Five in the library. He's loud, combative and everywhere, which is why so many people call him an 8. We take that argument seriously in the Rabbit Hole below. But watch where he goes when things get bad.

He goes into the problem.

## How Elon Musk turns fear into a map

<span id="dj-reasoning"></span>

Watch Musk in a long interview and you'll notice the pause. He listens, goes quiet, and answers from a few layers down. What matters is what the knowledge does for him.

Musk has said he tries to minimize being ["confidently wrong"](https://lexfridman.com/elon-musk-4-transcript). His engineering method starts by questioning every requirement and deleting whatever shouldn't exist, and only then optimizing what's left. [The method is built to catch bad assumptions](https://lexfridman.com/?p=5969), his own included.

Before SpaceX, he didn't know how to build a rocket. So he read the textbooks and grilled the experts. Jim Cantrell, an early SpaceX executive, described Musk absorbing rocket-science texts and interrogating people until he could work inside the field himself. [Books, experts and experiments became one map](https://time.com/4033395/elon-musk-learning/). In a [Tsinghua University dialogue](https://www.sem.tsinghua.edu.cn/en/info/1135/2367.htm), he traced rocket prices back to raw materials, which is how reusability became the whole bet.

The Model 3 crisis is the cleanest test. In 2018, Musk admitted Tesla had leaned on too much automation and too much new technology at once. He tore out machinery, took over the line and slept at the factory.

Listen to what he said brought him back, though. He had only recently gained a ["clear understanding of the path out of hell"](https://www.cbsnews.com/news/elon-musk-tesla-model-3-problems-interview-today-2018-04-13/). His confidence returned when the map did.

Uncertainty got specific, the knowledge came back, and the action sped up once the system made sense again. That's the Five sequence, and you'll see it in every crisis below.

## Elon Musk and Asperger's: running "human" in emulation mode

People joked about Musk being autistic for years. In May 2021 he beat them to it. Hosting *Saturday Night Live*, he told the audience he was ["the first person with Asperger's to host SNL"](https://www.thewrap.com/snl-no-elon-musk-isnt-the-first-host-with-aspergers-video/), "or at least the first to admit it."

Then came the line that tells you how he thinks about it. He wouldn't make a lot of eye contact with the cast, he said, but not to worry: he was "pretty good at running 'human' in emulation mode."

Asperger's is an older label, now folded into autism spectrum disorder. 9takes doesn't diagnose anyone, and his own words are the evidence here. But the disclosure matters for a personality read, because autism and Type 5 are two different layers.

Autism describes how a brain takes in social information. Type 5 describes what a person does with fear. Plenty of autistic people aren't Fives, and most Fives aren't autistic. (Our [neurodivergence guide](/enneagram-corner/mental-health/enneagram-neurodivergence-guide) explains why personality tests mix them up.)

In Musk, the two layers seem to stack. "Emulation mode" describes social behavior as software he runs on purpose. That's a man who learned people the way he learned rockets: from the outside, by building a model.

You can watch both layers in his 2023 BBC interview. The reporter said hateful content on Twitter had gone up. Musk asked for one specific example. Then he asked again. When none came, curiosity hardened into confrontation.

A literal mind wants the data point. A Five wants it because, without it, he's being asked to accept a claim he can't check. Later in the same conversation, Musk admitted the criticism of his leadership hurt, then immediately [reframed it as feedback he shouldn't lose](https://www.rev.com/transcripts/elon-musk-interview-with-the-bbc-4-11-23-transcript). The hurt turned into data within a sentence.

That's also the context for the DealBook line at the top of this page. Whatever the cause, Musk says he doesn't feel the pull to be liked. In a factory crisis, that's an asset. With the people closest to him, it's expensive.

## How retreat became Elon Musk's operating system

Musk's childhood gave withdrawal a job. After his parents divorced, he chose to live with his father, Errol, a decision he later described with deep regret. He was badly bullied at school. He read for hours at a time, taught himself to code, and sold a video game he wrote at twelve.

Isaacson's biography describes a boy who kept leaving an unpredictable human world for one that ran on rules. Decades later, his second wife, Talulah Riley, watched that past break through.

<EvidenceFigure evidenceId="elon-talulah-riley-childhood-quote" />

People could humiliate him, leave him or overwhelm him. A system could be studied. The boy who escaped into books became the man who can disappear into a broken production line while standing in the middle of the factory.

His humor does the same job. A meme makes contact without much exposure. If the joke lands, he gets instant connection. If it bombs, it was irony.

With an audience of millions, the stakes of a post go up. In August 2018 he posted that he was considering taking Tesla private at $420 a share, "funding secured." The SEC charged him with fraud and said he picked $420 partly because of the number's marijuana connotation. Musk maintained the funding was real. He and Tesla paid $40 million combined, he gave up the chair, and in 2023 a jury found him not liable to shareholders who had sued.

The posting habit survived all of it.

## Why Elon Musk bought Twitter

Start with the logic that built SpaceX. Humanity needs to become multiplanetary. Nobody is building cheap rockets. He can learn to build rockets. So he builds them.

Now run Twitter through the same machine. Civilization needs a place where people can speak freely. The platform that matters most isn't one he trusts. He can afford to buy it. So he buys it.

In April 2022, hours after making his offer, he said almost exactly that on the [TED stage](https://www.ted.com/talks/90833). The deal was "not a way to make money." His "strong intuitive sense" was that "having a public platform that is maximally trusted and broadly inclusive is extremely important to the future of civilization."

That's first-principles reasoning. We think there's also a layer underneath. Twitter was where Musk had fought his critics for a decade. It was where "funding secured" had cost him his chairmanship. A Five's deepest instinct is to control the inputs, and buying Twitter meant owning the room where people talked about him.

Then he tried to back out. Twitter sued in Delaware to hold him to the deal, and he closed it in late October 2022. Within days he cut about half the staff. On November 16, he emailed the rest. Twitter 2.0 would need to be ["extremely hardcore,"](https://fortune.com/2022/11/16/elon-musk-email-twitter-extremely-hardcore-long-hours-high-intensity/) with long hours at high intensity, and anyone who didn't opt in by the next evening would get three months' severance. Hundreds more left.

A year later, the platform he bought hosted the post he would later call his worst. On November 15, 2023, a user posted a claim that Jewish communities push hatred against white people. Musk replied: "You have said the actual truth." That's in his own words, on the record. White House spokesman Andrew Bates called it an ["abhorrent promotion of antisemitic and racist hate."](https://fortune.com/2023/11/17/elon-musk-biden-white-house-anti-semitism-tesla/) IBM, Apple, Disney and other major advertisers pulled back from X.

Two weeks later at DealBook, Musk apologized and cursed in the same interview. He called the reply foolish: ["Of the 30,000 it might be literally the worst and dumbest post I've ever done."](https://www.rev.com/transcripts/dealbook-summit-2023-elon-musk-interview-transcript) Then he turned to the advertisers. If they were trying to blackmail him with money, he said, "go fuck yourself."

Look at what he did with the two halves. The post was a bad output, so he ranked it: worst and dumbest. The advertisers leaving was people telling him what it cost, and he treated that as an attack on the system. The pattern doesn't excuse the post. It explains why his apologies so often arrive with a counterpunch attached.

## When every escape route becomes another company

Pressure does something odd to Musk: it makes his life wider. In March 2023, five months into the Twitter chaos, with advertisers gone and the company losing money, he quietly incorporated another company: xAI. That's the move. Every new arena brings fresh obligations, but it also hands him a fresh problem he can model.

The logic reads almost like an engineering note:

<p class="inner-thought">Too many dependencies. Own the stack.</p>

It looks like limitless energy. Our read is that stopping would mean facing the demands with no technical task standing between him and them.

The twist is that the expansion isn't random. By 2026, rockets, satellites, social data, AI models, chips, robotaxis and humanoid robots had been pulled into one connected bet. Count the companies and he looks scattered. Count the ideas and he looks relentlessly focused.

He answers too many demands by making himself more indispensable.

## Elon Musk and politics: from the stage to the checkbook

Politics is where the pattern got most expensive.

In 2024, Musk went all in. He appeared onstage at Trump rallies and spent [more than $290 million](https://ktvz.com/politics/cnn-us-politics/2025/02/01/elon-musk-spent-more-than-290-million-on-the-2024-election-year-end-fec-filings-show/) on the election, according to year-end federal filings. After the win, he ran DOGE, the cost-cutting project that treated the federal government like a production line with too many parts.

On January 20, 2025, at an inauguration-day rally in Washington's Capital One Arena, Musk put his hand on his chest and swung his right arm out, palm down, twice. "My heart goes out to you," he told the crowd. It's on video.

Ruth Ben-Ghiat, a historian of fascism, [wrote](https://www.salon.com/2025/01/21/it-was-a-nazi-salute-historian-dismisses-claim-that-musks-raised-arm-was-mere-awkward-gesture): "It was a Nazi salute and a very belligerent one too." The Anti-Defamation League called it ["an awkward gesture in a moment of enthusiasm"](https://www.aljazeera.com/news/2025/1/22/adl-faces-backlash-for-defending-elon-musks-raised-arm-gesture) and not a Nazi salute. Musk's reply: "Frankly, they need better dirty tricks. The 'everyone is Hitler' attack is sooo tired."

Notice the shape of that reply. He didn't explain what he meant. He filed the accusation under bad-faith inputs and moved on, the same move he made with the BBC reporter. That explains his response. It doesn't settle the argument about the gesture.

The partnership broke fast. In May 2025, Musk said of his political spending, "I think I've done enough," and he left DOGE at the end of that month. In early June he called Trump's spending bill a "disgusting abomination," then posted, and later deleted, a claim tying Trump to the Epstein files. On June 11 he wrote: ["I regret some of my posts about President @realDonaldTrump last week. They went too far."](https://www.semafor.com/article/06/11/2025/musk-says-he-regrets-some-of-his-posts-about-trump)

That's the most person-to-person apology in his public record, and it complicates our read. He can repair a relationship when he decides it matters. It also went to the head of the government that awards SpaceX's largest contracts.

A few weeks later, in July 2025, he announced a third party, the America Party, then quietly shelved it.

In July 2026 he told *The Economist*: ["I got a little too involved in politics, got carried away, frankly."](https://elonmuskarchive.org/video/economist-elon-musk-2026-07-23) In the same interview, pressed on the human cost of DOGE's cuts, he disputed the premise and called the backlash "a lot of complaining." He could admit he'd misallocated his time. Sitting with someone else's account of harm was harder.

### The 2026 midterms: money instead of microphones

Six days after the "carried away" interview, [Axios reported](https://axios.com/2026/07/29/musk-pac-midterm-election) that Musk was planning a major push for Republicans in the midterms. By mid-October he was the biggest known donor of the cycle, including [$158 million to the Senate Leadership Fund](https://www.pbs.org/newshour/politics/elon-musk-quietly-becomes-biggest-known-donor-in-midterm-contests-as-he-tries-to-save-republicans). By election day, [NPR put his total at more than $445 million](https://www.npr.org/2026/11/04/nx-s1-6029650/elon-musk-midterms-democrats-texas-congress).

On paper, that looks like a man who ignored his own lesson. But the 2026 Musk wasn't the 2024 Musk. The [AP noted](https://www.pbs.org/newshour/politics/elon-musk-quietly-becomes-biggest-known-donor-in-midterm-contests-as-he-tries-to-save-republicans) he "generally kept a lower profile than in 2024, when he appeared on stage next to Trump at rallies." No rallies, no jumping onstage. Just money.

We read part of that as defense. House Democrats had been [demanding investigations](https://ross.house.gov/2025/2/democrats-demand-investigation-into-musk-s-alleged-conflicts-over-billions-in-fed-contracts) of his DOGE role and his companies' federal contracts since February 2025, and a Democratic majority would have the subpoena power to run them. ## Elon Musk in 2026: what he revises and what he defends

**June: fold the ventures together.** SpaceX's [public offering](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1181412/000162828026036936/spaceexplorationtechnologi.htm) presented the newly acquired xAI as a core pillar, with the money funding launch, satellites and compute. Line them up and the boundary shows. When a benchmark says he's behind, he updates the ranking. When an org chart fails, he rebuilds it. When a court rules against him, he argues the procedure.

When a system fails, Musk reaches for a rebuild. When a person describes the cost, he argues the inputs.

## The difference between showing up and taking over

Musk's best leadership moments put his body next to the consequence. After the third Falcon 1 failed in 2008, he stood in front of his employees and promised to keep going. The fourth rocket reached orbit. During the Model 3 crisis, he slept at the factory. During the Twitter takeover, he slept at headquarters. The demands were brutal, but the man making them was in the building.

Taking over looks different. It's an early-morning email with a next-day deadline. It's firing engineers who criticized him in public, or a late-night post that thousands of people have to clean up. Speed goes up. The distance between his decision and the people living with it gets longer.

Twitter had both in the same month. The man sleeping at HQ also sent the "hardcore" ultimatum.

His growth edge is the one thing money can't shorten: the walk from his decision to the people who live with it.
