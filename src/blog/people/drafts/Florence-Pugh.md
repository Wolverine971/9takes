To play Dani, a grief-stricken student whose family has just been killed in Ari Aster's horror film _Midsommar_ (2019), the British actor Florence Pugh imagined her own family dying ([AP via Gulf News, July 13, 2019](https://gulfnews.com/entertainment/hollywood/florence-pugh-brings-the-chills-in-horror-film-midsommar-1.64917847)). By the middle of the shoot, "I actually needed to imagine the coffins." By the end, "I actually was going to my whole family's funeral." ([Variety, November 11, 2025](https://variety.com/2025/film/news/florence-pugh-midsommar-abuse-depression-six-months-1236574037/)) In December 2019 she summed up the method: "unfortunately, I can't cry on cue. So, I had to really just destroy myself for an entire three months, but I was happy with the work." ([Deadline, December 5, 2019](https://deadline.com/2019/12/florence-pugh-little-women-black-widow-midsommar-fighting-with-my-family-interview-1202799412/))

She had arranged to walk almost straight into [Greta Gerwig](/personality-analysis/greta-gerwig)'s _Little Women_: Deadline reported that she got Aster and Gerwig to work out a schedule so she could go from one film to the next. "Coming off Midsommar, Amy March in Little Women was the best therapy for me," she told Deadline. She got to "prance around in petticoats" and flirt on camera with [Timothée Chalamet](/personality-analysis/timothee-chalamet). Being on that set, "playing and cuddling everyone," was "the perfect way to balance who I was two weeks previous." ([TheWrap, December 25, 2019](https://www.thewrap.com/florence-pugh-little-women-midsommar-paige-wwe-fighting-with-family/))

Six years later, on _The Louis Theroux Podcast_, she told the same story with a different ending. _Little Women_ "was such a fun experience and obviously a completely different tone from 'Midsommar,' so I think shelved all of that. And then when I got home for Christmas, I was so depressed." Looking back, she said, "I think ['Midsommar'] made me sad for like six months after and I didn't know why I was depressed." (Variety, November 11, 2025)

"There is something about her that can't be knocked down or overtaken," Gerwig said in 2019 (TheWrap). Yet that first Christmas after _Midsommar_, by her own later account, the woman who couldn't be knocked down was depressed over something she had not dealt with.

Pugh was born on January 3, 1996, in Oxford, England ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Florence_Pugh)). She broke through in _Lady Macbeth_ (2016), earned Academy Award and BAFTA nominations for _Little Women_, and has played Marvel's Yelena Belova since _Black Widow_ (2021). She joined Zoe Kazan's _East of Eden_ as an executive producer in 2020 and plays its Cathy Ames ([The Hollywood Reporter, October 3, 2026](https://www.hollywoodreporter.com/tv/tv-features/florence-pugh-east-of-eden-zoe-kazan-boosted-confidence-1236721680/)); Netflix released the series in October 2026. Nearly everything public about her comes from press tours and cover stories, where candor and warmth are part of the brand.

## What is Florence Pugh's personality type?

Florence Pugh most likely fits Enneagram Type 7, the Enthusiast, at medium confidence. Her own accounts keep returning to one habit: meeting pain with motion and fun, a lighter set, a dance, the next job, and counting the cost afterward. Collaborators' descriptions make Type 8 the strongest alternative, and her caretaking keeps Type 2 close.

[Type 7](/enneagram-corner/enneagram-type-7) is organized around keeping life open, stimulating and free of pain and limits. Boldness can't settle her type; a bold actress can look like a Seven, an [Eight](/enneagram-corner/enneagram-type-8) or a Three. The [Three](/enneagram-corner/enneagram-type-3), built on a polished image, fits her drive but not her relationship to polish: "Anytime an opportunity of me being perfect onscreen comes up, I panic," she told [Vogue in 2020](https://www.vogue.com/article/florence-pugh-cover-february-2020). Two questions do more work: what does she do with her own pain, and what is her pushback defending? An Eight guards vulnerability so as not to be controlled or seen as weak. A Seven moves away from pain to keep things light and moving, and resists anything that boxes her in. A [Two](/enneagram-corner/enneagram-type-2) sets her own needs aside to stay close to the people she cares for.

This is a reading of public interviews, not access to her private life, and the evidence against it gets its own sections below.

## "That annoying kid on every stage"

Her mother, Deborah, is a dancer, and her father, Clinton Pugh, a restaurateur. She has an older brother, the actor and musician Toby Sebastian, an older sister, the actor Arabella Gibbins, and a younger sister, Rafaela. As a small child she had tracheomalacia, a weak windpipe, and was in and out of hospital; the family moved to southern Spain when she was about three and came back to Oxford when she was about six.

She refuses to make the illness a story about her. "I never want this to be a sob story," she told Vogue in 2023, "because it's never been a story in my life." ([Vogue, January 12, 2023](https://www.vogue.com/article/florence-pugh-winter-cover-2023-interview)) This profile takes her at her word.

A school nativity play gave her a feeling she still recognizes. "It was the first time I knew the power of being on stage. I remember thinking, 'Oh God, they're waiting for me, they're listening to everything I say and I have complete control.' I still feel that now," she told [The Guardian in 2018](https://www.theguardian.com/fashion/tvandradioblog/2018/oct/06/florence-pugh-lady-macbeth-king-lear-le-carre). At 18 she called herself "that annoying kid on every stage at school, in every talent contest." ([The Observer, December 28, 2014](https://www.theguardian.com/culture/2014/dec/28/rising-stars-2015-actor-florence-pugh))

"Complete control" sounds as much like an Eight, or a Three holding an audience, as a Seven chasing the thrill of the live moment. The childhood record shows a performer, not yet a motive.

## The tape she almost didn't send

At 17 she heard about an open audition for _The Falling_ and nearly let it pass. Her brother's career had taught her caution. "My brother was in the industry, and I knew that's not how you're going to get a part, handing in a three-minute tape." Her mother talked her into it: "We know you aren't going to get it, but this is something you want to do with your life, so it's kind of a free go." ([W, July 12, 2017](https://www.wmagazine.com/story/florence-pugh-lady-macbeth-movie)) She got the part.

Her earliest interview on record, at 18, struck two notes she would keep striking: an appetite for risk and a doubt that she had earned anything. "I do like a bit of danger," she told The Observer. "Guns, cars, running, bullets. I'm up for it." And of her break: "It's a bit of a fluke." The word was still in her accounts in [2017](https://www.bbc.co.uk/news/entertainment-arts-37274640) and 2022.

At 19 she was cast as the lead in _Studio City_, a Fox pilot. While waiting to hear whether it would go to series, she was eager: "I'm more than happy to move to LA for my career." ([Oxford Times, April 23, 2015](https://www.oxfordmail.co.uk/leisure/focus/12909725.Florence_Pugh_goes_straight_from_A_levels_to_LA/)) Fox passed. Her first account of what had gone wrong came in 2018: the producers had wanted "to change me," and "if you go out not knowing what it is that you're representing, and you are just a canvas, they will make you into the thing they need you to be." Weeks after the pilot died she auditioned for _Lady Macbeth_. "I swore I would never go back [to LA] until I knew what it was I was representing." ([The Guardian, October 6, 2018](https://www.theguardian.com/fashion/tvandradioblog/2018/oct/06/florence-pugh-lady-macbeth-king-lear-le-carre)) In 2022 she told The Telegraph, as relayed by Variety, that the people behind the pilot tried to change "my weight, my look, the shape of my face, the shape of my eyebrows," and that "it's far too easy for people in this industry to push you left and right." ([Variety, November 1, 2022](https://variety.com/2022/film/news/florence-pugh-body-shamed-studio-execs-change-face-1235419750/))

She never had to refuse anything; the show simply died. The defiant version came afterward, first in 2018 and in more detail in 2022, while her one account from the moment was eager to go. But the value at its center was in her vocabulary by 21. "I don't like being told how to do things," she told Grazia in 2017, describing scenes where the men around her _Lady Macbeth_ character confine her: "it makes Katherine angry, and it made me angry." ([Grazia, April 27, 2017](https://graziadaily.co.uk/celebrity/news/florence-pugh-lady-macbeth-interview/)) That is shared ground: Sevens resist being boxed in, and Eights resist being controlled.

## The thrill first, the cost later

At the time, _Midsommar_ was a high. "To get it perfect, I knew, would be so exhilarating and the payoff would be a fantastic feeling — which it was," she told the Associated Press in July 2019. The cost was not hidden: that December she told Deadline she had been "scared" and had to "destroy myself." But in that telling the bill was paid, and _Little Women_ was the cure. In March 2023 she said she "most definitely abused my own self in order to get that performance" ([Variety, March 30, 2023](https://variety.com/2023/film/news/florence-pugh-midsommar-abuse-1235569340/)). In November 2025 came the six months of depression, and a rule: "But I don't do that anymore. It really fucked me up." (Variety, November 11, 2025) The depression itself arrived within months, that first Christmas, once the fun set had ended. What took six years was her public account of it, and the link she drew between the fun and the shelving.

The Valentino dress shows the same order in her telling, over a shorter span. On July 8, 2022, at Valentino's couture show in Rome, she wore a sheer pink gown ([Harper's Bazaar, August 16, 2022](https://www.harpersbazaar.com/culture/film-tv/a40772062/florence-pugh-dont-worry-darling-interview/)), and online comments went after her body and breasts. Her Instagram reply led with nerve, "not a wink of me was nervous," named the harm in political terms, "how easy it is for men to totally destroy a woman's body, publicly, proudly," and ended on a punchline: "Why are you so scared of breasts?" ([TODAY, July 11, 2022](https://www.today.com/style/style/florence-pugh-see-through-sheer-gown-rcna37548))

About a year later the fear entered the account: "I think the scariest thing for me are the instances where people have been upset that I've shown 'too much' of myself" ([ELLE UK, August 29, 2023](https://www.elle.com/uk/life-and-culture/culture/a44863369/florence-pugh-interview-elle-style-awards/)). In September 2024, when a British Vogue interviewer raised the online commentary about her body, she named the hurt plainly: "It's really painful to read people being nasty about my confidence or nasty about my weight. It never feels good." ([British Vogue, September 18, 2024](https://www.vogue.co.uk/article/florence-pugh-british-vogue-interview))

People process hurt over time, and a reflective cover interview invites admissions. None of this proves she was hiding pain in 2022. What it shows is the order in which she tells her own stories. In both episodes the thrill or the comeback led her telling and the hurt followed: about a year later for the dress, six years later for _Midsommar_.

## "The last person that I look after"

The Theroux episode contains her most direct account of the habit. Discussing a past relationship she has not named, one she described as "causing you pain," she said: "I think when you're such a bubbly person and you fill in a lot of energy for other people, ... I'm definitely the last person that I look after ... I needed to be good for everybody else, I needed to be upbeat for everybody else and my issues, my worries, my pains weren't worthy of being spoken about ... because it would bring people down." She called it "that people pleaser thing where you just try and keep things afloat without anybody else knowing that you're paddling for dear life." ([The Louis Theroux Podcast, November 11, 2025, about 48 to 50 minutes in](https://podcasts.apple.com/us/podcast/florence-pugh-discusses-intimacy-coordinators-jumping/id1725833532?i=1000736183127))

Print write-ups tidied this passage and some dropped "upbeat"; the wording here is checked against the episode audio.

The reason she gives for hiding pain concerns the room. Her pains "would bring people down." She says nothing here about looking weak. The method, staying "upbeat," is the Seven's; the reason, other people's comfort, is shared with the Two.

Her 2024 health disclosure fits only halfway. On the _SHE MD_ podcast she said she had assumed her symptoms were part of being a woman before learning she had polycystic ovary syndrome and endometriosis; then she spoke publicly because the conversation was not loud enough ([SHE MD, November 19, 2024](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=KLKLJQSjcOA)). The first half is the "last person" habit. The second is not.

When no relationship is at stake, the move appears without concealment. In March 2020, locked down in Los Angeles, she told her Instagram followers she had been feeling a bit "glum." Then: "My dad called me yesterday and said, 'Why aren't you dancing and why aren't you cooking?' ... So this morning I woke up and I danced and I felt better and now I'm going to cook." Her advice: "we have to do things that keep us happy, keep us bubbly and keep moving basically." ([IBTimes, March 31, 2020](https://www.ibtimes.com/we-have-little-women-star-florence-pughs-father-thank-her-cooking-tutorials-2950336))

She described her teenage years the same way. "So many of the things that I did were quick fixes," she told Vogue in 2023. "Things were so wrong in my head and things were so wrong with school life and things were so dark and scary. And so I would try and do quick things like, Oh, well, I can change my nails. Oh, I can buy an eyelash kit."

Alone, none of these would carry much weight: lockdown cooking was everywhere, and teenage quick fixes are ordinary. Together, from her teens through 2020, with the _Midsommar_ sequence as the anchor, they suggest a pattern: distress met with movement, pleasure and a lighter register, with the reckoning afterward. That is the Seven's signature, the habit of reaching for [the next thing](/enneagram-corner/why-the-next-thing-wont-fix-it-type-7) to outrun the hard one. It is the strongest strand in her record, and it rests mostly on her own retrospective accounts.

## The case for Type 8

The Eight reading is serious, and it starts with the people who have worked beside her. Cate Shortland, who directed her in _Black Widow_: "She is bloody scary. Steely. Absolutely will not back down. She has a healthy amount of anger in her as a person, at the injustices she sees around her." (Vogue, 2020) [Scarlett Johansson](/personality-analysis/scarlett-johansson) in 2022: "She's unapologetically herself." (Harper's Bazaar) Denis Villeneuve in 2026: "There is something unapologetic about the way she inspires respect." ([Vanity Fair, September 25, 2026](https://www.vanityfair.com/story/cover-story-florence-pugh)) Pugh agrees: "I'm already a bit of a big mouth. I already love telling people what's what." (TheWrap, 2019)

She forces physical commitments past other people's caution. Cutting her own hair on camera in _A Good Person_ (2023) was her idea, drawn, Vogue reported, from her own distress as a teenager; Zach Braff, its writer-director and her former partner, recalled that "she just kept saying, 'You'll figure it out, you'll figure it out.'" ([Collider, March 24, 2023](https://collider.com/zach-braff-a-good-person-interview/)) When Marvel balked at letting her do the Merdeka 118 skyscraper jump in _Thunderbolts*_ (2025) over "a crazy insurance ordeal," she told Fandango, she kept emailing Marvel Studios president Kevin Feige: "I had to keep on pushing." ([Variety, April 14, 2025](https://variety.com/2025/film/news/florence-pugh-marvel-jumped-off-second-tallest-building-thunderbolts-1236369280/)) She tells it as a lark, not a war.

She fights in public for people she loves. In April 2020, a birthday post for her then-partner Braff, 21 years older than her, drew so much abuse that she turned off comments for the first time. Her video reply told the abusers to "please unfollow me because the abuse that you throw at him is abuse that you throw at me, and I don't want those followers." ([Nylon, April 8, 2020](https://www.nylon.com/florence-pugh-zach-braff-birthday))

She accepts real costs for causes. At the Together for Palestine benefit in London in September 2025 she said, "Silence in the face of such suffering is not neutrality, it is complicity" ([The Hollywood Reporter, September 25, 2026](https://www.hollywoodreporter.com/news/general-news/florence-pugh-palestine-support-hollywood-sympathy-1236710258/)). She told Vanity Fair in 2026, "I was never scared of it," and estimated she had lost hundreds of thousands of followers. In late September 2026, after a former Cornell University student filed a civil lawsuit alleging a 2024 sexual assault, Pugh posted about the case. As of September 30, 2026, Variety reported, the case was pending and some of the accused denied wrongdoing. When Instagram put a content warning on her post, she posted again, and on _The View_ she said: "Rape is something that lives. The fear of it lives in our bodies, and when stories like this come out and it's really not picked up in the way that you hope it's picked up, it makes me feel scared, it makes me feel anxious, it made me feel so much rage." ([Variety, September 30, 2026](https://variety.com/2026/tv/news/florence-pugh-cornell-7-instagram-posts-censored-sexual-assault-1236893808/))

She wants control of her own work and its exposure. She joined _East of Eden_ as an executive producer in 2020 and says "I don't like messy sets." (Vanity Fair, 2026) Asked in 2023 why she shows her cellulite, she gave the answer she once gave her grandfather: "Well, I'd much rather do it than they do it, and then I feel ashamed." ([Vanity Fair, February 15, 2023](https://www.vanityfair.com/hollywood/2023/02/florence-pugh-hollywood-issue-2023))

On this reading, the line about being the last person she looks after describes one painful relationship, not a type. Her softness on sets, which comes up next, is a strategy forced on women in her industry, and she says as much herself.

## Why the Seven reading still leads

The two questions from the start don't point the same way. The second favors the Eight: her pushback defends her autonomy and the people and causes she loves, which is Eight ground at least as much as Seven ground.

The first question tips the call once two moves are pulled apart. Hiding pain from other people because it "would bring people down" separates her from the Eight but not from the Two. What she does with her own pain, shelving _Midsommar_ in a fun set, dancing off a glum lockdown, reaching for quick fixes, is the Seven evidence, and it does not depend on concealment. Some evidence cuts the other way. Showing her cellulite before critics can shame her fits an Eight controlling exposure as well as a Seven heading off a bad feeling, and in 2026 she said, "I don't find it hard to talk about what doesn't sit correctly in my body" ([Vanity Fair, September 30, 2026](https://www.vanityfair.com/story/florence-pugh-cornell-seven-rape)).

Two cautions apply. The switch to _Little Women_ was booked in advance, so the Seven evidence is her account of what the fun did ("shelved all of that"), not the move. And the hook is the hardest fact for this reading: three months of imagining her family's funerals is not pain avoidance. A Seven reading absorbs it only partly. She framed the method as a test with an "exhilarating" payoff, and the pain-seeking stayed on set while, off it, she reached for the lighter job. This profile counts what she does on camera as craft and what she does once the camera stops as evidence about her, a judgment that shields the Seven reading from its hardest counterexample.

Her best-known story about pushing back on set weighs against the Eight without pointing to the Seven. Talking to Theroux, she said that early in her career "I found myself arguing a lot" with directors, "something that I still do in a softer way now." For women, she said, it can be "a bit stickier," in case they are called "a nightmare." After about six takes of a crying scene the director wanted another. "And when I'm in that state, I'm a bit mushy, so I'm like, 'Okay, we'll do it again.'" Her co-star intervened: "Don't do that, man. Don't put her through that." The director agreed at once; the same words from her, she said, "wouldn't have been received well." ([Pearl & Dean, November 11, 2025](https://www.pearlanddean.com/florence-pugh-reveals-male-co-star-spoke-up-when-director-demanded-more-takes-of-difficult-scene/)) She also said, "I've always been able to make sure that I'm heard" ([official clip](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=QdWZdBSRXZw)). Going along when depleted is accommodation, which fits the Two as well as the Seven.

What she chases is where the Seven pulls ahead of both rivals. "Part of the reason we all do this is because we run away with the circus," she said in 2022. "I think that one of the pulls for me is that I get to see places, see people, befriend people, fall in love with people, and then move on and do it again." (Harper's Bazaar) Those are beginnings, not bonds to keep or battles to win.

The Eight ranks ahead of the Two because its case rests on independent witnesses, four directors and co-stars from 2019 to 2026, and on public stands that cost her; the Two case rests mostly on her own words. Shortland's "will not back down" and her public anger over Gaza and the Cornell case fit the Eight better, even with the fear she named beside the rage. A Seven with a fierce protective streak, or an Eight who learned to soften on sets: the public record can't fully separate them.

One episode readers often raise settles nothing. _Don't Worry Darling_ (2022), a thriller with Pugh in the lead, came wrapped in tabloid stories, including a September 2022 Vulture report of an on-set screaming match, which a statement signed by about 40 crew members denied. Reports of tension with the director, Olivia Wilde, rested largely on anonymous sources, and in 2026 Wilde said, "I've never had a screaming match on my set." ([Variety, June 24, 2026](https://variety.com/2026/film/news/olivia-wilde-florence-pugh-screaming-match-dont-worry-darling-1236789286/)) Before the premiere she criticized the film's marketing for reducing it to its sex scenes (Harper's Bazaar, 2022); since then she has declined to discuss the production. In 2024 she gave her reason: "I always think it's unfair to take the space away from the movie that I'm talking about. So I'm going to politely move away from that." (British Vogue) She deflected again in her September 2026 Vanity Fair cover story. She has given a professional reason, so this profile counts the silence for no type.

## Could she be a Type 2?

The [Type 2](/enneagram-corner/enneagram-type-2) reading starts with her own words. Being "the last person that I look after" is a Helper's phrase. So is this, from 2024: "I am someone that loves falling in love. I love looking after people. I love caring for people. I love the feeling of someone being there." (British Vogue) The actor Ashley Park told Vanity Fair: "She's a great chef, she's a great host, she's a great designer, she's a great planner." Hosting runs in her family's trade, though (Vogue described "a family of hosts" in 2020), so Park's praise says less about motive than Pugh's own words do.

Romance can't settle this one. Loving to fall in love is a Seven's thrill and a romantic Two's devotion alike, and in 2024 she called it a pattern she was trying to break: "Falling is the most amazing feeling, but unfortunately if that's the only thing that you know in a relationship, then that's the thing that you chase. That's not gonna last." (British Vogue) The non-romantic evidence does more work. A Two's pattern runs toward being needed. Hers, by her own account, ran toward beginnings: places and people she gets to leave and "do it again."

She rejects approval as her engine ("I don't think it's confidence in hoping people like me. I think it's just, like, I don't want to be anyone else"), though she also keeps friends from age 13 close because "I don't want to be someone that no one else likes outside of this industry." The Two reading is not ruled out. The observation that would separate the two best, how she reacts when her care goes unreturned, is missing from the public record.

## What the Seven reading can't explain

She moves toward pain in her work. "I've never found it a challenge to be acting in pain," she said in 2024. "I sometimes prefer it." (British Vogue) Of her roles: "the majority of my roles that I've taken on I've been terrified by" ([Q with Tom Power, October 17, 2024](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=wBKwNBGUuwM)).

Put that beside the record's anxious notes and a fourth reading appears. A [counterphobic Six](/enneagram-corner/enneagram-type-6) is a security-seeking type who handles fear by charging at it, the classic lookalike for an Eight. Pugh kept calling her break a fluke, nearly didn't send the tape that started her career, takes social media off her phone "from the moment I can feel that anxiety" (Vanity Fair, 2023), and after the Merdeka 118 jump said, "I can basically pretend to myself not to listen to my instincts" ([Who What Wear, May 1, 2025](https://www.whowhatwear.com/fashion/cover-features/florence-pugh)). In 2024 she said what she wanted was "safety, family, a home and security." (British Vogue) The Seven reading keeps its lead, narrowly, because she usually describes the rush of a frightening job ("so exhilarating") rather than the relief of surviving it. That distinction is thin, and it is where this reading is most likely to be wrong.

In interviews from 2024 to 2026 she turned toward home and privacy. Of her relationship with the actor Finn Cole, she told Vanity Fair in 2026, "I feel solid and safe and sturdy." That could be maturity, a good relationship, or the security motive a Six reading would predict.

And the record has limits. "I walked away from our process not understanding any better than I did beforehand how she works," Kazan told Vanity Fair. The public sees what she shows on press tours, not how she behaves at home.

## Learning to unpack

By her account, _We Live in Time_ (2024), a romantic drama in which she and Andrew Garfield play a couple facing her character's cancer, changed something. Finishing it left "this emptiness," she said, and "I definitely wanted to wake up to my own decisions and I didn't want to push things anymore" ([Reign with Josh Smith, January 2, 2025](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=7vyExn--DtA)). In April 2025: "I've worked back-to-back since I started, and I've missed so much," and, "I don't want to have things just happen to me any more." ([Harper's Bazaar UK, April 14, 2025](https://www.harpersbazaar.com/uk/culture/culture-news/a64455983/florence-pugh-cover-interview-thunderbolts/)) Shortly before May 2025, when "something fudged in the schedule," she took a three-week holiday, her first of that length, while admitting of rest, "I always say this, but it never happens." (Who What Wear)

Vanity Fair reported in 2026 that she planned to spend a long break at home rather than traveling. "I love packing," she said. "I hate unpacking." (Vanity Fair) It was a throwaway, and the Seven pattern in miniature: the pleasure is in the leaving. Whether the turn toward home is a Seven learning to stay or the security a Six wants, the record can't yet say. The work she described in 2026 is the other half, staying put long enough to unpack.
