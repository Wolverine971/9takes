To play Dani in Ari Aster's horror film _Midsommar_ (2019), a grief-stricken student whose family has just been killed ([AP via Gulf News, July 13, 2019](https://gulfnews.com/entertainment/hollywood/florence-pugh-brings-the-chills-in-horror-film-midsommar-1.64917847)), the British actor Florence Pugh imagined her own family dying ([Variety, November 11, 2025](https://variety.com/2025/film/news/florence-pugh-midsommar-abuse-depression-six-months-1236574037/)). By the middle of the shoot, "I actually needed to imagine the coffins." By the end, "I actually was going to my whole family's funeral."

Her reason, as she put it in December 2019: "unfortunately, I can't cry on cue. So, I had to really just destroy myself for an entire three months, but I was happy with the work." ([Deadline, December 5, 2019](https://deadline.com/2019/12/florence-pugh-little-women-black-widow-midsommar-fighting-with-my-family-interview-1202799412/))

She had arranged to walk almost straight into [Greta Gerwig](/personality-analysis/greta-gerwig)'s _Little Women_: Deadline reported that she got Aster and Gerwig to work out a schedule so she could go from one film to the next. "Coming off Midsommar, Amy March in Little Women was the best therapy for me," she told Deadline. Being on that set, "playing and cuddling everyone," was "the perfect way to balance who I was two weeks previous." ([TheWrap, December 25, 2019](https://www.thewrap.com/florence-pugh-little-women-midsommar-paige-wwe-fighting-with-family/))

Six years later, on _The Louis Theroux Podcast_, she told the same story with a different ending. _Little Women_ "was such a fun experience ... and obviously a completely different tone from Midsommar," so, as she tells it, she shelved all of that.

> "And then when I got home for Christmas I was so depressed. I was like, oh, I think that's from Midsommar and I didn't deal with it." ([The Louis Theroux Podcast, November 11, 2025](https://podcasts.apple.com/us/podcast/florence-pugh-discusses-intimacy-coordinators-jumping/id1725833532?i=1000736183127))

Looking back, she said, "I think it made me sad for like six months after that and I didn't know why I was depressed."

"There is something about her that can't be knocked down or overtaken," Gerwig said in 2019 (TheWrap).

That Christmas, by her own later account, the woman who couldn't be knocked down was depressed over something she hadn't dealt with.

Pugh was born on January 3, 1996, in Oxford, England ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Florence_Pugh)). She broke through in _Lady Macbeth_ (2016), earned Academy Award and BAFTA nominations for _Little Women_, and has played Marvel's Yelena Belova since _Black Widow_ (2021). She plays Cathy Ames in Zoe Kazan's _East of Eden_, which she has executive produced since 2020; Netflix released it on October 1, 2026 ([The Hollywood Reporter, October 3, 2026](https://www.hollywoodreporter.com/tv/tv-features/florence-pugh-east-of-eden-zoe-kazan-boosted-confidence-1236721680/)). Two of her franchises are scheduled for the same day, December 18, 2026: [_Avengers: Doomsday_](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Avengers:_Doomsday), with her as Yelena, and [_Dune: Part Three_](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dune:_Part_Three), with her as Princess Irulan alongside [Timothée Chalamet](/personality-analysis/timothee-chalamet), [Zendaya](/personality-analysis/zendaya) and [Robert Pattinson](/personality-analysis/robert-pattinson).

## What is Florence Pugh's personality type?

Florence Pugh's personality type is Enneagram Type 7, known as the Enthusiast. Enthusiasts are in the head triad, and their core emotion is fear. Her own stories keep showing the Seven's answer to it: turn the fear into a thrill, stay in motion, and count the cost later.

Head types meet the world by anticipating what comes next, as opposed to leading with gut instinct or emotion, and fear is the feeling Pugh keeps naming when she talks about work. "The majority of my roles that I've taken on I've been terrified by," she said in 2024 ([Q with Tom Power, October 17, 2024](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=wBKwNBGUuwM)). She told Theroux that fear is what she looks for in a script:

> "There has to be a moment in the script where I'm not scared of the horror, but where I'm scared of if I'll be able to do it, so that's the thing that makes me lurch forward."

"The moment I get like pangs of nerves," she went on, "I know that I'm probably going to end up doing it." Her example was the skyscraper jump in _Thunderbolts*_ (2025).

Running at fear is also what a counterphobic Six does, a security-seeking type that handles fear by charging straight at it. What tips her to Seven is what she calls it afterward. The jump was "such an exciting stunt." _Midsommar_ was going to be "so exhilarating."

[Sevens](/enneagram-corner/enneagram-type-7) handle fear by turning toward excitement. The frightening thing becomes an adventure, the next good thing is already lined up, and there's never a quiet hour for the pain to land. The pain doesn't disappear. It shows up later, once the fun stops, which is the step Pugh described to Theroux six years after the fact.

The serious challenge comes from the [Eight](/enneagram-corner/enneagram-type-8), the Challenger. The [Two](/enneagram-corner/enneagram-type-2) and the counterphobic Six get a hearing too. The image-focused [Three](/enneagram-corner/enneagram-type-3) barely needs one. "Anytime an opportunity of me being perfect onscreen comes up, I panic," she told [Vogue in 2020](https://www.vogue.com/article/florence-pugh-cover-february-2020).

## "That annoying kid on every stage"

Her mother, Deborah, is a dancer, and her father, Clinton Pugh, a restaurateur. As a small child she had tracheomalacia, a weak windpipe, and was in and out of hospital; the family moved to southern Spain when she was about three and came back to Oxford when she was about six ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Florence_Pugh)).

She refuses to make the illness a story about her. "I never want this to be a sob story," she told Vogue in 2023, "because it's never been a story in my life." ([Vogue, January 12, 2023](https://www.vogue.com/article/florence-pugh-winter-cover-2023-interview)) This analysis takes her at her word.

A school nativity play gave her a feeling she still recognizes:

> "It was the first time I knew the power of being on stage. I remember thinking, 'Oh God, they're waiting for me, they're listening to everything I say and I have complete control.' I still feel that now." ([The Guardian, October 6, 2018](https://www.theguardian.com/fashion/tvandradioblog/2018/oct/06/florence-pugh-lady-macbeth-king-lear-le-carre))

At 18 she called herself "that annoying kid on every stage at school, in every talent contest." ([The Observer, December 28, 2014](https://www.theguardian.com/culture/2014/dec/28/rising-stars-2015-actor-florence-pugh))

The childhood record shows a performer, not yet a motive.

## The tape she almost didn't send

At 17 she heard about an open audition for _The Falling_ and nearly let it pass. Her brother's career had taught her caution. "My brother was in the industry, and I knew that's not how you're going to get a part, handing in a three-minute tape." Her mother talked her into it: "We know you aren't going to get it, but this is something you want to do with your life, so it's kind of a free go." ([W, July 12, 2017](https://www.wmagazine.com/story/florence-pugh-lady-macbeth-movie))

She got the part.

At 18 she was already calling it luck. "I just happened upon the best possible start for my career. It's a bit of a fluke," she told The Observer. By the time _Lady Macbeth_ came out she was arguing with the word: the film was "a great opportunity for me to prove that maybe the outcome of The Falling was not necessarily a fluke" ([BBC News](https://www.bbc.co.uk/news/entertainment-arts-37274640)).

In that 2014 Observer interview she was open to less cerebral films, too. "I do like a bit of danger," she said. "Guns, cars, running, bullets. I'm up for it."

A decade later she jumped off a skyscraper for Marvel.

At 19 she was cast as the lead in _Studio City_, a Fox pilot, and while she waited on the network she was eager: "I'm more than happy to move to LA for my career." ([Oxford Times, April 23, 2015](https://www.oxfordmail.co.uk/leisure/focus/12909725.Florence_Pugh_goes_straight_from_A_levels_to_LA/)) Fox passed.

By 2018 she told it differently. The producers had wanted "to change me," she told [The Guardian](https://www.theguardian.com/fashion/tvandradioblog/2018/oct/06/florence-pugh-lady-macbeth-king-lear-le-carre), and "if you go out not knowing what it is that you're representing, and you are just a canvas, they will make you into the thing they need you to be." "I swore I would never go back [to LA] until I knew what it was I was representing."

She never had to refuse anything; the show simply died. The defiant version came afterward, while her one account from the moment was eager to go. But the value at its center was in her vocabulary by 21. "I don't like being told how to do things," she told Grazia in 2017, describing scenes where the men around her _Lady Macbeth_ character confine her: "it makes Katherine angry, and it made me angry." ([Grazia, April 27, 2017](https://graziadaily.co.uk/celebrity/news/florence-pugh-lady-macbeth-interview/))

## The thrill first, the cost later

At the time, _Midsommar_ was a high. "To get it perfect, I knew, would be so exhilarating and the payoff would be a fantastic feeling — which it was," she told the Associated Press in July 2019.

The cost wasn't hidden, but in her 2019 telling the bill was paid, and _Little Women_ was the cure. That version came on the _Little Women_ press tour, where praising the set is part of the job, so what matters is what she later said she did: shelved it, then was depressed by Christmas. In March 2023 she said she "most definitely abused my own self in order to get that performance" ([Variety, March 30, 2023](https://variety.com/2023/film/news/florence-pugh-midsommar-abuse-1235569340/)). In November 2025 came the six months of depression, and a rule. She used to "basically put myself through hell," she told Theroux, "but I don't do that anymore." It "really fucked me up."

The depression itself came within months, that first Christmas, once the fun set had ended. What took six years was her public account of it, and the link she drew between the fun and the shelving.

The Valentino dress shows the same order over a shorter span. After she wore a sheer pink gown to the label's couture show in Rome on July 8, 2022 ([Harper's Bazaar, August 16, 2022](https://www.harpersbazaar.com/culture/film-tv/a40772062/florence-pugh-dont-worry-darling-interview/)), online comments went after her body. Her Instagram reply said "not a wink of me was nervous" and turned the critics' fear back on them:

> "Why are you so scared of breasts?" ([TODAY, July 11, 2022](https://www.today.com/style/style/florence-pugh-see-through-sheer-gown-rcna37548))

About a year later the fear entered the account: "I think the scariest thing for me are the instances where people have been upset that I've shown 'too much' of myself" ([ELLE UK, August 29, 2023](https://www.elle.com/uk/life-and-culture/culture/a44863369/florence-pugh-interview-elle-style-awards/)). In September 2024, when a British Vogue interviewer raised the online commentary about her body, she named the hurt plainly: "It's really painful to read people being nasty about my confidence or nasty about my weight. It never feels good." ([British Vogue, September 18, 2024](https://www.vogue.co.uk/article/florence-pugh-british-vogue-interview))

A reflective cover interview invites admissions, so none of this proves she was hiding pain in 2022. What it shows is how she met each moment: the thrill or the comeback first, the hurt named later. About a year later for the dress, six years later for the _Midsommar_ depression.

## "The last person that I look after"

The Theroux episode holds her most direct account of the habit. Talking about a past relationship she has not named, which she described as "causing you pain," she said: "I think when you're such a bubbly person and you fill in a lot of energy for other people, ... I'm definitely the last person that I look after ... I needed to be good for everybody else, I needed to be upbeat for everybody else and my issues, my worries, my pains weren't worthy of being spoken about ... because it would bring people down."

Then she named it:

> "It's that people pleaser thing where you just try and keep things afloat without anybody else knowing that you're paddling for dear life." ([The Louis Theroux Podcast, November 11, 2025](https://podcasts.apple.com/us/podcast/florence-pugh-discusses-intimacy-coordinators-jumping/id1725833532?i=1000736183127))

The wording is from the episode audio. Print write-ups shortened it, and some dropped "upbeat."

The reason she gives for hiding pain concerns the room. Her pains "would bring people down." She says nothing here about looking weak. The method, staying "upbeat," is the Seven's; the reason, other people's comfort, is shared with the Two.

Outside a relationship, she does the same thing in the open. In March 2020, locked down in Los Angeles, she told her Instagram followers she had been feeling a bit "glum." Then: "My dad called me yesterday and said, 'Why aren't you dancing and why aren't you cooking?' ... So this morning I woke up and I danced and I felt better and now I'm going to cook." Her advice: "we have to do things that keep us happy, keep us bubbly and keep moving basically." ([IBTimes, March 31, 2020](https://www.ibtimes.com/we-have-little-women-star-florence-pughs-father-thank-her-cooking-tutorials-2950336))

She described her teenage years the same way. "So many of the things that I did were quick fixes," she told Vogue in 2023. "Things were so wrong in my head and things were so wrong with school life and things were so dark and scary. And so I would try and do quick things like, Oh, well, I can change my nails. Oh, I can buy an eyelash kit."

Alone, none of these would carry much weight: lockdown cooking was everywhere, and teenage quick fixes are ordinary. Together, from her teens through 2020, with the _Midsommar_ sequence as the anchor, they make a pattern. Distress gets met with movement, pleasure and a lighter register, and the reckoning comes afterward. That is the Seven's signature, the habit of reaching for [the next thing](/enneagram-corner/why-the-next-thing-wont-fix-it-type-7) to outrun the hard one.

## Why not Type 8?

The Eight case is serious, and the people who have worked beside her make it. Cate Shortland, who directed her in _Black Widow_, told Vogue in 2020:

> "She is bloody scary. Steely. Absolutely will not back down. She has a healthy amount of anger in her as a person, at the injustices she sees around her."

[Scarlett Johansson](/personality-analysis/scarlett-johansson) in 2022: "She's unapologetically herself." (Harper's Bazaar) Denis Villeneuve in 2026: "There is something unapologetic about the way she inspires respect." ([Vanity Fair, September 25, 2026](https://www.vanityfair.com/story/cover-story-florence-pugh)) Pugh agrees: "I'm already a bit of a big mouth. I already love telling people what's what." (TheWrap, 2019)

She pushes past other people's caution. When Marvel balked at the Merdeka 118 jump over "a crazy insurance ordeal," she kept emailing studio president Kevin Feige: "I had to keep on pushing." ([Variety, April 14, 2025](https://variety.com/2025/film/news/florence-pugh-marvel-jumped-off-second-tallest-building-thunderbolts-1236369280/)) She tells it as a lark.

She fights in public for people she loves. In April 2020, a birthday post for her then-partner, the writer-director Zach Braff, 21 years older than her, drew so much abuse that she turned off comments for the first time. Her video reply told the abusers to "please unfollow me because the abuse that you throw at him is abuse that you throw at me, and I don't want those followers." ([Nylon, April 8, 2020](https://www.nylon.com/florence-pugh-zach-braff-birthday)) Five years later she told Theroux, "I'll always defend people that I love, I will always stand up for them."

She accepts real costs for causes. At the Together for Palestine benefit in London in September 2025 she said, "Silence in the face of such suffering is not neutrality, it is complicity" ([The Hollywood Reporter, September 25, 2026](https://www.hollywoodreporter.com/news/general-news/florence-pugh-palestine-support-hollywood-sympathy-1236710258/)). She told Vanity Fair in 2026, "I was never scared of it," and estimated she had lost hundreds of thousands of followers.

In late September 2026 she posted about a civil lawsuit in which a former Cornell University student alleges a 2024 sexual assault, and posted again when Instagram put a content warning on the first post. On _The View_, co-host Sara Haines noted that the case is still pending and that some of the accused have denied wrongdoing, then asked why she had spoken up:

> "Rape is something that lives. The fear of it lives in our bodies, and when stories like this come out and it's really not picked up in the way that you hope it's picked up, it makes me feel scared, it makes me feel anxious, it made me feel so much rage." ([Variety, September 30, 2026](https://variety.com/2026/tv/news/florence-pugh-cornell-7-instagram-posts-censored-sexual-assault-1236893808/))

### Where the Eight case falls short

Eights are in the gut triad, and their core emotion is anger. Pugh has plenty of anger, and it's the best evidence the Eight case has. The difference is what she does with her own pain. An Eight guards vulnerability so no one can use it. Pugh describes shelving pain in fun and motion, and hiding it so the room stays "upbeat." Neither is about looking strong.

Her clearest story from a set is about a time she didn't push back. Early in her career, she told Theroux, "I found myself arguing a lot" with directors, "something that I still do in a softer way now." For women, she said, "it's a bit stickier. You can't really be a problem because people will say that you're a nightmare."

After about six takes of a crying scene, the director wanted another. "When I'm in that state, I'm a bit mushy, so I'm like, okay, we'll do it again." Her male co-star stepped in: "Don't do that, man. Don't put her through that." The director agreed straight away. The same words from her, she said, "wouldn't have been received well." She didn't name the film or the co-star ([Pearl & Dean, November 11, 2025](https://www.pearlanddean.com/florence-pugh-reveals-male-co-star-spoke-up-when-director-demanded-more-takes-of-difficult-scene/)).

Going along when she's depleted isn't what the Eight reading predicts, even allowing for the double standard she describes. Still, what her pushback defends, her autonomy and the people and causes she loves, is Eight ground at least as much as Seven ground. If this read is wrong, the likeliest right answer is an Eight who learned to soften on sets.

Readers often bring up _Don't Worry Darling_ (2022). Reports of tension with the director, Olivia Wilde, rested largely on anonymous sources, and in 2026 Wilde said, "I've never had a screaming match on my set." ([Variety, June 24, 2026](https://variety.com/2026/film/news/olivia-wilde-florence-pugh-screaming-match-dont-worry-darling-1236789286/)) Pugh criticized its marketing for reducing it to its sex scenes (Harper's Bazaar, 2022) but has declined to discuss the production, and in 2024 gave a professional reason: "I always think it's unfair to take the space away from the movie that I'm talking about." (British Vogue) The silence counts for no type.

## Why not Type 2?

The [Type 2](/enneagram-corner/enneagram-type-2) reading starts with her own words. Being "the last person that I look after" is a Helper's phrase. So is this, from 2024: "I am someone that loves falling in love. I love looking after people. I love caring for people. I love the feeling of someone being there." (British Vogue)

Romance can't settle this one. Loving to fall in love is a Seven's thrill and a romantic Two's devotion alike, and in 2024 she described it as a pattern she was trying to break. "Falling is the most amazing feeling," she said, "but unfortunately if that's the only thing that you know in a relationship, then that's the thing that you chase. That's not gonna last." (British Vogue)

What she chases outside romance does more work. "Part of the reason we all do this is because we run away with the circus," she told Harper's Bazaar in 2022, explaining why lockdown had her itching to get back to work.

> "I think that one of the pulls for me is that I get to see places, see people, befriend people, fall in love with people, and then move on and do it again."

A Two's pattern runs toward being needed and staying close. Hers, by her own account, runs toward beginnings: places and people she gets to leave and "do it again."

The Two reading is not ruled out. The observation that would separate the two best, how she reacts when her care goes unreturned, is missing from the public record.

## Why not Type 6?

The [Six](/enneagram-corner/enneagram-type-6) is the Seven's neighbor in the head triad, and its core emotion is fear too. The difference is direction. A Six looks for safety, support and certainty. The counterphobic kind, which charges at fear, is also the classic lookalike for an Eight.

Pugh's record has real Six material. She kept calling her break a fluke and nearly didn't send the tape that started her career. She takes social media off her phone "from the moment I can feel that anxiety" (Vanity Fair, 2023). After the Merdeka 118 jump she said, "I can basically pretend to myself not to listen to my instincts" ([Who What Wear, May 1, 2025](https://www.whowhatwear.com/fashion/cover-features/florence-pugh)).

The strongest point for the Six is her own vocabulary. In 2024 she said what she wanted was "safety, family, a home and security." (British Vogue) Of her relationship with the actor Finn Cole, she told Vanity Fair in 2026, "I feel solid and safe and sturdy."

That's a real point. Look at where the first line comes from, though. In the same British Vogue interview she said that "for the first time, I'm not allowing myself to go on a roller-coaster," and that falling in love had been the thing she chased. She was choosing security on purpose, against her own pattern.

The Seven keeps the lead on how she tells a frightening job: the rush, not the relief of getting through it. And the pull she names in her work is novelty, the circus and the next place, not a safe base.

## The part that doesn't fit

The hook is the hardest fact for the Seven reading. Three months of imagining her family's funerals is not pain avoidance, and she says she likes acting in pain. "I've never found it a challenge to be acting in pain," she said in 2024, of playing a woman with cancer in _We Live in Time_. "I sometimes prefer it." (British Vogue)

The Seven reading absorbs that only partly, by drawing a line. How she prepares inside a role is craft. How she picks jobs, and what she does with the feelings after one ends, is evidence about her. The coffins were preparation. The scripts she says she chooses for the fear, the lobbying to do her own jump, and the grief she says she shelved in a fun set are her. That line is a judgment call, and it's the one place the Seven reading needs help.

And the record has limits. "I walked away from our process not understanding any better than I did beforehand how she works," Kazan told Vanity Fair. The public sees what she shows on press tours, not how she behaves at home.

## Learning to unpack

By her account, _We Live in Time_ (2024), a romantic drama in which she and [Andrew Garfield](/personality-analysis/andrew-garfield) play a couple facing her character's cancer, changed something. Finishing it left "this emptiness," she said, and "I definitely wanted to wake up to my own decisions and I didn't want to push things anymore" ([Reign with Josh Smith, January 2, 2025](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=7vyExn--DtA)). In April 2025: "I've worked back-to-back since I started, and I've missed so much," and, "I don't want to have things just happen to me any more." ([Harper's Bazaar UK, April 14, 2025](https://www.harpersbazaar.com/uk/culture/culture-news/a64455983/florence-pugh-cover-interview-thunderbolts/))

She has changed the acting, too. Asked by Theroux how real the emotions get, she said, "I'm changing that. It used to be quite horribly real."

Vanity Fair reported in 2026 that she planned to spend a long break at home rather than traveling. "I love packing," she said. "I hate unpacking." (Vanity Fair)

It was a throwaway, and it's the Seven pattern in miniature: the pleasure is in the leaving. The work she described in 2026 is the other half, staying put long enough to unpack.
