<p class="firstLetter">Five stairs put Ibai Llanos at 150 beats per minute. He climbed them anyway, praying there was no second flight, because he knew the sweat was coming. He was in his late twenties. He was also, by most measures that matter on the internet, the most watched live human being alive.</p>

Hold both of those facts at once. The man who could not manage a staircase holds the world record for the largest simultaneous live audience in internet history. Five days ago, on July 25, 2026, eighty thousand people filled La Cartuja stadium in Seville for the sixth edition of a boxing event he invented, while millions more watched from three platforms at once.

And when he finally explained how his body got to that state, he skipped every excuse available to a rich man with a chef's phone number and reached for the sentence at the top of this page. Anger at whom? He has spent twelve years being the least angry man in streaming. That is the piece of the picture that refuses to sit flat, and it is where this analysis starts.

## What is Ibai Llanos's personality type?

### Ibai Llanos is an Enneagram Type 9

The clearest Enneagram fit for Ibai Llanos is [Type 9](/enneagram-corner/enneagram-type-9). One reflex organizes everything he has ever built: steady the room. He absorbs tension, declines feuds, and builds formats where everyone belongs and nobody has to lose. The evidence spans his conflict-free rise and a boxing empire complete with referees, weight classes, and hugs.

In March 2022, a rival handed Ibai a loaded weapon. El Xokas, then clawing at the top of Spanish Twitch, confessed to running a secret alt account he used to attack other streamers and praise himself. Auronplay swung hard on stream (Infobae, March 2022): "For starters, Xokas, you're not number one. Number one is between me and Ibai." Every big creator in the scene piled on.

Ibai's whole response, in the same March 2022 Infobae roundup: "He's in a situation where he needs help. Real help. He needs therapy."

Watch what happened there. Given a free shot at a competitor, his instinct was to lower the temperature of the entire room, with just enough edge that nobody mistook it for weakness. He has repeated that move for a decade: in streamer beefs he declines to join, in dramas he waves off, in a lifelong habit of treating open conflict as a fire to be smothered rather than fed.

Nines are the Enneagram's mediators, driven by a need for steadiness and belonging so deep that their own preferences get quietly filed away. That wiring is scarce among streaming's giants for a reason. The medium rewards friction, outrage, and picking fights on camera; the algorithm feeds people who escalate. Ibai got bigger than all of them by doing the opposite.

Look at what he actually built. His flagship interview show is called _Charlando Tranquilamente_. Chatting calmly. The name is a mission statement.

His Balloon World Cup turned a viral clip of two brothers batting a balloon around a living room into an international tournament, a sport whose entire objective is keeping something fragile from touching the ground. He staged it in an arena with national jerseys and slow-motion replays, called a balloon hanging in the air with the gravity he once spent on pentakills, and crowned a world champion from Peru.

And La Velada del Año, his crown jewel, is the most revealing of all: a boxing card. The man who cannot stand a fight monetized fighting. He just rebuilt it first, with referees, medical checks, weight classes, training arcs, and embraces at the final bell. Conflict, fully domesticated. Beef converted into sport, where nobody has to stop being friends.

He has organized six editions. He has never once put himself on the card.

## Ibai Llanos's childhood in the 2008 crisis

A reflex that deep gets built early. Ibai Llanos Garatea was born in 1995 in Deusto, a working-class district of Bilbao. His first name is Basque for "river." When Spain's economy collapsed in 2008, the crisis came home. He has said it flatly: the crash "caused a difficult financial moment in my house." Évole, who spent days filming with him, put it harder: the family was left "muy tocados," badly hit, economically wounded.

Picture a thirteen-year-old in a small flat where money is the constant, unspoken weather. Some kids in that situation act out. Ibai went the other way. He disappeared into video games with his best friend Ander Cortés, uploading Call of Duty clips under the name ibai95, then narrating amateur League of Legends matches for free, matches played at bronze rank, the lowest tier the game has. Nobody asked him to. There was no money in it. It was a room he could control the temperature of, a place where the heaviness upstairs could not follow.

The lesson a kid absorbs in a house like that: don't add weight. Keep it light, keep it funny, need nothing. It is a survival skill that looks, twenty years later, exactly like a career.

## Six years of shouting for 1,040 euros a month

In August 2014, on the final day of auditions, Ibai and Ander walked into a casting call for the LVP, Spain's professional esports league, with a cheap microphone and no plan B. The tape still circulates as a relic. He got the job at 19. What followed is best read as a ledger, because the two columns refuse to move together:

<div class="scroll-table">

| Year | What he earned                    | Who was listening                              |
| ---- | --------------------------------- | ---------------------------------------------- |
| 2014 | around 100 euros a month          | a few thousand League of Legends diehards      |
| 2016 | 1,040 euros net, after moving city | hundreds of thousands per broadcast            |
| 2018 | 2,400 euros a month               | the largest esports audience in Spain          |
| 2021 | his own channel, his own terms    | 1.7 million concurrent on a single stream      |

</div>

He won the Trasgo de Oro, Spanish esports' best-caster award, year after year from 2015 to 2018, while earning less than a supermarket manager. "I've been through some rough moments," he admitted years later about that first paycheck (El Español, 2023). He moved to Barcelona for the job in 2016, away from his family, his city, his room. That is when his body sent its first invoice.

<p class="inner-thought">Why can't I walk all of a sudden? Why can't I go out into the street? I'm dying. I have to be dying, because nothing else explains this.</p>

That is nearly verbatim how he described it to Jordi Évole in 2021: "I didn't know it was anxiety or panic attacks. I thought I was dying." The most reassuring voice in Spanish esports, the one paid to make chaos feel like a party, was privately being ambushed by his own nervous system and had no word for it. He kept casting. The audience never noticed a thing. Making sure nobody notices is the exact skill he had been practicing since 2008.

## How Ibai built the biggest live audience on the internet

The escape hatch opened in February 2020: G2 Esports, one of Europe's biggest esports brands, hired him to just be Ibai, and he brought his caster friends with him. A month later Spain locked its population indoors, and a country stuck at home found out what he was for. Within days he had organized the LaLiga Santander Challenge, a FIFA tournament with one professional player from each of the twenty first-division clubs competing from their living rooms; over a million people watched him narrate footballers' amateur defending like a Champions League final, and it raised 180,000 euros against the virus (LaLiga, March 2020).

That New Year's Eve he rang in 2021 by hosting the traditional midnight bells from his room, and 552,000 concurrent viewers chose a gamer's webcam over the television networks that had owned that ritual for sixty years (El Español, 2024). By the time Spain went back outside, Ibai had become the person a whole country was used to having in the house.

In February 2021, Ibai sent a Twitter DM to Gerard Piqué asking him onto a new interview show. "He contacted me on Twitter and I said go ahead," Piqué recalled. "I was already following him, and I liked everything he did." Of course he was following him. Half the country was. The call was scheduled for an hour. It ran two and a half, and Ibai closed it by showing the Barcelona defender a highlight reel of his career mistakes. Within a year the two had founded the esports club KOI, launched at the Palau Sant Jordi.

The guest list that followed reads like a hoax: Iniesta, Ronaldinho, Ed Sheeran, Bad Bunny. When Messi left Barcelona in tears in August 2021, Sergio Agüero walked Ibai into the farewell dinner, and days later the first sit-down with Messi in Paris went to a 26-year-old streamer, ahead of every broadcaster on earth.

The scale kept compounding: the Kings League with its twelve creator presidents, the Balloon World Cup, and a Velada audience ladder that climbed from 3.3 million concurrents in 2022 past a Guinness World Record 3.8 million in 2024 to a number nobody in live internet history had touched: 9.33 million devices connected to one Twitch channel on July 26, 2025 (Streams Charts). Awards followed the same slope, three straight Esports Awards Streamer of the Year wins, then an Ondas, Spain's legacy broadcasting prize, for a man who has never worked a day in television.

Jordi Évole, Spain's most decorated interviewer, went to Ibai's house to film for days and came back sounding converted:

<div class="pull-quote">
"He struck me as a guy far better put together than people assume when they see him screaming at video games. He has charisma, he has brains, he comes from the bottom and he knows it." — Jordi Évole, El Hormiguero, 2021
</div>

Évole added the detail that explains the empire: "To make one hour of television a week, I need a team of 30 to 40 people. He does it with three cameras."

And he marveled at the politics of it, because this multimillionaire had just told him, on camera, that he was happy for half his income to go to Spanish taxes. "With the other half I have more than enough," Évole quoted him saying, calling it "the best campaign the Tax Ministry could run in its history." While other top streamers moved to Andorra, Ibai stayed, paid, and shrugged. Leaving would have meant standing apart from the country watching him. Standing apart is the one cost he has never been willing to pay.

His housing history says the same thing. When the money arrived, he did not buy a penthouse for one. He rented a mansion outside Barcelona that once belonged to the footballer Samuel Eto'o, 15,000 euros a month, pool and tennis court included, and filled it with the caster friends he had been broke with at the LVP.

When the housemates finally moved out at the end of 2021 (Crónica Global, December 2021), the house did not empty; it became the headquarters of KOI. The roommates became the payroll. And Ander Cortés, the best friend from the Deusto bedroom uploads, sat at the commentary table of La Velada VI five days ago, the way he has for every edition. The audience scaled from dozens to millions. The people in the room never changed.

## Why Ibai Llanos slept with a breathing machine

While all of that compounded, so did something else. By 2024 Ibai weighed 152 kilos and stopped breathing about 40 times every night. The prescription was a CPAP machine, a mask strapped to his face pushing air down his throat while he slept. The first weeks, he ripped it off unconscious. Then something more revealing happened: he got used to it. More than used to it. "I almost got addicted to it," he told The Wild Project. "It became my moment of relaxation. When I put on the mask, I know it's time to sleep."

Sit with that. A machine existing solely because his body was suffocating became cozy. He is so gifted at adapting to discomfort, at absorbing what should alarm him and rebranding it as fine, that he tamed his own medical emergency the way he tames everything else. The Enneagram's body types are famous for exactly this: registering distress so dimly that it files itself under tiredness, routine, one more plate. His endocrinologist finally said the quiet part: "If you don't change within a year or two, you could pass away at any moment."

His own autopsy of how he got there is the most honest thing any streamer has said on a microphone. He doesn't smoke. He doesn't drink. He doesn't party. He would finish streaming at 12:30 at night, order delivery, "and instead of one burger it's two, with ice cream." Two and a half plates of macaroni and then custard, while his daily step count, he joked, was "120 steps, in League of Legends."

And underneath the jokes, the sentence this piece opened with: the eating was anger, paid out sideways, at no one who could answer back. For years the internet called him fat like it was a personality. "I put up with people systematically calling me fat for years," he told GQ in July 2025. What the mockery never saw: everyone in that body already knows. "You see yourself in the shower. Every fat person sees it and knows it. It's a mental problem, and the head is a real bastard."

On June 26, 2024, he posted a video announcing day one of a physical change, using his superpower against himself for the first time: an audience as a commitment device. His trainer, Valentín Colominas, watched the mechanism up close: "The key with Ibai is that he started by hitting very small goals, and then he got insanely motivated." Even here the comedian stayed in charge of the edit: the routine video he posted six months in opens with him swearing it will not look like an infomercial, mock-announcing "miracle diet, lose 40 kilos in just two weeks" in the voice of the scams he refused to become (YouTube, December 2024).

A year after day one he had lost 52 kilos, walking ten kilometers a day, every day. "I've walked the equivalent of the distance from my house to Russia," he told El Español in September 2025. The mask is gone; it left with the first 25 kilos.

What he refuses to give the story is a redemption-arc gloss on the man he was: "I love myself at 98 kilos and I loved myself at 150. But for health, I had to do it."

## What Kun Agüero saw when Ibai finally snapped

The weight story explains where the anger went. It leaves a harder question open: whether the calm was ever real. There is a version of it a smart skeptic should distrust, and it goes like this: of course he never takes sides. He is a business. His empire runs on being everyone's friend, every brand's safe bet, every feud's Switzerland. Niceness at his scale is a moat.

The skeptic is not wrong about the incentives. What the incentives cannot explain is August 2023.

It did not come out of nowhere. That whole year, the club he had founded with Piqué was quietly coming apart: KOI's American merger partner, Infinite Reality, was running up unpaid bills to players, workers, and the league itself, and Spanish esports press reported that Ibai covered some three million euros of salaries out of his own pocket to keep his people whole (Dexerto, 2023). He absorbed the cost, kept the room calm, and ended the partnership that November without one public word about any of it. The anger had nowhere sanctioned to go.

That summer, mid-crisis, Ibai's Kings League team and his team in the women's Queens League were both knocked out in the semifinals. The other presidents, Iker Casillas among them, poked fun at the double elimination. A joke. The exact currency Ibai deals in daily.

And the man who absorbs everything did something nobody had seen: he vanished from the shows, then fired off a message in the presidents' group chat that crossed lines, reportedly airing details of a private project of Agüero's. The Kun went on stream genuinely wounded: "The thing with Ibai is that when you joke with him and he gets angry, he goes after your private life." And then the stranger detail: "I've seen him distant. When I'm in Barcelona and I text him, he ignores me."

A jab in a group chat. Messages left on read. Weeks of silence toward one of his closest friends. This is what fury looks like in a man who has spent his whole life making sure the room never breaks: it exits through side doors, delayed, at targets who never saw the original wound. [Stress works differently for every Enneagram type](/enneagram-corner/enneagram-types-in-stress), and the Nine's version is disappearance. The panic attacks in Barcelona, the eating at 12:30 a.m., the ghosting of the Kun. One mechanism, three exits.

Which is the real answer to the skeptic. A brand calculation produces public smoothness and private indifference. Ibai's smoothness is the opposite: publicly effortless, privately expensive, paid for by his own body at 40 interruptions a night. Nobody chooses that price as a marketing strategy.

## The smallest event Ibai ever organized

Every event Ibai Llanos has ever produced was for other people. Other people's fights, other people's concerts, other people's farewells to Barcelona. The stadiums, the world records, the 9.33 million: all of it, a man building rooms he could stand at the edge of.

The June 26, 2024 video had none of that. No lineup, no stage design, no Juanes. A camera, a sentence, a start date. He has run the event every single morning since: ten kilometers, headphones on, no chat, no record at stake, the distance to Russia and counting. Somewhere inside those kilometers, a flight of five stairs went back to being nothing at all.

He says he might fight at La Velada one day. It would be the first time in six years of fight nights that he books the ring for himself. Anyone who watched him walk out of a 152-kilo body knows the booking already happened. The opponent never had a name. It was the third plate of macaroni, the message left on read, the anger with nowhere to go. That fight went the full distance, every round of it off camera, and the quietest man on the internet won it in front of nobody.
