In February 2013 the novelist Hilary Mantel gave a [lecture on royal bodies](https://www.lrb.co.uk/the-paper/v35/n04/hilary-mantel/royal-bodies) in which she said "Kate Middleton, as she was," "appeared to have been designed by a committee and built by craftsmen, with a perfect plastic smile." Kate, she went on, "seems to have been selected for her role of princess because she was irreproachable," chosen "without the risk of the emergence of character."

Mantel was describing an image, not a woman she knew, and her lecture ends by asking the public to "back off and not be brutes." It was reported as an attack on Kate anyway. Prime Minister David Cameron called Mantel "completely misguided and completely wrong," [NPR](https://www.npr.org/sections/thetwo-way/2013/02/19/172434738/booker-winning-authors-remarks-about-kate-middleton-play-out-in-u-k-media) reported. Kate never answered it.

In November 2026 she gave a rare interview to the nature writer Robert Macfarlane for an issue of _Country Life_ guest-edited by her husband. Explaining why she goes outdoors, she said of nature, according to [PA Media](https://www.irishnews.com/news/uk/kate-says-nature-doesnt-judge-us-or-expect-us-to-perform-in-rare-interview-KWFBNYG77ZIKHNXZ5VH6TEOE5M/):

> "It doesn't ask anything of us. It doesn't judge us or expect us to perform."

"It simply allows us to be. It really has been my sanctuary," she went on.

The woman accused of being a performance values a place that doesn't expect one. That thread runs through her own words from 2010 to 2026.

Catherine Elizabeth Middleton was born on 9 January 1982 in Reading, the eldest of Michael and Carole Middleton's three children; her mother founded the party-supplies firm Party Pieces in 1987. She read art history at the University of St Andrews, met Prince William there in 2001 and graduated in 2005. They married at Westminster Abbey on 29 April 2011 and have three children. She has been Princess of Wales since 9 September 2022. In March 2024 she announced she was being treated for cancer, and in January 2025 she said she was in remission.

## What is Kate Middleton's personality type?

Kate Middleton is an Enneagram Type 9, the Peacemaker. She keeps the people around her steady, plays down her own pain and stays out of public fights. Under pressure she looks for ways to set the load down, and she restores herself in quiet and the outdoors. When she won't move, she digs in without a scene.

[Type 9](/enneagram-corner/enneagram-type-9) sits in the instinctual, or body, triad, and the emotion under the body triad is anger. That's anger as opposed to shame, which drives the heart types, or fear, which drives the head types. An Eight lets the anger out. A [One](/enneagram-corner/enneagram-type-1) turns it into a standard and holds everyone to it, herself first. A Nine puts it to sleep, keeps the room calm, and lets her own wants sink below everyone else's.

A Nine works it in four plain steps:

1. Go along with the people and the plan around you.
2. Push your own discomfort and anger down so nothing gets disturbed.
3. Look for quiet, simplicity and agreement, and recover there.
4. When you finally refuse, refuse quietly and don't budge.

## Five years of going along

After St Andrews, Kate spent most of five years in a relationship with no public commitment and photographers outside her door, while working part-time as an accessories buyer at Jigsaw and for her parents' firm ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Catherine,_Princess_of_Wales)). The press called her "waity Katie," the [BBC](https://www.bbc.com/news/uk-11767308) noted in 2010, for appearing to spend her time "shopping and socialising while waiting for a proposal."

In April 2007 the couple split. In their November 2010 [engagement interview](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=2hPi38x90ks) with ITV's Tom Bradby, she said she "wasn't very happy about it but actually it made me a stronger person." Then:

> "You can get quite consumed by a relationship when you're younger."

She said she "really valued that time for me as well," though she hadn't thought so then.

Said three weeks after the proposal with William beside her, that has limits. But it is the Nine's central habit in her own words: merging with the person you're close to, and finding yourself again only when the merger breaks.

The summer of 2007 cuts both ways. In April 2007 she joined the Sisterhood, an all-female dragon-boat crew training to cross the Channel for charity. The couple were seen together again by June, and she kept training. In late July [HELLO!](https://www.hellomagazine.com/royalty/344334/middleton-rowing/) reported she was to be given the tiller, the seat where, a crewmate said, "you have to do all the shouting." In August she reportedly gave up her place at the Queen's urging ([IBTimes](https://www.ibtimes.com/why-kate-middleton-wanted-pose-underwear-during-prince-william-split-2784878), citing The Guardian). Taking the loud seat is drive. Giving it up when the Queen asked fits going along, though almost any royal girlfriend would have done the same.

Bradby asked whether she had ever wanted William to hurry up. The two of them turned it aside: "we've had our conversations." William said he wanted "to give her a chance to see and to back out if she needed to." Waiting can be love, strategy or discretion. What stands out is that she never forced the question in public, through years of mockery for not forcing it.

## "Not totally my responsibility"

The clearest window into her is a 2020 episode of Giovanna Fletcher's podcast [Happy Mum, Happy Baby](https://shows.acast.com/happymumhappybaby/episodes/theduchessofcambridgeontheearlyyears). It promoted her early-childhood survey, so it's a campaign appearance. It's also thirty-five minutes of her talking, and the same ideas keep coming back.

One is what she wants. Of her own childhood she said "the things that really resonate with me most is the simple things," like "watching a fire on a really rainy day." Asked what she'd want her children to remember, she set two weekends side by side:

> "Is it that I'm sitting down trying to do the maths and spelling homework over the weekend or is it the fact we've gone out and bonfire and sat around trying to cook sausages that hasn't worked because it's too wet."

She picked the soggy bonfire. "Not a stressful household where you're trying to do everything." Then, paraphrasing the National Trust founder Octavia Hill: "our lives are so busy, our lives are so hectic. All we need is quiet."

Another is what relief looks like. Talking about guilt over working, she recalled being told that a child is better off with more loving people around it than the parents alone. It took a weight off her shoulders: "Actually, it's not totally my responsibility to do everything because we all have good days, bad days." The guilt stayed, "a constant challenge." The relief came from setting part of the load down.

A third is how she works. Since her marriage she'd been "listening to what makes people tick, and actually the things that unify people, but also the struggles that people are facing." Talking to mothers taught her that "everyone has something in common." Early childhood has been her own project since 2018, from a national survey to the Shaping Us campaign. She calls it "generational change," a reformer's ambition, and runs it a Nine's way: listening first, a survey before an opinion, a cause built on common ground.

And it outlasted her illness. In the 2026 _Country Life_ interview she remembered childhood as "simply time alone in my own world, enjoying the simplicity of ordinary things." These answers were probably prepared and approved by her office. They still say what the 2020 podcast said.

## The brave face

A Nine plays down her own pain. Kate does it in ways you can measure.

During her first pregnancy she was admitted to hospital with hyperemesis gravidarum, a severe form of pregnancy sickness, and it came back in the next two ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Catherine,_Princess_of_Wales)). On the podcast: "I got very bad morning sickness, so I'm not the happiest of pregnant people." Then: "Lots of people have it far worse."

The cancer statements run in the same order. In her [22 March 2024 video](https://www.itv.com/news/2024-03-22/princess-of-wales-statement-in-full) she said she and William had been "doing everything we can to process and manage this privately for the sake of our young family," and that it had taken time to explain everything to the children "and to reassure them that I am going to be ok."

When chemotherapy ended she marked it publicly, in a family video on 9 September 2024 that called the past nine months "incredibly tough for us as a family." She also said it had reminded her and William to be grateful for "the simple yet important things in life, which so many of us often take for granted. Of simply loving and being loved" ([CBC News](https://www.cbc.ca/lite/story/1.7317564)). That's common after illness, but she said "the simple things" in 2020 too, about bonfires.

On 2 July 2025, visiting Colchester Hospital, she told patients what recovery had been like, according to the pool report in [Hello!](https://www.hellomagazine.com/royalty/841652/kate-middleton-opens-up-difficult-phase-cancer-recovery-colchester-hospital):

> "You put on a sort of brave face, stoicism through treatment, treatments done, then it's like 'I can crack on, get back to normal' but actually the phase afterwards is really, is really you know difficult."

That is the Nine's second step in her own words: set your own state aside and carry on as if nothing happened. She also said it didn't work. "You have to find your new normal and that takes time."

Kensington Palace released the trailer on 21 November 2026 for _Heal_, a film about her recovery that the BBC broadcasts on 29 November. In it her brother James asks whether you ring the bell when you leave the Royal Marsden. "Yeah, but I didn't ring the bell," she says, according to [GB News](https://www.gbnews.com/royal/kate-middleton-cancer-film-heal-james-middleton). "Maybe this is your ringing of the bell," he replies. She skipped the hospital's end-of-treatment ritual, though not the end itself, and the trailer gives no reason.

## Silent in public, firm in private

Near the end of the 2010 interview Bradby asked about criticism. Of her work she said, "I'm there pulling my weight." Asked whether criticism of her family hurt, she pointed to the friends and family who mattered:

> "You sort of have to ignore a lot of what's said, obviously take it on board, but you know you have to be yourself really."

She was newly engaged, in a palace-arranged interview, so even then the setting favoured restraint. She has kept to it since. Mantel in 2013, Meghan's account to Oprah Winfrey in 2021, Prince Harry's memoir _Spare_ in 2023 and the Dutch edition of _Endgame_ that reportedly named her later that year all went publicly unanswered.

The monarchy's rule is never complain, never explain, and her office decides who talks to her, so the silence can't be credited wholly to temperament. But people who have worked with her describe it as temperament, not rule. Jamie Lowther-Pinkerton, the couple's former private secretary, told the Sunday Times for her 40th birthday in January 2022, as relayed by [Woman & Home](https://www.womanandhome.com/life/royal-news/how-kate-middletons-old-fashioned-attitude-to-drama-has-helped-her-cope-throughout-turbulent-year) and the [NZ Herald](https://www.nzherald.co.nz/lifestyle/daniela-elser-kate-middletons-secret-double-life-exposed-in-40th-birthday-interviews/POEOMXGIY7NPTPSIQN66EGIOG4/):

> "She has that almost old-fashioned, Queen Mother attitude to drama — she just doesn't do it."

"She never changed her manner with anybody," a close friend of the couple told the same paper. These are loyal voices from one birthday profile, but it's what a Nine looks like from outside.

Calm isn't the same as compliant. Before her 2016 Vogue cover shoot, according to a source close to her quoted in the NZ Herald's round-up, the magazine had gowns standing by in case it could change her mind. She "looked at them with a smile and said, 'No, we're going with plan A.'" That's the fourth step: no argument, and no movement either.

She pushes back through lawyers the same way, from a 2010 privacy claim over tennis photographs, brought before the engagement, that won £5,000 and an apology, to a 2025 privacy win against Paris Match ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Catherine,_Princess_of_Wales)). None came with a public argument.

And in private she argues. An unnamed royal aide told the biographer Robert Jobson in 2022 that "the Duke and the Duchess give as good as they get if their disagreement results in raised voices" ([IBTimes UK](https://www.ibtimes.co.uk/kate-middleton-no-pushover-during-heated-rows-prince-william-1699591)). Anonymous and third-hand, it still cuts against reading her as someone without anger.

## Where the anger goes

If a Nine puts anger to sleep, where does Kate's go? Two places show up. A third would count against the Nine reading: a public correction of a critic, or a grievance kept up in public. Both are missing from the record.

The first is sport. On the podcast _The Good, The Bad & The Rugby_ in September 2023, Mike Tindall asked about her competitive streak and she jokingly denied having one. Then, of William: "I don't think we've managed to finish a game of tennis, the two of us." And: "It becomes a mental challenge between the two of us." Of swimming in the dark and rain: "I will go and seek out cold water. I love it." ([ITV News](https://www.itv.com/news/2023-09-09/mike-tindall-reveals-princess-of-wales-plays-beer-pong-during-interview))

That's real counterevidence. This analysis reads sport as the place her drive and aggression are allowed out, in the body and inside rules. That is an interpretation; she has never described it that way.

The second is private conflict, where the only first-hand witnesses are on the other side. After a 2018 dispute over bridesmaid dresses (below), Prince Harry describes in _Spare_ a four-way "summit" at Kensington Palace, which The Daily Beast dates to December 2018. By his account Kate opened by owning her part: "I know, Meghan, that I was the one who made you cry," as [HELLO!](https://www.hellomagazine.com/royalty/511148/9-things-prince-harry-said-spare-about-princess-kate) quotes it ([The Daily Beast](https://www.thedailybeast.com/prince-harry-claims-kate-told-meghan-i-know-i-was-the-one-that-made-you-cry) has "that made you cry"). Then she raised a grievance of her own: Meghan had once put her forgetfulness down to "baby brain." Harry writes, as quoted by [Newsweek](https://www.newsweek.com/everything-prince-harry-said-kate-middleton-meghan-markle-tensions-1854404):

> "Kate looked out into the garden, gripping the edges of the leather so tightly that her fingers were white, and said she was owed an apology."

"You hurt my feelings, Meghan," she said in his telling, and: "We're not close enough for you to talk about my hormones!" Meghan apologised, and William backed Kate: "It's rude, Meghan. It's not what's done here in Britain." The meeting ended, Harry writes, with the four of them hugging.

The content of that grievance is a breach of manners, which fits a One as well as a Nine. What this analysis reads as Nine is the order: kept down until it had to be said, said as a hurt after owning her own fault, and ended in an embrace. It doesn't settle who was right, and it rests on one hostile witness writing years later.

## The controversies

She has publicly answered only one of these.

**The bridesmaid dresses (2018).** In late 2018 British papers reported that Meghan had made Kate cry at a fitting before the Sussexes' May 2018 wedding.

Meghan told Oprah Winfrey, in an interview broadcast on 7 March 2021, that "the reverse happened," and that Kate apologized and took "accountability," sending flowers and a note. She called it a "turning point" when the family chose not to correct the record, [NBC News](https://www.nbcnews.com/news/world/meghan-knocks-down-rumor-she-made-kate-cry-says-reverse-n1259938) reported.

In Harry's _Spare_, four days before the wedding, Kate said "Charlotte's dress is too big, too long, too baggy," and, offered the tailor, "No, all the dresses need to be remade." Asked if she knew what was going on with Meghan's father, "Kate said she was well aware, but the dresses." The next morning she came with flowers and a card. At the later summit, Harry writes, Kate acknowledged the papers' stories "were totally false," and Meghan asked "what was being done to correct them?"

A palace source told the Sunday Times, "I've had hundreds of hours of conversations with her [Kate] and it never came up." Kate has never commented, and every first-hand account comes from one side.

The Sussexes' critique is that her silence let a story she privately called false stand against Meghan, protecting her at someone else's cost. The record can't separate that from the Nine reading: a Nine avoiding the fight would leave the story standing too.

**The Caribbean tour (2022).** In March 2022 Kate and William toured Belize, Jamaica and the Bahamas. The tour drew protests and calls for slavery reparations. The PR consultant Mark Borkowski told [CBC News](https://www.cbc.ca/lite/story/1.6405427), "It felt slightly colonial, the whole exercise." Her own view is not on record, so the tour stays out of the type argument.

**The _Endgame_ naming (2023).** In late November 2023 the Dutch edition of _Endgame_, by the journalist Omid Scobie, reportedly named King Charles and Kate as the two royals behind what Meghan had described in 2021, without names, as "concerns and conversations about how dark his skin might be when he is born," about her son Archie. Scobie said the English version "has no names in it." The publisher recalled the Dutch edition, citing an "error," and [NBC News](https://www.nbcnews.com/news/world/questions-royal-family-racism-reignited-pair-high-profile-names-dutch-rcna127569) said it "has not independently verified that such conversations took place." No first-hand account names her. She has not addressed it, and it is not evidence for any type.

**The photograph (2024).** The one row she answered is the one she caused. She describes herself as an "enthusiastic amateur photographer" and has taken official portraits of her children for years ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Catherine,_Princess_of_Wales)). After abdominal surgery in January 2024 she was out of sight for weeks, amid "widespread speculation and a slew of online conspiracy theories," [NBC News](https://www.nbcnews.com/news/world/princess-kate-apologizes-confusion-edited-photo-rcna142722) reported. On 10 March, Mother's Day in Britain, Kensington Palace released a photograph of her with her three children. The Associated Press, Reuters, Getty Images and AFP withdrew it, AP saying: "At closer inspection it appears that the source has edited the image." On 11 March she posted:

> "Like many amateur photographers, I do occasionally experiment with editing. I wanted to express my apologies for any confusion the family photograph we shared yesterday caused. I hope everyone celebrating had a very happy Mother's Day. C."

The original was not released. She had begun chemotherapy in February and announced her cancer eleven days later.

CNN's Lauren Said-Moorhouse and Max Foster made the case against it in an [analysis](https://abc13.com/princess-kate-middleton-royal-family-photo-editing/14516554/) on 12 March 2024: "the palace wasn't transparent about the fact it had been adjusted. That will have damaged the trust between the palace and media organizations."

The type explains the shape of the response, not the act. Why she edited the picture is not on record beyond her line about experimenting. The response is: a hobby, an apology for the confusion, and no further word. That fits the Nine habit of closing a subject quickly. It doesn't excuse handing news agencies an altered image.

## Why not Type 1?

Type 1, the Reformer, is the strongest rival reading of Kate, and the case for it is real.

Mantel's word was "irreproachable." On the podcast, asked whether she struggled with mum guilt, Kate said, "Yes absolutely – and anyone who doesn't as a mother is actually lying!" and "Yep – all the time," according to [Yahoo's transcription](https://www.yahoo.com/lifestyle/kate-middleton-admits-to-having-mom-guilt-anyone-who-doesnt-as-a-mother-is-actually-lying-212939179.html). And:

> "And always sort of questioning your own decisions and your own judgements and things like that, and I think that starts from the moment you have a baby!"

She "definitely would have done things differently" in pregnancy. In a [2017 maternal mental health speech](https://www.royal.uk/speech-duchess-cambridge-best-beginnings-out-blue-film-series-launch) she said "there is no rule book, no right or wrong - you just have to make it up and do the very best you can to care for your family," and added: "For many mothers, myself included, this can, at times lead to lack of confidence and feelings of ignorance." Having no rules shook her confidence.

The One's best evidence is behavioral too. Asked about press criticism of her work in 2010, she answered first "I'm there pulling my weight." The photograph statement opened by normalising the edit. On Harry's account she rejected a tailor's alteration, pressed the dresses while "well aware" of Meghan's family crisis, and demanded an apology for the hormones remark. Resentment at an improper act is the One's signature anger.

So an exacting woman is visible, mostly to herself. The strongest One is lenient with others and strict inside, and Kate's campaign lines ("It's ok not to find it easy," or "I'm not going to mark you at the end" to a mother filling in her anonymous survey) are what any spokesperson for those causes would say. As type evidence they cancel out.

What does is how she gets relief. A One gets it by meeting the standard. Kate's own examples all come from setting it down. Being told she didn't have to carry everything took a weight off her shoulders: "it's not totally my responsibility to do everything." For her own children she chose the rained-out bonfire over the weekend homework. After cancer she valued nature because it doesn't "judge us or expect us to perform." A One longs for that rest too, but earns it by finishing the job. A One's relief comes from getting it right. Hers comes from being let off the judgment altogether.

Her self-defence stops short the same way. "Pulling my weight" and the amateur-photographer line are each a sentence long, and each time she then let the subject go. She has yet to correct a named critic in public.

[Type 3](/enneagram-corner/enneagram-type-3) is the image reading: the polish, the controlled Vogue cover, the edited photograph, the unfinished tennis matches, a Three Peaks climb filmed for a documentary. But a Three takes the credit the moment offers. Kate turned down credit for her suffering ("Lots of people have it far worse") and didn't ring the bell. [Type 6](/enneagram-corner/enneagram-type-6) would explain "reassure," which recurs in her speech, and her reliance on family. But when she describes where she gets steady, it isn't trusted authority or preparation. It's quiet and open ground: "All we need is quiet," and nature as "my sanctuary."

## What the type doesn't explain

It doesn't explain the appetite for hard physical tests. In June 2026, a little over two years after starting chemotherapy, she climbed the highest mountains in Scotland, England and Wales within 24 hours to raise money for the Royal Marsden's cancer charity, according to the [Associated Press](https://www.cp24.com/news/world/2026/11/21/catherine-princess-of-wales-reflects-on-life-after-cancer-diagnosis-in-a-new-documentary/). That is drive, not inertia. Nor does it explain why someone who wants quiet stayed in the most watched job in Britain, or fully separate her calm from the institution's. Most testimony comes from loyal friends and former staff, and the one hostile first-hand witness, Prince Harry, describes a more exacting woman.

It also leaves the Sussex rupture open. Russell Myers, a biographer sympathetic to the Waleses, reported in February 2026 that "Catherine had less interest than her husband in trying to persuade Harry to stay in his current role" ([Marie Claire](https://www.marieclaire.co.uk/royal-news/prince-william-princess-kate-reaction-to-sussex-resignation)). That is one unsourced account. If it's right, she let a family split stand, which is withdrawal, not the peacemaking the type's name promises.

What holds from 2010 to 2026 is the order she does things in: everyone else steadied first, her own cost admitted much later, as in the 2025 "brave face" line. In the _Heal_ trailer she adds that "it's clear we're not meant to carry life's challenges alone," as the AP reported.

Mantel thought she saw a woman without the risk of character. The record shows someone who has acted, since 2010, as if keeping things calm were her job, and who since her 2024 illness has started counting the cost. Fletcher ends each episode by asking her guest to finish three sentences. Kate's last answer was that she's happy "when I'm with my family outside in the countryside and we're all filthy dirty."
