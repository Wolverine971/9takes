> "People may think there's some hidden business logic behind this, but it's mainly driven by curiosity." — Liang Wenfeng, on buying 10,000 Nvidia GPUs, _36Kr Waves_ interview, 2023 (Zihan Wang translation, ChinaTalk)

<p class="firstLetter">Yan Junjie got to the restaurant early. A young man in a T-shirt was already at the table, and before the food arrived he was asking Yan detailed technical questions, one after another. Yan, who runs the AI company MiniMax, assumed he was talking to somebody's assistant, a sharp one. After half an hour he asked when Mr. Liang would be arriving. "I am Liang Wenfeng," the man said. (Yan's account, relayed by investor Zhang Jinjian; Tencent News, January 2026)</p>

That dinner was before anyone outside Chinese finance knew the name. Liang Wenfeng had already built High-Flyer, one of China's largest quant hedge funds. By July 2026 the Bloomberg Billionaires Index put his fortune near $36 billion and called him the richest founder of any AI model company. DeepSeek, which he still controls, had closed its first outside funding round at a valuation near $50 billion and was preparing a 2027 stock listing (Bloomberg, July 14, 2026).

Then, on July 23, a 42-page transcript leaked, claiming to record Liang talking to prospective investors for nearly four hours in May. The viral lines were blunt: China is about two years behind the United States on roughly one-twentieth of the compute, and DeepSeek still runs almost entirely on Nvidia. Two days later Bloomberg reported that Liang himself had told the next set of backers, a round Chinese financial media sized at 50 billion yuan (about $7 billion), that they would not be signing in the coming days, a pause Bloomberg's sources put down partly to his frustration that the remarks had leaked.

The same transcript, in the Tencent News edit of the purported May 2026 recording, holds the sentence people quoted back at him all summer: "Restraint is a strategy." As of August 2026 DeepSeek had neither confirmed nor denied the document; an institution that invested in the company told National Business Daily the meeting happened and the content was credible. It describes a lab that priced its product to pay back its hardware in ten months, a rule its founder calls "not profit-maximizing," run by a man who says money "is definitely not a problem" (Tencent News, 2026).

A quant fund exists to find mispriced things and take the difference. Liang got rich doing exactly that. Then he built the most mispriced asset in his industry, a frontier model sold for a fraction of what buyers would pay, and walked away from the difference. The pattern first shows up in a stamp album in a Wuchuan primary school.

## What is Liang Wenfeng's personality type?

### Liang Wenfeng is an Enneagram Type 5

The type that fits Liang Wenfeng is the Enneagram Type 5, the Investigator, and the cleanest evidence is his answer when a reporter asked what excites him most about building DeepSeek: "Verifying whether our hypotheses are correct. If they are, that's immensely satisfying." (36Kr, 2023) Most founders answer that question with a market. He answered it with an experiment.

A Five feels safe in proportion to how much he has figured out, and guards whatever the figuring requires: time, attention, and in Liang's case, compute. Asked by the Chinese tech outlet 36Kr in 2023 why a hedge fund had stockpiled 10,000 GPUs, he recited the count like a lab notebook, from "a single card in the early days" to "10,000 cards," and then gave the explanation at the top of this page.

Luo Yonghao, the Smartisan founder turned livestream seller, whose team Liang had helped with model access, came away with the same reading from the outside. Liang, he told The Paper in June 2025, in an interview SCMP carried, "carries enormous curiosity and very seriously chases a question to the end," asks "very directly, never circles," and has not been "domesticated" by the high-EQ social style that usually arrives with money.

What makes him a distinctive Five is what he did with the wealth. Most people who get rich expand. He used the money to need less. "I only want to eat one piece, only the piece I'm best at," he told investors in the purported May 2026 transcript, as Tencent News edited it. No overtime, "because it's just not that hard." No sales staff for the API; "users come on their own." No finished consumer products: "we haven't gone to fill them in. This is also a culture of ours."

That is half of the quant puzzle. A quant exploits mispricing because the money is the point. Liang built the fund to afford the experiment, and once the experiment was funded, taking more turned into a cost: more products to maintain, more people to manage, more attention pulled off the one question he cares about. The same transcript, in the Tencent News edit of 2026, has him say it plainly: "Those who take more will be defeated by those who take less."

None of this hangs on a leaked document. The 2023 and 2024 36Kr interviews already hold the pattern: no KPIs, a GPU cluster explained as a piano bought to be played, weights given away for the respect it earns, a price rule of neither subsidy nor exorbitant profit. The August 2026 price rise, covered below, came with DeepSeek's own reason, resource allocation, and compute is the one thing he has never taken less of. The other half of the puzzle is in the same leaked document, and it does not flatter the thesis. It can wait.

## Liang Wenfeng's childhood in Wuchuan: two teachers, a stamp album, and one school on the form

Liang Wenfeng was born in 1985 in Mililing, a village attached to Wuchuan, a county-level city on the Guangdong coast that he calls "fifth-tier." Both parents taught primary school (Wuchuan Daily, 2002; Jimu News, 2025).

His sixth-grade teacher, Mr. Li, remembers a boy who built model planes and boats after school and collected stamps by a method the teacher describes with precision: "using stamps to fund stamps." He sold what he received, bought back in, and "barely spent his father's money." (Zhanjiang Cloud Media, January 2025) It reads like a child who wanted something and designed a loop so that nobody would have to give it to him.

By middle school the maths had run out. His homeroom teacher, Mr. Rong, told reporters that Liang "finished high-school maths in middle school and had already started university maths" (Yangcheng Evening News, January 2025).

In 2002 he scored 806 on Guangdong's 900-point scale, the top result in Wuchuan. His final-year teacher, Yang Yahong, remembers the application form better than the score. Students listed several universities. Liang listed one, Zhejiang, and when she asked whether he was sure, answered with a single word: "Certain." (Yangcheng Evening News, July 2026)

A teammate's post on a university forum, later reproduced online, recalled him skipping lectures the textbook had already outrun, and cycling across several provinces alone "without spending much money."

Self-taught, self-funded, self-transported. He finished a master's in 2010 with a thesis on tracking moving targets with cheap cameras. Then he disappeared.

## The cheap flat in Chengdu where Liang Wenfeng failed before he got rich

After graduating he moved into what the 36Kr interviewer called "a cheap rental in Chengdu" and spent the next few years applying AI to one field after another and failing at each (36Kr Waves, 2023). A friend building "unreliable" flying devices in Shenzhen asked him to join; he declined, and the friend's company became DJI.

Finance was the field where the failures stopped. High-Flyer, the quant fund he co-founded in 2015, managed more than 100 billion yuan by 2021 and set no quotas. "We don't have KPIs or so-called quotas," Liang said in 2023, years before that line went viral.

In December 2021, after its worst drawdown on record, High-Flyer published an apology: "We feel deeply guilty." The letter blamed its own AI, which, "when market style switches violently," had "tended to take bigger risks to chase more return" (Tencent News, December 2021). The machine had done the one thing its owner refuses to do. It reached for more, and the firm said sorry for it in public. The lab it announced sixteen months later already had the hardware to run on.

"If purely for quant investing, even a small number of GPUs would suffice," he admitted in 2023. The real purpose was a question: whether there are "simpler ways to express" the entire financial market. "It's like someone buying a piano for a home," he said. "Such a group of people are eager to play beautiful music on it."

Even the extravagance was frugal. "NVIDIA GPUs hold their value well," he noted, "and older cards still find buyers." (36Kr, 2023) The stamps had become chips.

"AGI may be one of the next most challenging frontiers," he said that spring, "so for us, the question is not 'why' but 'how'."

## How DeepSeek accidentally became a catfish, and what the critics get right

Less than a year old, in May 2024, DeepSeek released V2 and priced it at one yuan per million input tokens, a fraction of what ByteDance, Alibaba, Baidu, and Tencent were charging. Within weeks all four cut prices (Forbes, January 2025). Chinese media called DeepSeek the industry's catfish, the fish dropped into the tank to make the others swim.

"We didn't mean to become a catfish," Liang told 36Kr that summer. "We just accidentally became a catfish." The principle was arithmetic, "we don't subsidize nor make exorbitant profits," and the model had simply become cheaper to run.

Eight months later R1 shipped, matching the best American models on public benchmarks, weeks after a V3 paper had put one training run at about $5.6 million. The market concluded the world might need fewer chips. On January 27, 2025, Nvidia lost about $589 billion of market value, the largest one-day drop for any company up to that point (CNBC). Liang was home in Wuchuan for Spring Festival, and spent that afternoon playing football with his old classmates, according to his middle-school teacher (Zhanjiang Release, January 2025). The village had put up a welcome arch; within days he was gone again (Jimu News, January 2025).

A week earlier he had spoken at Premier Li Qiang's symposium and, the Wall Street Journal reported on January 26, 2025, told the premier that chip export controls remained a bottleneck. Three weeks after the crash he was in the room again, for Xi Jinping's meeting with private entrepreneurs. Six founders, Ren Zhengfei and Lei Jun among them, had been designated to address the president; Liang, like Jack Ma, was not among them (CGTN / CNBC, February 17, 2025). On camera, the most talked-about founder on earth that month said nothing. In the footage he looks like a man who came to listen and was getting what he came for.

Others had plenty to say about him. At Davos, [Alexandr Wang](/personality-analysis/alexandr-wang) of Scale AI said on CNBC: "My understanding is that DeepSeek has 50,000 H100s. They can't talk about it obviously because it's against the export controls." Elon Musk replied with one word: "Obviously." TIME's Harry Booth later wrote that the famous $6 million training figure "ignored the roughly $1.6 billion" DeepSeek had spent assembling its cluster, and called the founder's "formidable self-mythologizing" a market force (TIME, August 2025). The charge that traveled furthest was imitation: OpenAI said it had evidence DeepSeek had distilled its models, Microsoft investigated, and White House AI adviser David Sacks called the evidence "substantial" (Bloomberg, January 28, 2025). It was never adjudicated; DeepSeek never answered it.

Concede the core of it. The $5.6 million was one training run, stated as such in the V3 paper; High-Flyer had spent about a billion yuan on Fire-Flyer 2 alone. A man who presents himself as restrained was, on the evidence, one of the most aggressive accumulators of GPUs of any fund its size. Those facts hold, and no personality lens dissolves them. The purported 2026 transcript goes further: it has him telling investors "we can buy some non-compliant cards," in a passage putting DeepSeek's needs "an order of magnitude" below the internet giants'. [Dario Amodei](/personality-analysis/dario-amodei), who cited DeepSeek in arguing for tighter export controls, still described its people as "smart, curious researchers who just want to make useful technology" (January 2025). Both can be true of the same lab.

What the lens adds is the shape of the appetite, and the shape is a split. R1, V4, and V3 since its March 2025 update have all shipped with open weights under an MIT license. "Open source is more of a cultural behavior than a commercial one, and contributing to it earns us respect." (36Kr, July 2024) What he has not disclosed is how many cards he has bought since the October 2022 ban: the only post-ban figure is the purported transcript's, and that meeting's moderator asked attendees not to circulate "card quantities." That is the number Alexandr Wang was pointing at. He publishes the map and hides the engine, and nobody has ever had to hand him either one.

The behavior people mock most, the price-slashing that wrecked rivals' margins and the candor that went viral out of a private investor meeting, runs on the same engine, and he has described the feeling under it in his own words. For thirty years, he said, Chinese tech had been "lying at home waiting 18 months for better hardware and software to emerge," used to "Moore's Law falling out of the sky." "The real gap is the difference between originality and imitation." (36Kr, 2024) Cheap tokens and blunt assessments are what it looks like when a man who spent his twenties failing in a rented room decides the imitation stops with him. The premier heard it. The investors heard it. Nobody gets the flattering version.

## Four of the 118 points: what the leaked Liang Wenfeng transcript says about restraint

What the investors heard, in Tencent News's edit of the purported May 20 recording, runs to 118 numbered remarks; four of them do the most work.

**#4 and #46. "Actually, we have no organization."** The company, he says, is "vision-driven, organized by a vision," and "this vision isn't even written down." Bottom-up, "each person does whatever they want to do themselves, no one manages them, no KPIs."

**#11. "Restraint is a strategy."** The fuller line: "Sometimes you can give up some things to exchange for more of other things." His example was the previous year's user war. He declined to spend against ByteDance because "there are still watermelons behind, and what's in front is maybe just sesame seeds."

**#37. "Our greatest core interest is maintaining the stability of the team."** Then, for emphasis, this "can even be considered our only core interest." Read that beside #42, where he says the gap with America "is mainly in resources, and the gap in people isn't very big," and the fear under the restraint shows. Cards can be rebought. A fortune can be remade. The people who hold the unwritten vision in their heads cannot be, and from July 23 the document that says so, in his own words, was public. Read that week from his side. The sentence he would least have wanted loose, that his people are the one thing he cannot rebuy, was the one moving fastest, and the backers he had picked for their lack of hostility were reading it with everyone else. Two days later he told them they would not be signing.

**#91 to #93. The price.** DeepSeek's API price, he said, was set so that a batch of servers paid for itself in ten months. He volunteered that this is "actually not profit-maximizing": demand is inelastic in that range, so doubling the price would barely dent consumption. Early on, worried about demand, they set the price high, "and people on the team weren't very happy. Later I brought the price back down, down to a quarter, and everyone was happy." When prices fall, he said, "many people in the company group cheered."

On August 6, 2026 Bloomberg reported the round had reopened at a valuation near $74 billion, with signing expected late in the month. Nothing in the document had been retracted.

A fifth point, the other half of the puzzle left open above: the quant who walked away from the difference. In 2023 he explained a 10,000-GPU purchase as "mainly driven by curiosity." In the purported 2026 transcript his habitual question is "what has the greatest return for me to do at this moment?" Read on and return means choosing the experiment over the product: "obviously, I feel doing products right now doesn't have the greatest return." The harder counterevidence is in the same document. "If the AI era will produce many trillion-level companies, I think we're one of them" (#117). He would call it a "super good" year if procurement let him spend 20 billion yuan on cards. Two funding rounds followed a 2024 interview that ruled out near-term financing. Three years and roughly $36 billion sit between the curiosity and the trillion-level company. Either the appetite for return is the discipline that guards the curiosity, or it is what curiosity turns into once the experiment starts paying.

The price was the test, and it started running that August. On August 6, 2026 DeepSeek told developers a "significant" increase was coming. It landed ten days later: peak hours billed at twice the off-peak rate, "to allocate resources more reasonably," and the floor itself moved, V4-Pro output from $0.87 per million tokens to $1.98 off-peak and $3.96 at peak (DeepSeek release note, August 13, 2026; Engadget). All of it came before any listing. Whether the ten-month rule survived, nobody outside the company can check. What can be checked is why the next rise comes. A price that tracks the cards, peak hours rationed, a payback recomputed on scarcer chips, is the Five guarding a resource. A rise after the listing that capacity cannot explain is the restraint having been a phase.

## The watermelons behind Liang Wenfeng

The transcript contains one remark that reads as an accidental self-portrait. "One strange thing is, the thing you most want to get, you can't get. Whereas the thing you don't care much about, is actually fairly easy to get." He meant users. Chinese internet giants fought "bloody battles" for consumer attention in 2024, and the traffic went to the company that did not want it. "The users just couldn't be driven away."

The fortune arrived the same way. He built a fund to pay for chips, chips to test a hypothesis, and a lab that, through the summer of 2026, charged ten months of hardware. The market, unable to find the price he declined to set, set it for him: $36 billion in July 2026, the richest AI-model founder alive by Bloomberg's count.

The 2027 listing Bloomberg reported him preparing would hand him a number that updates every morning, the kind of thing he has spent his life declining to want. What he does want, the watermelon behind all of it, he named once, in 2023, as a frontier where the question was "how" and never "why," and has otherwise only ever called a hypothesis to verify. And by the rule he gave his investors, the thing you most want is the one you can't get.
