> "I've put so much weight on my art that I essentially feel like, oh, I'll bleed out and die for this." — Machine Gun Kelly, the New York Times Popcast, August 7, 2025

<p class="firstLetter">A critic called his album catchy and it made him angry.</p>

This was October 2020. _Tickets to My Downfall_ had debuted at number one on the Billboard 200 with 126,000 equivalent album units, the first chart-topper of a career then eight years deep. He was sitting next to Travis Barker, on camera, when a review came up.

"I actually got really, really mad yesterday when reading a review that was like, oh, it has great catchy pop tunes or whatever," he told Zane Lowe on Apple Music in October 2020. "And I was just like, god, you just missed it. You just missed it."

Hold that next to the story everybody tells about Machine Gun Kelly. In that story he is a chart-chaser: a Cleveland rapper whose rap career stalled, who bought a guitar, who caught a nostalgia wave and rode it to number one. In that story, catchy is the win condition.

He heard it as somebody not paying attention.

There is a harder set of dates around that same album. His father died on July 5, 2020. The number one landed in October. And somewhere in the months between, alone in a house while Megan Fox was overseas shooting a film, he put a shotgun in his mouth and the shell jammed. The best professional year of his life and the worst personal one were the same twelve months, and he has spent every album since arranging for that to keep being true.

## What is Machine Gun Kelly's personality type?

### Machine Gun Kelly is an Enneagram Type 4

Track one of _Lost Americana_ hands you the diagnosis in a single bar.

<div class="pull-quote">
All the pages are blank till my life goes to shit. I know I do that on purpose just to write again.
</div>

Asked whether he ever lets himself feel like he has won, he pointed at that line. "It truly sums up the totality of what I do almost before every album cycle," he told the New York Times Popcast in August 2025. Then he went further, unprompted: "We watched it with this one. You could see it in the news. I'm like, I got to ruin everything."

Take that with salt. He said it on a promotional podcast, in the middle of a cycle, about the cycle. It is also the kindest available account of a rehab stay, a broken engagement, and two years of deleted music: the claim upgrades a pattern of wreckage into a method and self-sabotage into craft, and it lets him keep the wreckage.

The reason to believe him anyway is the calendar. The confession is a year old. The behavior is old enough to vote.

[Type 4](/enneagram-corner/enneagram-type-4), the Individualist, is built on the conviction that ordinary is a kind of death. The core fear is being unremarkable; the core desire is to be understood as the genuine article. Pain, in this wiring, gets promoted from consequence to resource, the evidence that something real is happening underneath. Which means the supply has to be maintained.

That typing deserves suspicion, because Type 4 is the lazy answer for a musician. Of the 75 musicians profiled on 9takes, 28 are Fours: 37.3%, against a 14.5% baseline across all 401 published profiles (9takes corpus, August 2026). Give a man a guitar and a bad childhood and the sticker applies itself.

So run the pressure test. He has two number one albums and says openly he wants a third for the hat trick. He picked a fight with the most commercially dominant rapper alive and came out bigger. He has changed genre three times in fifteen years, each time toward whatever the culture was warming up. All of that points at Type 3, the Achiever.

The tiebreaker is December 2023, and it is on his skin.

He booked thirteen six-hour sessions with a Los Angeles tattoo artist named Roxx and had his torso and arms blacked out in solid ink, 44 needles at a time. She told TMZ in February 2024 that he came in wanting order, that his tattoos had started to feel too chaotic to him. He posted the result on February 20, 2024, and Billboard ran it that week under a headline that just quoted his caption back at him: "for spiritual purposes only." Fifteen years of the visual identity that made him famous went under the block.

The next day, February 21, 2024, he released a song that told on him: "I had a breakdown and tatted my entire body except one line."

An Achiever does not destroy the merchandise. And a man who genuinely wanted to be rid of the old version would have finished the job. He went to a professional, paid for seventy-eight hours of pain, and left one line showing.

## Machine Gun Kelly's childhood: the attic and the Listerine

The need to be read correctly did not start in a tattoo chair.

He gave the long version of it once, thirty-five years in, to the New York Times Popcast on August 7, 2025. Everything quoted below comes from that sitting unless another source is named.

Colson Baker was born in Houston in 1990 to missionary parents and spent his first years abroad. He learned Arabic before English. "I grew up in Africa till I was six."

Then America, and a body that arrived before the rest of him did. He was too tall. "Poverty kind of ate up my clothes," he said. "I had an outfit." Wrong ear pierced. Jim Carrey impressions at kids who had already decided about him. "What's up with Ace Ventura over here."

His mother left when he was nine. He and his father moved into an aunt's basement in Denver, and the house went dark in a way he has only recently started describing in detail.

"Listerine to this day triggers me so hard, because I shared a bed with my dad, and he would always go and throw up in the morning and come back in the bed and he would have Listerine on his breath."

Later, in Cleveland, he made a decision about architecture.

"I literally had a room next to him, and then I moved to the attic so I could be prepared by the time he got upstairs to whatever was going to happen to me."

<p class="inner-thought">The stairs are the whole reason. They make a sound. The sound arrives first, and after it there is time to arrange his face and decide where to put his hands.</p>

His father was not a simple villain, and Colson knows the file. He laid it out on Bunnie XO's _Dumb Blonde_ podcast in August 2024, as the story had been told to him: James Colson Baker was in the room at nine when his own father died of a gunshot wound, and he and his mother stood trial for it. Both were acquitted. "He never took a chance to heal," Colson said in his 2022 documentary. "All that stuff, it came out on me as I grew up."

Two nine-year-olds, two generations apart. One lost a father to a gun and a courtroom. The other lost a mother to a door.

The smaller detail explains the next fifteen years better than the trauma does. He tried selling mixtapes at his Cleveland high school in a fur coat borrowed from his boss at an airbrush shop downtown, a man who weighed 350 pounds. The coat swallowed him. The response came back fast: "Bro, who is this goofy? Do your math homework. You're never going to blow up." Then he started battle rapping.

"I'll never forget my yearbook the year before I started battle rapping, and my yearbook after," he told the Popcast. "No signatures. The year after I started battle rapping: you're going to blow up, man, you're going to be big time, let's hang out."

Ask him if that was armor and he says no. "I don't think I had armor. I think that's why I just took it, bro."

A Four's formative math usually gets described in the abstract, as a feeling of standing outside the group. His version fits in two books, and the only variable was whether he had made himself into something people reacted to.

## Seven Album Titles, One Confession

Names are the first material he ever worked on himself. Colson Baker borrowed his from George Kelly Barnes, the Prohibition bank robber remembered mostly for how fast he could fire. Then, once the world had learned all three words, he cut it down to three lowercase letters.

Most artists name a record after a feeling they have moved past. Read his seven studio albums in order and nothing is in the past tense.

<div class="scroll-table">

| Album                  | Year | What the title admits                                                       |
| ---------------------- | ---- | --------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Lace Up                | 2012 | A promise to the outcasts, made by one                                      |
| General Admission      | 2015 | The cheapest ticket in the building                                         |
| Bloom                  | 2017 | Named for the part that has not happened yet                                |
| Hotel Diablo           | 2019 | He booked a room in hell and printed the address                            |
| Tickets to My Downfall | 2020 | He sold you a seat to watch him lose                                        |
| Mainstream Sellout     | 2022 | He printed the insult on the cover before the critics could reach for it    |
| Lost Americana         | 2025 | Even the reinvention gets filed under lost                                  |

</div>

_Bloom_ is the row that should break the pattern. It is the only optimistic word he has ever put on a cover, spent on a flower that has not opened, and the songs under it are called "Rehab," "27," and "Let You Go." The two that went to number one both have a wound in the name.

Look at what happened on July 5, 2020. He had set the day aside to celebrate the one-year anniversary of _Hotel Diablo_. Instead he posted on Instagram: "my father took his last breath this morning, and ive never felt a pain this deep in my life."

He named a record after hell and the calendar collected.

Two years later a whole genre called him a fraud and he printed the word on his own cover. He explained it to Rock Sound in 2022 without flinching: "I made the statement I needed to make with _Tickets to My Downfall_, and then with _Mainstream Sellout_ it was kind of just like, haha, jokes on you, I'll do what the fuck I want."

Naming the injury first is the oldest move a Four has. If you say it before they can, you own it, and then it belongs to your interior life instead of their comment section.

Travis Barker, who produced both number ones, described the same instinct from inside the studio. "At first listen you hear these songs that are upbeat and almost sound happy," he told Zane Lowe in October 2020, "but then they have the darkest undertones and they're telling the darkest, most unfortunate story."

That is a man selling you a bright pink guitar and a song about dying.

## Why Machine Gun Kelly Went to War With Eminem

Owning the insult first only works when you see it coming.

In 2012 he tweeted that [Eminem](/personality-analysis/eminem)'s teenage daughter was attractive, framed as a compliment to her father. Six years later, in August 2018, Eminem answered on _Kamikaze_ with a verse that named him.

He recorded "Rap Devil" in a locker room within hours of the album dropping. "I just did that on the spot," he told Complex's _Everyday Struggle_ in September 2018, "hours after that shit released." Eminem came back with "Killshot," which outperformed it, and the consensus scorecard has never moved. Asked about reconciliation years later he offered a format instead of an apology: put us on a track, see who comes harder.

This is where the Four reading is weakest. A Four insulted in public is supposed to go quiet and come back in eighteen months with something devastating and beautifully produced.

His own account is simpler. "I grew up a battle rapper," he told the Times in 2025. "I am prepped for war pretty much at all times." Later in the same conversation: "If I need to reawaken that guy, he's always there."

The cypher is where the blank yearbook stopped being blank. It is also a room where the fastest, cruelest, most personal answer wins. He learned to be somebody there at fifteen. The reflex was the first tool that ever worked for him.

## Why People Call Machine Gun Kelly a Sellout

The room he walked into next did not run on cyphers.

In February 2021, on Cutter's Rockcast, Corey Taylor of Slipknot said the thing the whole scene was thinking: "I hate the artists who failed in one genre and decided to go rock, and I think he knows who he is."

Taylor has the sequence right. _Hotel Diablo_ did not go to number one, the rap career had plateaued, he picked up a guitar, and the next record topped the chart. Anyone arguing otherwise is arguing with a chart.

Then he went to number one twice with pop punk and left the genre. He spent two years on the follow-up, decided he could not stand it, and killed it. "I erased all of it," he told the Popcast in 2025. Chart-chasers do not delete two years of finished masters.

The behavior people mock is the reaching. The painted nails, the pink, the guitar he learned late, the announcement that this album is the real him and so is the next one. It reads as a man permanently auditioning, and he has described the audition from the inside. He was the kid nobody signed, the goofy in the borrowed fur coat. The word he has used about himself in every era since is the same one, and he used it again in that same conversation: "I think as a songwriter I've been really slept on for a long time."

"It's difficult to have an album that you're super proud of," he told Kevan Kenney at Audacy in 2022, "and then know that when you give it to the world, they'll find everything that's wrong with it."

The sharpest version of that test was not a record. In 2022 he co-wrote, co-directed and starred in a stoner comedy called _Good Mourning_ with Mod Sun. The Razzies gave it seven nominations and handed him Worst Director. He held every credit on it. The one time nobody misread him, they hated what they saw.

Sympathy for that kid does not cover the Riot Fest speech. In September 2021 he took a stage in Chicago and mocked a singer in his forties for wearing a mask, which is a grown man throwing rocks. He said as much when the documentary came out the following summer: "we all acted ridiculous."

But the reaching explains why he keeps moving. Every new genre is a room where the verdict has not come in yet.

## Machine Gun Kelly and Megan Fox: What She Signed Up For

Most of that dark stuff had a witness.

They met on a film set in early 2020 and the tabloid version took over immediately: twin flames, matching bubblegum hair, a vial of blood, an engagement under a banyan tree. Very easy to read as theater.

The most revealing thing either of them said arrived early, before any of the mythology hardened. In November 2020, [Megan Fox](/personality-analysis/megan-fox) told Nylon what her actual role was.

"There's never an attempt to control him on my end. It's more that he looks to me to avoid his own self destructive tendencies. And that's where I'm useful, because on his own and left to his own devices, I don't know how much interest he has in caring for himself."

She wrote that in month eight, in public, and then the job arrived. When the paranoia came after his father's death, when he started sleeping with a shotgun by the bed, she was the phone call. In the 2022 Hulu documentary _Life in Pink_ he described the night he snapped, barrel in his mouth, Fox on the line: "I go to cock the shotgun, and the bullet as it comes back up, the shell just gets jammed. There is a specific way a Four comes apart, and he ran it in full. He had handed one relationship the entire weight of his own survival, and that night it took the call.

Then the grief stopped being only his to spend. In November 2023 Fox published a book of poems, _Pretty Boys Are Poisonous_, disclosing that she had miscarried at roughly ten weeks. Three months later he answered on record, in the same song that admitted to the tattoo: "How can I live with the fact that my hand wasn't on her stomach when we lost the baby?"

He was not there. That is the whole line. The man who converts private damage into public material at industrial scale was somewhere else during the worst hour of the relationship, and what he made of it afterward was a verse about the absence.

They separated at the end of 2024, weeks before he checked into a facility.

She told a magazine in month eight exactly what she was for. She did it for four more years, and then she stopped.

## Room Three: What Happened to Machine Gun Kelly in Rehab

He spent late November, all of December, and New Year's of 2024 in a facility. No phone. He had a daughter due in March.

Track twelve of _Lost Americana_ reports from inside it: "Wrote this in room three, spending Christmas in rehabilitation. I got no phone, just a cell that I'm trapped in while my home's vacant."

Jelly Roll drove out on weekends, had lunch with him, and let him fall apart at the table. "Let me ball my eyes out and be like, dude, I need help," he told the Popcast in 2025. "Like, I'm lost. He was sitting across that table from me."

He came home on January 6, 2025, and started a water fast. On day four the Palisades fires reached his neighborhood. He was standing at Michael B. Jordan's house watching the flames, delirious from four days without food, deciding what to carry out.

<p class="inner-thought">Nothing in the house is worth anything at four days empty. The question is narrower than money. Which of these things, if it goes, takes some proof that he was ever here.</p>

He grabbed letters, a Banksy, and a Picasso.

"The Picasso, it's in pencil," he told the Popcast. "So even if someone brings an eraser, I'm like, whoa. Not in my house."

The house survived. The Malibu studio where he had recorded for years burned to the ground. He bought a pile of analog synthesizers with a producer named Slim and made a record in his living room with the friends he had before any of it started. Then Bob Dylan posted an old clip of him rapping in a Florida record store, and narrated the 2025 album trailer himself: "a tribute to the spirit of reinvention."

The album needed an intro and did not have one. Megan was overdue and would not go into labor, which both of them found strange, because her babies always came early. He went in, cut "Outlaw Overture," drove home, and lay down on the bed.

Her water broke. Saga Blade Fox-Baker arrived on March 27, 2025.

His reading of that, on the Popcast: "My baby was up there like, nah, dad, you're going to finish this album first." Even the birth of his daughter came out of him as a story about the record.
