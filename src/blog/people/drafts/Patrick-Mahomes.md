<p class="firstLetter">Nine teams had already passed. Patrick Mahomes wanted to know whether his mother's phone was working.</p>

It was the night of April 27, 2017, and the strongest arm in the draft had skipped the green room to watch from a lakeside restaurant outside Tyler, Texas. Between picks he kept turning to his mother, Randi Martin. "Mom, is your phone working? What if we have no service out here?" she remembers him asking, in *The Kingdom*, the ESPN docuseries that aired in 2025. So they called each other during the commercial breaks to make sure the line would hold when a team finally dialed. "They're going to be able to get a hold of us," she told him. "I promise."

Four years later, on the [WHOOP Podcast in 2021](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=azP_kImHHR8), a host asked whether it bothered him that nine teams passed. "I didn't think about it at all." His father, who had expected Chicago to take him third, remembers it differently. "It kind of hurt him," he told Audacy's [670 The Score](https://www.audacy.com/thescorechicago/sports/chicago-bears/patrick-mahomes-wholeheartedly-thought-bears-would-draft-him-2017) in 2023. "It really did."

Both are true. That is the whole puzzle of Patrick Mahomes.

He is the face of the NFL: two MVPs, three rings, and a style of play so loose it has its own vocabulary. No-look. Sidearm. Backyard. He is also, in August 2026, a quarterback born in 1995, eight months past the first torn ACL of his life and the first Kansas City season without a playoff game since 2014, cleared for camp and pointed at an opener nobody in the building would promise out loud.

The loose half is the one you can see from the couch. The other half is the one checking the phone.

## What is Patrick Mahomes's personality type?

### Patrick Mahomes is an Enneagram Type 6

Patrick Mahomes is an <a href="/enneagram-corner/enneagram-type-6">Enneagram Type 6</a>, the Loyalist, with a Seven wing that supplies the grin. The evidence is the preparation underneath the improvisation, the trusted voices he follows to the letter, and the way he only trusts himself once someone he respects has tested him.

The two crowd reads each have a case. The Seven case is the highlight reel: the left-handed flip, the tongue out, the man who "just wanted to win, I didn't care how it looked." The Three case is the hardware and the hunger: "What would be success this year? Super Bowl. That's it. Failure? Not winning the Super Bowl," he says in [*The Kingdom*](https://www.chiefs.com/news/espn-disney-and-skydance-sports-announce-premiere-date-and-unveil-new-trailer-for-the-kingdom), the 2025 ESPN docuseries. Asked by Nate Burleson on CBS in 2023 what it is like when the game slows down, Mahomes credited neither.

"I think it's all from preparation... Kobe always talks about the shots that he practices thousands and thousands of times to take once, to take twice. So whenever those moments do come up during the game it's not like it's the first time I've done it. I've prepared for that. And I think that's why it slows down. You see the highlights and everybody's like, oh, it's a highlight reel, it's being Superman out there, but they don't see that preparation."

That is a Six talking: a man whose first instinct is to imagine the moment going wrong, and whose answer is to have already lived it. The type runs on the fear of being caught without support or a plan. Its signature is that the most anxious person in the room becomes the steadiest one when the crisis is real, because the crisis has been rehearsed. "How can you be focused in chaos, how can you not let the moment be bigger than what it is," he said in 2022 on [*New Heights*](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=QdAQcCtY6GY), the podcast his tight end Travis Kelce hosts with his brother Jason. "Coach Reid puts us in those situations all training camp, all during the season. When you've been put in those situations you go back to those fundamentals."

The type has two modes and he runs both, split by domain. About his body he is phobic, the mode that seeks cover: the safest helmet made, the early slide. About the throw he is counterphobic, the mode that moves toward what it fears. "If you're not toeing that line," he said on the same *New Heights* episode, "I feel like you're not playing aggressive enough."

The league has the tape. Divisional playoff, January 23, 2022: Buffalo scores to go up three with 13 seconds left. Reid finds his quarterback on the sideline. "When it's grim, be the Grim Reaper and go get it," he tells him, as [ESPN](https://www.espn.com/nfl/story/_/id/33135688/patrick-mahomes-kansas-city-chiefs-prevail-buffalo-bills-win-wild-afc-divisional-game-over) reported that night. Two throws, 44 yards, a tying field goal at zero, a touchdown in overtime. His explanation, as [Sports Illustrated](https://www.si.com/nfl/2022/01/24/chiefs-bills-divisional-round-daily-cover) printed it on January 24, 2022, was the flattest sentence of his career: "It wasn't hard to keep them focused. We believed." The situation was new to the stadium. It was not new to him.

And where a Three wants the crowd and a Seven wants the next thing, a Six wants a trustworthy authority and a structure that holds. On draft night, he says in *The Kingdom* (ESPN, 2025), the nerves had a specific shape. "It's probably one of the most nervous I've ever been in my entire life, because I just loved Coach Reid and I thought that if I wanted to be who I wanted to be, I needed someone like that coaching me."

He had a second or third-round grade from the draft advisory board. He went tenth. And the thing he was afraid of losing was the coach.

## How Patrick Mahomes Rehearses a No-Look Pass

Jason Kelce set the trap on *New Heights*. Mahomes had just said he never cared how a play looked, only whether it worked. "For someone that doesn't care how it looks," Jason said, "you manage alright."

Mahomes did not argue. He confessed.

"I've for sure practiced. I try to put myself in those situations during the offseason and practice the sidearm throws, no-look throws." Then the part the highlight never shows: "In the back of my head all I think about is, if this doesn't work right here... I just make sure, if I do throw the no-look pass or the sidearm pass, that I make sure it's completed."

That is the sentence the Seven reading cannot survive. The no-look pass is a worst-case scenario that has been drilled until the worst case has nowhere left to hide. When it happens in a game, Mahomes says he sometimes blacks out: "I do stuff on the field and I don't even remember doing that." The body is running a program the mind wrote months earlier.

The program has a cost, and for one season the cost was logged. On the WHOOP Podcast in 2021 the host read his 2020 season back to him off the wrist strap: an 18 recovery score before the AFC Championship Game against Buffalo. "That means I wasn't sleeping well at night," he said. "I was definitely thinking about everything, and too much visualizing before, and too much adrenaline pumping." He told [Logan Paul](/personality-analysis/logan-paul) in 2024 where a heart-rate experiment found the fear: "When I'm on the field it's back normal. When I'm not on the field I'm just like any other fan in the stadium." The dread belongs to the waiting. It leaves the moment he has something to do.

The program also has a schedule, and the schedule is borrowed. In 2017 the Chiefs sat their rookie behind Alex Smith for a year. By his own telling Mahomes shadowed more than he asked; Smith, in *The Kingdom*, remembers "a thousand questions." Both agree on the hours. "'Hey, what time are you going to be in the building Monday?' He's like, 'I'm coming in at nine.' I'm there at 8:45 sitting in the room waiting for him."

The week he copied, as he laid it out for the Kelces in 2022:

<div class="timeline">
<p><b>Monday and Tuesday.</b> Watch the game just played, then five or six of the opponent's games "to get a general sense of the feeling of what they're doing."</p>
<p><b>Base-install day.</b> Before leaving the building, watch the third-down cut-up. "Because Alex did that. It taught me to get a day ahead on the third downs before I step in here."</p>
<p><b>The next day.</b> Same thing with the red zone. Then "the same thing the next day" with goal line, stacking one situation at a time "so it didn't overwhelm me."</p>
<p><b>Every week, forever.</b> "I'm very superstitious, so whatever I did in my rookie year I do exactly the same now. I'm baseball-player superstitious."</p>
</div>

[Travis Kelce](/personality-analysis/travis-kelce) pulled up beside Mahomes's car one Tuesday and found the rookie with a notebook "just full of stuff that I can't even read." His reaction: "Man, I ain't doing that. I need to be doing that."

## Patrick Mahomes's Childhood in Major League Clubhouses

The shadowing did not start with Alex Smith. Pat Mahomes Sr. pitched eleven seasons in the majors, and his son grew up in the clubhouse, shagging fly balls while the adults worked.

What he took from the clubhouse was a method, copied whole. His godfather, the pitcher LaTroy Hawkins, told [GQ in 2020](https://www.gq.com/story/patrick-mahomes-cover-profile-august-2020) that most kids in that room ate the bubble gum and ran around. "Patrick was trying to figure out how I threw my fastball. Talking to A-Rod, asking: 'How can I hit the ball to right field like you hit the ball to right field?'" His father made him hit off a tee until he watched Alex Rodriguez start every day on one, a lesson he says "stuck with me forever."

Even the tongue that pokes out of his mouth on every dropback is inherited. "That's an old Silky P thing," he told the Kelces, using his father's nickname. "I'm watching my dad play professional baseball, so I'm trying to do everything he's doing." His daughter Sterling does it now.

The adults who were supposed to know the way did not agree on it. On a recruiting visit to the University of Texas as a high-school sophomore, he told the WHOOP Podcast, "they had kind of sent me over to play safety, and I knew I wasn't going to be a safety." On the drive home his father told him to drop football for baseball and basketball. Texas Tech's Kliff Kingsbury offered him a scholarship anyway, "whenever I had no real offers," he told ESPN in 2022, and in June 2014 the Detroit Tigers [drafted him in the 37th round](https://www.mlb.com/news/patrick-mahomes-went-from-texas-tech-to-super-bowl) too, knowing he was playing football.

He stayed at quarterback, he told the same podcast, for a reason that had nothing to do with ceilings. "More than anything I just said, I didn't want to not be there playing with all my buddies when they were in football season." Brittany Matthews, his high-school girlfriend, remembers the moment Whitehouse High's quarterback graduated. "Patrick said, 'I think I'm going to give it a shot.' I was like, 'Wait, what?'"

That is the pattern at fifteen and at thirty. Given a choice between the optimal path and the people he trusts, he chooses the people, then outworks the path. He married Brittany; they have three children, born in 2021, 2022 and 2025, and he has said on CBS that "if I didn't have Brittany I wouldn't be in the position I am now." On draft night, after the phone finally rang, the family went to "the hole-in-the-wall bar my dad's been going to forever" and played darts.

## The Coach Patrick Mahomes Said He Needed

Sixes run on trust, and Mahomes has organized his career around a short list of people who earned it.

Andy Reid told Alex Smith one thing about the rookie: "Don't worry about that guy. You just go have your best year." Smith let Mahomes into his world anyway. "I learned how you become a pro," he told the Kelces in 2022.

Then there is the sentence he retold on the same episode. January 2019, overtime, AFC Championship. The Patriots have just ended his MVP season. Tom Brady finds him on the way to the car. "He just said, 'Hey man, I want to let you know you're doing it the right way.' Just him saying that validated everything that I did that year."

Notice the word. Validated. A 50-touchdown season, an MVP, a home playoff crowd, and the thing that made it real was an older quarterback confirming the method. "The GOAT is literally saying you're doing it the right way."

The flip side of that trust is a specific dread. As a rookie he overslept a 15-minute walkthrough after a night out, eight alarms on a dead phone. "There is no chance I'm walking through the defense late to get to the offense," he said on *New Heights*, so he sat in the locker room instead, "just sitting there shaking my head." Upstairs: "I get like the max fine... feel like an idiot." Years later, when State Farm wanted Reid in a commercial, they asked Mahomes to make the ask. He told the Kelces his answer: "A hundred percent not. I would not ask him."

Trust runs downhill from him too. Trent McDuffie, after four seasons as his cornerback, described it on *Bussin' With The Boys* in August 2026: what he loved was watching Mahomes "show up at a certain time, do a certain thing, like, every day."

## Why Patrick Mahomes Wears the Safest Helmet in the NFL

The quote at the top of this page has a second half. "I might get a few jokes at me from the teammates," he told CBS. "I'm like, man, I'm trying to protect myself for the long term."

Put that next to the other things he does with his body. "I'm the guy where when I run I try to never take a hit, so I always go down," he said on *New Heights*. When a pass rusher arrives a half-second late, Mahomes talks to him. "I'm a big compliment guy. They get close to me, 'Hey, great rush, dude.' I'm not trying to make these guys mad. Why would I want them to hit me harder?" In the [Netflix series *Quarterback*](https://fox4kc.com/sports/chiefs/netflixs-quarterback-more-patrick-mahomes-moments-from-episodes-2-4/) he said the quiet part: "They have the decision, if it's consciously or subconsciously, to finish those hits. I feel like if those guys like me, they won't finish as hard."

The charm stops at his own sideline. Tyreek Hill told [*The Pivot* in 2024](https://www.si.com/nfl/2024/03/06/tyreek-hill-gained-respect-patrick-mahomes-after-chiefs-qb-berated-him-drops) about the day Mahomes called the whole offense up and went receiver by receiver. "Tyreek, you think you're so f-ing fast, but you can't catch the f-ing ball. You want to be the best receiver in the game?" Hill's response: "It's go time now." Pass rushers get compliments. The people inside the structure get held to it.

Both halves are one man running a threat assessment on every snap. Read from the other sideline, the charm half is also the most criticized thing about him.

The case against Mahomes is that he games the whistle. In the [January 2025 playoff game against Houston](https://www.foxnews.com/sports/patrick-mahomes-speaks-out-flopping-allegations-referee-favoritism-after-mass-fan-outrage), Troy Aikman watched him slow down near the sideline and said it on air: "He's trying to draw the penalty. You see that rather than run out of bounds, he slows down. That's been the frustration, and I get it." Ravens linebacker Roquan Smith had called it in Week 1: "He flopped. Yeah, he flopped." Four days after the Houston game, on Kansas City radio, Mahomes gave up the sideline play: "I probably shouldn't have done that."

The critics are describing something real. The sideline slow-down was bait for a flag, and he has said so. The fall that drew the roughing call is a different play, and he defended it [on the same radio hit](https://sports.yahoo.com/patrick-mahomes-dismisses-talk-chiefs-222819925.html): "I was just trying to get out of the way of getting smoked by the defensive lineman running in... that's the smart way to stay in the football game." Set the bait aside and what is left, the helmet, the early slide and the "great rush, dude," is one behavior. A man whose method is to remove surprise from his life has found the one place surprise still lives, a 280-pound lineman arriving from the blind side, and he is using every tool he has to make that moment smaller. Including tools that make him look soft. He would rather be mocked than unprotected. Most Sixes make some version of that trade. His is just televised.

And when the structure he leans on fails him, the calm goes with it. On [December 10, 2023](https://www.nfl.com/news/chiefs-qb-patrick-mahomes-hc-andy-reid-take-issue-with-offensive-offsides-call-i), at Buffalo, an offside flag on Kadarius Toney erased a Kelce lateral that would have put Kansas City ahead late. Mahomes slammed his helmet, had to be held back on the sideline, and found Bills quarterback Josh Allen afterward to tell him it was the "wildest" call he had ever seen, with an adjective in the middle. Two days later, as ESPN reported: "I was still hot and emotional, but you can't do that, man." Then the line that explains it: "Can't be that way towards officials, or really anybody in life."

<p class="inner-thought">The rules are supposed to hold. That is the deal. I do the film, I do the third-down cut-up, I do the red zone and the goal line until it is perfect, and the moment is never bigger than what it is. So what am I supposed to do with a flag that just erased the best play I have ever seen?</p>

## What Patrick Mahomes Said About His Father's Arrest

Not every surprise arrives from the blind side. Eight days before Super Bowl LVIII, Pat Mahomes Sr. was [arrested in Tyler, Texas](https://www.cbssports.com/nfl/news/patrick-mahomes-sr-father-of-chiefs-qb-arrested-on-suspicion-of-dwi-in-texas-ahead-of-2024-super-bowl/), for driving while intoxicated. At Opening Night in Las Vegas his son was asked about it and gave an answer with the door shut. "I don't really want to get into it too much, but he's doing good for whatever the situation is. It's a family matter, so I'll keep it to the family."

Then nothing. Eighteen months of nothing. When he finally spoke about it, he did it inside [*The Kingdom*](https://www.foxnews.com/sports/patrick-mahomes-reveals-sobering-reality-fathers-dwi-arrest-just-before-chiefs-2024-super-bowl-victory), with his father sitting for the same cameras. "It became a story, and so I had to answer questions about it," he said. "I think just knowing that it hurt me woke him up to know that, like, you can't keep doing the same things."

Pat Sr. told his side, as [People](https://ca.news.yahoo.com/patrick-mahomes-speaks-fathers-2024-020000575.html) transcribed it. "For him to have to answer questions about me was probably the most embarrassing thing I've ever been through in my life. I called him right after and I told him, I said, 'I'm sorry that it's taking away from your time and taking away from your focus.'" He said he made a vow about drinking.

The boy who copied his father's tongue and his father's tee work and his father's hole-in-the-wall bar had become the reference point his father calibrated against. And the son's pain was disclosed in a room he chose. He did not hide it. He waited until he trusted the structure.

Pat Sr. has one more line, delivered in the tunnel after Super Bowl LVII, on camera for Netflix's *Quarterback*, still holding him: "I've never seen anything like you. You're different."

## The First Time Patrick Mahomes's Body Said No

The season was failing before the knee did. In February 2025 the Eagles beat Kansas City 40-22 in Super Bowl LIX, one win short of a three-peat, and Mahomes said afterward, as KCTV5 reported, that the losses "hurt probably more than the wins feel good." [Sports Illustrated](https://www.si.com/nfl/patrick-mahomes-honest-heartbreaking-end-chiefs-season) reporters watched him in the locker room that night: facing the wall, a slammed foot locker, then a walk past the media, shoes in hand, to Reid's office.

In the fall of 2025 the offense never came back. Behind a line that could not hold and a receiving corps that had thinned, the Chiefs went 1-9 in games decided by one score. His own accounting, to reporters on July 24, 2026, per Yahoo Sports: "I feel like I wasn't playing my best football at the end of last year before I got hurt," and, more specifically, "I think I ran a little bit too much last season." The man who never takes a hit was taking them.

For eight seasons and most of a ninth, the body had held anyway. He dislocated a kneecap in Denver in 2019, saw it "in the side of my calf," as he told *New Heights*, and asked the trainer whether he was going back in.

Then, on [December 14, 2025](https://www.espn.com/nfl/story/_/id/47306436/patrick-mahomes-exits-knee-injury-chiefs-playoff-hopes-end), with 1:53 left and the Chargers up 16-13, he rolled right, planted his left foot to throw the ball away, and the knee buckled. Torn ACL and LCL. The Chiefs were eliminated that night. "I asked one of the doctors if I could get a brace and just finish the game," he said in January 2026, per Sports Illustrated. "But they wouldn't let me."

The next morning, per cornerback Trent McDuffie: "I saw him the next day in the training room, like, working out. Knee busted, and he's in there working out." Surgery in Dallas with Dr. Dan Cooper, then the part of rehab built for him. "The doctor kind of gives you goals to get to," he said at the same January availability, per Chiefs Wire, "and I just try to maximize those, and they hold me back, because I always want to go a little bit further." Reid, at the league meetings in March 2026, put the number at seven hours a day and added: "I would never bet against him."

The clearance came in late July 2026, the way he needed it to. Cooper put him through a test designed to exhaust him and then make him cut and jump on the tired leg, a doctor who "wasn't going to take it easy on me," as Mahomes told reporters on August 8, 2026. "Once he cleared me, it wasn't like he was just going to clear me to clear me. And so I knew that I could go out there and be myself."

Read that sentence twice. The most gifted quarterback of his generation did not believe in his own knee until a doctor he trusted tried to break it and failed.

At the same August 8, 2026 availability he was already a problem for his own caution. "I'm trying to hold myself back... all of a sudden I'm running and cutting back and trying to pitch the ball... I'm not going to lose who I am. But I'm going to try to keep myself smart." He sat out the first two August preseason games on Reid's orders. "I really wanted to play today," he told [KSHB](https://www.kshb.com/sports/football/chiefs/they-would-not-let-me-play-chiefs-qb-patrick-mahomes-eager-to-play-again) after the second one, "but they would not let me."

The interview turned to the Monday night opener on September 14, 2026, against the Broncos, the team that took the division while he sat on a table. Would he take the first snap? "It's up to Coach Reid," he told KSHB on August 22, 2026, "and I have to trust that he's going to do right by me."
