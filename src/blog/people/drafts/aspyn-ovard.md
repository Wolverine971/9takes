On April 1, 2024, Aspyn Ovard posted a birth announcement for her third daughter. That same day she filed for divorce from her husband of eight years, Parker Ferris, in Salt Lake City. By the next day the filing was national entertainment news. ([E! News via NBC 7, April 2, 2024](https://www.nbcsandiego.com/entertainment/entertainment-news/youtuber-aspyn-ovard-files-for-divorce-from-parker-ferris-same-day-she-announces-birth-of-baby-no-3/3478687/))

She had not planned to say a word. "My initial plan was to just phase that man out," she told two longtime friends on the _Wild 'Til 9_ podcast in March 2025. "I was not going to talk about anything." ([Wild 'Til 9, March 25, 2025](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=XIy4gkapHG8))

The filing was a public court record, reported before she said anything. That took the plan away. Her answer, in the same conversation:

> "Sorry but if you take away my privacy I'm going to take it and run with it."

Running with it meant a string of joking, provocative TikToks about the divorce. In May 2025 she described the result to Isaac Rochell on his podcast. Once she decided to "give the people what they want cuz again, I'm not getting my privacy anyways," she said, "it brought me essentially more followers, more money, more control, more opportunity." ([Isaac Rochell, May 22, 2025](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=PeOq04oCRw4))

Most people would hear the money in that list. Look at the third item.

In the next breath she said what it all "boils down to" for her: "I can provide for my children with this." Control and provision sit in the same answer.

She won't even use the word most outlets used for what happened. "I hate the word leaked," she told Rochell, because "it just doesn't align with like how I think of myself."

Ovard has spent more than half her life sharing it with strangers, and the line she keeps drawing is about who decides what gets shared. Six months after the filing, she put it in one sentence on Avery Woods' podcast (Woods later joined the Hulu cast): "that's the thing about me though is I will have whatever level of privacy I want." ([Cheers! with Avery, October 14, 2024](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=SDawFriTZhc))

## Who Aspyn Ovard is

Ovard was born in 1996 and grew up in St. George, Utah, the oldest of four kids. She started posting on YouTube at 14, as a beauty creator. ([Girlscamp, March 19, 2025](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Ny1jhdAR_P0)) By December 2014 her channel had passed a million subscribers. ([Tubefilter, December 18, 2014](https://www.tubefilter.com/2014/12/18/aspyn-ovard-youtube-millionaires/))

St. George is heavily Mormon, and she isn't. She was baptized at nine and attended briefly, but of high school she says, "I obviously was not Mormon." Her father, she says, "had drug addiction for most of my life, alcoholism," and they had "a very complicated very distant relationship." He died in June 2024. ([Girlscamp](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Ny1jhdAR_P0))

She married Ferris, her high school boyfriend, in October 2015, when she was 19. ([E! News, November 12, 2026](https://www.eonline.com/news/1437669/mormon-wives-aspyn-ovards-daughter-diagnosed-with-terminal-cancer)) For most of the next decade they vlogged their family life together. In July 2024 she moved with the kids to California, with "no desire to go back to Salt Lake." ([People via AOL, August 22, 2024](https://www.aol.com/lifestyle/youtuber-aspyn-ovard-speaks-divorce-202347776.html))

After the divorce she started dating women. "I just prefer women," she told viewers in May 2025. ([sexuality q&a, May 23, 2025](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=zxs43h1DnJc)) By March 2026 she was describing her relationship with Bri Davis and Bri's husband, Billy, as "three people instead of two." ([People via AOL, March 10, 2026](https://www.aol.com/articles/aspyn-ovard-addresses-dating-woman-155310759.html))

As of November 15, 2026, she is a cast member of Hulu's _The Secret Lives of Mormon Wives: Orange County_, which premiered on November 12, 2026, alongside castmates including [Bobbi Althoff](/personality-analysis/bobbi-althoff). ([SiriusXM, November 12, 2026](https://www.siriusxm.com/blog/secret-lives-of-mormon-wives-oc-aspyn-ovard-throuple)) She co-parents with Ferris. In the premiere she disclosed that one of their daughters has a terminal cancer diagnosis, which she had kept private for more than three years. ([E! News, November 12, 2026](https://www.eonline.com/news/1437669/mormon-wives-aspyn-ovards-daughter-diagnosed-with-terminal-cancer))

The pattern started long before the show.

## What is Aspyn Ovard's personality type?

Aspyn Ovard's personality type is Enneagram Type 8, the Challenger. Eights sit in the body triad, where the core emotion is anger, and they organize their lives around not being controlled. Her record shows the pattern: she refuses to be talked out of decisions, armors up when hurt, keeps her grudges and takes charge of her own story.

## Anger, and what she does with it

[Type 8](/enneagram-corner/enneagram-type-8) belongs to the body triad, whose core emotion is anger. The head types run on fear and the heart types on shame. Within the body triad, a Nine numbs the anger and a One turns it into rules. An Eight lets it out and uses it to hold ground.

A heart type watches how she comes across, and a head type scans for the threat. Ovard does neither with criticism. She told Josie Van Dyke about a stranger who once commented that she didn't look good with curly hair. Her reply: "Yeah, I do." Then: "Unless you know me personally, I probably just don't really care about your opinion of me." ([Weekly Trash, September 17, 2024](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=bkDx_CMy5tM))

She doesn't hide the anger either. Six months after filing, she told Woods, "I just look back and I'm like so angry at this point." Asked if she was in her angry phase, she said, "I definitely am for sure." She called it "where I'm at like in the healing process," with "so many things to grieve." ([Cheers! with Avery](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=SDawFriTZhc))

Anger in a year like that says little on its own. What marks her is where she points it. In an October 22, 2024, TikTok she said she would "never forgive" friends who knew about a betrayal in her marriage and stayed quiet. (Eleven days earlier she had said she didn't think she'd "ever been cheated on to my knowledge.") She stated her rule for what they should have done: "You need to tell her, and if you don't, I will." ([People via AOL, November 4, 2024](https://www.aol.com/going-aspyn-ovards-divorce-parker-174345963.html))

Two months later, in a divorce Q&A on her channel, a viewer asked whether it was hard to forgive Ferris enough to co-parent:

> "I'm not a person who forgives. I think it's one of my toxic traits."

She added that she does it anyway because her kids come first. Asked whether she had tried talking to Ferris before filing, she said, "You guys, you know me. Obviously, a thousand million freaking times. I'm not someone who takes stuff lying down." ([divorce q&a, December 14, 2024](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=vM0C_UqU6n0))

## The Eight pattern, step by step

Eights run a familiar sequence. They refuse to be controlled. When hurt, they armor up and hide the soft part. Then they take the ground back with force, and the people inside the wall get protected and provided for. Each step shows up in something Ovard did.

**She refuses to be steered.** Marrying young was normal where she grew up, so the wedding at 19 proves little. What she says about it tells more. Looking back, she figures her mother asked whether she should wait. "I can't be talked out of something either," she told Van Dyke. "I just was not going to hear that from anybody. I'm doing what I want." ([Weekly Trash](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=bkDx_CMy5tM))

The clearest act came after the filing. In July 2024 she moved her daughters to California, the youngest about three months old. "I'm just such a doer in that way," she said on _Wild 'Til 9_. "I want to do this. I'm going to do this." And she did.

This is not new. In a December 2014 interview, at 18, she said, "I'm never afraid or ashamed to share what I believe or what I think about things." She added: "I always just walk to the beat of my own drum and don't worry about what other people are doing or saying about me." ([Tubefilter](https://www.tubefilter.com/2014/12/18/aspyn-ovard-youtube-millionaires/)) That was a decade before the divorce, which helps separate temperament from circumstance.

**She armors up.** Ovard told her _Wild 'Til 9_ friends that she has "always had such thick skin like nothing really gets to me." Then she described what the public filing did to that skin:

> "I had a kind of a hard exterior in that way where I was like you can't get to me and then it just like pushed me even farther and made me even harder in that way."

"There's definitely like a softer side of me that I don't show quite as much," she said in her divorce Q&A, "because I do kind of always have a little bit of an attitude."

Her biggest act of armor was a silence. For more than three years she kept her daughter's diagnosis off her channels and posted "as normally as I could," she told People. ([Grazia, November 12, 2026](https://graziadaily.co.uk/life/tv-and-film/mormon-wives-aspyn-ovard/))

She still keeps the soft side off her own camera. Asked in May 2026 why she shared things on the Hulu show that she never shared on YouTube, she said, "I don't like to like set my phone up and like or my camera and like film myself crying." With a crew there, "I'm not having to like hold myself together when I'm like filming myself." ([q&a, May 27, 2026](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=BCgRYRwIsus))

The armor has gaps, and she names them. Asked by Rochell when she last felt lost, she said, "I feel like literally all the time." She has talked about depression on podcasts and on her channel, and credits Bri and Billy with "lifting me up, basically carrying me here." She will say she is struggling. She won't perform it alone for her own camera.

Letting people in takes her a long time. In October 2026 she said that even with a friend who had always been kind to her, it took a while to drop her guard, because "I've just been burned by so many people." ([unedited personal q&a, October 18, 2026](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=4RJF4YKmwcA))

**She takes the ground back.** That is the opening story: months of silence, then followers, income and, in her word, control.

She also refuses to let a wrong go unanswered. On Becca Moore's podcast in May 2025 she described the bind of staying quiet about her marriage: "sometimes I feel like if I'm stifling myself then I'm like letting someone get away with bad behavior." ([For the Girls, May 13, 2025](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=LMEY-KK6G3k))

Silence, for her, can feel like losing.

It is also why she put her relationship with Bri and Billy on the show: "I just didn't want other people talking about something that like I wasn't talking about." ([q&a, May 27, 2026](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=BCgRYRwIsus))

**She protects her people.** Ovard told Rochell she will "always take care of my people," and that includes her ex-husband:

> "When it comes to my family, like I will always do everything that I can to protect them, provide for them, just make sure everyone is taken care of. And of course, that includes him, even if we are divorced."

On the Hulu show she told Ferris in an argument, "You know that I will always take care of you because I always have!" Then: "When everything goes to s--t and things get really hard, I don't want you to have to do anything." ([E! News, November 12, 2026](https://www.eonline.com/news/1437669/mormon-wives-aspyn-ovards-daughter-diagnosed-with-terminal-cancer))

That is care delivered as provision and protection, with force behind it, not care offered in the hope of being loved back. In her divorce Q&A she said she had never "experienced anything other than being the one who makes the money." Told by Rochell that she sounded selfless, she said, "I feel like I'm a little bit selfish actually," because "you can't pour from an empty cup." That pushes against a [Type 2](/enneagram-corner/enneagram-type-2) reading of her caretaking.

## Why not Type 7?

The strongest rival is [Type 7](/enneagram-corner/enneagram-type-7), and the case for it is good. Assertive Sevens and Eights are easy to confuse, and Ovard says a lot that sounds like a Seven.

She copes the Seven way. Asked by Rochell about a provocative divorce TikTok of her straddling Ferris, she said, "I think I cope with humor through all things hard," that "nothing is a big deal to me," and that "I'm just here to have a good time until I die." On _Wild 'Til 9_ she said she could have posted a simple statement after the filing, "but like this was so much more fun."

Asked for her biggest takeaway from the divorce, she said "having the ability to like start over and have a fresh start was like really what got me through." Her everyday method, on _Girlscamp_: "I have like one chance on this Earth I just want to have a good time."

Her fear of being trapped is also specific. It is about the exit. Explaining in December 2024 why she won't legally marry again, she said, "I would be terrified to be so trapped again," meaning stuck in "the legal process of divorce." On _For the Girls_ she said, "I have to be able to run away which is like toxic," and that a traditional partner "feels like suffocating to me." That is the Seven's core fear, of being trapped in pain or limitation. It fits a Seven at least as well as an Eight.

What separates them is what she does when someone wrongs her. A Seven tends to move away from the pain and toward the next good thing. Ovard moves toward the person. She talked to Ferris, by her count, a thousand million times before she filed. She told friends who stayed silent what they owed her.

Her humor works the same way. The jokes came straight at the attention, including the posts answering his mother. A Seven often uses a joke to get out of a hard conversation. She uses one to stay in it.

The grudge points the same direction, with a limit. Her "so angry" stretch was grief by her own account, and by November 2025 she was talking about "seeing myself heal and grow." ([juicy questions, November 29, 2025](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=UhyofzF8lPg)) What she hasn't walked back is the trait she named in December 2024, that she is "not a person who forgives."

So the Seven material is real, and the humor is clearly hers. But when she is wronged, she turns and faces it.

## Why not Type 3?

A [Type 3](/enneagram-corner/enneagram-type-3) reading starts from the obvious: a career built on an audience since she was 14, a public filing she turned into followers and money, and a reality show. She calls herself "always such a planner and like looking forward to the next big thing."

Her own words keep pointing away from image as the engine. Right after that list of followers and money, she told Rochell, "I don't care about, you know, the attention or the clout or the actual number of the followers." The point, again, was her children. About critics of her relationship, in May 2026: "I don't ever feel like I have to like prove anything to anyone."

These are self-reports, and a Three can disclaim striving while living it. But her record keeps landing on control and provision, not on being seen to win.

## The controversies

**The same-day filing.** Ovard filed for divorce on April 1, 2024, the day she posted the birth announcement for her third daughter. ([E! News via NBC 7](https://www.nbcsandiego.com/entertainment/entertainment-news/youtuber-aspyn-ovard-files-for-divorce-from-parker-ferris-same-day-she-announces-birth-of-baby-no-3/3478687/)) The timing set off months of fan theories. ([Grazia, November 12, 2026](https://graziadaily.co.uk/life/tv-and-film/mormon-wives-aspyn-ovard/))

Her account, given on _Cheers!_ in October 2024, is that the baby was a few weeks old by then, that she had held off posting while the baby was in the NICU, and that "I didn't think anybody would see it." Her first public word on the divorce, an August 22, 2024, TikTok, said she would "continue to stay unbothered and not share anything to protect my peace." ([People via AOL](https://www.aol.com/lifestyle/youtuber-aspyn-ovard-speaks-divorce-202347776.html))

**The mother-in-law exchange.** In October 2024, Ferris' mother posted an Instagram story reading "Mama bear is coming out. Stay tuned," with a poll asking, "Would you like to hear another side of the story?" Ovard answered with what Distractify called "a posting binge of irreverent clap-backs," including one of her straddling Ferris with the caption "His mom can't do what I can." ([Distractify, October 15, 2024](https://www.distractify.com/p/aspyn-ovard-mother-in-law))

That is Distractify's description; the original posts were not reviewed for this profile.

To Rochell she explained the straddling video as humor.

This is the Eight pattern at its least flattering: a challenge, then a counterpunch. It explains the reflex. It doesn't excuse aiming it at a family member in public.

**The diagnosis disclosure.** In the November 12, 2026, premiere, Ovard disclosed that one of her daughters has a terminal cancer diagnosis, and that "I've been living like that for over three years." ([E! News](https://www.eonline.com/news/1437669/mormon-wives-aspyn-ovards-daughter-diagnosed-with-terminal-cancer)) On November 10, 2026, two days before the premiere, she wrote on Instagram, "I'm saying goodbye to the internet being the last piece of normalcy that i've had in my life for the last few years." She said she was taking "the rest of the week off," maybe longer, and added, "i hope the world is gentle with my story." ([E! News, November 13, 2026](https://www.eonline.com/news/1438096/aspyn-ovard-taking-social-media-break-after-mormon-wives-oc-debut))

Some viewers argued she held the story for a paycheck. One of the bluntest versions came on November 13, 2026, from the host of _The Dad Challenge Podcast_, who gives only his first name, Josh: "She absolutely knew how and she held on to it till the moment was right so she can make the most money from it." In the same episode he read a listener who had changed their mind after watching: "What I saw on the show wasn't her exploiting her daughter's illness." ([The Dad Challenge Podcast](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=b39jQmTSMmY))

Her stated reasons are different. In May 2026, before the diagnosis was public, she described them as "very heavy parts and pieces of my life that I have not known how to share." Filming them herself felt inauthentic, she said, and it was "something that I knew I would always have to talk about and I didn't know when or how." ([q&a, May 27, 2026](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=BCgRYRwIsus)) Grazia reports she knew the diagnosis couldn't stay secret forever. She told People that "being able to hand my story over and have help in crafting that" took "some weight off me." ([Grazia, November 12, 2026](https://graziadaily.co.uk/life/tv-and-film/mormon-wives-aspyn-ovard/))

As of November 15, 2026, no reporting on her pay for the show was found. This profile says nothing more about her daughter's health.

**Sharing her children online.** For years Ovard's channel was a family vlog. By September 2024 she had stopped showing her daughters, and she told Van Dyke she regretted ever showing them: "I should have known better." She didn't spread the blame: "I was really the one who was sharing probably 90%" of it. ([Weekly Trash](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=bkDx_CMy5tM)) Stopping is a choice many family vloggers have made, so it says little about type on its own. How she owned it, without a "we," is more like her.

## The "10-year secret"

In October 2024 she hinted at a long deception, asking viewers to "imagine if they lied for like 10 years or something?" ([People via AOL](https://www.aol.com/going-aspyn-ovards-divorce-parker-174345963.html)) On the Hulu series she says what it was:

> "My dad struggled with alcoholism, so the only version of him that I knew was like some level of intoxicated. Finding out that Parker had been drinking behind my back for years, it was literally earth shattering."

([Entertainment Tonight, November 14, 2026](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Yr0piSHx_34))

That is her account. She says viewers will also see Ferris talk about it. E! News reports that she found out two months after her father died. She has said he died in June 2024, which puts the discovery around August 2024, after the April filing. E! calls it the secret she says "ultimately contributed to the end of her marriage." ## What the type doesn't explain

Ovard is not a natural room-taker. At 19 she said, "I like to stay in my comfort zone, so acting was something totally different." ([Yahoo, November 4, 2015](https://news.yahoo.com/diy-youtuber-aspyn-ovard-224521814.html)) After high school she moved to LA because "that's what all the YouTubers did," and was back in Utah within a few weeks at most. "But I moved like back to my comfort zone," she told Rochell. She calls herself "very, very shy" around people she doesn't know.

Her force shows up at home, over her own decisions and when she is attacked, not as social dominance. The shyness is a real limit on the read.

There is also an anxious streak. After the divorce she worried about vlogging alone: "how am I going to be interesting enough like by myself." In October 2026 she said, "I always want everything to like be so perfect." She told Van Dyke she cleans and reorganizes when "I feel out of control of my life." She frames even that as control, not correctness.

Much of the anger on this page also comes from 2024 to 2026: the public filing, the divorce and her father's death in June 2024, all while she was privately carrying a child's diagnosis.

Circumstance explains some of it. The 2014 interview shows her independence from outside opinion a decade earlier. It doesn't show the grudges. Every grudge and anger quote here dates from 2024 or later; the nearest earlier evidence is her 2024 memory of early YouTube: "I've always just had very thick skin."

She also reverses herself openly. She said she wouldn't talk about the divorce online, then made it the subject of a Q&A. Asked in May 2026 what she had said she would never do and then did, she listed: "Be with a man, probably reality TV, share a relationship publicly. There's a lot of things."

She makes absolute declarations, and when she changes them, she changes them on her own terms and says so.

## The line she keeps drawing

For more than half her life Ovard has decided what goes in front of her camera. When that choice was taken from her in April 2024, she fought to get it back. What she defends isn't secrecy. She kept her daughter's diagnosis to herself for more than three years, then chose when and where to tell it.

What she defends is the right to decide. She opened her December 2024 divorce Q&A by calling her own reversal funny. On TikTok she had said, "I'm not going to talk about it online," and "now it's my whole personality." Then she set the terms herself:

> "So, we're just going to give the people what they want and we're going to talk about it because I do not care."
