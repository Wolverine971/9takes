In a deposition on October 1, 2025, for [Elon Musk](/personality-analysis/elon-musk)'s lawsuit against OpenAI, a lawyer for OpenAI walked Ilya Sutskever through the memos he had written two years earlier: the case he had put to OpenAI's board for firing its chief executive, [Sam Altman](/personality-analysis/sam-altman), on November 17, 2023, and a second one about its co-founder and president, [Greg Brockman](/personality-analysis/greg-brockman). One line said that "it is my understanding that" Brockman "was essentially fired from Stripe as well." What was the basis for that? "Mira told me." Did you seek to verify it with Greg? "No." Why not? "It didn't occur to me." Mira is [Mira Murati](/personality-analysis/mira-murati), then OpenAI's chief technology officer. The Stripe claim is unverified in any source opened here, and neither Brockman nor Murati has publicly addressed the memos in them. The testimony quoted comes from the 62 pages the parties filed in court, of a transcript of roughly 365. ([deposition, October 1, 2025, pp. 309–310](https://storage.courtlistener.com/recap/gov.uscourts.cand.433688/gov.uscourts.cand.433688.340.1.pdf))

Geoffrey Hinton tells a story about the first time he met him. A young student knocked on his University of Toronto office door and said he was cooking fries over the summer but would rather be working in Hinton's lab. Make an appointment, Hinton said. "How about now." Hinton handed him the Nature paper on backpropagation. A week later the student came back and said he hadn't understood it, and Hinton was disappointed: "it's only the chain rule." "Oh no, no, I understood that," the student said. "I just don't understand why you don't give the gradient to a sensible function optimizer," a question Hinton says "took us quite a few years to think about." ([Geoffrey Hinton, Sana AI Summit, 2024](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=4W5askrV-wY))

The student who understood backpropagation in a week and wanted to know why the gradient was not handed to a better optimizer grew into the man who built the most consequential case of his life from a colleague's screenshots and, asked twice under oath why he had not checked what they said with the people they named, answered both times that it did not occur to him. That gap is the subject of this profile.

Sutskever was born in 1986 in what was then Gorky, in the Soviet Union, grew up in Jerusalem from about age five, and emigrated to Canada at 16. The University of Toronto admitted him out of Grade 11; he stayed through a PhD under Hinton ([U of T Magazine, 2022](https://magazine.utoronto.ca/people/alumni-donors/heard-it-through-the-ai-openai-ilya-sutskever/); [U of T News, 2025](https://www.utoronto.ca/news/ilya-sutskever-leader-ai-and-its-responsible-development-receives-u-t-honorary-degree)). He co-founded OpenAI in 2015 and became its chief scientist, voted to fire Altman, reversed within days, left in May 2024 and founded Safe Superintelligence Inc., its CEO since July 2025 as of October 2026. Musk, who cofounded OpenAI as a nonprofit in 2015 and says he put roughly $38 million into it, left its board in 2018 and sued in 2024, accusing Altman and Brockman of turning it into a for-profit for their own enrichment ([Al Jazeera, May 19, 2026](https://www.aljazeera.com/news/2026/5/19/musk-vs-altman-what-to-know-about-the-openai-verdict)). In 2026 two events put him back in the news. On May 11 he testified at the trial and disclosed an OpenAI stake worth about $7 billion ([Reuters, May 11, 2026](https://wtvbam.com/2026/05/11/former-openai-executive-sutskever-discloses-nearly-7-billion-stake-in-ai-firm/)). And Luca Guadagnino's film _Artificial_, reviewed out of the New York Film Festival on October 5, 2026, has Yura Borisov playing him as "an eccentric, eager, prodigy incapable of lying" ([TheWrap, October 5, 2026](https://thewrap.com/creative-content/movies/artificial-review-new-york-film-festival)). The film is a screenwriter's construction. The record is more specific, and stranger.

## What is Ilya Sutskever's personality type?

Ilya Sutskever most likely fits Enneagram Type 5 at medium confidence. The case rests less on why he fired Sam Altman, a candor motive Type 6 shares, than on how: a colleague's screenshots he did not check with those named, and 770 colleagues he expected to feel nothing. Type 6 is the strongest alternative, Type 1 a credible third.

[Type 5](/enneagram-corner/enneagram-type-5) would rather have the answer than the room. It spends its attention on one problem and counts intrusion as the main cost of living. Sutskever's version is unusually pure about the machine and, on the sworn record, unusually unguarded about people: he accepted one colleague's account of others whole, and did not foresee how the company would react. A [Type 6](/enneagram-corner/enneagram-type-6) reading explains much of the same record through vigilance about who holds power and a need for trusted allies. A [Type 1](/enneagram-corner/enneagram-type-1) reading fits his moral vocabulary and his taste for a single uncompromised goal. The question that separates the Five from the Six is practical: when the threat stopped being abstract, did he read the room or fail to see it?

This is a reading of the public record, with no access to his mind and no clinical judgment. The most damaging material consists of allegations he made against Altman, never adjudicated, and anonymous accounts gathered during a factional fight; both are labeled, and the responses on record sit beside them.

## "How about now"

Hinton's other memories run the same way: "he always thought for himself." Years before the field agreed, "Ilya was always preaching that you just make it bigger and it'll work better, and I always thought that was a bit of a copout." ([Hinton, Sana AI Summit](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=4W5askrV-wY)) The bet paid in 2012, when he, Hinton and Alex Krizhevsky built AlexNet, which MIT Technology Review calls "deep learning's Big Bang moment" ([MIT Technology Review, 2023](https://www.technologyreview.com/2023/10/26/1082398/exclusive-ilya-sutskever-openais-chief-scientist-on-his-hopes-and-fears-for-the-future-of-ai/); [U of T News](https://www.utoronto.ca/news/ilya-sutskever-leader-ai-and-its-responsible-development-receives-u-t-honorary-degree)). Sutskever's version, in October 2023: "I had one very explicit belief, which is: one doesn't bet against deep learning." ([MIT Technology Review](https://www.technologyreview.com/2023/10/26/1082398/exclusive-ilya-sutskever-openais-chief-scientist-on-his-hopes-and-fears-for-the-future-of-ai/))

The question underneath has not changed in twenty years. To his alumni magazine in 2022 he recalled thinking as a teenager "a lot about the nature of existence and consciousness," and feeling "very strongly that learning was this mysterious thing: humans clearly learn, computers clearly don't." ([U of T Magazine](https://magazine.utoronto.ca/people/alumni-donors/heard-it-through-the-ai-openai-ilya-sutskever/)) To [Jensen Huang](/personality-analysis/jensen-huang) in March 2023 his starting point was "curiosity about what is consciousness." ([NVIDIA GTC, March 2023](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=I6qQinoY9WM)) On camera for the documentary _iHuman_, asked what inspires him: "I like thinking about the very fundamentals, the basics." Then: "What is learning? What is experience? What is thinking?" ([Guardian, iHuman footage, 2023](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=9iqn1HhFJ6c)) Three audiences, one question. Origin stories get polished. What these establish is what he says drew him, held steady across a career.

## "Even more real, perhaps, than you are"

Two writers who sat with him a year apart saw the same person. The alumni magazine in 2022 called him "a fairly private, work-oriented man" and noticed that when he spoke about theory, "his eyes go over your head, as he moves into a private space where the ideas are real to him," adding: "even more real, perhaps, than you are." ([U of T Magazine](https://magazine.utoronto.ca/people/alumni-donors/heard-it-through-the-ai-openai-ilya-sutskever/)) MIT Technology Review in 2023 found him "deliberate and methodical when he talks," and noted: "He does not seem interested in talking about himself." His own account: "I lead a very simple life," he said. "I go to work; then I go home. I don't do much else." ([MIT Technology Review](https://www.technologyreview.com/2023/10/26/1082398/exclusive-ilya-sutskever-openais-chief-scientist-on-his-hopes-and-fears-for-the-future-of-ai/))

Both profiles were written while he was OpenAI's chief scientist, and plenty of researchers describe their lives this way whatever their temperament.

## Three weeks after "Open"

He joined OpenAI after a 2015 dinner with Altman, Musk and Brockman. "We just spoke and we vibed and we continued the conversation. Two days after the dinner I emailed Sam to say, I'd like to be involved if you're interested." ([U of T Magazine](https://magazine.utoronto.ca/people/alumni-donors/heard-it-through-the-ai-openai-ilya-sutskever/)) He turned down a $6 million a year offer from Google to do it, and Brockman later said the two of them were "joined at the hip." ([Wired, May 12, 2026](https://web.archive.org/web/2026id_/https://dnyuz.com/2026/05/12/ilya-sutskever-stands-by-his-role-in-sam-altmans-openai-ouster-i-didnt-want-it-to-be-destroyed/))

On January 2, 2016, three weeks after the founding, he wrote to Musk, Altman and Brockman: "As we get closer to building AI, it will make sense to start being less open. The Open in openAI means that everyone should benefit from the fruits of AI after its built, but it's totally OK to not share the science." Musk replied: "Yup." ([email exhibits, LessWrong](https://www.lesswrong.com/posts/5jjk4CDnj9tA7ugxr/openai-email-archives-from-musk-v-altman-and-openai-blog)) OpenAI published the exchange in 2024 to defend itself against Musk. It still records an instinct, nine years before his sealed lab: once knowledge becomes powerful, hold it.

The second email is harder for the Type 5 reading, and it belongs here. On September 20, 2017, during the fight over whether Musk would control a for-profit OpenAI, an email signed "Greg & Ilya" went from Sutskever's account to Musk and Altman. It opened by admitting that the two had withheld their concerns: "we were afraid of harming the relationship, having you think less of us, or losing you as partners." It told Altman: "we haven't been able to fully trust your judgements throughout this process, because we don't understand your cost function," and "We don't understand why the CEO title is so important to you. Your stated reasons have changed." ([email exhibits](https://www.lesswrong.com/posts/5jjk4CDnj9tA7ugxr/openai-email-archives-from-musk-v-altman-and-openai-blog)) Two authors share these sentences, so none can be assigned to Sutskever alone. But the fear of losing partners and the distrust of a leader's shifting reasons are the language of a [Type 6](/enneagram-corner/enneagram-type-6), and six years later the same two concerns, control and candor, drove the firing.

His picture of the danger was already fixed on power rather than malice. On camera between 2016 and 2019: "I think AI has the potential to create infinitely stable dictatorships." ([The Guardian, iHuman footage](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=9iqn1HhFJ6c)) The frame survived the decade. In November 2025: "The whole problem is the power. The whole problem is the power." ([Dwarkesh Podcast, November 25, 2025](https://www.dwarkesh.com/p/ilya-sutskever-2))

## Feel the AGI

Here is the behavior the type does not neatly explain. Two days after the firing, The Atlantic reported that Sutskever "began to behave like a spiritual leader, three employees who worked with him told us." His refrain was "feel the AGI": artificial general intelligence, AI that can do most of what people can. At OpenAI's 2022 holiday party he "led employees in a chant: 'Feel the AGI! Feel the AGI!'" At a 2023 leadership offsite, according to two people, he commissioned a wooden effigy representing an "unaligned" AI and "set it on fire." According to Hinton, the magazine reported, the rapid progress of OpenAI's models had made Sutskever more confident that AGI would arrive soon and more focused on preventing its dangers. In July 2023 OpenAI put him in charge, with Jan Leike, of a new "superalignment" research team backed by "20 percent of the company's existing computer chips." Sutskever did not respond to a request for comment. ([The Atlantic, November 19, 2023](https://web.archive.org/web/2023id_/https://www.theatlantic.com/technology/archive/2023/11/sam-altman-open-ai-chatgpt-chaos/676050/))

Fives rarely lead chants. The employee sources were anonymous and speaking during a factional fight, and ritual in a lab whose product is belief may be management craft rather than temperament. He has never addressed the chant, the party or the effigy himself, but something he said later offers one way to read it. He told Dwarkesh Patel in 2025 that "in practice, it's very hard to feel the AGI. It's very hard to feel the AGI. We can talk about it, but imagine having a conversation about how it is like to be old when you're old and frail." ([Dwarkesh Podcast](https://www.dwarkesh.com/p/ilya-sutskever-2)) Read that way, the man leading the chant was trying to drag an abstraction into feeling, starting with himself.

He has never been cold about the machine. Two weeks before the board vote, asked for the most surprising thing in his career: "the fact that when I speak to it, I feel understood." ([No Priors, November 2, 2023](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Ft0gTO2K85A)) It was the people in the room he did not model.

## A year of considering

What that looked like in practice is in his sworn account of November 2023, the most revealing part of the record and a partial one: the litigants chose the public excerpt, OpenAI's lawyer objected throughout, and the memos are not public. He had been considering Altman's removal, he testified, for "At least a year." Planning? "'Planning' is the wrong word because it didn't seem feasible." At trial he said he had discussed removing Altman with Murati after the two had discussed his behavior "for a long time," and under oath he agreed that he had been waiting for board dynamics that would allow it: "That the majority of the board is not obviously friendly with Sam." ([deposition, pp. 128–131, 324](https://storage.courtlistener.com/recap/gov.uscourts.cand.433688/gov.uscourts.cand.433688.340.1.pdf); [Reuters](https://wtvbam.com/2026/05/11/former-openai-executive-sutskever-discloses-nearly-7-billion-stake-in-ai-firm/))

The memo came at the directors' request. He had discussed it with OpenAI's three independent directors, Adam D'Angelo, Helen Toner and Tasha McCauley, and "either Adam or the three of them together" asked him to collect supporting screenshots: screenshots of messages, "most or all" of which came from Murati. "And I was pretty careful," he told Musk's lawyer; the point was "to paint a picture from a large number of small pieces of evidence or items." Its first page read: "Sam exhibits a consistent pattern of lying, undermining his execs, and pitting his execs against one another." He sent it to the three independent directors through a disappearing link and not to Altman, "Because I felt that, had he become aware of these discussions, he would just find a way to make them disappear." ([deposition](https://storage.courtlistener.com/recap/gov.uscourts.cand.433688/gov.uscourts.cand.433688.340.1.pdf))

OpenAI's lawyer then tested what stood behind the screenshots. "I fully believed the information that Mira was giving me." A section headed "Subtle Retaliation in Response to Mira's Feedback" named people who had been in the meetings it described; why not speak to any of these individuals who are named? "It didn't occur to me." On one allegation: "In hindsight, I realize that I didn't know it. But back then, I thought I knew it. But I knew it through secondhand knowledge." Asked whether he had since learned facts that changed his view of it, he answered: "No." Part of what had changed, he said, was method: "I've learned the critical importance of firsthand knowledge for matters like this." ([deposition, pp. 309–318](https://storage.courtlistener.com/recap/gov.uscourts.cand.433688/gov.uscourts.cand.433688.340.1.pdf))

These are allegations. Altman was reinstated within days and still led OpenAI as of October 2026, and the memo's claims were never tested in court: Musk's case was dismissed on May 18, 2026 as time-barred, with Musk saying he would appeal ([Al Jazeera, May 19, 2026](https://www.aljazeera.com/news/2026/5/19/musk-vs-altman-what-to-know-about-the-openai-verdict)). OpenAI's board commissioned an outside review by the law firm WilmerHale and on March 8, 2024 published a summary: the firing was a "consequence of a breakdown in the relationship and loss of trust," not of concerns about safety, finances or statements to investors, and, in TechCrunch's account of the firm's opinion, the prior board had acted within its rights but Altman's conduct had not required his removal ([TechCrunch, March 8, 2024](https://techcrunch.com/2024/03/08/openai-announces-new-board-members-reinstates-sam-altman/)). On the day Sutskever testified in 2026, OpenAI's chairman Bret Taylor said of Altman, "He's been forthright with me and the other board members," and Microsoft's chief executive Satya Nadella said he "never got clarity" about the lack of candor behind the firing. Sutskever, as of that testimony, had not retreated: an "environment where executives don't have the correct information" is not "conducive to reach any grand goal." ([Wired](https://web.archive.org/web/2026id_/https://dnyuz.com/2026/05/12/ilya-sutskever-stands-by-his-role-in-sam-altmans-openai-ouster-i-didnt-want-it-to-be-destroyed/))

The weekend is where the record gets strange. It was Sutskever who told Altman he was out ([Fortune, November 25, 2023](https://fortune.com/2023/11/25/elon-musk-what-scared-ilya-sutskever-openai-sam-altman-return/)). OpenAI's lawyer read him a reporter's account that he "had expected the employees of OpenAI to cheer." "I had not expected them to cheer, but I have not expected them to feel strongly either way." He was not cut off from the board: that weekend, by his recollection, he sat on a board call with Toner and the other directors, then on a call with the leadership of Anthropic, the rival lab whose chief executive, [Dario Amodei](/personality-analysis/dario-amodei), had spent about five years at OpenAI, about a merger in which Anthropic would take over. He was "very unhappy about it." Why? "Because I really did not want OpenAI to merge with Anthropic." Why not? "I just didn't want to." ([deposition, pp. 319–326](https://storage.courtlistener.com/recap/gov.uscourts.cand.433688/gov.uscourts.cand.433688.340.1.pdf)) But he told the court he "avoided the internet for most of the weekend," Reuters reported, and was not initially aware that Microsoft, which funded OpenAI and supplied its computing, proposed to absorb Altman, Brockman and other employees into a new subsidiary ([Reuters](https://wtvbam.com/2026/05/11/former-openai-executive-sutskever-discloses-nearly-7-billion-stake-in-ai-firm/); [Wired](https://web.archive.org/web/2026id_/https://dnyuz.com/2026/05/12/ilya-sutskever-stands-by-his-role-in-sam-altmans-openai-ouster-i-didnt-want-it-to-be-destroyed/)).

By Monday, November 20, the staff had decided otherwise. An open letter told the board to resign and reinstate Altman and Brockman, or the signers "may choose to resign from OpenAI and join the newly announced Microsoft subsidiary"; by 5:10 pm Eastern that day, 738 of OpenAI's roughly 770 employees had signed, and the signers included Sutskever, a member of the board it was addressed to ([Wired, November 20, 2023](https://www.wired.com/story/openai-staff-walk-protest-sam-altman/)). Shortly before the letter went out he posted: "I deeply regret my participation in the board's actions. I never intended to harm OpenAI. I love everything we've built together and I will do everything I can to reunite the company." ([post on X, November 20, 2023](https://techstartups.com/2023/11/20/i-deeply-regret-my-participation-in-the-boards-actions-chatgpt-4-creator-ilya-sutskever-says/)) Altman returned, and on Wednesday, November 22, Sutskever posted: "There exists no sentence in any language that conveys how happy I am." ([Fortune](https://fortune.com/2023/11/25/elon-musk-what-scared-ilya-sutskever-openai-sam-altman-return/)) At trial in 2026 he described signing the letter as a last resort to keep the company from being destroyed, in Forbes México's account ([Forbes México, May 12, 2026](https://forbes.com.mx/?p=1399426)), and gave two reasons for the reversal. One was attachment: "I felt a great deal of ownership of OpenAI," he said. "I felt like I put my life into it, and I simply cared for it, and I didn't want it to be destroyed." The other was process: he faulted his board colleagues for rushing, for inexperience, and for accepting "legal advice that wasn't very good." ([Wired](https://web.archive.org/web/2026id_/https://dnyuz.com/2026/05/12/ilya-sutskever-stands-by-his-role-in-sam-altmans-openai-ouster-i-didnt-want-it-to-be-destroyed/))

## If he is a Six

The rival case deserves its strongest form. A [Type 6](/enneagram-corner/enneagram-type-6) is organized around security: scanning for threat, testing authority, binding to trusted people. Read that way, the 2017 email is the key document, with its fear of "losing you as partners" and its inability to trust a leader whose "stated reasons have changed." The worry that runs from "AGI dictatorship" in 2017 through "infinitely stable dictatorships" to "The whole problem is the power" is a Six's lifelong question: who holds it? A year of discussing a distrusted leader with one trusted ally, patience for a friendlier board majority, a strike followed within three days by retreat, regret and a signature on the staff letter against the board he sat on, rituals that make colleagues share a belief and a fear, and his 2025 prediction about AI companies once the AI "will start to feel powerful" all fit: "They'll become much more paranoid." ([Dwarkesh Podcast](https://www.dwarkesh.com/p/ilya-sutskever-2))

Type 1 has a case too. Musk, by then Altman's adversary and a competitor, told the New York Times DealBook summit in late 2023: "I think Ilya actually has a strong moral compass." He added: "He really sweats it over questions of what is right." ([Fortune, December 9, 2023](https://fortune.com/2023/12/09/elon-musk-says-openai-ilya-sutskever-should-join-xai-or-tesla/)) The memo is a moral indictment, the burned effigy of an "unaligned" AI is a purification rite, his lab is built around "one goal and one product," and his research taste is a doctrine of correctness: "Ugliness, there's no room for ugliness. It's beauty, simplicity, elegance, correct inspiration from the brain." ([Dwarkesh Podcast](https://www.dwarkesh.com/p/ilya-sutskever-2))

## What he did when it was real

The test is what a person does when the threat stops being abstract. A Six under real danger does not necessarily go wide. Sixes often fix on one trusted channel, act in secret out of suspicion, freeze when the danger is close, and spend a long time in ambivalence before moving against an authority. A Five withdraws into the private mind and treats the social world as a variable not yet computed. So most of November 2023 fits both readings and settles nothing: the year of considering, the reliance on one colleague's screenshots, the disappearing link, the weekend off the internet, the months of quiet that followed. The same goes for the coalition facts. Discussing a chief executive with one ally, working through the independent directors and waiting for a board majority are what anyone trying to remove a chief executive does, whatever their type.

What a Six does do under threat is anticipate the group. The type's signature is reading the room for who will turn and bracing for it. Three observations in the record are hard for that reading to produce. The first is the one the sworn account turns on: he expected roughly 770 colleagues to feel nothing much either way, and 738 of them signed against him within three days. The second comes from two writers a year apart who watched him leave the room while still in it, and from his own account of a life that is work, home and little else. The third is where he went after losing. He took capital and compute partners, which any lab needs, and founded SSI with Daniel Gross as chief executive and Daniel Levy as president, so he did take allies of a kind. But he sought no new institutional home and no protector, and he chose a structure that kept the decisions his: when Meta offered to buy the company and Gross was willing, he said no and took the chief executive's title himself. A Six vigilant about concentrated power might also refuse absorption into Meta; the refusal counts for Five only together with what the company was built to be, a sealed room for one idea.

The reversal is the Six reading's best moment, and this profile does not claim it. That he cared for the company and promised to reunite it, said as the staff letter gathered signatures, is as native to a security-seeking Six as to a Five's attachment to what he built. He did not say he had been wrong about Altman; at trial he still defended the firing and blamed the process. A Six who still distrusts the authority but yields to the group looks exactly like that. The reversal is the point the Five reading does not settle.

The One reading strains on different facts. A One holds the principled line against group disapproval; he kept the belief and dropped the action within three days. He reports his own errors with amusement. About misjudging ChatGPT: "what the hell, it is true." ([MIT Technology Review](https://www.technologyreview.com/2023/10/26/1082398/exclusive-ilya-sutskever-openais-chief-scientist-on-his-hopes-and-fears-for-the-future-of-ai/)) The effigy is the One-flavored item, and it stands alone. Named testimony from anyone who reported to him day to day, where a One's rigidity would show, has not been opened for this profile.

The limits are real. A phobic Six can also freeze and go quiet. Accepting a stack of secondhand screenshots whole from one trusted colleague looks like loyalty as much as detachment. The deposition answers were given under objection and may be legally minimal. The record favors Five. It does not eliminate Six, which is why this profile holds the type at medium confidence.

## Scale in peace

"After almost a decade, I have made the decision to leave OpenAI," he posted on May 14, 2024, promising "a project that is very personally meaningful to me." It was, Gizmodo noted, his "first tweet in over five months," whether by temperament or by the terms of a negotiated exit; he had been absent from the company's major launches, and Jan Leike, his co-lead on the superalignment team, resigned the same day. Altman called him "easily one of the greatest minds of our generation, a guiding light of our field, and a dear friend," and added that "his warmth and compassion are less well known but no less" important. ([Gizmodo, May 14, 2024](https://gizmodo.com/ilya-sutskever-leaves-openai-1851477658)) Among the named colleagues quoted in this profile, that is the fullest description of his warmth, and it comes from the man he voted to fire. Musk, by then Altman's adversary, had called him a "good human" with a "good heart" ([Fortune](https://fortune.com/2023/11/25/elon-musk-what-scared-ilya-sutskever-openai-sam-altman-return/)).

The founding statement of Safe Superintelligence Inc., dated June 19, 2024, promised "the world's first straight-shot SSI lab, with one goal and one product: a safe superintelligence." Its reasoning ends: "This way, we can scale in peace." ([SSI founding statement](https://ssi.inc/)) It is the 2016 email built into a company: a sealed room for one idea, with no product, no papers and no customers until it is done. Whether the room is temperament or a reaction to the safety-versus-product fight he had just lost at OpenAI, the record cannot separate; the lab does repeat the instinct of an email written eight years before that fight existed.

The room held under pressure. In July 2025 his co-founder and CEO Daniel Gross left and Sutskever took the title ([TechCrunch, July 3, 2025](https://techcrunch.com/2025/07/03/ilya-sutskever-will-lead-safe-superintelligence-following-his-ceos-exit)) In November he named the suitor: "we were fundraising at a $32 billion valuation, and then Meta came in and offered to acquire us, and I said no. But my former cofounder in some sense said yes." Asked about one of his research ideas, he declined: "unfortunately, we live in a world where not all machine learning ideas are discussed freely, and this is one of them." ([Dwarkesh Podcast](https://www.dwarkesh.com/p/ilya-sutskever-2)) Secrecy is standard at frontier labs and doubles as a fundraising story. Refusing Meta's approach while raising money at a $32 billion valuation, at what he describes as the cost of a co-founder, is not standard.

## Constant struggle

At his alma mater on June 6, 2025, he offered graduates one piece of advice: "to accept reality as it is and to try not to regret the past and try to improve the situation." Then he stopped. "But it's hard. It's hard. It's a constant struggle with one's emotion. And that's why I mention it to you." A moment later: "And also a reminder for myself. Constant struggle." ([U of T convocation address](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=zuZ2zaotrJs)) Feeling, in his telling, is managed by perspective, and the managing is work.

On May 11, 2026 he took the stand in Oakland and testified for about an hour "barely making eye contact with anyone." Wired's reporter read him as "dejected about no longer being involved with OpenAI"; that is one journalist's reading of a face. ([Wired](https://web.archive.org/web/2026id_/https://dnyuz.com/2026/05/12/ilya-sutskever-stands-by-his-role-in-sam-altmans-openai-ouster-i-didnt-want-it-to-be-destroyed/)) He undercut the man suing, and the premise of the suit. Asked whether he had promised Musk that OpenAI would stay a nonprofit, he said, "I made no such promise." Then: "The mission of OpenAI is larger than its nonprofit or for-profit structure." ([courtroom posts, May 11, 2026](https://chatgptiseatingtheworld.com/2026/05/12/musk-v-altman-trial-week-3-ilya-sutskever-provides-captivating-testimony-favorable-to-openai/))

The film, in one early review, gives audiences a prodigy "incapable of lying," "so pure in his pursuit of research." The record gives something more exact: a man who checked the machine for twenty years and did not think to check the room, who turned down $6 million a year and then Meta's offer to buy a lab that was raising money at a $32 billion valuation to keep one question his own, and who says the hardest work he does is emotional. The first words of the longest public conversation he had given since founding SSI, in November 2025, were not about any of it. "You know what's crazy? That all of this is real." ([Dwarkesh Podcast](https://www.dwarkesh.com/p/ilya-sutskever-2))
