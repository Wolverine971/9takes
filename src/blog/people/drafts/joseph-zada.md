The call came at 7 a.m. in Sydney in April 2025, and his entire team was on the line, "so I more or less knew immediately." Joseph Zada had won the role of young Haymitch Abernathy in _The Hunger Games: Sunrise on the Reaping_, the prequel about the District 12 tribute who grows into the mentor Woody Harrelson played. Lionsgate said it had auditioned "hundreds of gifted performers from around the world" ([Deadline, April 23, 2025](https://deadline.com/2025/04/the-hunger-games-sunrise-on-the-reaping-joseph-zada-whitney-peak-1236375468/)). His only released lead in a series was the Australian _Invisible Boys_. His first reaction: "I just started swearing, like, 'Now I have to do this and it's going to be really hard.'"

He told the story to Elle Fanning, who plays Effie Trinket in the same film, for Interview Magazine. Fanning laughed and asked, "You were more scared after finding out?" He said "Yes," and moved straight on: "Then the announcement came out a day after, so everyone found out at the same time as me. It was pretty full-on." Only his younger sister was home that morning: "I walked upstairs and we jumped up and down and cried together and I gave her a big hug." ([Interview Magazine, published August 10, 2026](https://www.interviewmagazine.com/film/joseph-zada-elle-fanning-hunger-games))

Read quickly, the swearing is a joke, and it was told as one, more than a year later, to a friendly co-star after the film was safely shot. It was also promotion for a franchise in which winning is where the danger starts. One reaction shows little on its own. It matters beside a pattern that runs through most of what Zada, an Australian actor born in 2005, has said in public: when the stakes rise, he gathers people he trusts, and the judges he fears most are the people he admires. That pattern is the case for Enneagram Type 6, the type organized around security, trust and who can be relied on.

## What is Joseph Zada's personality type?

Joseph Zada looks most like Enneagram Type 6, at medium confidence. His clearest pattern is how he handles pressure: he looks for solid footing in people he trusts before a big test, and he names the judgment of admired collaborators as the thing he fears. Type 9 is the serious alternative, and Type 3 needs testing too.

Nerves about a role this size would fit [Type 6](/enneagram-corner/enneagram-type-6), [Type 3](/enneagram-corner/enneagram-type-3) or [Type 9](/enneagram-corner/enneagram-type-9). What differs is what the alarm is about and what settles it. A Six fears the ground giving way and settles when trusted people and structure are in place. A Three fears failing in front of people who matter and settles through visible performance. A Nine fears disruption and settles by smoothing things over or tuning pressure out. Zada's record favors the Six version mainly through what settles him. It is thin on other Six markers, such as testing whether people can be trusted or ambivalence toward authority, which keeps confidence at medium.

A limit shapes everything below. Almost every substantial source on Zada is an interview published between February 2025 and August 2026, inside the promotional windows of four projects. The partial exception is a Variety Australia set visit during the early-2024 _Invisible Boys_ shoot, when he was 18 and unknown. Nervousness, gratitude and "the cast became a family" are the house style of those formats, and a newcomer sharing a cast list with Glenn Close and Ralph Fiennes would produce them whatever his type. So this reading leans on what he did, especially when it cost him something, and on statements that repeat across years and interviewers.

## A family trade, learned in pairs

Acting was the household business. His father, Jeremy Cumpston, has worked as a director, actor and doctor, and his mother, Jessica Brentnall, as a film producer ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Joseph_Zada)). "My whole family and every family friend was somewhat connected," he told Fanning. "We grew up watching movies every single day. We did acting courses on our school holidays." That upbringing is the plainest explanation for his access and his ease on a set.

"It only properly appealed to me at about 15, when I started helping my older brother with auditions when he'd come back from America," he told Fanning. A [Flaunt profile](https://www.flaunt.com/post/joseph-zada) from around the June 2025 release of _We Were Liars_ names that brother as Hal, an actor six years older who had won a role in the American series _The Walking Dead: World Beyond_ ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hal_Cumpston)). He had been on camera earlier, in _Bilched_ (2019), a teen comedy Hal wrote, their father directed and Hal cast with his younger brothers and friends: family participation more than pursuit.

What he found at 15 was a method built with a partner. "We'd work up to eight hours on auditions," he said. "We'd drive each other to the proper limits of a scene and try to do it 100 different ways just for fun." Those are conversational numbers, and the auditions were his brother's. The habit is what matters: his preparation formed with someone beside him.

The name came from the same family. "My original name is Cumpston, which my older brother still goes by," he told Fanning. Zada is his grandmother's surname. "When I was starting out, about to go for my big break, I was on my last callback and I'd been thinking about changing my name." He went on: "My grandmother's Australian Indigenous and also Afghan. The name comes from my great-grandfather, who was an Afghan cameleer." He says "our family built the first mosque in Australia," and that he felt "a weird spiritual connection" to the name. Wikipedia records his Afghan-Aboriginal heritage and a Barkandji connection, which fits his account. The name claims that heritage publicly, and a separate credit is practical when a brother works as Cumpston. He adds a third reason: "now I feel like I've got my great-grandfather and my grandmother guiding me as my lucky stars." Company is the reason that recurs.

## Two accounts of leaving school

His school years left two accounts that do not match. On the Perth set of the Stan series _Invisible Boys_, filmed in early 2024 ([Stan, March 12, 2024](https://www.stan.news/stan-announces-powerful-lgbtqia-stan-original-series-invisible-boys-currently-filming-in-western-australia)), he told Variety Australia's Julie Hosking: "I had a lot of stuff going on when I was younger and some schools just don't know how to handle that, and I ended up being kicked out." He added: "I was going to an all-boys school and there was a bit of a stigma around acting. I just thought it was a very vulnerable place to be." The piece ran on the series' premiere day ([Variety Australia, February 13, 2025](https://au.variety.com/2025/tv/features/invisible-boys-shines-light-on-queer-stories-20235)).

Wikipedia relays a different version from a December 2025 GQ Australia interview: "My attendance was too low. They said, 'If you do that audition, you're not coming back.' So I dropped out to do an audition." The GQ interview itself could not be opened for this analysis. Both accounts can be partly true, since an attendance problem can end in an ultimatum and an exit. They still place the decision on opposite sides. This profile does not choose between them or build a psychological claim on either.

The Variety account does show the setting he remembers: a school where acting carried stigma and exposure felt risky. That was a real and common constraint, so it should not be read as a formative wound.

The work that followed barely paused. "As I turned eighteen, I got the lead role in _Invisible Boys_, and then I jumped straight to _We Were Liars_, and then straight to _East of Eden_, and then straight to _Hunger Games_," he told Mckenna Grace, who plays Maysilee Donner in _Sunrise on the Reaping_ ([HERO, March 23, 2026](https://hero-magazine.com/article/294797/inside-heroine-24-mckenna-grace-in-conversation-with-joseph-zada)). Being cast repeatedly explains that schedule as much as appetite does.

## The three-week audition

The Haymitch search began for him in January 2025, when he met casting director Deb Zane, he told Fanning. The audition arrived while he was playing Cal Trask, the role James Dean made famous, in Netflix's seven-episode _East of Eden_, which began filming in New Zealand in October 2024 and wrapped in March 2025 ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/East_of_Eden_(2026_miniseries))). They asked for one improvised scene. He was still shooting. He told them, "I'll have it to you in three weeks," because "I had a plan to shoot the scenes properly and edit them." Asked whether Florence Pugh, who plays Cal's mother, had helped, he said no: "I flew back to Australia from New Zealand just so I could work with my older brother. He helped me, and so did my dad and a couple of friends."

The simplest explanation is skill: a director father and an actor brother were the best crew for a properly shot and edited tape. His phrasing names a person, though, not a camera or a location, and it cannot rule out that the trip also fit a break in the schedule. What it does show is the partner from his teens back at the center of his preparation, now for his own test.

After _East of Eden_ wrapped, the process moved to Los Angeles for chemistry reads and callbacks. His _We Were Liars_ co-star Emily Alyn Lind later told People she reached him on FaceTime at his hotel there. In her telling he said, "You can't tell anyone but it's down to me and a couple [other] people for this _Hunger Games_ thing," and "he's naming names that are really big that he's up against." ([The Hollywood Reporter, relaying People, June 26, 2025](https://www.hollywoodreporter.com/movies/movie-news/joseph-zada-against-big-names-hunger-games-prequel-1236300634/))

## Afraid of the people he admires

In July 2025, in Berlin preparing for _Sunrise on the Reaping_, he told his friend and _We Were Liars_ co-star Esther McGregor that _East of Eden_'s legacy was not the problem: "funny enough, it wasn't so much the legacy of the story that scared me; it was the cast and the filmmakers. These are people I really admire. I was just afraid of how they would perceive me." Then: "But I got over that quickly." ([Behind The Blinds, July 9, 2025](https://www.behindtheblinds.be/love/sharing-the-ride))

On its own, that fear does not favor a type. Worry about how admired people will judge you belongs to Threes as much as Sixes, and to most newcomers. What separates the readings is what settles it.

The 1955 film came up at his first _East of Eden_ callback. He had planned to watch it "maybe after I got the role" until someone on the production said, "Promise me you won't watch the film." "I said, 'Yeah, sure. I guess I won't watch it.'" He waited about eight months. "I didn't necessarily feel pressure until I finished the job and watched the film. Then I was like, 'Oh, shit.'" He also noted he is only in the last two episodes. That was an instruction he followed, with no approval afterward, in a famous role that produced no pressure until the work was done.

Harrelson was different. The two did not meet until the film was shot, and what Zada describes choosing is to keep Harrelson out of his head while he worked: "For my nerves and for me to really attack it, I had to not think about that at all. There was already too much of a task at hand." When they did meet, "I had his blessing." The Harrelson case has a clear order: keep the looming authority out of mind while working, then receive its approval afterward.

The counterevidence belongs here too. After _East of Eden_, he told Flaunt his confidence had grown: "I just learned that these people are actually normal people and they're doing the same thing that I'm doing. I have it in me to go toe to toe with Florence [Pugh], I think. I feel like I can go toe to toe with anyone now." That is a young actor finding his footing, and a reader could fairly stop there. The mechanism he names is still worth noticing. His confidence arrived once the people he feared had been reclassified as peers.

The reclassification did not hold. Asked by Fanning in June 2026, after both shoots, whether any actors were on his bucket list, he answered: "I love the actors I love, but I'm shit scared of working with them." Fanning laughed. Of Pugh: "She's scary." Then: "It was very daunting, but she's so friendly and makes everyone feel so comfortable." The fear was back eleven months after Behind The Blinds, paired with what settles it: being made comfortable.

## What settles him

When Zada explains how nerves resolve, specific people usually become reliable. Of the start of _We Were Liars_, he told McGregor: "I worked myself into a state of so much anxiety and put too much pressure on myself. But by the time I got into the flow, I found my footing." He credited "being more confident in myself, and in the people around me." In Berlin he said he was "phenomenally nervous," then offered his usual fix: "like every other project I've done, I think once I step on set and meet everyone, I'll be fine."

Talking to Swooon on June 11, 2025, a month before filming began, he said the Haymitch auditions "put me through the wringer because [the role is] very important." The outlet reports him adding that it was essential to "go through that process as well, not just to learn about the character, but to meet all the people I [will be] working with." ([Swooon, June 11, 2025](https://www.swooon.com/1197320/joseph-zada-haymitch-audition-sunrise-on-the-reaping-interview/)) Of Glenn Close, he told People: "It means so much to have someone of such stature support you, and Glenn in particular made it clear she was always there for me." ([People via Yahoo, July 7, 2026](https://www.yahoo.com/entertainment/movies/articles/joseph-zada-teases-young-haymitch-110000632.html))

Company works the same way. Asked by McGregor for her favorite memory of the two of them, he described nightly hangouts in Halifax and gave a reason: "I feel both of us get lonely; we need someone around." On the _Sunrise_ shoot in Spain, Grace made him come to dinner with her mother every night when he was too tired from stunts. He told her he was grateful for "being forced to spend family time so I wasn't chilling in my own head and going crazy by myself." The family was Grace's, assembled on location. The two remarks were published about eight months apart, in July 2025 and March 2026.

His own family appears at the edge of big jobs. From Berlin he said: "I'm here with my older brother, and I just brought over my dad and my little brother. We're going to Portugal tomorrow for the weekend, which will be really fun, just a little boys' trip right before I jump into the deep end with this." A first large pay cheque explains a family holiday as well as any motive does. With the flight home for the Haymitch tape, it gives two clear cases of family around his own threshold.

None of this is proof. Promotional interviews reward this kind of gratitude, so most of these answers are consistent with a Six reading rather than a demonstration of it. Press style does not explain the flight home, or a fear of admired actors that came back after he said it had passed.

## The case for Type 9

Type 9 is organized around keeping inner and outer peace, often by merging with the people nearby and tuning out what would disturb it. Much of Zada's own language fits. He told Flaunt he is "really happy, actually," and said of industry pressure, "You've just got to roll with it." After he jokingly called himself "extremely talented, extremely chill, and extremely humble," which she notes was "all said in jest," the writer credited him with "an uncommonly zen mentality." When Fanning asked, "Who are you when you're not a character, Joe?" he answered: "I'm chilling."

The strongest Nine clue is emotional distance. On the _Invisible Boys_ set, where he played Charlie Roth, a punk outsider outed on social media in a small town, Hosking found him "remarkably upbeat for someone who has spent much of the past week in tears." He told her, "I don't tend to wear my emotions on my sleeve like Charlie does," and said the role had "taught me to be vulnerable. I have more access to my emotions, and I think that's been a very big gift." Read as a Nine, that describes a habitual distance from his own feelings that a role had to crack open. On that reading the family trips are nesting, the company is comfort, and the nerves are press idiom laid over an unruffled person.

Two observations resist it. The first is what settles him. A Nine tends to settle by tuning pressure out, and Zada does say he blocks out noise; he also kept Harrelson out of his head while he worked. Comfort alone would not separate the types, since a Nine also relaxes in a friendly room. But when he describes his nerves resolving, the fix is usually specific people becoming dependable, down to his confidence "in the people around me" and Close having "made it clear she was always there for me." That is closer to securing footing than to retreat, though it is a lean rather than a clean split.

The second is that he holds his own line more than a merging Nine usually does. He set his own three-week deadline for the Haymitch tape. Of an early _Invisible Boys_ scene, he told Grace in a conversation published in March 2026, "All the sound people hate the idea of you having something actually playing," yet "I begged them to let me play it because I needed it to give me that energy." And he told Fanning the same year, "My mom doesn't really like that idea. She'd rather I just stay home. But I do really want to go live in L.A. or New York, or maybe London at some point."

The win itself does not settle this. A huge new obligation disrupts a Nine too, and that morning also brought joy with his sister. Nor does diligence: Grace's report that "you kept the most intricate notes" shows a work habit Nines can have in abundance.

He even qualifies his own calm. When Fanning followed up on "I'm chilling," he added: "There hasn't been a whole lot of chill until very recently. But yeah, Australia is a safe place."

One smaller detail should be held lightly. Chilling is also the word he used for what company protects him from: "chilling in my own head and going crazy by myself." This suggests his easygoing surface and his need for company are more connected than the Nine reading allows. Two uses of a casual word cannot prove it.

## "He just gets what he wants"

A Type 3 reading, organized around achievement and being seen to succeed, is weaker than the Nine case and still worth testing. Its best evidence is Lind. From the private call she reports only what he did: he shared the shortlist in confidence and named the big contenders. In the People interview she recalled answering him, "I'm like, 'You'll get it, because you're you,'" and then gave her verdict: "He just gets what he wants," and "He is going to be one of these guys." It is an affectionate prediction from a friend, given to the press, and it describes appetite. Add the unbroken run of jobs and a dated ambition: "I've already started writing. I want to direct a film before I'm 30, that's my goal," he told McGregor in July 2025. His fear of how admired collaborators would perceive him also sits comfortably in Three territory.

The Three reading struggles with how he talks about his own ability. "I have a lot of ways of preparing but I'm also very new to the game, so I don't want to set anything in stone right now," he told [Polyester](https://www.polyesterzine.com/features/joseph-zada-on-the-hunger-games-playing-tennis-and-becoming-johnny-sinclairnbsp) in June 2025. To Flaunt: "I'm just constantly reminding myself that I'm still learning and not to be afraid of whether it's good or not." Even the directing goal came with a disclaimer: "I'm not saying I'll be good at it, but I want to try." He can also credit himself plainly ("I can go toe to toe with anyone now"), so the hedging is a tendency, not a rule.

Those hedges carry a serious caveat. They are exactly what a well-advised teenager says in his first press cycle opposite Hollywood veterans, and all three come from about a month in mid-2025. If he speaks with settled authority about his method once his work is released, the hedging was career position more than temperament.

The sharper difference is what he reaches for under pressure. A Three would be expected to lean on performance and visible results. Zada's recurring move is to bring trusted people in first.

The Six reading does not fully answer Lind either. A Six can want something intensely, and this reading accounts for the manner of his pursuit: tracking the competition quietly, confiding in a friend, preparing with family. It does not fully explain the appetite she saw, and that tension stays open.

## What the record cannot settle yet

He told Flaunt: "I'm kind of in a weird place right now. I don't want anyone to know anything about me. But now I'm in these situations where I do have to talk about it." He gave the reason himself, and most of his later openness came in promotional interviews he was expected to give. What stays open is how much he volunteers beyond the job, from his family name to his nerves to his mother's wish that he stay home, and the record does not show how he weighs that against his stated wish for privacy.

One remark is this analysis's question rather than his claim. When Grace praised the young cast of _IT: Welcome to Derry_, he said: "Child actors are just so free, so loose. It's unattainable." In context he was admiring a craft quality. Whether "unattainable" says anything about his own looseness is unknown.

As of September 2026, his released series leads were _Invisible Boys_, which premiered on Stan on February 13, 2025, and _We Were Liars_, released in June 2025. He was also one of four credited stars of _The Speedway Murders_, a true-crime documentary with dramatized scenes released in select theaters and on streaming on June 21, 2024 ([Deadline, March 1, 2024](https://deadline.com/2024/03/the-speedway-murders-documentary-acquired-vertical-1235842007/); [WIBC, June 20, 2024](https://wibc.com/378875/film-on-1978-burger-chef-murders-in-speedway-debuting-friday/)). In June 2026 Grace told Variety Australia he was "genuinely about to become one of the biggest stars ever" ([Variety Australia, June 19, 2026](https://au.variety.com/2026/film/news/joseph-zada-the-hunger-games-sunrise-on-the-reaping-mckenna-grace-interview-37823/)). At that point all seven episodes of _East of Eden_ were scheduled for Netflix on October 1, 2026, and _Sunrise on the Reaping_ was scheduled for US theaters on November 20, 2026 ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Hunger_Games:_Sunrise_on_the_Reaping)). How those releases land does not change the dated pattern above. The Six reading predicts that fame will not settle him, and that at the next threshold he will again bring his people with him before he jumps into the deep end.
