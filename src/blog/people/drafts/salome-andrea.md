In November 2023, Avery Woods opened a podcast interview with her friend Salomé Andrea by saying, "I feel like I know everything yet absolutely nothing about you at all."

That was the deal Salomé had with her audience for more than a decade. She has posted her life online since about 2011: travel photography first, then outfits, workouts and her twin daughters, Luca and Rue. By April 2026 she had 1.6 million TikTok followers, [The Hollywood Reporter](https://www.hollywoodreporter.com/lists/secret-lives-mormon-wives-orange-county-cast/) reported. People knew her kitchen, her closet and her kids' birthday parties. Almost nobody knew what she thought.

Then Hulu cast her in _The Secret Lives of Mormon Wives: Orange County_. She explained the decision in a TikTok on April 24, 2026.

That year and a half turned out to be the season.

When the show dropped on November 12, 2026, the quiet one had the messiest storyline. She and her husband, the photographer Aaron Brimhall, had separated in 2025 with divorce papers ready. She had briefly dated Chase McWhorter from the original Utah series. So which is the real Salomé: the calm, soft-focus feed, or the woman confronting her husband on Hulu?

Both. That's what makes her a Nine.

## What is Salomé Andrea's personality type?

Salomé Andrea is an Enneagram Type 9, the Peacemaker. She goes along easily and keeps her anger out of sight until it has built into resentment, which she named on _Mormon Wives: OC_. Then she works her way back to calm and to the family she missed.

[Type 9](/enneagram-corner/enneagram-type-9) sits in the instinctual triad, the gut types, and the core emotion there is anger. That can sound wrong for someone whose whole brand is ease. But Nines are the gut type that sends anger underground. A [Type 6](/enneagram-corner/enneagram-type-6) runs on fear and keeps scanning for what could go wrong. A [Type 4](/enneagram-corner/enneagram-type-4) runs on shame and keeps asking who they really are. Salomé mostly does neither. She settles in, says yes, and stays comfortable, until she can't anymore.

Here's how the Nine pattern works, in plain steps:

1. Agree first. Notice later what you gave up.
2. Don't argue in the moment. File the irritation away.
3. Filed long enough, it hardens into resentment.
4. The resentment comes out all at once, often harder than the moment calls for.
5. Then work your way back to calm.

Her public record has all five, and she narrated step three herself.

## "I just had zero opinion"

She is from Guam, where her mother's family lives, and moved to Utah with her mother at six. Her father has lived in Asia since she was in middle school.

She met Aaron through his sister. In a 2023 TikTok the couple filmed together, she told how she had a crush on him in ninth grade, before he knew who she was. "I would stay up all night making sure he had like a tie-dye shirt for school the next morning. I was committed and I had a vision." They've been together since she was 17.

That's worth holding onto. When Salomé wants something, she goes after it.

What she wanted was him. Much of the rest got decided around her. She married Aaron at 20, in 2012, on a beach in Guam, and told Woods in 2023 that the wedding mostly happened to her.

> "I swear I didn't plan a single ounce of my wedding. Like I walked into the wedding dress store, found a dress that was on sale for like $600. My mom was like, this one. I was like, sure. Like I just had zero opinion."

She gave her own reason right after: "I was so young and I'm like, I didn't even know what my style was."

Photography went the same way. "I loved photography. I would have Aaron hang out with me to take photos. And then he became in love with it." Aaron turned it into his career while she worked what she called "random jobs," from greeting guests at a hotel in Macau, where her father lived, to captioning phone calls.

Fitness stayed hers. She "got super into working out before I had kids," trained through the pregnancy, and after the twins were born in 2018 she "could not miss my hour every day."

Nines don't lose all of themselves. They lose the parts that would have needed a fight to keep.

## The calm people follow her for

Fans don't follow Salomé for drama. They follow her because watching her is restful: soft narration, the girls in matching Gap pajamas, taco night, a pumpkin patch. That calm is real, and it's the Nine's gift. She makes a full life look easy. She has said as much about her style: "more effort trying to make it look effortless."

The calm also shows up in what she says about home. Before the 2024 move she told her followers the old house had "kept us safe and I feel like this house loved us back." Leaving Guam after a family trip that February, in a vlog posted in June 2026, she stopped at the airport escalator. "This escalator, it makes me emotional. This is where grandma and grandpa would always come say bye."

On that Guam trip the family surprised her grandparents and booked a hotel. "We normally stay with family but it was still last minute. I didn't want to impose on anyone."

Not imposing is a Nine instinct. So is the one that frustrated her father in 2023. He was texting her every day, she told Woods that November, asking when she was coming to Taiwan.

> "And I never respond. He's like, why are you mad at me? Cause you're ignoring me. I'm like, I'm not. I'm just like, my life is chaos."

No argument and no date, just quiet, while she still meant to go. And she went. The family spent Lunar New Year 2026 with him in Taipei, and in October she said he'd be coming to stay a week after the baby was born.

That 2023 overwhelm had a cause. Her mother's husband "died last year unexpectedly," she said in a July 2024 vlog, and her mother had "lived with us for a year." Anyone would go quiet in that year. What makes it a Nine pattern is that the quiet was a delay, not a break.

The calm costs her something. Nines often don't feel their limits, so they say yes to everything. In an October 2026 vlog, 37 weeks pregnant, she mentioned volunteering to make paper chains for the twins' class party "as if I don't have so much to do in my life." Then:

> "It's weird because I kind of like thrive on stress. I'll say yes and add pretty much anything I can to my plate when I know that I shouldn't."

Saying yes is easier than choosing what to drop. That's how a quiet person ends up filming "about 10 ads" in the same week as a house closing and a trip to Thailand, as she did in August 2024.

## "A lot of resentment on my end was building"

This is the line that explains her season.

In a confessional quoted by [People](https://people.com/slomw-oc-salome-andrea-confronts-husband-about-rumored-fling-during-separation-12251358), she described how the marriage went wrong. "Early in our marriage, we had so much fun," she said. Then:

> "Early 20s, we were doing so much together, but as time went on, a lot of resentment on my end was building."

That's steps two and three, in her own words. Not an affair, not one big betrayal. Resentment, piling up over years, mostly unspoken.

Then something shifted. "As soon as I started making money on social media, I was like, 'Wait, I can do my own thing here a little bit,'" she said, "so I know for a fact that I'm fully capable of taking care of this baby on my own." For a woman who once had "zero opinion" about her own wedding dress, "I can do my own thing" is a big sentence.

Step four showed up in how they fought. On the show, People reported, she said the two of them "fell into some really toxic patterns with our communication." And: "Every fight just spiraled and snowballed into something so big that we became just miserable around each other."

Snowballing is what banked anger does. When a Nine finally lets it out, it carries every earlier moment she let pass. Aaron put it his way on camera: "You and I, growing up, were very jealous people of each other, so I'm a little scared of you." In an April 2023 Q&A she'd been asked about her patience. "Aaron's way more patient than I am," she said. "I'm working on it."

In 2025 they separated. "When Aaron and I separated, we truly thought we were getting divorced. We had divorce papers ready to go, so we both were dating other people," she said on the premiere, as quoted by [Parade](https://www.yahoo.com/entertainment/tv/articles/salom-andrea-reveals-past-romance-051932934.html). "I definitely enjoyed my single era." For her, that included a brief relationship with Chase McWhorter, the ex-husband of original-series cast member Miranda McWhorter.

## Why she went back

Every fan asks this. Her answer, as far as she has given one, is about the family, not about Aaron changing.

On Avery Woods' podcast on November 16, 2026, Woods and Aspyn Ovard recalled Salomé sending voice-memo updates during the separation. Around January, after the holidays, they said, she told them she missed her family unit. The next time they saw her, the couple was in therapy and working on it.

She wrote something similar on Instagram on October 22, as [E! News](https://www.eonline.com/news/1438136/mormon-wives-salome-andrea-shuts-down-aaron-brimhall-cheating-rumors) reported: "part of me is holding on a little tighter to the family we've been for all these years."

That's the Nine's pull: back toward the whole, the familiar shape of things. A Nine who has separated doesn't usually go back because the other person won an argument. She goes back because, once the anger is spent, she misses the peace of the life they had.

They reconciled early in 2026. She learned she was pregnant about three months later, Parade reported.

## The fight Hulu aired

This is where the calm broke on camera.

After she announced the pregnancy on Instagram in May 2026, she said on the show, as quoted by [Reality Blurb](https://realityblurb.com/2026/11/16/slomw-oc-star-salome-andrea-addresses-rumors-her-husband-aaron-brimhall-cheated-on-her/), "I got a screenshot from my friend of a text from her friend, saying Aaron hooked up with some girl." The woman was 22, according to Aaron's own words on camera, and the encounter happened during the separation.

What hurt was the lie. "When we got back together, we agreed on being very open and honest for this to work, so to find out he's been lying makes me question how committed he really is." And the double standard. Later she sat Aaron down, People reported: "You gave me such a hard time about Chase being in Salt Lake, and then I find out two days ago that you hooked up with somebody in Salt Lake."

"It happened once and I was drunk," he answered. "You know how you get anytime I tell you the small sort of thing." Then: "To me, I want to communicate with you and talk, but you're just too abrasive."

That's a husband's word in the middle of a fight, on a show built on conflict. It's also the outside view of step four.

Her confessionals were the rawest things she has said in public. "Such a happy moment was hijacked by this dumb information," she said, in [People's season recap](https://www.yahoo.com/entertainment/tv/articles/biggest-revelations-cast-secret-lives-224000811.html). "If my husband is going after somebody who is like 11 years younger than me, does he even find me attractive?" And:

> "Before the separation, I feel like I trusted Aaron fully and now I feel like that's completely gone for me. I am committed to making our marriage work, but I think if he keeps lying, the lies keep happening, then I will have a breaking point."

Notice the order. Committed first, then the limit. When the clip spread and a commenter called Aaron gross, she shut it down on Instagram on November 14: "He never cheated on me just be to clear," as [Mandatory reported](https://www.yahoo.com/entertainment/celebrity/articles/mormon-wives-star-salom-andrea-083700199.html). On the timeline, she's right: the hookup happened while they were apart. What she objected to on camera was the lie.

Not every viewer bought the reconciliation. Instagram creator Rachel Myklebust posted a reaction video and wrote, "This is toxic, you deserve better," Mandatory reported. The season ends on a "to be continued" message, [Bustle](https://www.bustle.com/entertainment/mormon-wives-orange-county-madison-bontempo-kyler-fisher-update) reported, with Salomé, in [Marie Claire's](https://www.yahoo.com/entertainment/tv/renewed-canceled/articles/secret-lives-mormon-wives-orange-203544092.html) words, "working through a potential reconciliation."

This analysis takes no side on the marriage. The type explains how she fights. It doesn't decide who was right.

## In the group: blunt at a distance

In the scenes the press has reported, she doesn't start the group fights. She picks a side and says what she thinks in the confessional.

In one group conversation, People reported, McCall DaPron ended up in tears when Salomé and Avery Woods backed Bobbi Althoff's position. McCall's sister Mayci then sent Jen Affleck a text threatening the women who had made her sister cry. Salomé's [confessional](https://www.yahoo.com/entertainment/celebrity/articles/mormon-wives-oc-cast-slam-202754729.html): "Personally it feels very middle schoolish, but I think that's what Mayci is used to. She's not a part of this group, so she should stick to her own MomTok."

Late in the season, Madison Bontempo's husband Kyler Fisher invited Chase to his birthday party. Her [confessional](https://www.yahoo.com/entertainment/tv/articles/secret-lives-mormon-wives-oc-160000415.html) went straight at him: "Kyler was very much aware of the Chase situation with me, so I feel like I can see right through him and his manipulation."

Nines aren't anger-free. They're anger-indirect. Salomé is cutting about people outside her circle and says it to a camera, not across the table. Inside the circle, she is loyal and close. Aspyn Ovard came in already close with Woods, Althoff and Salomé, [Bustle](https://www.bustle.com/entertainment/aspyn-ovard-mormon-wives-oc-interview) reported. Avery has said the first scene they ever filmed for the show was Salomé telling her she was pregnant, on the beach.

## Neither Mormon nor ex-Mormon

The running question about _Mormon Wives: OC_, as [Grazia's cast guide](https://graziadaily.co.uk/life/tv-and-film/cast-slomw-oc/) framed it, is how many of the cast are actually Mormon. Salomé got ahead of that in her April TikTok: "So I did grow up Mormon for those of you who think I have no association."

Her mother converted from Catholicism to the LDS Church, and she grew up in it, "but I always am like, disclaimer, we weren't Utah Mormon." Asked by Woods in 2023 whether she was still in the church, she started with "It's hard to say because here's the thing."

> "I don't like to come out and say I am not Mormon because I'm like, I have my own relationship with God."

She was plain about the rest: "We don't go, we drink, we have tattoos." Her mother sometimes takes the girls to church, because "we do want our girls to grow up with some sort of religion." And about the label: "I feel like I shouldn't have to say I'm not Mormon. People assume that I'm not because of like our lifestyle."

That middle position is very Nine. She doesn't want to defend the church and she doesn't want to fight it. She keeps her faith, drops what doesn't fit, and declines to pick a team.

## Why she said yes to a "side quest"

Her explanation for joining Hulu's loudest franchise sounded nothing like ambition. To the people asking why she would do it: "Most people if they're presented with something like that like they're gonna take it."

It came to her, and she went with it. She knew the cost. "We really didn't hold back, which is very scary," she said in a September 29 TikTok. "We're opening ourselves up to a lot of judgment and scrutiny." She planned her exit in advance: "I may log off completely when the show comes out to save myself and my sanity."

And she did step back. A week before the premiere, she posted from home with the caption "My girls are out there."

"I do not have FOMO. I feel great about what I did last night, which was nothing. I'm very content."

Shiloh was born on November 13, the day after the season dropped. She's named for the 1991 children's novel. "One of Sal's favorite books growing up was _Shiloh_, so that name always stuck with her," the couple [told People](https://www.yahoo.com/entertainment/celebrity/articles/slomw-oc-star-salom-andrea-180000471.html). "Everyone is in love and we are just soaking in the newborn baby time."

The most private person on the show told the most private story on it, then went home to the quiet she prefers.

### Why not Type 6?

This is the real rival, and her own words make the case.

She has described postpartum anxiety that was "just so high." She didn't take the twins to the gym until they were about two because she "was so nervous about them being sick." New places are "always just like intimidating." Online criticism starts a loop: "OK, am I doing something wrong?... Then I would question myself, which I don't like." When people said she shouldn't get a hair treatment while pregnant, she settled it the Six way: "I asked my doctor and he was fine with it." And her season storyline is about trust, ending on a condition: "if he keeps lying... I will have a breaking point."

An anxious Nine and a Six share a lot of vocabulary. Here's why the call stays Nine.

**What built up?** A Six's marriage breaks down through suspicion: testing, checking, needing proof. Salomé's account of her marriage is about "resentment," built over years of going along. That's banked anger, the instinctual triad's emotion, not fear.

**Which way does she move under pressure?** Sixes move toward the threat to check it. Salomé mostly moves away: the unanswered texts, "Maybe we just stay," the urge at a 2024 house closing to "block their emails and just not buy a house anymore," the plan to log off for the premiere.

**What does she return to?** After the flare-up, a Six sets terms and keeps watching. Salomé did set a condition. Then she closed the matter in public and went home to the "family unit" she missed. Her confessionals say "committed" before they say "breaking point."

The Six evidence is real, and the connecting lines explain it. Under stress, Nine moves toward Six: worry, doubt, scanning for betrayal. That's Salomé after the screenshot.

### Why not Type 7 or Type 4?

Type 7 has some support. She once tried to book a flight to Italy "because I'm so bored," and in August 2026 she flew to LA on short notice because "I get bored when I'm like alone."
