Partway through the shoot for _Clayface_, the DC Studios film in which he plays the title role, a stunt called for a car to drive into the side of Tom Rhys Harries's leg. That was the moment the Welsh actor could no longer keep his secret. He took the stunt coordinator aside. "Jimmy, I'm so sorry, man. I actually don't have an ACL," he recalled telling him on the [Empire Film Podcast](https://www.goloudnow.com/podcasts/the-empire-film-podcast-397/the-interviews-ft-tom-rhys-harries-taika-waititi-peyton-elizabeth-lee-jacob-tremblay-615255) in October 2026.

He had torn the ligament about six months before cameras rolled. Then he booked the first studio lead of a fifteen-year career, and, he told [Cultured](https://culturedmag.com/article/2026/10/23/film-tom-rhys-harries-clayface-interview/), his thought was, "Well, I’m not gonna have time to do the surgery." He said nothing to anyone on the production. Telling the story to the podcast host, unscripted and mostly for laughs, he dropped the reason into the middle of it in one plain sentence:

> "And for fear of losing this job, I didn't tell anybody."

Director James Watkins, speaking to [The Hollywood Reporter](https://www.hollywoodreporter.com/movies/movie-features/clayface-director-tragic-ending-joker-easter-eggs-1236740387/) on October 26, 2026, confirms the order of events, using the name of Harries's character, Matt Hagen: "There’s a moment in the film when Matt gets hit by a car, and that’s when it came out. Tom had a quiet word with the stunt man about it." The team reworked the stunt. "He still did the jump and the fall, but he was frank about it." Then Watkins answered the question the secret raises: "it’s not like he was cast to play Premier League or NFL football. He’s acting, so [having foreknowledge of his injury] wouldn’t have made any difference to me." He has since had the surgery: "I’ve put it back in since," he told Cultured.

In a July 2026 _Entertainment Weekly_ cover story, as [relayed by Instinct](https://instinctmagazine.com/tom-rhys-harries-clayface/), he had put it more starkly: "I actually shot all of Clayface with a torn ACL. I hadn’t told the stunts or anyone on production." His later podcast account and Watkins's agree that the secret came out mid-shoot instead. The detail that matters survives either version. Nobody had to drag a motive out of him. He volunteered one, and the word he used was fear, about a threat that, by the director's account, would not have cost him the role.

Harries, born in Cardiff in 1990, had been working for well over a decade before that: the film _Hunky Dory_ (2011) while still a drama student, a lead role in Netflix's _White Lines_ (2020), and West End stage work including _The Seagull_ opposite Emilia Clarke, shut by the pandemic after five performances in 2020 and revived in 2022. DC Studios cast him in [June 2025](https://deadline.com/2025/06/clayface-tom-rhys-harries-1236435237/), ahead of a reported shortlist that included Jack O'Connell, Tom Blyth and George MacKay. The film opened at No. 1 in the US on October 23, 2026, with about [$24 million](https://www.news4jax.com/entertainment/2026/10/25/you-can-see-everything-surges-with-195m-at-box-office-clayface-takes-no-1/) by studio estimates. The more interesting question is what kind of person names fear as his motive, out loud, on the release-week press tour for his own breakout.

## What is Tom Rhys Harries's personality type?

Tom Rhys Harries reads most clearly as an Enneagram Type 6, the security-seeking type, at medium confidence. Since at least 2022 he has said fear holds his focus, and in 2026 he called preparation a crutch. He credits people who have his back. Type 7 is a close rival; Types 3 and 9 are in play.

The Enneagram groups [Type 6](/enneagram-corner/enneagram-type-6) with Types 5 and [7](/enneagram-corner/enneagram-type-7) as the anxiety-driven head types. What separates them is the response. A Seven usually reaches for more options. A Six reaches for firmer ground: preparation, rules, people it can count on. A counterphobic Six moves toward the threat instead, and shooting on a torn ligament could be read that way, though the concealment itself avoided a feared conversation and most of the coping Harries describes is preparation, not confrontation. Two more rivals come up below: [Type 3](/enneagram-corner/enneagram-type-3), driven by the need to be seen succeeding, and [Type 9](/enneagram-corner/enneagram-type-9), which tunes disturbance out to stay steady.

The case rests mostly on his own words in promotional interviews, where nerves and gratitude are expected talking points. As of November 2026, no one who has known him for twenty years has spoken on the record outside a promo cycle. His wing is not argued here; the record does not settle it.

## Fear, named early

The fear language did not arrive with the superhero film. In June 2022, [Square Mile](https://squaremile.com/culture/film-tv/tom-rhys-harries-interview/)'s Michael Rudling reported that Harries lives with ADHD, and in the same interview Harries said he is "shit at focusing on something if I’m not interested in it." Acting is the exception, he said, and he explained why:

> "I’ve always had that focus with acting because it’s scary. The impulse to distraction is quietened by the fear of being criticised. I think I was aware from quite a young age that fear was a big driving factor for me."

Harries describes fear of being judged as the thing that holds his attention in place, and fear of criticism is something a Three would feel too. ADHD is also a real alternative explanation for the restlessness and the bursts of obsession; he says it is "kind of a relief to be able to focus." This profile does not count his restlessness as type evidence for that reason.

The earliest first-person source found dates from 2015, when he was already a working stage actor. Recalling his third year at drama school in Cardiff, around 2011 or 2012, he told [WhatsOnStage](https://www.whatsonstage.com/news/5-minutes-with-tom-rhys-harries-strindberg-is-a-headache-but-a-good-one_38686/) he auditioned in London and "spent thousands of pounds on transport," then added: "I used to really beat myself up if I wasn’t able to go to one." A missed audition was not a shrug to him. One reading is that it already registered as a lapse in vigilance, though cost and plain ambition explain the self-reproach as well.

Home, by his account, was not the source of the alarm. He grew up a first-language Welsh speaker in what he called, in that 2015 piece, a "very musical family." In 2020 he told [The Rakish Gent](https://www.therakishgent.co.uk/pages/spotlight-mr-tom-rhys-harries): "Both of my parents are teachers and I grew up in a great and safe family environment, but I’ve been self sufficient since I was 17." He expected to go into music and, he told [WWD](https://wwd.com/eye/people/tom-rhys-harries-readies-for-suspicion-1235059888/) in 2022, "kind of stumbled into acting." That offhand entry sits awkwardly next to the vigilant student, and the type reading does not need to explain it away.

He describes the boy as "quite disruptive growing up," energy that "often" came out in "having fights," steadied by "really strong, protective and nurturing women," including a teacher he is still in contact with. "I wouldn’t have finished school without her," he said in 2022. Four years later, after _Clayface_ opened, that teacher was still in the story. He told [The Hollywood Reporter](https://www.hollywoodreporter.com/movies/movie-features/clayface-tom-rhys-harries-surviving-multi-day-screen-test-1236742744/) on October 29, 2026, that his school years were "very much a case of, ‘if this kid doesn’t find something to focus on, he’s fucked’" and that "If this teacher wasn’t there, I would not be having this conversation with you right now."

This is his account of his childhood, not a diagnosis of it, and it does not explain his type. What it shows is a loyalty that has held steady. He credits his survival to the specific adults who backed him, and he keeps saying so.

## The crutch

Ask Harries how he works, and the answer is preparation. Ask how he feels about preparation, and the answer is more revealing. In a conversation with Kit Connor published by [Interview](https://www.interviewmagazine.com/film/tom-rhys-harries-and-kit-connor-on-fear-and-vanity-in-hollywood) in September 2026, he said:

> "I really prepare, which has been a crutch. I just shot an indie I loved, and I was like, “This character will benefit from me going in a bit blind,” which was terrifying. I’m so used to having that crutch to lean on."

The word he chose for going in unprepared was "terrifying." He did it anyway, by choice, on a film he loved, and that matters when the Seven case comes up. But the default he describes is bracing, and the exception is the part that frightened him.

The _Clayface_ casting tested him for about three months. Watkins told [The Hollywood Reporter](https://www.hollywoodreporter.com/movies/movie-news/clayface-movie-actor-tom-rhys-harries-director-james-watkins-1236733512/) at the October 2026 premiere, "I don’t think I’ve ever auditioned anyone that much." Many actors would hear doubt in that. Harries heard a safety net: "I’m untested in this space, so I was glad he did it with me really because it meant that we’d had time to workshop the thing before he got onto set."

He got through the wait with escapes. "I played Elden Ring to switch my brain off during the three months I was screen testing for Clayface," he told Connor. When Connor called it a tough game to switch off to, Harries answered, "It probably talks to my level of anxiety throughout that period." When the call came, "I was out riding my bike trying to distract myself," he told _People_ (as [relayed by SuperHeroHype](https://www.superherohype.com/news/681328-tom-rhys-harries-reveals-dcu-clayface-role)). On their own, the escapes say little about type; the Seven section explains why.

What is more telling is how he controlled what could reach him. "I actually cut off my agent and said, ‘Do not tell me who else it is,’" he told _The Hollywood Reporter_ in his October 29 interview. "I just needed to be in the zone, without thinking about who they’re considering." He asked for the names only after the first test, when "by then it would no longer affect me negatively because I was done." Called back for a second test, "I had to put my blinders on."

In that interview he also ran a threat assessment on himself from the studio's side of the table. The other contenders had a track record leading studio films. With him, he said, "there are so many moments where an unknown quality could falter," and "they haven’t got a great reference point as to what I’d be like with the crew, the rest of the cast, and in the press." He was modeling the worst case, from the decision-maker's chair, about himself. That context makes the hidden ACL easier to read. One reading, which is this profile's and not his: he already saw himself as the risky bet and was not going to hand anyone a reason to agree. A Three protecting how the studio saw him fits that reading as well as a Six protecting the job.

## Who has his back

If preparation is one support, people are the other. The pattern runs from 2020 to 2026.

In 2020, after _White Lines_ became a Netflix hit, he told The Rakish Gent that good work comes from surrounding yourself with people you believe in: "I had real faith in Alex Pina." In 2026 he told Connor that the longer he does this, "the more it seems to be an exercise in letting go and engaging with people you’re willing to take a leap of faith with." Then, about the edit he will never control: "You just hope the editor’s got your back, don’t you?"

He gave Connor his own gloss on fear in the same conversation: "I think fear is a mark of caring about something and wanting to contribute to the greater piece." Then, straight on: "One of the things I loved most about doing Clayface was how much I’m needed. I felt like I was part of the crew." That framing softens the threat reading. Fear, for him, is partly a sign of investment, which is common actor rhetoric, but what he attaches it to is the group, not his own image. Telling Cultured what he wants, he went straight to reliability: "Ultimately, I just want to be a good, reliable work partner, a good boyfriend, a good brother, son, friend."

There is a co-star's account too, though it comes second-hand. Max Minghella, as [relayed by Creepy Catalog](https://creepycatalog.com/max-minghella-says-clayface-would-have-fallen-apart-without-tom-rhys-harries/) on October 26, 2026, called him "Just incredibly disciplined, amazing leadership on set," and, by the site's account, Harries phoned Minghella the day he was cast and kept in touch to make sure he felt included. That is premiere-season praise from a castmate, published by a site that gets his age wrong, so it carries limited weight. It still describes the behavior his own words predict: tending the group, not just his own performance.

One post-release interview adds a dated checkpoint. On October 29, 2026, six days after the No. 1 opening, he told _The Hollywood Reporter_ he was grateful "because you realize how many people have invested their time and work into this thing." Leading a big production turned out to be "counterintuitive, but it’s actually easier when you have so many people around you." Asked about what comes next, he said "fundamentally nothing is going to change — it might open a few more doors in terms of who I’m able to talk to," and that he wants projects "from creators who I back fundamentally."

Gratitude is press etiquette, so this is one interview, consistent with Six and not decisive. What is specific is where he put the win: in the people around him and the collaborators he trusts, the same place he put it in 2020.

## The case for Type 7

The strongest rival deserves a real hearing, because there is a lot of Seven on the surface.

He is curious in a way interviewers keep noticing. As a kid he would go around Cardiff listening to film soundtracks and "pretend I was in a Truman Show-type situation," he told [Schön!](https://schonmagazine.com/interview-tom-rhys-harries/) in written answers in 2020. "I found that made my paper round less mundane." He wants to "go study clowning at Le Coq in Paris." He plays guitar and, he said in 2022, has ridden motorbikes for years.

He has framed his whole career as a gamble. "I chose to be an actor which on the surface seems to be a pretty reckless choice of career," he told The Rakish Gent in 2020, adding, "With optimism, I will always make decisions that I feel might come off." When Kit Connor said in 2026 that he had learned from Harries to be silly on set, Harries answered, "I may have taken the license to be silly and run with it." Connor added, "You taught me how to misbehave on a film set." Near the end of the _Clayface_ shoot he pitched an unscripted club sequence, and production added a day to film it. "That was not in the script," he said in a clip [written up by The Express Tribune](https://tribune.com.pk/story/2637412/tom-rhys-harries-breaks-down-how-he-created-the-unexpected-club-scene-in-clayface).

Even the knee story has a Seven note in one telling. In _EW_, as relayed by Instinct, he described "running around Liverpool at 1 a.m. or 2 a.m. trying to get away with it." That is glee, not dread. And the best unscripted evidence for Seven comes early in the Empire podcast, when Harries turns the tables on host Chris Hewitt, quizzing him about his golf handicap ("I have my sources, Chris") and joking, "I rarely know what's going on."

So why does Seven trail? Not because of the escapes. Seven is also an anxiety-driven type, and distraction is its classic way of handling anxiety, so Elden Ring and the bike rides during the screen tests fit Seven at least as well as Six. Calling them anxiety relief does not count against Seven.

The difference is narrower, and it rests on two statements. He calls going in without preparation "terrifying" (2026), even though he chose to do it. And the escape he describes most fondly points toward certainty, not novelty. He told Square Mile in 2022 that working on motorbikes is "an antidote" to acting:

> "Acting is so subjective and sort of heady but there’s nothing subjective about whether an engine works or if a sprocket’s out of place."

A Seven wants more doors open. This is a man who relaxes when a question has one right answer.

Two quotes four years apart show a tendency, not proof. The ADHD disclosure cuts against both readings. It explains the breadth of interests without needing Seven, and it explains the hyperfocus without needing Six. That is why the case here leans on motive statements, not on how scattered or focused he seems. It also means the margin over Seven is narrow. The playfulness is not a costume. It is part of him that a cautious Six does not predict.

## The Three question

[Type 3](/enneagram-corner/enneagram-type-3) is the other live alternative, and it has real evidence.

The first piece is a journalist's observation. Square Mile's Rudling found him "unshy about dropping huge names" in 2022. While Harries told a story involving Benedict Cumberbatch, Rudling wrote, "He catches my eye to note my reaction." Rudling's detail suggests a man monitoring how he lands. The second is the fear of criticism he named in that interview, which is exactly what an image-focused type would feel. The third is the injury. Hiding a torn ACL to keep a career-making role is what a Three might do, and he was thinking about how he would come across "in the press." When Connor asked about vanity, he said, "the vanity of it never interested me. I find it interesting because I feel like I have a relationship to it." He did not explain what that relationship is.

Against Three is the way he keeps undercutting his own success story. In 2020 he told [Mr Porter](https://www.mrporter.com/en-gb/journal/fashion/tom-rhys-harries-interview-white-lines-1277006) "I’m not a celebrity." In 2026 he guessed that most of Comic-Con "would go, “Who?!”" ([Instinct](https://instinctmagazine.com/tom-rhys-harries-clayface/), relaying _EW_). Days before release he told Cultured, "I’ve had way more noes than I’ve had yeses," and "luck plays a huge part in anybody’s perceived success, and the whole thing isn’t a meritocracy."

Self-deprecation can be its own image management, so none of this is decisive. Neither is the knee. "For fear of losing this job" is how almost anyone might explain hiding an injury from a studio, and losing a career-making lead is a blow to image as much as to security. Both types would have hidden the knee, and both could have said those words.

Two things keep Six ahead, narrowly. The first is where his fear attaches. A couple of days before release, when a Three would feel most exposed, he told Cultured, "I went through a phase of being nervous," and then, "I’ve gone past the point of nervousness." Now, he said, "Hey, that’s the film. I hope you like it." The anxiety he describes clusters around preparing and performing, not around how the result is received. The second is where he puts credit: in trusted collaborators after _White Lines_ in 2020, in the people invested in _Clayface_ in 2026. A Three tends to keep the success story centered on himself. Both points rest on press interviews, and if later ones show him reframing the win as earned arrival, Three moves up.

## Nine, and the calm on the surface

A fear-forward story can hide a fourth reading. Interviewers keep describing a calm man. "The first thing you notice about Tom Rhys Harries is how relaxed he is," Rudling wrote in 2022, calling him "a man rarely in a rush." Cultured's writer in 2026 found him "clear-eyed and methodical." In 2020 he told [Glamour](https://www.glamourmagazine.co.uk/article/tom-rhys-harries-white-lines-netflix-interview), "I have a very thick skin."

He leans on fatalist sayings, too. "I really hold onto, ‘what is meant for you, won’t pass you by.’ I am not religious in particular, but I do believe that everything happens for a reason and that gives me some kind of peace," he told Glamour in 2020. Even "nothing is going to change" is a sentence a Nine could say.

Nine fits that surface. Six stays ahead because a Nine tends to play disturbance down, and Harries keeps naming it: fear as a driving factor, anxiety through the screen tests, preparation as a crutch, rival names withheld so they could not "affect me negatively." He builds defenses against disturbance rather than tuning it out. The calm may be partly what those defenses buy him. That is an inference.

## What the Six reading leaves out

A type is a pattern, not a cage, and some of Harries does not fit.

There is a provocateur in him. In May 2020 [WalesOnline](https://www.walesonline.co.uk/lifestyle/tv/tom-rhys-harries-unforgotten-netflix-18336526) asked how a _White Lines_ scene in which his character loses a tooth was done, and printed his answer: "I actually pulled it out! It’s real, that whole scene is real. I haven’t told anyone that." Weeks later he told Mr Porter otherwise. "No! She’s misquoted me," he said. "I was doing a gag." Then: "It was just me not reading the room. But no, I didn’t pull my tooth out." It is his word against a printed quote. Either way, a deadpan claim to have pulled out his own tooth for a scene, delivered to his hometown paper, is not what a careful, image-guarding personality does. The correction, with its self-blame, is more Six-like than the joke.

As a junior actor on _Parade's End_, he walked up to the director and told him one of his lines was wrong. "I don’t know who the fuck I thought I was," he said in 2022. Benedict Cumberbatch, he recalled, "said he’d had the same concern as me, but that he’d checked the book and it was right." He tells the story mainly to praise Cumberbatch as "so studious" and to laugh at himself.

The jokes, the fights, the challenge to a director and the unscripted club scene show a man who is not timid. The counterphobic version of Six could absorb some of this, but this profile does not count any of it as Six evidence. It stays partly unexplained, and it is the best reason to keep Seven close.

## The fear and the remedy

From a 2015 recollection of student self-reproach to the 2026 press tour, the record keeps returning to two things. Fear is what he says drives him, in his own words since at least 2022. And his remedy is rarely to escape it. He builds supports: preparation he calls a crutch, information he refuses until it is safe, a director who tests him for three months, an editor he hopes has his back, a teacher he still thanks.

What would change this read? Testimony from long-term collaborators, given outside a promo cycle, would help most. If the post-_Clayface_ Harries starts describing his success as earned arrival, Three gains ground. If he starts chasing the next thrill and calling preparation a cage, Seven does. If the fear talk fades into easy, unbothered acceptance, Nine does. And a run of interviews in which he treats preparation as optional and stops crediting the people who steady him would count against Six, whatever the subtype.

On October 29, 2026, six days after his first studio lead opened at No. 1, he said "fundamentally nothing is going to change," while allowing that it might open a few more doors. Through this profile's lens, that is the remedy again: keep what held. A Nine could say the same sentence. What he reaches for the next time the ground moves will say more.
