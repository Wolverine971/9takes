<QuickAnswer question="Is Artificial a true story?">
Yes. <em>Artificial</em> dramatizes real events: OpenAI's board fired CEO Sam Altman on November 17, 2023, and within five days it had agreed to bring him back. Luca Guadagnino directed it from Simon Rich's screenplay, with Andrew Garfield as Altman and Yura Borisov as chief scientist Ilya Sutskever. Early reviews say it follows the real timeline, starting in 2003, but changes the motives, so treat its scenes and dialogue as the filmmakers' version. It premiered at the New York Film Festival on October 5, 2026, and Neon opens it in U.S. theaters on December 25.
</QuickAnswer>

<p class="firstLetter">On a Friday in November 2023, Sam Altman was in Las Vegas for the Formula 1 Grand Prix when he joined a Google Meet call. Minutes later, the board of the company he ran had removed him. Its statement said he had not been "consistently candid in his communications."</p>

Within five days, OpenAI had agreed to bring him back.

In between, the people around him read the same news in very different ways. Mira Murati, suddenly interim CEO, tried to keep the company from splitting. Ilya Sutskever voted Altman out on Friday and signed a letter demanding his return on Monday, by which time Greg Brockman had already quit. Microsoft's Satya Nadella offered Altman a landing spot while saying he still backed OpenAI.

_Artificial_ premiered at the New York Film Festival on October 5, and the first reviews describe two big choices. The film begins twenty years before that Friday, with a 17-year-old Sutskever walking into Geoffrey Hinton's office in Toronto. And it picks a hero and a villain. Awards Radar calls Borisov's Sutskever "the moral center of the film." The Playlist's Gregory Ellwood posted that Garfield gives Altman "the most epic character assassination ever."

The film builds its story around those two men. Here is everyone involved, and what the record shows about each of them.

## How accurate is Artificial?

Most people can't see it until December, so for now the best evidence is the premiere reviews. They describe a film that follows the real timeline but changes why people did what they did.

|                      | The film, per reviews                       | The record                                      |
| -------------------- | ------------------------------------------- | ----------------------------------------------- |
| Where it starts      | 2003, with a teenage Sutskever and Hinton   | Sutskever studied under Hinton in Toronto       |
| Why Altman was fired | He chases profit; Sutskever turns the board | The board's stated reason was candor            |
| Who voted            | Reads as Sutskever's coup                   | Four directors voted together                   |
| Altman               | "Changes his story" for each listener       | Disputed; WilmerHale found removal not mandated |

Film descriptions come from reviews in Deadline, Rolling Stone, and Awards Radar.

Deadline's review describes two breaking points for Sutskever. First, a model solves a problem by lying about it, and he says: "I thought AI could be like humans but better, not worse." Then, once ChatGPT takes off, Altman "goes for profit," and Sutskever "persuades the Board to fire him." In 2023 his written complaints were about Altman's honesty. The 52-page memo he sent the board opened: "Sam exhibits a consistent pattern of lying, undermining his execs, and pitting his execs against one another." His 2025 deposition showed that some of its claims had reached him secondhand and went unverified. Deadline also quotes a line that puts the candor question into dialogue: "A visionary is someone who lies to themselves. A liar is someone who lies to others. Which one is Altman?"

The reviews also skip three of the four directors who voted. None we read mentions D'Angelo, McCauley, or Toner, and in their telling the firing reads as Sutskever's act. Toner later explained her reasons publicly. Most of the cast is still unmatched to roles, so check this one in December.

Details drift fast once a story becomes a movie. IndieWire's first-reactions roundup said Altman was forced out by "a board that included Elon Musk." Musk left that board in 2018. Whether the film repeats that mistake is still unknown.

Every type on this page comes from an existing 9takes analysis built from the person's own words and documented actions: interviews, posts, sworn testimony, emails that became public. None comes from the trailer or the film. Garfield's choices about how Altman sounds when he's hurt, or how long he holds a pause, are evidence about Garfield, Rich, and Guadagnino.

## What actually happened in November 2023

| Date             | What happened                                                                                                                                                                                                                                            |
| ---------------- | -------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Friday, Nov. 17  | Directors Ilya Sutskever, Adam D'Angelo, Tasha McCauley, and Helen Toner remove Altman, saying he was not "consistently candid in his communications." Greg Brockman is removed as board chair and quits within hours. Mira Murati becomes interim CEO.  |
| Sunday, Nov. 19  | The board names former Twitch CEO Emmett Shear to replace Murati.                                                                                                                                                                                        |
| Monday, Nov. 20  | Microsoft says Altman and Brockman will lead a new AI research team there. 745 of OpenAI's 770 employees sign a letter threatening to quit unless the board resigns. Sutskever signs it and publicly regrets his "participation in the board's actions." |
| Within five days | OpenAI agrees to bring Altman back as CEO under a new board: Bret Taylor as chair, Larry Summers, and Adam D'Angelo.                                                                                                                                     |

The argument kept going long after the reinstatement. In March 2024, an outside review by the law firm WilmerHale concluded that Altman's conduct "did not mandate removal," and he rejoined the board. That May, former director Helen Toner said publicly that the board had "learned about ChatGPT on Twitter" and that Altman had given it "inaccurate information" about safety processes. Taylor and Summers rejected her account and pointed to the outside review. Sutskever left OpenAI the same month, and a month later he co-founded Safe Superintelligence.

The firing came up again during [Elon Musk's 2026 lawsuit against OpenAI](/pop-culture/musk-vs-altman-trial-personality-dynamics). In sworn testimony shown at trial, Murati was asked whether Altman had been candid with her. She paused, then said: "Not always." In May 2026 the jury decided the case on timing, so it never ruled on the 2023 firing.

## Five people, one Friday: what each one heard

That week had five people at the center. The Enneagram groups its nine types by three core alarms: shame, fear, and anger. Where 9takes has published a typing, the reaction below is read through that type's alarm. Where it hasn't, the section sticks to the record.

### Why the firing hit Altman as a verdict on who he is

The night he was fired, Altman posted that it felt "sorta like reading your own eulogy while you're still alive." Months later, he told Lex Fridman it had been "the most painful professional experience of my life, and chaotic and shameful and upsetting."

9takes reads him as a [Type 4](/enneagram-corner/enneagram-type-4) with a 3 wing, an Individualist who puts his identity into leading OpenAI. Shame is the alarm of the heart types, and a Four's specific dread is being exposed as ordinary, with no significance of his own. Being removed from the company you co-founded, in front of the whole world, hits that dread directly. The [full Altman analysis](/personality-analysis/sam-altman) reads the 745 signatures as the return on years of personal bonds.

### Mira Murati helped build the case, then fought to undo it

Her own sworn words about that weekend: "OpenAI was at catastrophic risk of falling apart. I was concerned about the company completely blowing up."

The New York Times reported that before the firing she had sent Altman a private memo questioning his management and shared concerns with the board. Sutskever's memo leaned heavily on her material. Murati has called her comments "all feedback Sam already knew." Then, as interim CEO, she worked to bring him back. That Sunday, Altman texted her asking whether things looked "directionally good or bad." She wrote back: "Directionally very bad."

9takes reads Murati as a [Type 9](/enneagram-corner/enneagram-type-9), the Peacemaker, with a 1 wing, and the [Murati analysis](/personality-analysis/mira-murati) reads the memo and the weekend as one pattern. Nines tend to voice anger quietly, in writing and through channels, and to avoid open blowups. When the blowup came, she spent the weekend trying to stop it.

### Sutskever's Friday vote and Monday signature

Sutskever co-founded OpenAI, ran its research as chief scientist, and in 2023 became co-lead of its new Superalignment safety project. His doubts about Altman were on paper six years before the vote. In a 2017 email to Musk and Altman titled "Honest thoughts," written for himself and Brockman, he told Altman: "We don't understand why the CEO title is so important to you. Your stated reasons have changed, and it's hard to really understand what's driving it."

He compiled the memo against Altman, some of it secondhand through Murati, and voted to remove him. Three days later he signed the employee letter and posted: "I deeply regret my participation in the board's actions. I never intended to harm OpenAI. I love everything we've built together and I will do everything I can to reunite the company." He left OpenAI in May 2024.

His post gives the same reason Murati gave under oath: the company was coming apart. Any typing of Sutskever would have to explain, from his own words, how Friday's vote and Monday's signature fit together.

### Greg Brockman quit within hours

Brockman learned on Friday that he had been removed as board chair. Within hours he posted a message to the team that ended: "But based on today's news, i quit." On Monday, Microsoft announced he would join Altman there. Within days he was back at OpenAI as president. Reviews describe Hoffman's Brockman as Altman's loyalist.

The record is more tangled than loyalty alone. The 2017 "Honest thoughts" email questioning Altman's interest in the CEO title spoke for Brockman too. In August 2024 he took a sabbatical that lasted until November, which the [Altman analysis](/personality-analysis/sam-altman) reads as the cost of his bond with Altman. At the 2026 Musk trial, lawyers confronted him with a 2017 journal entry asking what would take him to $1 billion. He testified that his stake was worth nearly $30 billion and maintained that money was never the point. 9takes hasn't published a Brockman analysis yet.

### Satya Nadella kept both doors open

Nadella isn't in the published cast listings, but his moves that weekend shaped the outcome. Microsoft had put roughly $13 billion into OpenAI. On Friday, it said it remained committed "to Mira and the team." By Monday it had a new team ready for Altman and Brockman. That day Nadella told CNBC that Microsoft wanted to keep partnering with OpenAI, adding: "Obviously, that depends on the people of OpenAI staying there or coming to Microsoft." In the 2026 trial record, he called the board's execution "amateur city."

9takes reads him as a [Type 5](/personality-analysis/satya-nadella), the Investigator, a leader who studies a problem before committing. Under fear, Fives prepare for every outcome before they move, and Nadella had a plan ready for both.

## The people who were gone by 2023

Three of the film's characters had no part in the 2023 vote.

### Elon Musk left five years earlier

Musk co-founded OpenAI in 2015 and left its board in early 2018, after his co-founders rejected his proposal to take majority control or fold OpenAI into Tesla. He told the team its "probability of success was 0." Reviews place him early in the film, courting Sutskever alongside Altman, and Rolling Stone says Barinholtz plays him as "part Bond villain and part grimacing buffoon."

9takes reads Musk as a [Type 5](/personality-analysis/elon-musk), someone who uses competence as shelter from helplessness. [The trial analysis](/pop-culture/musk-vs-altman-trial-personality-dynamics) sums up the Musk-Altman fight in a line each. Musk's version: give me the controls because I understand the danger. Altman's version: let me author the institution because I can keep the coalition together.

### Geoffrey Hinton taught the scientist who voted Altman out

Hinton never worked at OpenAI, yet reviews say the film opens with him, as the mentor who keeps urging Sutskever to slow down. The real link runs through Sutskever, his former doctoral student at the University of Toronto. In 2012 the two built AlexNet with Alex Krizhevsky, and Google bought their company the next year.

In May 2023, Hinton left Google so he could "talk about the dangers of AI without considering how this impacts Google." In October 2024, the day he won the Nobel Prize in Physics, he said he was proud that his former student had fired Altman. Later that year he backed a legal challenge to OpenAI's for-profit conversion, warning that letting the company "tear all of that up when it becomes inconvenient sends a very bad message to other actors in the ecosystem."

### Dario Amodei walked out over safety

In December 2020, three years before the firing, OpenAI's VP of research Dario Amodei left over how seriously the company was treating safety. Several colleagues followed, including his sister Daniela, and in 2021 they founded Anthropic. In Deadline's summary of the film, he is one of the early supporters who "fall away." 9takes reads him as [another Type 5](/personality-analysis/dario-amodei).

Anthropic shows up in the board crisis too. Toner had co-written a paper that cast Anthropic's safety approach more favorably than OpenAI's. She later alleged that Altman "started lying to other board members in order to try and push me off the board" over it. OpenAI's new board rejected her account.

## Why the winning take on this story keeps flipping

The internet collapses nine perspectives into one winning take. This story's winning take has already shifted twice, and the film turns it upside down.

In November 2023 the winning take was simple: a clumsy board ambushed a beloved founder, and 745 employees proved it. By mid-2024, Toner's account and the WilmerHale review had divided opinion. By 2026, a _New Yorker_ investigation and sworn trial testimony had put the board's concern about candor back on the record, two years after the outside review found it did not mandate removal.

By the reviews' account, _Artificial_ turns the first version over: Altman is the villain and Sutskever is the conscience. Both versions need a villain, and a story built around one villain has little room for Murati's weekend, Nadella's two doors, or Brockman's fast exit and faster return.

[The trial analysis](/pop-culture/musk-vs-altman-trial-personality-dynamics) argues that two things can be true: the board may have had serious reasons to distrust Altman, and it may have handled them so badly that he came back stronger. Whether the board was right about his candor is still open. Stop mistaking someone else's alarm for a defect, and Murati's memo and Sutskever's Monday signature start to make sense.
