<QuickAnswer question="Is Artificial a true story?">
Yes. <em>Artificial</em> dramatizes a real event: OpenAI's board fired CEO Sam Altman on November 17, 2023, and he was back in the job within five days. Luca Guadagnino directed it from a screenplay by Simon Rich, and Andrew Garfield plays Altman. The film is billed as a biographical comedy-drama, so its scenes and dialogue are the filmmakers' version. It premieres at the New York Film Festival on October 5, 2026, and Neon opens it in U.S. theaters on December 25, 2026.
</QuickAnswer>

<p class="firstLetter">On a Friday in November 2023, Sam Altman was at the Las Vegas Grand Prix when a Google Meet link arrived. Minutes later, the board of the company he ran had removed him. Its statement said he had not been "consistently candid in his communications."</p>

Within five days he was CEO again.

In between, a handful of people looked at the same facts and heard different alarms. One man felt his identity erased in public. One woman watched the company she had held together start to split. One director voted to fire Altman, then signed the letter demanding his return. One co-founder quit in six words. One investor had already drawn up a plan for either outcome.

_Artificial_, Luca Guadagnino's film about that week, runs 140 minutes and has one top-billed star. It has to decide whose week it was. This page gives you everyone else back: who the real people are, who plays them, what the record shows, and how each one read the crisis.

## What actually happened in November 2023

| Date             | What happened                                                                                                                                                                                                                                            |
| ---------------- | -------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Friday, Nov. 17  | Directors Ilya Sutskever, Adam D'Angelo, Tasha McCauley, and Helen Toner remove Altman, saying he was not "consistently candid in his communications." Greg Brockman is removed as board chair and quits within hours. Mira Murati becomes interim CEO.  |
| Sunday, Nov. 19  | The board names former Twitch CEO Emmett Shear to replace Murati.                                                                                                                                                                                        |
| Monday, Nov. 20  | Microsoft says Altman and Brockman will lead a new AI research team there. 745 of OpenAI's 770 employees sign a letter threatening to quit unless the board resigns. Sutskever signs it and publicly regrets his "participation in the board's actions." |
| Within five days | OpenAI agrees to bring Altman back as CEO under a new board: Bret Taylor as chair, Larry Summers, and Adam D'Angelo.                                                                                                                                     |

The argument kept going long after the reinstatement. In March 2024, an outside review by the law firm WilmerHale concluded that Altman's conduct "did not mandate removal," and he rejoined the board. In May 2024, Sutskever left OpenAI, and a month later he co-founded Safe Superintelligence. That same May, former director Helen Toner said publicly that the board had "learned about ChatGPT on Twitter" and that Altman had given it "inaccurate information" about safety processes. Altman has said he could be "more transparent" but has rarely engaged her specific claims.

Then the firing came back under oath. During [Elon Musk's 2026 lawsuit against OpenAI](/pop-culture/musk-vs-altman-trial-personality-dynamics), Murati was asked in sworn testimony whether Altman had been candid with her. She paused, then said: "Not always." The jury ended the case on timing in May 2026. Nobody in that courtroom ruled on who was right in 2023.

## Five people, one question: should Altman still run OpenAI?

9takes is built on one question, nine perspectives. That week had fewer than nine people at the center, but the method still works. Put one question to everyone involved, then look at the emotion underneath each answer.

The Enneagram sorts its nine types by three core alarms: shame, fear, and anger. Each person below was guarding something different. Each answer makes sense from inside that person's alarm.

### Sam Altman heard a verdict on who he is

9takes reads Altman as a [Type 4](/enneagram-corner/enneagram-type-4) with a 3 wing, an Individualist who pours his identity into leadership instead of moodiness. The night he was fired, he posted that it felt "sorta like reading your own eulogy while you're still alive." Months later, he described the experience to Lex Fridman in three words: "Chaotic. And shameful. And upsetting."

Shame is the alarm of the heart types. For a Four, the specific dread is being exposed as ordinary or unreal. Being removed from the company you co-founded, in front of the whole world, hits that dread directly. The [full Altman analysis](/personality-analysis/sam-altman) reads the 745 signatures as the return on years of personal bonds. People chose him because of how he had related to them for years. Authority had little to do with it.

### Mira Murati heard the company coming apart

9takes reads Murati as a [Type 9](/enneagram-corner/enneagram-type-9), the Peacemaker, with a 1 wing. Her own sworn words about that weekend: "OpenAI was at catastrophic risk of falling apart. I was concerned about the company completely blowing up."

Her role is the hardest one to compress into a movie scene. The New York Times reported that before the firing she had sent Altman a private memo questioning his management and shared concerns with the board. Sutskever's memo to the board leaned heavily on her material. Murati has called her comments "all feedback Sam already knew." Then, as interim CEO, she worked to bring him back. On Sunday night Altman texted her asking whether things looked "directionally good or bad." She wrote back: "Directionally very bad."

From outside, that looks like a reversal. The [Murati analysis](/personality-analysis/mira-murati) reads it as one consistent move. A Nine's anger travels quietly, in writing and through channels. What a Nine cannot do is want the explosion. When the explosion came, she spent the weekend trying to stop it.

### Ilya Sutskever voted, then signed the letter

9takes hasn't published a Sutskever analysis, and a movie performance is no shortcut to one. Here is what the record shows.

He co-founded OpenAI, ran its research as chief scientist, and in 2023 became co-lead of its new Superalignment safety project. His doubts about Altman were on paper years before the vote. In a 2017 email titled "Honest Thoughts," later published by _The New Yorker_, he wrote: "We don't understand why the CEO title is so important to you. Your stated reasons have changed, and it's hard to really understand what's driving it."

Before the firing, he sent the board a 52-page memo accusing Altman of lying, manipulating executives, and creating internal division. He voted to remove him. Three days later he signed the employee letter demanding Altman's return and posted that he regretted his "participation in the board's actions." In May 2024 he left.

The question his record poses is specific: what changed between Friday's vote and Monday's signature? Any honest typing of Sutskever has to answer it from his own words.

### Greg Brockman quit in six words

Brockman learned on Friday that he had been removed as board chair. Within hours he posted: "Based on today's news, I quit." On Monday, Microsoft announced he would join Altman there. Within days he was back at OpenAI as president.

His loyalty moved as fast as anyone's that week. In August 2024 he took a sabbatical that lasted until November, which the [Altman analysis](/personality-analysis/sam-altman) reads as the cost of that bond. At the 2026 Musk trial, lawyers confronted him with a 2017 journal entry asking what would take him to $1 billion. He testified that his stake was worth nearly $30 billion and maintained that money was never the point. 9takes hasn't published a Brockman analysis yet. When it does, that fast loyalty is the first thing it has to explain.

### Satya Nadella had already mapped both exits

Nadella doesn't appear in the published cast listings, but his moves that weekend shaped the outcome. 9takes reads him as a [Type 5](/personality-analysis/satya-nadella), the Investigator: a leader who observes, builds a model, and then commits. Microsoft had put roughly $13 billion into OpenAI.

On Friday, Microsoft said it remained committed "to Mira and the team." By Monday it had a new team ready for Altman and Brockman. In a CNBC interview that Monday, Nadella said: "We know what we need to do either way." Fives handle fear by building the map before they move, and his map covered both outcomes. In the 2026 trial record, he called the board's execution "amateur city."

## The people who weren't on that board

Two of the film's characters had no seat in that boardroom in 2023. A third central figure isn't in the announced cast at all.

### Elon Musk left five years earlier

Musk co-founded OpenAI in 2015 and left its board in early 2018, after his co-founders rejected his proposal to take majority control or fold OpenAI into Tesla. He told the team its "probability of success was 0." He played no part in the 2023 firing. Ike Barinholtz still plays him, and how much of the film he occupies is something we'll learn on October 5.

9takes reads Musk as a [Type 5](/personality-analysis/elon-musk), someone who uses competence as shelter from helplessness. [The trial analysis](/pop-culture/musk-vs-altman-trial-personality-dynamics) puts the Musk-Altman fight in one line each. Musk's version: give me the controls because I understand the danger. Altman's version: let me author the institution because I can keep the coalition together. In May 2026, a jury found Musk had waited too long to sue.

### Geoffrey Hinton taught the scientist who voted Altman out

Hinton never worked at OpenAI. His link to the story runs through Sutskever, his former doctoral student at the University of Toronto. In 2012 the two built AlexNet with Alex Krizhevsky, and Google bought their company the next year.

In May 2023, Hinton left Google so he could "talk about the dangers of AI without considering how this impacts Google." In October 2024, days after winning the Nobel Prize in Physics, he said he was proud that his former student had fired Altman. Later he joined a letter urging state attorneys general to block OpenAI's for-profit conversion, and wrote: "I would like them to execute that mission instead of enriching their investors." 9takes hasn't typed him. Why a film about a boardroom casts Mark Rylance as Hinton is one of the things the premiere will answer.

### Dario Amodei walked out first

The fault line under 2023 opened three years earlier. In December 2020, Dario Amodei, then OpenAI's VP of research, left over how seriously the company was treating safety. Fourteen researchers followed him, and in early 2021 they founded Anthropic. 9takes reads him as [another Type 5](/personality-analysis/dario-amodei).

Anthropic shows up in the board crisis too. Toner had co-written a paper that cast Anthropic's safety approach more favorably than OpenAI's. She later alleged that Altman "started lying to other board members in order to try and push me off the board" over it.

## Why every winning take on this story has flipped

The internet collapses nine perspectives into one winning take. This story has had at least three.

In November 2023 the winning take was simple: a clumsy board ambushed a beloved founder, and 745 employees proved it. By mid-2024, Toner's account and the WilmerHale review had split the room. By 2026, a _New Yorker_ investigation and sworn trial testimony had put the board's original concern about candor back on the record. Each version felt complete to the people holding it.

On December 25, a 140-minute film will hand millions of viewers a fourth version, with a lead actor and a third act. For many of them, it will be the only version they ever see. A movie has to choose a center. That's the form doing its job, and it's also how eight perspectives disappear.

[The trial analysis](/pop-culture/musk-vs-altman-trial-personality-dynamics) holds both readings at once: the board may have had serious reasons to distrust Altman, and it may have handled them so badly that he came back stronger. The type lens adds the layer underneath. Altman's alarm is shame, so a firing sounded like a eulogy. Murati's buried anger sits on top of a dread of things breaking apart, so the person whose concerns fed the case spent the weekend holding the company together. Nadella's alarm is fear, so he had both exits drawn before Monday.

None of those reactions is a defect. Each is an alarm doing its job, sometimes badly. Stop mistaking someone else's alarm for a defect, and the five days stop looking like a story with one villain.
