Daniela Amodei stopped eating meat before her brother did. "It's like the only thing that I did first," she told Walter Isaacson in an [October 2026 interview](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=1OdoSzfUkec). "Dario did everything first because he's older."

Dario guessed he followed her "three years later."

"Oh, not even," she said. "You got on board real quick."

He had a four-year head start on everything else. "He had four years without me. Poor guy," she joked on [CNBC](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=GMXnmaky9FY) in January 2026.

He got to Lowell, San Francisco's most competitive public high school, first. Classmates told the [San Francisco Chronicle](https://www.sfchronicle.com/tech/article/dario-daniela-amodei-lowell-high-school-22491736.php) he had finished high school math by freshman year and took math at colleges off campus. He graduated in 2001. Daniela graduated in 2006, by which point he was in graduate school at Princeton studying biophysics.

He found their cause first. He was "a very early GiveWell fan," Daniela said on a [2022 podcast](https://futureoflife.org/podcast/daniela-and-dario-amodei-on-anthropic/), and as students the two "started donating small amounts of money" to global health charities.

He found AI first, too. "He had this really strong conviction that artificial intelligence is going to be a big deal," she said at [Bloomberg Tech](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Kfz-NxEvu-U) in June 2026, years before most people believed it. "This is like early 2010s." He joined Baidu's AI lab in 2014, Google in 2015 and OpenAI in 2016.

## How Daniela followed her brother into AI

Daniela has never been shy about following him. She went into international development partly because "it was an interest area of Dario's at the time," she said in 2022.

> "I'm a few years younger than him and so I was very keen to understand the things that he was working or interested in"

"I was always so inspired by how he used that for good," she told [_The Oprah Podcast_](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=w5dJqHilu5s) in May 2026. Asked by Arianna Huffington, in an [interview posted this October](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=IEQJmtTqY2g), to name one person who inspired her, she apologized in advance: "I hope this won't come across as corny, but Dario is a person who has inspired me a lot in my life."

There's one twist. The time she got somewhere first, she held the door for him. Greg Brockman had been her boss at Stripe, and when he was starting OpenAI, she pitched him her brother. "The smartest person that I know is Dario," she told him, as she recalled in [Anthropic's co-founder video](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=om2lIWXLLN4). "You would be really lucky to get him." Dario joined OpenAI in 2016. Brockman recruited Daniela two years later, and she followed. She believed in AI's potential, she joked, "because, I mean, I know Dario a little bit."

Dario tells it as a joint project. "Every career move we would discuss," he told Oprah. "Our paths were quite different. But the two kind of converged."

Today the younger sister runs the company day to day. By June 2026 every Anthropic executive except Dario's chief of staff reported to her, [TechCrunch reported](https://techcrunch.com/2026/06/10/anthropics-dario-amodei-has-just-one-direct-report/). Anthropic's [IPO registration statement](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/2130357/000162828026067168/anthropicpbc-sx1.htm), filed October 26, 2026, lists him as chief executive and her as president and chair of the board. It includes a line most filings never need: the two "are siblings." The girl who followed him into everything now chairs the board he answers to.

## Best friends who still fight

"We were always just like best friends like since we were really little," Daniela told Isaacson. When she was in high school, Dario said at a [Bloomberg event in 2024](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=SAu2jgAF1i8), "every week we'd play the original Final Fantasy 7" on the PlayStation. They still play every week, now the remake on a PS5.

They also fight. "Dario and I have been fighting and getting over it for almost 40 years," she told a [Stanford GSB audience](https://www.gsb.stanford.edu/insights/daniela-amodei-says-curiosity-underrated) in 2026, "and there's no question we will love each other at the end of that, no matter what." When they disagree, she starts with a question: "why do you see it that way? Because usually he'll have a piece of information that I don't have." About 80 to 90 percent of the time, she said at Bloomberg Tech, they meet in the middle.

At work they split the job cleanly. On the [Cheeky Pint podcast](https://cheekypint.transistor.fm/5/transcript) in August 2025, Stripe co-founder John Collison asked what it's like to start a company with a sibling. Dario said running a company takes two jobs. Somebody has to "operationally execute." Somebody has to set the strategy and "see the most important thing or the thing that no one else sees." He does the seeing. She does the executing. When Collison raised the trust that comes with a lifelong relationship, Dario agreed: "Yeah, yeah, where you have just total and complete trust."

Daniela puts the split in years. He is "often thinking about like the five to 10 year time frame," she said on [Notion's _First Block_](https://www.notion.com/blog/first-block-with-daniela-amodei), while her role is "the one to two year time frame." She repeats their chief people officer's summary with approval: "Dario makes us ambitious and I make us successful."

## What are Dario and Daniela Amodei's Enneagram types?

The Enneagram sorts people by the emotion that drives them. Its nine types fall into three groups. The head types run on fear, the heart types on shame, and the gut types on anger.

[Dario Amodei](/personality-analysis/dario-amodei) most likely fits [Type 6](/enneagram-corner/enneagram-type-6), the Loyalist, a head type. A Six's fear rarely looks like panic. It looks like vigilance: a mind that runs ahead to what could go wrong, argues with its own conclusions, and goes looking for people and rules it can trust. Sixes tend to be intellectual about their fear. They think their way through it. Type 5 is his strongest alternative.

[Daniela Amodei](/personality-analysis/daniela-amodei) most likely fits [Type 1](/enneagram-corner/enneagram-type-1), the Reformer, a gut type. A One's anger rarely explodes either. It shows up as steady irritation when something isn't right and an urge to fix it. A One's quiet faith is that things go well when people do them right. Types 2 and 6 stay live for her. Both readings are medium confidence, built from interviews and reporting.

Put simply, Dario plans for it all to go wrong. Daniela plans for it to go right, provided everyone does the work properly. Anthropic is built on both plans.

## Dario has been planning for the worst since high school

At 16, Dario was worried about Y2K. His AP Physics teacher at Lowell, Richard Shapiro, told the Chronicle that Dario was "literally upset and worried about it." Shapiro thought the worst case was a few days of hassle. "I wasn't in the end-of-the-world camp, and Dario was."

Two decades later he looked at GPT-2. In 2019 it could summarize a document and write a few lines of code, none of it impressive on its own. Dario saw where scaling would take it. "I personally was super freaked out by it," he told Isaacson. "It really scared me." Then came the questions: "Is this the right place to be developing it?" And: "Who do we tell about this?"

First the alarm, then a search for the right people to tell. That's the Six sequence.

The vigilance never switched off. On keeping Anthropic's values as it grows, he told [Bloomberg](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=x2VHFgyawPE) in June 2026, "I've been paranoid at every scale." Asked how much he sleeps: "I've never been someone who slept all that well."

He writes the way a Six thinks. His January 2026 essay, [_The Adolescence of Technology_](https://darioamodei.com/essay/the-adolescence-of-technology), argues that "it's important to be paranoid and to try to obtain safety and reliability in several different, independent ways." It also keeps arguing with itself: "The best possible counterargument is…" and "There are plenty of ways in which the concerns I'm raising in this piece could be moot."

He doesn't call himself a doomer, though, and it's worth taking him at his word. In [_Machines of Loving Grace_](https://darioamodei.com/essay/machines-of-loving-grace) he wrote that people assume he's "a pessimist or 'doomer'" and answered: "I don't think that at all." The risks, he argues, are "the only thing standing between us and what I see as a fundamentally positive future." For a Six, fear is a planning tool more than a forecast. His also has a personal edge. His father died of a disease that, by his account, became curable a few years too late, and he cites that loss as a reason for urgency. Our [Dario profile](/personality-analysis/dario-amodei) covers that in full.

## Daniela plans for it to go right

Daniela leads with the other half of the sentence. Near the end of the Isaacson interview, talking about what people will do once AI handles much of the work, she allowed, "I obviously don't think it's going to be that simple." Then:

> "So I think if we do things right, there's the ability to actually expand what our sources of meaning are"

She makes the same move with the risks. "We're going to have to get all of those things right," she told [ABC News](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=qUDG06BvWxc) in February 2026, after listing risk mitigation, legislation and partnerships with policymakers, "in order for us to realize all of the incredible benefits." Dario uses the same phrase, but he opens with the risk. She opens with the fix.

The anger in her is the One's kind. It's aimed at a wrong, and it moves toward a repair. "There's something that feels really wrong to me about the fact that I was born in America," she told Stanford GSB, explaining her first career. Then she asked herself: "How do I make that more fair?" Inside Anthropic, going behind a colleague's back to avoid a hard conversation gets a flat rule: "We have no tolerance for that."

Her optimism has a condition attached, and the condition can scare her. "It's scary because we can lose it at any time," she said, in remarks [The Atlantic](https://www.theatlantic.com/technology/2026/09/openai-v-anthropic-inside-biggest-rivalry-tech/688819/) published in September 2026. "Are we accidentally making things worse?" A Six fears what's coming. A One fears doing it wrong.

On Oprah's show the two halves fit into a single sentence. Dario started it:

> "I think we worry about it every day, and the fact..."

Daniela finished it:

> "that we worry about it every day is maybe the thing that most gives us hope"

He supplied the worry. She turned it into a reason for confidence.

## Why Dario trusts his sister to run the machine

Every two weeks Dario talks to the whole company for an hour. The sessions are known as DVQs, for "Dario vision quest," a name he says he resisted because it makes him sound like he's off "smoking peyote." The first rule is that nothing he says is a decision, which "allows me to just speak freely." A CEO who thinks out loud by design needs someone else to own what becomes real. At Anthropic, that's the person the executive team reports to.

He calls the arrangement "incredibly freeing." The division of labor, he told Isaacson, "allows both things to be done to a much higher standard" than either would be done alone. On Oprah's show he began one answer: "I'd just say the same thing as Daniela in different words."

For a Six, the scarcest resource is a person whose judgment he doesn't have to re-check. Our Dario profile makes the point from the org chart: the time he guards is guarded by his sister. The trust runs both ways. "Dario is probably the person who can kind of see the future," she said on CNBC.

To be clear about the record: Dario praises her role and their trust in general terms. He hasn't described a specific call where he deferred to her over his own read.

## Daniela corrects the signal

Daniela does correct her brother in public, though gently. More often she adjusts his signal. When his alarm runs hot, she turns the volume down. When it matters, she backs him.

When Isaacson assumed GPT-2 had frightened her as much as it frightened Dario, she declined the premise: "There wasn't something about GPT2, the model itself that made us, you know, concerned." The model was "kind of laughably wrong about a lot of things," she said. What she took from it was the trajectory, the way you'd watch a toddler start to walk and picture the adult.

Dario's best-known warning is that AI could wipe out half of entry-level white-collar jobs. At Bloomberg Tech, asked about job losses, she didn't repeat his number. "I don't necessarily think we know the future," she said. Anthropic's research so far, she added, finds replacement is "a tiny tiny tiny fraction" of what AI is doing.

Sometimes she adds what he left out. When the talk with Isaacson turned to the scientists who mocked GPT-2 in 2019, she told Dario, "I think you were quite polite about it," then described critics who bullied his researchers online. Dario delivered the verdict: "You have no credibility on this matter."

She wants the same correction built into the product. Asked whether Claude could turn manipulative, Daniela called it "a constant, constant worry." The flaw she singles out is sycophancy, a model telling people what they want to hear, which she says is "harder to stamp out" than lying or harmful answers. Rivals who sell ads, she argued, earn more when users stay longer. "Do you then want to tell your user that they're wrong? No, you don't."

She submits to the correction herself. She uploads her own upward feedback to Claude, she told Stanford GSB, and sometimes it tells her, kindly, "Maybe you should get some extra coaching, Daniela."

## Same deal, two questions

In February 2026 the Pentagon demanded "any lawful use" of Anthropic's models, which meant dropping the company's two exceptions: mass domestic surveillance and fully autonomous weapons. Anthropic refused. The S-1 records the cost: a presidential directive telling federal agencies to stop using its technology. OpenAI then announced its own deal with the military.

Isaacson asked how that felt. Daniela answered first, and she went to principle:

> "was this a really principled stand that another technology company took based on their own ethics or was this an opportunistic moment?"

Dario went to the system: "I actually think it's a beautiful illustration of the democratic process." Minutes earlier he had counted it as a win that Congress was moving to write their red lines into law: "That's success."

Each answer fits the type. The One measured the other company's act against a standard. The Six went to the structure meant to hold everyone, and his case against autonomous weapons comes from the same place. Human soldiers can refuse an illegal order. An AI can't. So he fears "one person having their finger on a button that controls a million robots."

## At OpenAI, Dario named the threat and Daniela held the line

The fullest picture of them under pressure comes from OpenAI between 2018 and 2020. The Wall Street Journal and The New Yorker reported it in 2026, mostly from unnamed sources and, in one case, from Dario's own notes at the time. Daniela hasn't described these episodes publicly, and Sam Altman disputes parts of them.

The man she had recommended her brother to became the first test. Brockman wanted a role in the early GPT research. Dario, then research director, "wanted him nowhere near it," the [Journal reports](https://www.tovima.com/wsj/the-decadelong-feud-shaping-the-future-of-ai/). Daniela, who co-led the project, told Brockman no. When Altman pushed, "she offered to step down as head of the project rather than allow Brockman on it."

Later Altman told the siblings he had it on "good authority" that they were plotting a coup, according to [The New Yorker](https://www.newyorker.com/magazine/2026/04/13/sam-altman-may-control-our-future-can-he-be-trusted). Daniela "lost it" and brought in the executive Altman had named, who denied saying anything. When Altman then denied making the claim, she answered: "You just said that." Altman told the magazine it was "not quite his recollection."

In December 2020 seven people left OpenAI to start Anthropic, and Daniela led their side of the exit negotiations. The pattern holds in both stories. Dario names the threat. Daniela enforces the boundary and puts the facts in the room. In the Brockman case her standard and her loyalty pointed the same way. For a Six, that's the ideal partner: the person he trusts most is also the one enforcing the rule.

## They built a company that can outvote them

The S-1 gives their caution a legal form. Control of the public company runs mainly through one share of Class F stock that carries 50.1% of stockholder voting power. It's held by an LLC whose members are Anthropic's seven co-founders, and the LLC votes "as determined by a majority of the members." The siblings hold two votes of seven, which means the other five can outvote them. The S-1 also states: "Each co-founder is paid equal compensation."

That fits both types. A Six distrusts any single point of control, his own included. A One wants rules that bind the rule-maker. Daniela told Oprah that anyone at Anthropic can disagree with the founders, anonymously if they want, so that "if we start to stray from the path we're at least going to know."

One check is missing: the board's chair is the CEO's sister. Their answer is the co-founder majority and forty years of trust. Shareholders will decide whether that's enough.

## The blind spot in a Six and One partnership

A Six sorts the world by who can be trusted. A One sorts it by what's right. When the siblings agree, nothing inside the pair pushes back. Some OpenAI leaders saw Daniela, Dario and her husband, Holden Karnofsky, as "an inflexible triumvirate," The Atlantic reports, and some there call the Amodeis "power-hungry virtue-signalers, not principled humanitarians."

Their best defense is what their stands have cost. The Pentagon refusal brought a federal ban. Keeping ads out of Claude gave up revenue that investors pushed for. Still, a pair this sure of its own good faith can miss the moment it's wrong, and the co-founder majority exists for exactly that moment.

## What going public will test

Keeping ads out of Claude was "never a question" for the co-founders, Daniela told Oprah, though "there were quiet forces," inside the company and among investors, who saw "a great source of revenue." Her answer:

> "sorry, that's just not how we're going to do things. We'll find another way."

Public shareholders are a louder force than any private investor. Four signs will show whether the partnership keeps its shape:

- **Claude stays ad-free.** It's her line, and the easiest one to check.
- **The red lines survive the courts.** Anthropic largely won in district court in August 2026, then lost before a split appeals panel in September. It was seeking a rehearing as of the filing.
- **Dario keeps one direct report.** If that changes, the division of labor has changed.
- **All seven co-founders stay.** The founder majority only works as a check while the other five are in the room.

## If you're the Dario or the Daniela

Most working pairs have a version of this split. One person sees what could go wrong in five years. The other fixes what's off this week.

If you're the Dario, your alarm is information. Dario was worried about GPT-2 while, by his count, about 90 percent of the people around him shrugged. But an alarm with nobody to hand it to stays a worry. He handed his to his sister and gave her every executive but one. Find the person who turns your five-year fear into a two-year plan, then let them run it.

If you're the Daniela, aim your correction at a standard you both answer to. "Is this in keeping with our values," the test she says they hold each other to, lands differently than "you're wrong." And before you correct the call, ask her question first: what does he know that I don't?

Mapping a pair like this is how you stop mistaking your partner's alarm for a defect, and their correction for an attack.
